# The Galley (Brass's cooking, the Gut Check, and sickbay tonics) **[HR]**

Brass cooks breakfast and dinner aboard the *Night Fury*. She is a medic first and a cook under protest, and the food is a gamble the crew has made peace with. This doc turns the running bit into something you can roll at the table in ten seconds: the **Galley Roll** (what's on the plate), the **Gut Check** (the Con save you've been calling for fun), and what good and bad meals actually do. Keep it fast. A meal should resolve in one roll, one save, and one line from Brass.

Character material, scenes, and arc beats for Brass live in `Brass - Scenes & Bits.md`. Her stat block is in `Stat Blocks.md`.

---

## 1. The Galley Roll

**When:** each morning Brass is on breakfast, and at any dinner you want to matter. Roll it openly so the table can groan or cheer. One roll covers the whole crew's meal.

**The die is a d20.** Older docs say d4; the table has been rolling a d20 since session 1 (the octopus was a natural 20), so this makes that official. If you'd rather keep the d4, see the conversion at the end of this section.

| d20 | Result | Gut Check | What it means |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Galley Incident** | **DC 15** | Something went wrong in a way that will be discussed for weeks. Roll on the Bad table, then make it worse. |
| **2–6** | **Rough** | **DC 12** | Roll on the Bad table. Edible in the technical sense. |
| **7–11** | **Edible** | none (DC 8 on seconds) | Fine. Filling. Named something grand. Nobody mentions it. |
| **12–16** | **Solid** | none | Genuinely decent. Someone says so out loud and Brass pretends not to hear. |
| **17–19** | **Good** | none | Roll on the Good table. Everyone who eats is **Well Fed** (section 3). |
| **20** | **A Brass Classic** | none | Roll on the Good table, or reach for the Hall of Fame. **Well Fed** plus **5 temporary hit points** until the next long rest. |

**Modifiers (stack them, and advantage and disadvantage cancel as normal):**
- **Fresh from port:** +2 for the first three mornings after a real restock. A restock is **10 gp** on top of the monthly operating cost (it's Aerion's ledger line to approve or refuse).
- **A gift of ingredients:** someone brings Brass something specific (Orena's 3 gp basket in session 2 is the model). **Advantage** on the next roll that uses it. Brass remembers who gave it, on a napkin.
- **A second pair of hands:** a helper proficient with cook's utensils grants **advantage**. Anyone else who helps adds **+1** and a scene.
- **Stale stores:** **-2** once the ship is ten or more days out from its last port.
- **Brass ran dry:** **disadvantage** if she spent every leveled spell slot since her last long rest, or went down in a fight. She cooked with what was left of her. "Medic first."
- **Heward's Handy Spice Pouch** (`Tables.md`, if the crew ever finds one): up to five times a day, a Rough result becomes Edible. Brass with this item is dangerous.
- **The galley at 0300:** no roll. It's always Good, never a Classic, and off the clock she will not admit she cooked at all.

**Someone else cooks.** If Brass is laid up, busy, or the crew stages a coup: the cook rolls **d20 + Wisdom modifier**, plus proficiency with cook's utensils. A natural 1 is still an Incident and a natural 20 is still a Classic. Same table, same saves. (It is fine and funny if Oz turns out to be reliably, boringly Solid.)

**Keeping the d4 instead:** 1 Rough, 2 Edible, 3 Solid, 4 Good. On a 1, roll the d4 again: a second 1 is a Galley Incident. On a 4, roll again: a second 4 is a Classic. Modifiers become advantage or disadvantage only.

## 2. What's on the plate

Brass names every dish, and the name has nothing to do with what's in it. Say the name first, let the table react, then say what it actually is.

### Bad table (d10, for Rough and Galley Incident)

| d10 | Brass calls it | What it actually is | The tell |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Nebula Porridge** | Oats, reconstituted milk, and a spoonful of something purple she swears is fruit. | It is still warm an hour later. It shouldn't be. |
| 2 | **Captain's Hash** | Yesterday's dinner, fried until it gave up. | Estra eats it without comment, which is somehow worse. |
| 3 | **Starboard Stew** | Brown. Just brown. There are shapes in it. | Finny fishes out a button. "Is this a garnish?" |
| 4 | **Void Loaf** | Hardtack she tried to revive with steam. It is now denser than before. | Oz knocks on it politely. It knocks back. |
| 5 | **Comet Tails** | Dried fish she rehydrated in the wrong liquid. | The whole deck smells of it by the second watch. |
| 6 | **Deep Space Frittata** | Powdered eggs, and the powder won. | It squeaks when you cut it. |
| 7 | **The Bral Special** | A "local delicacy" she bought from her guy at the last port. She won't say what it is. | The guy, it turns out, does not eat it either. |
| 8 | **Tidewater Chowder** | A good idea (Orena mentioned Leilon once) made with the wrong fish. | It is almost a nice gesture. It is almost soup. |
| 9 | **Sunspot Surprise** | Brass discovered a new pepper. | Tack's heat sensor pings from across the room. |
| 10 | **Mystery Crate Curry** | Crate 14-C. The manifest says medical. Aerion says it's ammunition. It was neither. | Aerion stares at the ledger for a long time afterward. |

**Galley Incident: make it worse** (pick one or roll a d6): 1 it's still moving, a little · 2 the stove caught and Finny has already turned it off (he always does) · 3 it has stained the pot permanently, and Tack is upset about the pot · 4 Flurry sniffs it, sneezes frost, and leaves the room · 5 it is somehow both burnt and raw · 6 Brass tasted it first, went quiet, and served it anyway "on principle."

### The Hall of Fame

Classics that happened at the table. A Hall of Fame dish cooked again with the right ingredients gets **+5** to its Galley Roll. It never becomes automatic; Brass has tried.
- **The Octopus** (session 1, before departure from Bral). A natural 20, universally loved, in front of witnesses. Aerion offered, unprompted, to fund future octopus nights. That offer is his to keep or quietly forget; if a port sells octopus, mention the price and let the ledger decide.

## 3. The Gut Check

**What it is:** a **Constitution saving throw** against a meal. Call it on a Rough or Incident result, on seconds of anything Edible (DC 8), or whenever someone eats something a sensible person wouldn't. Everyone who ate rolls at once. Keep it loud and quick.

**Brass eats first.** Medic's rule. She rolls her own Gut Check (+1) before anyone else. If she fails, she knows, and she says so ("...okay. That one's on me."). Anyone may then **decline** the meal: no save, no effect, and Brass takes it personally for exactly one day. If she passes, nobody gets a warning.

**Results:**
- **Pass:** nothing happens, and you may brag. On an Incident, a pass earns a **mark** on the Iron Gut tally (section 5).
- **Fail:** you are **Queasy** until your next short rest.
- **Fail an Incident:** you are **Poisoned for 1 hour**, then Queasy until your next short rest.
- **Fail by 5 or more:** Finny gives you a nickname that lasts the week. Let the table suggest it.

**Queasy** (house condition): **disadvantage on Stealth checks** (your stomach is narrating) and on the **first Constitution saving throw** you make before it ends. It ends on a short rest, a Brass tonic (section 4), or *lesser restoration* (Brass will not spend a slot on it, and says so).

**Well Fed** (from a Good or Classic meal): **advantage on your first Constitution saving throw** before your next long rest. A Classic adds **5 temporary hit points**.

**The crew's Gut Checks at a glance:**
- **Estra +2.** Eats everything, comments on nothing. Fifteen years of worse.
- **Oz +2.** Thanks Brass for every meal, including this one, and means it. Asks permission before declining.
- **Finny +1.** Halfling **Lucky** applies, so he rerolls his 1s. Complains the loudest anyway.
- **Brass +1.** Always first. Never apologizes for the food, only for the medicine.
- **Tack.** Doesn't eat (construct). Attends every meal anyway, sets his own place, and offers a sensor reading of each dish. "Carbon content: ambitious."
- **Flurry.** Doesn't roll. She is the canary: **Flurry will eat a boot.** If Flurry won't eat it, raise the DC by 2, and everyone saw her walk away.
- **Orena and Aerion** roll their own Constitution saves. Whether either of them eats the Rough stuff in the first place is their players' call.

**Other Gut Checks worth calling:**
- **Brass's hot sauce, "Sunspot":** DC 13. Pass and she respects you. Fail and you're Queasy, and she respects you a little.
- **Leftovers of unknown age:** DC 10, +1 per day Brass can't account for.
- **Port street food:** DC 10 at a respectable stall, DC 14 in Bral's Low Side.
- **Other peoples' cuisine:** a giff mess-hall meat pie (DC 12, Oz insists it's mild), mercane ceremonial tea (DC 11, served at a temperature that isn't a temperature), anything Gary "Sugary" of the Burnouts cooked (DC 10, it's actually excellent, the save is for the grudge).

## 4. Sickbay Tonics

Where the medic and the cook meet. Brass brews these during a **short rest** from sickbay stores: **2 gp of supplies each** (Aerion's ledger), **one tonic per person per long rest**. They all taste terrible. Drinking one is a **DC 10 Gut Check**, but the tonic **works either way**; the save only decides your dignity. On a fail, you make a face the whole table sees, and Brass says, "Good. That means it's working."

| Brass calls it | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Liquid Sunrise** | Ends Queasy, and Poisoned if it came from food. Advantage on saves against poison for 8 hours. |
| **Comet Tea** | Drunk during a short rest: reroll 1s on any Hit Dice you spend on that rest. |
| **Homesick Soup** | For drift-sickness and the astral's pull on memory. Advantage on the next Wisdom saving throw within 8 hours against an effect that alters memory or perception. |
| **Deck Legs** | Advantage on the next check or save within 8 hours against being knocked prone or thrown off balance by gravity shifts. |

Brass won't brew anything she can't explain. If someone asks for something stronger, that's a license question, and a scene (`Brass - Scenes & Bits.md`).

## 5. Trackers (optional, Brass keeps them on napkins)

**The Iron Gut tally.** Each passed Gut Check against a Galley Incident is a mark. Three marks earns **Cast-Iron**: advantage on Gut Checks from then on, and Brass stops watching you eat. Only Incidents count, so this takes months. Brass announces each mark at breakfast like a promotion.

**Operation Clean Plate.** Established in session 2: Brass's real goal isn't the license, it's getting Aerion (who eats almost nothing but white rice and dry toast) to finish three of her meals. **Count: 0 of 3.** A plate counts only if Priscilla decides Aerion finishes it. Never assume or script it. Her attempts, in rough escalating order (pick or roll a d6):
1. **The yogurt parfait.** Already announced. Layered in a glass so he can see there is nothing hidden in it.
2. **Plain Rice, Improved** (Good table 9), with the optional dish placed a careful distance from the rice.
3. **A toast flight.** Four toasts, four breads, dry. She wrote tasting notes.
4. **Congee.** It's technically rice. She'll argue this point.
5. **Octopus again**, on his own tab, since he offered.
6. **Nothing.** She stops trying for a week, leaves a plate at his station at 0300, and never mentions it.

If the count ever reaches 3, see the payoff in `Brass - Scenes & Bits.md`.

**The Hall of Fame** (section 2). Add every Classic the table remembers.

## 6. Running it without slowing the table

- **Roll in the cold open.** The Galley Roll is a perfect first die of the night: it sets the mood in one number and gives every NPC a line.
- **Don't roll every meal.** Once per in-game day is plenty, and skip it entirely on days the session is busy.
- **Counterweight after a hot session.** If last session ran dark, fudge toward Good or let the 0300 galley happen. A Classic is a cheap, honest way to buy back warmth.
- **Failure is the fun.** Gabrielle plays Orena's whiffs straight. A failed Gut Check is a chance for a warm, silly beat, never a punishment.
- **The mechanics are small on purpose.** Queasy and Well Fed are nudges. If a Gut Check is about to decide a real fight, that's fine once; if it keeps happening, the food is doing too much work.
