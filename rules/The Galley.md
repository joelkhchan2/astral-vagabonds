# The Galley (Brass's cooking and the Gut Check) **[HR]**

Brass cooks breakfast and dinner aboard the *Night Fury*. She is a medic first and a cook under protest, and the food is a gamble the crew has made peace with. One roll for the meal. A good one is a small Heroes' Feast; a bad one means a Con save. One line from Brass. That's the whole system.

Character material, scenes, and arc beats for Brass live in `Brass - Scenes & Bits.md`. Her stat block is in `Stat Blocks.md`.

---

## 1. The Galley Roll

**When:** once a day, when Brass cooks. Roll a **d20** openly so the table can groan or cheer. One roll feeds the whole crew.

| d20 | Result | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **1–5** | **Bad** | Roll on the Bad table. Everyone who eats makes a **Gut Check** (section 3), **DC 12**. On a natural 1, it's a **Galley Incident**: DC 15, and roll on "make it worse." |
| **6–15** | **Fine** | Roll on the Fine table. Filling, named something grand. Nobody mentions it. |
| **16–20** | **Good** | Roll on the Good table. Everyone who eats gains **1d10 temporary hit points** until their next long rest (roll once for the table). A natural 20 is a **Brass Classic**: **2d10** instead, and it goes in the Hall of Fame. |

**Advantage** when she has good ingredients (a fresh restock, or a gift like Orena's basket) or a helper in the galley. **Disadvantage** when stores are stale or she spent the day healing people. Both cancel, as normal.

**At 0300,** off the clock, there's no roll. It's always Good, temp HP included, for whoever couldn't sleep.

**Someone else cooks:** they roll d20 + Wisdom modifier, same table.

**Using a d4 instead:** 1 Bad, 2–3 Fine, 4 Good.

### The Hall of Fame

Classics that happened at the table. A Hall of Fame dish cooked again with the right ingredients rolls with **advantage**. It never becomes automatic; Brass has tried.
- **The Octopus** (session 1, before departure from Bral). A natural 20, universally loved, in front of witnesses. Aerion offered, unprompted, to fund future octopus nights. That offer is his to keep or quietly forget; if a port sells octopus, mention the price and let the ledger decide.

## 3. The Gut Check

A **Constitution saving throw** against a meal. Call it on a Bad result, or whenever someone eats something a sensible person wouldn't (DC 10 for iffy, DC 13 for nasty, DC 15 for Brass's hot sauce). Everyone rolls at once.

- **Pass:** nothing happens. Brag.
- **Fail:** **Queasy**, disadvantage on your next Constitution saving throw. Ends on a short rest.
- **Fail a Galley Incident:** **Poisoned for 1 hour** instead.

**Cast-Iron Stomach.** Every Bad meal you eat is a mark on Brass's napkin tally, pass or fail. Skipping the meal doesn't count. At **5 marks** you have **advantage on Gut Checks, and on any save against poison from food or drink, permanently.** Brass announces each mark at breakfast like a promotion, and the fifth like a graduation.

**Why a mini Heroes' Feast.** The real spell cures poison, grants immunity to poison and fear, and adds 2d10 to maximum hit points for a day. Brass gets the smallest slice of it: some temporary hit points on a good day, and the spell's own 2d10 on a great one (as temporary hit points, so they don't stack with other temp HP; keep the higher). Her food never cures anything. That's what the tonic is for.

**Brass eats first.** If she fails her own save, she says so ("...okay. That one's on me.") and anyone can skip the meal.

**The crew:** Estra +2, Oz +2 (thanks her anyway), Finny +1 (Lucky, rerolls 1s, complains loudest), Brass +1. **Tack** doesn't eat, but sets a place and reads out a sensor verdict ("Carbon content: ambitious"). **Flurry** doesn't roll. She'll eat a boot, so if she won't touch the plate, everyone saw. Orena and Aerion roll their own Con saves, and whether they eat a Bad meal at all is their players' call.

## 4. Brass's Tonic

Where the medic meets the cook. During a short rest, Brass brews a tonic from sickbay stores: it **ends Queasy or Poisoned**. One per person per long rest. It tastes terrible and works anyway. Brass: "Good. That means it's working."

## 5. Operation Clean Plate

Established in session 2: Brass's real goal isn't the license, it's getting Aerion (who eats almost nothing but white rice and dry toast) to finish three of her meals. **Count: 0 of 3.** A plate counts only if Priscilla decides Aerion finishes it. Never assume or script it. Her attempts, roughly escalating:
1. **The yogurt parfait.** Already announced. Layered in a glass so he can see nothing is hidden in it.
2. **Plain Rice, Improved** (Good table 9), with the optional dish placed a careful distance away.
3. **A toast flight.** Four toasts, four breads, dry. She wrote tasting notes.
4. **Congee.** It's technically rice. She'll argue the point.
5. **Octopus again**, on his own tab, since he offered.
6. **Nothing.** She stops trying for a week, leaves a plate at his station at 0300, and never mentions it.

If the count ever reaches 3, see `Brass - Scenes & Bits.md`.

## 6. Running it

- **Roll it in the cold open.** One number sets the mood and gives every NPC a line.
- **Don't roll every meal.** Once per in-game day at most, and skip it on busy nights.
- **Counterweight a hot session.** If last session ran dark, let the 0300 galley happen or nudge toward Good.
- **Failure is the fun.** A failed Gut Check is a warm, silly beat, never a punishment.
