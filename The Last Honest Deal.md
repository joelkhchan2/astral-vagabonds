# The *Last Honest Deal*

## Ship Profile
**Type:** Modified Squid Ship
**AC:** 15 (wood hull)
**HP:** 300
**Speed:** 40 ft (tactical) / 100 million miles/day (spelljamming)
**Crew complement:** 13 (running with 12 -- one berth technically empty)
**Weapons:** 2 ballistae, 1 piercing ram (after-market, occasionally fires on its own)
**Cargo capacity:** ~20 tons

## History
Previously a Pale Reach consortium trade vessel, officially "written off" after a pirate attack. Captain Estra acquired it at a CIV salvage auction -- an auction rigged in her favor by The Broker as part of their deal. Stories about how Estra got the ship vary depending on who's telling them and how drunk they are.

The ship has been repaired, modified, and patched so many times that very little of the original vessel remains. It is held together by stubbornness, clever engineering (Tack), and one load-bearing enchantment that nobody fully understands. The ship is a metaphor for the crew: battered, improbable, and somehow still flying.

**The name:** Estra named it herself after losing the Compact. It is not ironic.

## Ship Quirks
- **The temperamental helm:** Responds to the helmsman's mood, not just commands. A stressed helmsman makes the ship jittery. A calm one gets smoother handling. Estra's piloting is steady and precise. Wysp's is enthusiastic and slightly terrifying.
- **The float bay:** One cargo bay has a persistent low-gravity anomaly that nobody can fix. The crew uses it to store fragile goods. Also zero-G handball court. Finny holds the tournament bracket.
- **The creaking:** The ship creaks constantly. Gabrielle (bosun) is convinced it's structural. Tack insists "she's just talking." This is a running bit that can pay off dramatically if the creaking ever *stops*.
- **The ram:** The piercing ram was welded on after-market by a previous owner. It occasionally deploys on its own, usually at the worst possible time. Tack claims it's a wiring issue. Reeve thinks the ram is semi-sentient. Neither has been proven wrong.
- **The empty berth:** Crew complement is 13 but they run with 12. The 13th berth is always made up, always empty. Nobody uses it. Nobody talks about why. (DM hook: is it superstition? A memorial? Is Estra keeping it for someone specific?)

## Layout

### Upper Deck (above gravity plane)
- **Helm room:** Forward. The spelljamming helm (temperamental magic chair), navigation charts, Estra's card table. Priscilla's workspace is adjacent -- she's pinned charts, route calculations, and supply manifests to every available surface.
- **Forecastle / Crow's nest:** Reeve's domain. Elevated observation platform with surprisingly good acoustics (Reeve hears everything on deck from up here).
- **Main deck:** Open working space. Rigging access, ballista positions (port and starboard), general crew area. Gabrielle runs operations from here.
- **Galley:** Finny's kingdom. Always smells better than it should. The Manifest Board printout is pinned here. Crew meals happen at a long table that seats all 12 uncomfortably.

### Lower Deck (below gravity plane, gravity inverted)
- **Crew quarters:** 13 berths in close quarters. Personalization varies wildly -- Sable's has the tooth jar, Oz's is immaculate, Brass's is chaos.
- **Captain's quarters:** Slightly larger. The locked chest is here. Estra's quarters are sparse except for the star chart she won't let anyone see.
- **Cargo holds:** Two bays. One normal, one with the low-gravity anomaly. Priscilla and Brass manage inventory. Everything has a manifest tag because Priscilla insists.
- **Engineering:** Tack's workshop. Organized chaos. The autognome has named every major component of the ship and talks to them while working.
- **Medical bay:** Quill's domain. Over-equipped for a ship this size. Quill has requisitioned supplies from every port they've visited. There are labeled drawers for conditions that have never occurred aboard this ship and hopefully never will.
- **Gun bay:** Sable's workspace. Weapons maintenance, ammunition storage, and the tooth jar on its shelf. No one enters without knocking.
- **The float bay:** Low-gravity cargo hold / zero-G recreation space.

## The Ship as Character
The *Last Honest Deal* should feel like a character in its own right -- a living space with personality. The creaking, the temperamental helm, the self-deploying ram, the persistent anomaly. Over time, the crew's relationship with the ship mirrors their relationship with each other: held together improbably, understood through familiarity, loved despite (because of) the flaws.

Key ship moments to deploy over the campaign:
- The first time the ship takes serious damage and the crew rallies to save it
- The first time Gabrielle fixes something Tack couldn't (bosun earns the ship's trust)
- A moment where the ship "responds" to the crew in a way that suggests it might be more aware than anyone thought
- The creaking stops (something is very wrong)
- Someone offers to buy the ship or replace it with something better. The crew's reaction reveals how they feel about home.
