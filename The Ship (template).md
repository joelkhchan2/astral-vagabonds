## Layout

Sketch the decks once the class is known. Remember that **the gravity plane bisects the ship horizontally**, so the underside of every deck is walkable and both faces are usable space.

**Upper deck:** helm room, forecastle and crow's nest, main deck and weapon positions, galley
**Lower deck, gravity inverted:** crew quarters, captain's quarters, cargo holds, engineering, sickbay, gun bay

Note whose space is whose. That's most of what makes a ship feel owned.

## Guidance: The Ship as a Character

Carried over from the previous doc, because it was the best thing in it.

She should feel like a member of the crew: a living space with a personality. Over time the crew's relationship with the ship mirrors their relationship with each other, held together improbably, understood through familiarity, and loved because of the flaws rather than despite them.

**Moments to deploy across the campaign:**

- The first time she takes serious damage and the whole crew rallies to save her
- The first time [ORENA] fixes something Tack couldn't, and earns the ship's trust
- A moment where she seems to *respond* to the crew in a way nobody can explain
- The Signature Feature paying off dramatically after twenty sessions as a running joke
- If she creaks: **the creaking stops.** Something is very wrong.
- Someone offers to buy her, or to replace her with something objectively better. What the crew does with that offer reveals exactly how they feel about home.

**On naming components.** Tack will name everything within a week. Let him. The day the crew starts using his names without noticing is the day the ship becomes theirs.

**On the empty berth.** If they roll or choose that Signature Feature, never explain it. Let the table decide what it means, and let them be wrong for a long time.
