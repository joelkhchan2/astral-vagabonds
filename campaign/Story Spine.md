# Story Spine -- the overarching plot

> **GM-only. Spoilers for the whole campaign.** This is the current canonical overarching story, decided in prep after session 0. Where the older plot docs (`Campaign Bible.md`, `Plot Threads.md`, `The Pale Reach.md`, `Potential Endings.md`, `The Locker.md`) still describe the earlier "Drift / open-navigation-network" framing, **this document wins**; see "How this relates to the older docs" at the end.

## One line

The sky is going dark because civilization broke a living ecosystem it never understood, and the cure is to learn to **move with the living sky instead of owning it** -- a hopeful, solvable problem that a crew of capable people work out, contribute to, and prove.

## Genre and promise

This is **hopium** science-fantasy: The Martian, Project Hail Mary, Interstellar. The antagonist is a **hard problem, not a villain** -- the universe is lethal but indifferent, therefore *solvable*. The pleasure is capable people working the problem: observe, improvise, fail, adjust, crack it. Hope is earned, and earned through **connection across distance.** Dread in the early game is just *not understanding yet*; it turns to wonder as understanding grows. The table's session-0 instincts already fit this: counting every gold piece, rationing the 120-day air, running the ledger -- competence under constraint is the substance of the genre.

## The world truth: the living sky

Canon does most of the work here.

- **Wildspace is an ocean.** *Astral Adventurer's Guide*: "Wildspace systems are airless oceans teeming with space-dwelling life forms, including spores, space plankton, and larger creatures that resemble fish and aquatic mammals." There is a real food web out there.
- **The Tide.** (Formerly "the Shine.") Wildspace is not empty black between stars -- it is filled with a **living, faintly luminous sea**, a plankton-like medium that glows softly where it is alive and goes truly black where it dies. Call it **the Tide** (grounded in canon: kindori "feed on ambient astral radiance"). The Tide **flows in currents** -- and *those currents are the roads.* You do not navigate by aiming at a pinpoint of distant starlight across infinite dark; **you sail the living current that runs to a system.** Reach a port by following the water to it. A system is findable because a living current still reaches it; when the water around it dies, it strands.
- **Kindori are the gardeners of the Tide** (canon space whales): eighty-foot, mouthless, telepathic, drift in pods, sing by pulsing light, "considered near-sacred by spacefarers." Ground their role in **real ocean biology -- the whale pump:** whales feed in rich water and fertilize new blooms elsewhere as they migrate, spreading the living sea. So kindori graze the thick, bright current of busy regions and **carry it outward** -- through feeding and their glowing wake -- into the barren dark, growing fresh bloom and fresh roads where there were none. In balance they keep the whole sea *moving*: pruning the overgrown, seeding the empty, keeping old stars connected and opening new water. They tend the tide, sacred for a reason nobody quite remembers.
  - **Elder kindori (greater/mountain-whales).** Most kindori are eighty feet; a rare few are *ancient* -- centuries old, mountain-sized, solitary, so slow and vast that a ship reads them as terrain until they move. They are the campaign's cathedral-scale wonder: sighting one is a once-a-tier event, awe with a held breath. When one finally dies, it doesn't just vanish -- its body drifts, cools, and becomes a **whale fall**: a slow-blooming reef of living Tide and scavengers and salvage and small strange life, an entire temporary ecosystem hanging in the dark around a corpse the size of a hill. (Real deep-sea whale falls sustain a habitat for decades; ours does it with light.) The whale fall is a **future set-piece** -- see below and `campaign/Tier 1.md` -- part graveyard, part miracle, part salvage job, entirely the found-family crew standing very small in front of something enormous. It also literalizes the ecology: even in death the whale is *growing bloom* in the dark.
- **Void scavvers** (canon): "apex predator of the deep dark, old and large enough to consider a spelljamming ship a meal." They cull kindori and, more importantly, the *fear* of them keeps the pods **moving.** Predators drive migration; migration is the whole engine.
- **You navigate the currents, not the stars.** Canon: Wildspace systems drift "like corks bobbing in water," and you cross the Astral by reading the living water and directional awareness, not a static map. So a system's findability depends on a current still reaching it -- not on anyone remembering it. When the water goes out, the system is an **island the tide no longer touches.**

That last fact is the keystone: it means the disaster is **physical, not sentimental.**

## What broke -- and it's us (the cascade)

A classic trophic cascade (wolves-and-elk at Yellowstone; otters-urchins-kelp), in the sky.

Void scavvers eat ships. So for a century, whalers and the houses have **exterminated them** to make the lanes safe (and sold the hide). Kill the apex predator and two things happen: kindori **overpopulate**, and with nothing to fear they **stop migrating** -- they crowd the richest, brightest water and graze it bare, and they never travel out to carry the bloom into the dark. The Tide stops moving. The core is stripped; the frontier is never fed; and everywhere the whales stopped tending, the water dies back and the **currents dissolve.**

**How a star "goes dark" (this survives every logic test):** a system whose surrounding Tide has died back does **not** die and is **not** forgotten. Its sun still burns; its people are fine and remember everything. But **the living currents that connected it have dissolved**, so no road runs to it any more -- it is an **island stranded when the tide went out.** From out on the Astral it has simply dropped off the sea: you cannot see a current to it, so you cannot sail to it. It is **cut off, not killed.** Spacers call the spreading darkness **the Guttering** (stars guttering out like candles).

**Why the houses deny it:** they cleared scavvers hardest on the consolidated core lanes and keep the Tide drawn there, so the **bright core looks managed and fine while the frontier goes dark first.** The people who run Wildspace live in the light. This is the monoculture problem, and it is the denial engine (climate/fishery-politics energy). The cartel is **complicit and in denial, not evil** -- the profit is real, the damage is deferred, admitting it ends the business.

## The cure: move with the living sky

**The tempting wrong answer (keep it in play):** relight the Tide with artificial light/magic beacons on the highways. It backfires -- it feeds the addiction, draws kindori to the exact lanes you don't want grazed, and deepens the monoculture (light the profitable core, leave the frontier dark). The houses will champion it; the crew may help deploy it and watch it fail. That failure is the Tier 2 turn.

**The real answer reframes the whole conflict.** It isn't nature vs. industry -- it's **static vs. moving.** Industry wants fixed, safe, ownable, tollable highways and everything dangerous exterminated. A healthy sky is the opposite: a living thing that has to keep moving. Industry pinned a migrating ecosystem in place and killed the predator that kept it moving. **The grid itself was the disease.**

So the cure is **industry that moves with the sky instead of nailing it down** -- a living, nomadic, seasonal network:

- **Ships follow the circulation.** You sail the currents the kindori are seeding *now*, and the "highways" shift across the year as the pods migrate, the way fleets follow herds, fishing seasons, and trade winds.
- **Human traffic becomes the missing pressure.** Fleets that follow (and gently drive) the pods restore the migration the exterminated scavvers used to enforce -- the ships step into the empty niche and tend it, without being eaten.
- **The roads reinforce the cure.** Currents form from travel, so travel that follows the kindori wears the grooves exactly where fresh bloom is growing -- network and ecosystem become one system.
- **It can't be monopolized, because it moves.** You cannot own a river that changes course. The houses' fixed-toll model is obsolete against a seasonal network of moving routes. This is where "who owns the roads" finally pays off -- not as ideology, but as *physics*.
- **Live alongside the danger.** You don't repopulate the lanes with ship-eaters; you protect the scavvers' **wild breeding grounds in the deep** so they recover out there, and you learn to *read* the sky and route around a hunted pod. The loop (light draws kindori draws scavvers) isn't a bug to engineer away -- it's the healthy sky working, and the skill is sailing with it.

**Theme, in one line:** the sky was never ours to own -- only ours to tend, once we grew up enough to move with it. Industry *growing up*, from a child that tames and fixes and owns into an adult that reads, follows, and tends. The frontier crew who never had a fixed grid already live this way, which is why it's them.

**The ancient layer** (your Empty Frequency / current-readers): the ancient instruments are tools for **reading the living sea** -- where the Tide runs thick, where the pods are, where fresh roads are forming. The lost civilization "drifting in silence" were the last people who lived nomadically *with* the sky and left compasses for a living sea. Recovering the tech is **recovering forgotten wisdom**, not finding a weapon. The answer isn't invented; it's understood and remembered.

## The three acts (hopium structure)

1. **Survive it (The Martian).** Tier 1 (levels 5-8). The symptoms. Routes fail, a port goes dark, a star gutters. The crew flies the dying edges and improvises through the wreckage without knowing why.
2. **Understand it (Project Hail Mary).** Tier 2 (levels 9-13). Their day-job *is* the science -- charting anomalies, sampling dead water, tracking the whales -- until they crack the cascade: the whales aren't the threat, they're the cure, and we killed their predator. The false light-solution fails. Nobody believes them yet.
3. **Restore the balance (Interstellar).** Tier 3-4 (levels 14-20, or land it earlier for a tighter arc). Protect the last scavver breeding grounds, prove a nomadic living route works, relight one region, and force the truth into the open. **Contributors, not saviors:** they crack it, protect the keystone, relight one corner as proof, and hand the method up the chain. They don't personally re-shine Wildspace.

## The three characters -- three relationships to reading the living sky, across time

**The whole cast converges on one skill: navigation, understood as reading the living sky.** Estra tried it and it broke her; Orena learns to do it truly; Aerion builds it to last.

### Estra Vane -- the failed pioneer (the past)
Reframe the **Vane Compact**: it was the first fleet to try the nomadic method -- flying *with* the living sky, following the currents and pods, charting the moving roads, sharing them ("you chart it, you share it"). The right instinct, a generation too early, and it failed for two reasons:
1. **The cartel crushed it** -- a nomadic, un-tollable, shared network threatened the fixed-toll business, so the houses dismantled it.
2. **The method wasn't ready.** Her crews had the *art* (follow the currents, follow the pods) but not the *science* -- they didn't know the cascade, the Tide, the predator, *why* the sea moved. So they couldn't tell a living road from a death-trap forming, and some of her people were lost **to the sky itself**, sailing into guttering zones.

She carries a double guilt (the cartel destroyed her fleet *and* her own unfinished method killed some of her own), has done it in miniature from the bottom of the rankings for fifteen years, and is afraid to believe again. **The crew completes what she started by adding the missing half, the science** -- turning her dangerous art into something safe, teachable, scalable. Her redemption isn't beating the cartel; it's watching the thing she died for finally *work*, done right. Her locked star chart is her fifteen-year map of the kindori migration and the guttering.

### Orena -- the true navigator (the present)
**Navigation, not shepherding** (unless Gabrielle wants more). The cure is *reading the living sea* -- where the Tide runs thick, where the pods are grazing, where this season's roads are forming -- and she is the Navigator, a water genasi bard "of the water" who can *hear* the sea: the kindori song, the Tide's tone, the difference between living water and dead. She becomes the person who can navigate by the living sea instead of dead charts. She does not command the whales; she understands where the roads are because she can read them. That is the campaign's central skill and it is hers.

**How and why her crew was lost:** the **Wayfinder Knot** were exactly that -- navigators ahead of their time, trying to fly the old way because the cartel's fixed charts were already failing on the frontier. They tracked the **Stillwater Vocalise** into a frontier region -- and that region **guttered while they were inside it.** Here is what the Vocalise actually *is*: kindori call to each other across the sea by song, and a healthy pod's song is the sound of a living sky. The Stillwater Vocalise is its opposite -- **the drawn-out death-song of a starving, stranded pod as its water goes dark.** It is, literally, *the sound of the Tide dying.* A starving, overgrazed pod had grazed the region bare (on its own, or accelerated by a house stripping the same water for profit -- tying industry directly to her loss); the currents dissolved, the region went dark, and the ship and crew were **stranded inside a newly-dark pocket**: alive, unaged (the Astral is timeless), remembering everything, but off every road and invisible to every chart. Orena, attuned to the water and the song, felt the current go out and slipped across the threshold as it closed; her crew didn't. What "stilled" the Wayfinder Knot was the tide going out with them still inside it.

**The Vocalise recurs -- and pays off at a whale fall.** The death-song does not end with the whale; it **lingers and echoes** in dead water. So the Stillwater Vocalise is a real, recurring sound Orena keeps half-hearing across the campaign (session 1's note; a dark-leg echo; a deep-Astral rumour), not just a memory. The payoff is the **whale-fall recognition** (see "Future set-pieces"): when the crew finds a whale fall, the elder kindori's song is still echoing through the reef, and Orena recognizes it as the Vocalise -- her grief and the cosmic mystery revealed as one thing, inside the body of a dying whale. She can't find them because the place is, by definition, off every map -- **and the only way to find a dark region is to master reading the living sky, the exact skill that saves Wildspace.** Her private grief and the cosmic cure are the same navigation. (See `The Locker.md`: the dark pockets and the moored, unreachable are the same phenomenon; the instrument is the door.)

### Aerion -- the institution (the future)
Navigation is Orena's; the *system* is Aerion's. As bosun/quartermaster with the ledger, his problem is the one nobody else can solve: **how does a nomadic network function as a business?** Moving routes, a fleet that follows the sky, supply and money for a network you can't toll or own. His data is also where the cascade first becomes undeniable (dying frontier routes, the economics of the cull). His endgame is the campaign's thesis made personal: **he takes his father's fixed-toll cartel house and remakes it as the first nomadic house**, proving the model at institutional scale. His selfish goal (own the company) and the cure (reinvent what a company *is*) become the same act. His family is complicit in the old way, so beating -- or converting -- his father is industry-vs-nature fought at the dinner table. He is "industry growing up," in one person.

**The shape, in a line:** Estra tried and it broke her; Orena learns to do it true, to find what she lost; Aerion builds it to last, and wins his life by remaking it. The failed pioneer, the true navigator, the reformer -- three generations of *reading the living sky*, and the crew is where they meet.

## The cartel (the complication, not the villain)

The Pale Reach and its houses are a **fishery in denial**, not a dark lord. They run the cull and the fixed grid, profit from both, suppress the disappearances, and champion the false light-fix. Recurring friction -- Route Wardens, condescending brokers, House Windsong's own complicity, a cold fixer if you want a face -- but the real antagonist is the problem. Keep them human; the redemption door (a house that turns, a broker who defects, Aerion's own father) is open.

## How to run it (so the pleasure lands)

**Problems, not villains. Multiple solution paths. The players' cleverness is the point.** Every mystery should be crackable more than one way, and wins should feel like *the table figured it out.* Their survey-data day-job *is* the investigation -- charting anomalies for pay is literally doing the science. Wonder comes from *understanding*, dread from *not yet.* Keep the crew scenes warm (70%), the cosmic slice uncanny-turning-to-awe (20%), the danger real and usually *expensive* rather than lethal (10%).

## Tier 1 (levels 5-8): what the crew experiences

They do not learn any of the above. They experience **weather.** The arc of Tier 1 is *symptoms accumulating into a pattern.*

- **The felt problem:** routes are unreliable, jobs go sideways, a port is a few hours out of position, a run takes a day longer than the charts allow, a frontier colony has gone quiet. Played as bad-charts shrugs and mild unease, not alarm.
- **The wonder seed:** a pod of kindori, sighted at distance, singing by light. Near-sacred, harmless, beautiful. Plant them as awe early so the Tier 2 reveal ("they're the cure, and it's a cascade") lands. Orena feels *something* when they sing.
- **The personal seed:** Orena keeps hearing, under the silence of a guttering zone, the note from the night she lost her crew -- the Vocalise. (Session 1 ends on exactly this.)
- **The money reality:** every voyage, the operating cost comes out of the account; they've sworn off jobs over 60 days; combat is frightening because it's *expensive*. Competence under constraint, always.
- **Recurring texture:** homecomings at the Rock of Bral (Sprat, Meech, Senna, the Compass Rose), selling survey data (which is secretly the investigation), the Manifest Board creeping upward.
- **Tier 1 end-state:** the crew has a *pattern* they can't explain (dark routes cluster; the frontier goes first; a house delisted routes with no incident reports -- Meech's breadcrumb), a growing unease about the whales, and Orena's private thread pulling them outward. That hands into Tier 2's "understand it."

Concrete session-1 beats and the cold open are in `sessions/Session 1.md`.

## Tone guardrail (holds for the whole campaign)

**PG to mild T.** The two players run toward wonder and mystery, not fear. Uncanny, awe-struck, occasionally genuinely sad -- **never true horror.** No zombies, demons, undead, gore, or body horror; the scares are all *cosmic-uncanny* -- "the stars are in the wrong place," "something's aboard we can't see," "a beautiful thing is dying and we don't understand why." Magic and strangeness are the whole point; horror tropes are not. And every eerie beat **lands back in warmth** -- the workplace comedy and found family are the ground the wonder and dread stand on. When in doubt, dial toward *wonder*, not *terror*, and always come home to the crew. (This matches `campaign/Campaign Bible.md`'s horror calibration and `campaign/Tier 1.md`'s guardrail.)

## Future set-pieces (planted here, run later)

- **The whale fall** (any tier from mid-Tier-1 on, best when the crew already loves the whales): the crew finds the drifting body of an **elder kindori** and the impossible, temporary reef blooming around it. A salvage job, a graveyard, and a miracle at once; other salvagers may be working it (some reverent, some not). Awe, grief, and the crew being very small. See `campaign/Tier 1.md`.
  - **The Vocalise recognition (Orena's payoff).** A dying kindori's song does not stop when the whale does -- it **lingers and echoes** in the water around the body, and around a *whale fall* it is still sounding. When the crew reaches one, Orena hears that echo and **recognizes it: the Stillwater Vocalise.** The song she has chased and dreaded since night one is the song of a dying whale, and she is now standing inside one. This is the beat where her private grief and the cosmic mystery are revealed as the same thing -- and where "follow the song" becomes "learn to read the dying sea." Plant the Vocalise as hints for a long time first (session 1's note under the guttering star; a half-heard echo on a dark leg; a spacer's rumour of "singing in the deep") so this lands like a key turning.
- **The Dreameater** (drop-in bottle episode, `sessions/Episode - The Dreameater.md`): an invisible astral drifter eats the crew's dreams until they work out how to see it. Eerie, warm, resolved by understanding rather than violence -- the tone guardrail in miniature.
- **The Ambassadors** (drop-in comedy, `sessions/Episode - The Ambassadors.md`): first contact inverted -- the crew are the feared aliens. An optional thread ties the world's isolation to a guttered route, and seeds a world the cure can visibly reconnect later.
- **The Quiet Hull** (drop-in horror-lite, `sessions/Episode - The Quiet Hull.md`): a derelict with a **night scavver** aboard (the winnable fight) and a **void scavver** looming beyond it -- the crew's first sight of the exterminated keystone predator, alive. Stages the cascade's central creature as terror-and-awe before they understand what it means; pays off hard in Tier 2.

## How this relates to the older docs

This spine **refines and renames** the earlier "Drift" material rather than throwing it out:

- **The Drift -> the Guttering / the cascade.** The old three-part cause (consolidation + conduit die-off + phase shift) is superseded by the trophic cascade (predator cull -> kindori overgrazing -> the Tide dying back -> currents dissolving). The "conduit die-off" survives as flavor for dead water. Where `Campaign Bible.md` and `Plot Threads.md` describe the Drift, read the cascade.
- **"The Shine" -> "the Tide."** The living medium was renamed and firmed: it's a living, faintly luminous sea that flows in currents (the roads), glows where alive and goes black where dead; you navigate the currents, not the stars; kindori are its gardeners (the whale pump); a guttered system is an island stranded when the tide went out; and the Vocalise is the sound of the Tide dying. Wherever any doc still says "the Shine," read "the Tide."
- **The open-navigation network -> the nomadic living-sky network.** Same destination (a shared, un-monopolizable way of navigating) but grounded in ecology and reached by *moving with* the sky, not by open-sourcing static maps. Aerion, not "Priscilla's data platform," is the institution-builder.
- **Estra's Compact -> the failed first attempt at the nomadic method** (crushed *and* scientifically premature). Everything in `Captain Estra Vane.md` still holds; add the "she was right but not ready" layer.
- **The Locker** is unchanged and now clearer: the dark pockets and the moored-but-unreachable are the same phenomenon; Orena's crew are sealed in one; the ancient instrument is the door and the compass.
- **Potential Endings** still work; reskin "the network launches" as "the first nomadic route is proven and the fixed grid starts to fall."
