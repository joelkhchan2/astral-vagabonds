# Arc Map: Beginning to End

> **GM-only, full spoilers.** This is the altitude view across the whole campaign: how the five running threads interleave from Tier 1 through the endgame, and specifically where the Vellum/Carrow lockbox thread (born in session 1, shaped in session 2 prep) fits into everything else. It doesn't replace `campaign/Story Spine.md` (the world plot in depth), `campaign/Tier 1.md` (the beginning, mission by mission), `factions/House Windsong.md` (the family), or `campaign/Potential Endings.md` (the menu of climaxes). It's the map that shows how those documents are one story.

## Arcs: a pacing layer under the tiers

The three Acts above (`Story Spine.md`'s "survive it / understand it / restore the balance") are thematic, not a scheduling tool. **Arcs** are the planning unit underneath them, roughly three levels each, for sanity-checking pace against real session counts. Loose, not a commitment to exact session numbers:

| Act | Arc | Levels |
|---|---|---|
| 1, survive it | Arc 1 (already underway) | 5-8 |
| 2, understand it | Arc 2 | 9-11 |
| | Arc 3 | 12-14 |
| 3-4, restore the balance | Arc 4 | 15-17 |
| | Arc 5 (level 20 lived-in for a few sessions, then the finale as its own tight sub-arc) | 18-20 |

Real pace so far: level 5 took 2 sessions, level 6 took 4, level 7 is running longer still (the Amberwake arc alone is five). That's an accelerating curve, not a flat one, each Arc above should be expected to run longer than the last.

### The table of contents, one layer deeper

Each entry: estimated sessions (loose), the plot spine, one concrete wonder set-piece (not just politics), the crew beat it carries, and its real danger/combat beat. Session counts are a sanity check, not a commitment; players control the pace within each arc, see the "open deadlines" note below.

**Arc 1, Survive It (5-8, ~12 sessions, locked.)** Spine: Guttering as unexplained weather, the Windsong fraud buried, Estra's fleet dream reactivates, the Amberwake climax strands the crew on the Elemental Plane of Water. Wonder: the first kindori pod at a distance; an elder kindori's actual death and the real Vocalise. Crew beat: Tomlin folds into the family; Finny's gossip network starts paying off. Danger: the Reefside void scavver, Longscar at the Windward Edge, the blind Amberwake transit.

**Arc 2, Strange Shores (9-11, ~15-18 sessions.)** Spine: stranded, getting home drives fieldwork with House Delverane's waystation researchers at the Tide's literal source, fragments of the cascade earned, not explained; Orena gets real confirmation her lost crew is alive (`npcs/The Wayfinder Knot.md`'s stage one), likely through Delverane's own far-reaching network rather than Bral gossip. Wonder: standing at the Tide's own source; something native to the plane no chart has described. Crew beat: Brass's neogi slaver debt resurfaces (`campaign/Plot Threads.md`). Danger: that neogi encounter, a real fight, not just talky fieldwork.

**Arc 3, Coming Home With New Eyes (12-14, ~20-24 sessions.)** Spine: Vellum's evidence goes live, the houses' patch *publicly fails* (the fix doesn't work, not yet why), the fifth-seat race reopens, Orena's actual Wayfinder reunion (`npcs/The Wayfinder Knot.md`'s stage two, now that rank and reputation are live again), the Compact's dead and detained captains resolved (Sorrenson, Ashe, Renn, see `npcs/Captain Estra Vane.md`). Wonder: the banked Leaftully calves/attuned-ball scene, pure delight, a deliberate breather. Crew beat: Finny's "who is he paying" thread resolves. Danger: a real fight tied to Vellum's resistance, Darkshade's Black Parade or a hired enforcer.

**Arc 4, Prove It (15-17, ~22-27 sessions.)** Spine: protect the kindori breeding ground from the houses' escalating light-fix poaching (`campaign/Story Spine.md`), prove one nomadic route works (doubling as Orena's guided-crossing breakthrough), Estra's redemption climax (one last, harder Compact captain confronted first), Aerion's institution decision point. Wonder: the Locker becomes a real, plausible lead, tied to the Empty Frequency, not yet reached. Crew beat: Oz's discharge shame via a Giff mercenary company hired as a resisting house's muscle; Tack tested by a Pale Reach contact. Danger: houses actively fighting the new method, multi-session antagonist pressure.

**Arc 5, Force the Truth / the Finale (18-20, ~28-32 sessions.)** Spine: the *cause* goes public and named (who did this, why, and the real fix), relight one region as proof, level 20 reached and lived in before the sprint. Wonder: the actual voyage to the Locker, the ancient fleet, Flurry's lunar heritage reveal landing alongside it. Crew beat: whichever B-plots are still open (Sable, Judo included) get real send-offs during the level-20 breathing room. Danger: the finale fight itself, shaped by whichever `campaign/Potential Endings.md` combination's been earned.

**Still open, deliberately not decided here:** whether Aerion's arc resolves by winning the fifth seat or obsoleting it (`campaign/Story Spine.md` flags this), and which specific Compact captain gets the harder Arc 4 confrontation.

### Open deadlines, so the table keeps real control of pace

Every arc transition should have its own in-fiction reason the story doesn't rush the players, not just GM fiat. Examples worth locking in as they're built: Aurel Delverane's survey contracts run on her own patient, volume-over-time funding model, not a deadline. The seam back from the Elemental Plane of Water reopens on its own schedule, not Delverane's patience, which hands Arc 2 room to explore without anyone pressuring a return. Institutional failure (Arc 3) is slow by nature, a cartel admitting its fix doesn't work takes time regardless of the crew's own pace. Proving a migration route (Arc 4) genuinely requires waiting for the right season. Each of these means side jobs, B-plots, and job-of-the-week contracts can fill real time inside an arc without stalling the spine.

## The five threads

Read `Story Spine.md` and `Tier 1.md` for the full versions. Short form, so the tier breakdown below reads clean:

- **A. The Guttering** (world). Void scavvers got exterminated, kindori overpopulated and stopped migrating, the Tide stopped moving, the frontier is going dark. Nobody in the crew knows this yet. Discovery is the whole shape of Tiers 1-2.
- **B. House Windsong** (family). Aerion, overlooked fourth son, quietly saving to buy out his father's company. Vaelros wants the vacant fifth founding seat. Aeravellis is poisoning the navigation archive to hide his own losses.
- **C. Orena's search** (personal). Updated, see `npcs/The Wayfinder Knot.md`: the crossing happened on the Material Plane, not in Wildspace, and four of the five survived and are thriving on their own ship, the *Wayfinder* (~rank 99). Orena alone was scattered from them and doesn't know they're alive; they don't know she is either. The reunion fires through Board-adjacent rumor once the *Night Fury*'s reputation carries far enough, a mid-campaign event, not tied to mastering navigation the way the world-plot's own dark pockets are.
- **D. Estra** (undercurrent). The failed first attempt at the nomadic method. The Vane Compact, crushed by the cartel and scientifically premature. Her redemption is watching the crew finish what she started.
- **E. Vellum & Carrow** (new, session 1-2). The crew is holding proof that House Vellum knew about route-death decades before the Guttering had a name, and profited from letting **House Carrow** (the fifth founding house, collapsed 40 years ago) take the blame alone for losing the Amberwake corridor. **Updated, session 5: Vellum now suspects the *Night Fury* has it**, not that it was destroyed. The house's own scripted "visit" never fired; House Nightbreeze made an opportunistic play first (Prudence Hale's 20,000 gp offer, declined and reported honestly), and in doing so let slip that Vellum's quiet watch on the crew is real. A direct Vellum approach is still pending, see `campaign/Story Spine.md`.

**Thread E is the one this doc adds detail to.** It didn't exist in the original design; session 1 generated it live, and it turns out to slot cleanly into the existing shape, because House Carrow's fall *is* the Guttering's first casualty (`factions/Houses & Holdings.md`), which means thread E isn't a new story bolted on, it's thread A's origin story with a name and a house attached.

## Tier 2 (levels 9-13): the middle -- understand it

Their survey work *is* the investigation now. They crack the cascade: the whales are the cure, the culling did this, the houses' light-fix will fail. Full framing: `Story Spine.md`, act 2.

**This is where thread E stops being dormant and starts being useful, on purpose.** Once the crew can explain the Guttering as a mechanism (predator cull -> overgrazing -> dying Tide -> dissolving currents), the Vellum correspondence stops being "an embarrassing old cover-up" and becomes **the first documented proof that a founding house recognized the pattern decades before anyone else and chose silence over warning anyone.** That's the difference between gossip and evidence, and it's the reason this thread was worth writing into the world at all.

**How it can play out (present these as live options at the table, not a script):**
- **Leverage, played straight.** The crew uses the evidence to force Vellum's hand, in the fifth-seat race, in the cartel's public denial, or both. This is Aerion's institution-building thread (B) and the world-mystery thread (A) converging through a document he's holding personally. Big Priscilla spotlight if she wants it.
- **The offer.** Vellum tries to buy the silence instead of fighting for it, quietly funding the crew, smoothing their Board standing, offering Aerion something real, in exchange for the correspondence never surfacing. This mirrors Aeris's pattern exactly (`House Windsong.md`): genuine, generous help, at a price that compounds. A good test of what the crew has actually become by Tier 2.
- **It leaks anyway.** Wren Ostler heard the whole story to do the dispel, and what she does with that afterward was left open on purpose (`locations/The Bral Reach.md`). If nobody's touched that thread by Tier 2, this is a clean way to force the crew's hand without them choosing to.

**Where it touches thread B specifically.** The vacant fifth seat has been open for forty years because Carrow's corridor died. Vellum's complicity reframes *why* it's stayed vacant: not just tragedy, a cover-up that benefited the house best positioned to keep bidding for it quiet. If Vaelros ever learns his son is holding this, it's simultaneously the best leverage the family's had in a generation and a genuine danger to sit on. Whether Aerion tells him is entirely Priscilla's call (per the standing rule in `Story Spine.md`), but it's worth knowing the scene is loaded either way.

**Where it touches thread A directly.** The Amberwake is already the Guttering's confirmed first casualty. A region that guttered forty years ago and has sat untouched since (nobody's had a reason to fly back into a dead corridor) is, structurally, the same kind of object as Orena's dark pocket: **a place the tide went out and nobody's gone looking since.** That makes the Amberwake a strong candidate for an ancient-instrument or current-reader lead (the Empty Frequency thread, `campaign/Plot Threads.md`), since old, undisturbed, guttered space is exactly where recovered wisdom would still be sitting. Seed this as a discovery, a survey anomaly, an old chart, rather than announcing it.

**Tier 2 end-state.** The cascade is understood. The light-fix has failed publicly. Vellum's cover-up is either exposed, bought off, or leaking on its own schedule. The Amberwake has gone from "a name on an old map" to "a place worth actually flying to." Thread D and thread C are both primed for their Tier 3 payoffs (Estra visibly ready to try again; Orena's search narrowing toward a real destination).

## Tier 3-4 (levels 14-20, or land it earlier): the end -- restore the balance

Protect the last scavver breeding grounds, prove one nomadic route works, relight one region, force the truth into the open. See `Story Spine.md` act 3 and `Potential Endings.md` for the menu of climaxes (A/B/C/D/E, not mutually exclusive).

**The Amberwake as a candidate endgame destination.** If the table wants one big set-piece that pays off threads A, B, and E at once: a voyage into House Carrow's dead corridor, using the nomadic method the crew spent two tiers building, to (1) prove the method works on a region everyone else wrote off as permanently gone, (2) recover whatever ancient current-reading tech is sitting in forty undisturbed years of guttered space, and (3) surface Vellum's cover-up publicly, on the ground, in the place it actually happened, rather than as a document in a drawer. That's Ending A ("The Launch") and Ending D ("The New Map") sharing a location for free.

> **Note: this would be a second, different trip, not a repeat.** Tier 1 already sends the crew into the Amberwake once, blind, instrument-less, and nearly fatal (`Story Spine.md`'s Amberwake set-piece). A Tier 3-4 return would be the inverse image of that first trip: flying the same corridor with the nomadic method actually mastered, understanding what they're looking at this time. Worth being deliberate that a second visit reads as payoff, not reuse, if it's ever built.

**Whether Orena's lost crew is also in the Amberwake is a genuinely open question, and it's Gabrielle's to answer, not this document's.** It fits cleanly (same phenomenon, same forty-year silence, same "nobody's had a reason to look"), which is exactly why it's worth raising with her directly rather than deciding it here. If she wants her crew's dark pocket to be a different, separate place, the Amberwake still works as the A/B/E payoff on its own; it just doesn't also carry Ending B ("The Rescue"). If she wants them to be the same place, all five threads and up to three of the five endings converge in one final voyage.

**House Carrow's empty chair.** Whichever way the Amberwake resolves, surfacing Vellum's role reopens the question the whole political B-plot has circled for forty years: who deserves the fifth seat now. That's live material for Ending A's summit (a political climax where the houses negotiate a world that can no longer be tolled) regardless of what happens to the Knot.

**What this doc is not saying:** which ending is *the* ending. `Potential Endings.md` already says the real one will combine pieces, and that's still true; thread E just turns out to be unusually good scaffolding for stitching A, B, and D together into a single physical place and a single climactic voyage, if and when the table wants that. Nothing here is a commitment.

## Quick reference: what changes if the crew does nothing with the lockbox

Worth stating plainly, since "sit on it" is a legitimate and very in-character choice (Brass would approve): if the crew never uses the Vellum evidence at all, thread E simply stays a quiet, recurring pressure, occasional visits, occasional offers, and resolves itself passively whenever the Guttering becomes public knowledge in Tier 2-3 regardless of what the crew personally did with their copy. The document doesn't have to be the thing that cracks the case. It's leverage and color; the science (thread A) is what actually convinces anyone. Don't let the table feel like they're required to play a blackmail plot they didn't sign up for.
