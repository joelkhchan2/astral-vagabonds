# Tier 1 -- the arc (levels 5-8)

> **The "survive it" act.** Companion to `Contracts.md` (which has the mission mechanics, M1-M8) and `campaign/Story Spine.md` (the overarching plot). This file is the altitude view: what Tier 1 is *for*, the threads running through it, and how each beat sets up the larger story. Runs warm and funny; the cosmic dread is a slow drip, one image at a time.

## What Tier 1 does

The crew understands nothing yet. They fly the dying edges, do small jobs, and **log what they find -- which is secretly the science that pays off in Tier 2.** Tier 1's job is to:
1. Accumulate symptoms into a **pattern** (routes failing, ports drifting, colonies cut off).
2. Introduce the wider sky as **wonder**, and the whales -- *sparingly* -- as the thing at the center of it.
3. Seed all three character threads and Estra's undercurrent.
4. **Descend on purpose** to a trough, so Tier 2 can turn toward hope.

## The four threads

- **A -- The Guttering (world):** symptoms -> a pattern -> "there's something living behind this." The human cost comes first (session 1's stranded colony), the eeriness second, the ecology last.
- **B -- The Windsong fraud (family):** Aeravellis poisons the navigation archive to hide his losses; it collides with the crew's honest work; they trace it; the father buries it. This is the meat of Tier 1's mission spine.
- **C -- Orena's search (personal):** the first lost crewmate found; the Vocalise drip.
- **D -- Estra (undercurrent):** the failed-pioneer thread, kept almost entirely as subtext until Tier 2.

## Decentering the whales

The mystery's keystone is the kindori, but the *world* is the whole living ocean, and there's far more cool stuff than whales. **Use kindori maybe two or three times in all of Tier 1**, each time meaningful; fill the other weeks with the sky's grab-bag:

> a mercane vessel older than the cartel, passing on incomprehensible business · a chwinga "astronaut" on the forward rail that won't explain itself · a derelict with the table still set and no crew · a colony ship forty years still en route whose people have never seen a spelljammer · a time-slip that only Aerion's log notices · a colour that isn't on the spectrum · a murder-comet on a collision course · space clowns (regrettably) · the signal, faint, and everyone hears something they've lost.

Two of these per Tier 1 mission, rolled or chosen from `Tables.md`. The whales stay rare so they stay holy.

## Drop-in episodes (run any time you want a change of pace)

- **The Dreameater** (`sessions/Episode - The Dreameater.md`): a bottle episode. Something drifted aboard and is eating the crew's dreams, invisible until they work out how to see it. Pure found-family workplace comedy wrapped around an eerie, PG mystery, with one arc clue smuggled inside. Great after a heavy mission.
- **The Ambassadors** (`sessions/Episode - The Ambassadors.md`): first contact *inverted* -- the crew are the feared aliens visiting an isolated world that's never seen a spelljammer. The lightest, funniest episode; culture-clash comedy with a warm landing, and an optional thread (the world went dark when its route guttered) that seeds a re-contact payoff for the cure.
- **The Quiet Hull** (`sessions/Episode - The Quiet Hull.md`): the scariest episode (mild T), best around L6-7. A derelict with something still aboard -- a **night scavver** killed the original crew (the winnable fight), and the reason the ship is out here is the crew's first sight of a living **void scavver**, the exterminated apex predator the whole arc turns on (the flee-don't-fight reveal). Horror-lite, ends warm, and detonates in Tier 2.
- **The whale fall** (build target; any tier from mid-Tier-1 on): the crew finds the drifting body of an **elder kindori** and the temporary reef blooming around it. Its **death-song is still echoing** through the reef -- and Orena recognizes it as the **Stillwater Vocalise**, the sound she's chased since session 1. Her grief and the cosmic mystery become one thing, inside a dying whale. Plant the Vocalise as hints for a long time first so this lands. (Full: `campaign/Story Spine.md` future set-pieces; `campaign/Plot Threads.md`.)
- More to come as we build them (a murder-comet chase; a first-contact colony ship).

## Recurring bit to seed: the berbalang ("secrets for secrets")

Somewhere out past the settled lanes lives a **berbalang** -- a gaunt, vulture-headed astral loremaster (canon: `references/Astral Sea Bestiary.md`), truesighted, speaks every language, and able to split off spectral duplicates so it seems to be several places at once. Ours is **not a monster to fight**; it is a lonely, amoral *collector of knowledge* that trades on one rule: **a secret for a secret.** Bring it something it doesn't know and it will answer a question truthfully; it cannot be bought with gold, only with truth, and it always knows when you're lying (truesight, and it has heard every lie).

**The running gag:** the players have met berbalangs before, so when one turns up, the table already knows the drill -- everyone braces to give up a real secret to get one. Lean into that meta-rhythm; it's a bit *and* a mechanic. Great for dispensing a Guttering clue, a family secret, or a lead on Orena's crew -- at the cost of the crew revealing something of their own (which is its own drama: whose secret do they spend?). Fits the campaign's "knowledge as currency, silence as power" theme exactly. Seed it as a rumor in Tier 1 (an old spacer at the Compass Rose: "there's a thing out past the Verge that trades in secrets, and it never forgets a face it hasn't got one from yet"), pay it off in a Tier 1 or Tier 2 session.

## The emotional shape

It descends. Resupply-run warmth -> a star goes out -> dark ports that used to be full -> your neighbors robbed -> a beautiful thing culled for money -> the father buries a crime. Tier 1 ends in a trough: *the world is dying, the family is rotten, and we understand none of it.* That trough is the setup for Tier 2's hopium turn -- "we can't fix this" becoming "we can *understand* this."

## What Tier 2 inherits (at level 9)

A Guttering **pattern** they can't explain but have **seen the cull behind**; a family that showed its face; Orena with **one crewmate found and a contradictory story**; Estra **visibly hiding something**; Delverane/Senna as **allies-in-waiting**; the whales as a wound and a question; and, unspoken by anyone, **Amberwake.**

## Tone guardrail (holds for every beat)

PG to mild T. Uncanny, wondrous, occasionally sad -- **never true horror.** No zombies, demons, undead, gore, or body horror; the scares are "the stars are wrong," "something's aboard we can't see," "a beautiful thing is dying." Magic and strangeness, yes. And every eerie beat **lands back in warmth** -- the workplace comedy is the ground the wonder and dread stand on.
