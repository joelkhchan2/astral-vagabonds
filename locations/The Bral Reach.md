# The Bral Reach: Home Port

The standing set. They'll come back here after every voyage, so it needs to be small enough to memorize and specific enough to miss.

![The Rock of Bral](https://static.wikia.nocookie.net/forgottenrealms/images/9/97/Rock_of_Bral.jpg/revision/latest?cb=20220818013248)

**Canon:** the **Rock of Bral** is a mile-wide asteroid dominated by a single city, founded roughly 200 years ago by the pirate Captain Bral as a refuge for scoundrels, and gradually civilized by merchants who wanted somewhere stable to do business. 5e deliberately never fixes its location. Everything below the Rock itself is ours.

**The useful coincidence:** the Pale Reach incorporated within a generation of Bral's founding. The port created the trade and the trade created the cartel, and there are people on the Rock whose great-grandparents watched both happen.

---

## First Sighting -- the Rock, from the deck

**Use this the first time the players actually see Bral.** They live here, but the campaign hasn't shown it yet, so give it a real arrival. Read or paraphrase, leading with sound and motion, not a list:

> You see it long before you arrive: a whole mountain of a city burning gold against the black. A mile of grey rock crusted over with spires and gantries and ten thousand lit windows, stacked wherever they could be made to cling, and around it a slow endless storm of ships coming and going. As you slip into the lower berths the sound reaches you first through the opening lock, then the smell: smoke and hot metal, fish and spice, ten thousand people and their dinners. A bell rings somewhere. A hawker calls your ship the wrong name. You're home, for whatever that's worth.

**Three textures to sprinkle as they move through the city** (don't front-load them -- spend one per scene):
- **The gravity is a local argument.** Bral has one gravity plane through its long axis, so the underside is "down-is-up" and newcomers walk into walls. Locals never notice. Tethered goods, drifting dust, and a dropped coin that falls *sideways* sell the strangeness for free.
- **Everyone is from somewhere else.** Giff dockworkers, dohwar hawkers, a mercane gliding through a crowd that parts without being asked, astral elves who don't age and humans who do, all of it crammed into stone streets built for half as many. Nobody looks twice at a crew like theirs. That's the point of Bral.
- **It is old and it remembers being lawless.** Two hundred years ago this was a pirate's bolt-hole; the merchants civilized it but the bones show -- a shrine to Captain Bral nobody tends, street names that used to be warnings, an upper city that's all marble and a lower city that's all rope.

**One warm anchor for this crew:** homecoming means the same six faces. Sprat on the gantry, Meech behind his grille, the smell of the Compass Rose. Bral is enormous and indifferent; *their* Bral is small. Play the contrast.

## The Six Places

Keep it to six. Six is memorable. Everything else is scenery.

### 1. LOWSIDE BERTH 40: where the ship lives
A cheap outer berth on the underside of the Rock, where the gravity is other people's problem and the rent is thirty gold a month. Forty is at the end of a gantry that flexes. The neighbours are the *Grendel's Wake* at 39 and, at 41, whatever the harbourmaster is trying to get rid of.

**Harbourmaster's runner: SPRAT**, twelve years old, knows everything, sells information for candy and will honour the deal. First NPC of every homecoming.

**One secret:** Berth 40 is cheap because the previous three tenants left owing money. Someone will eventually turn up looking for one of them and be unhappy about the answer.
**One danger:** the gantry.
**One opportunity:** nobody official comes down here.

### 2. THE CHARTER WINDOW: the CIV
A brass grille in a basalt wall with a queue in front of it. This is where charters are stamped, contracts are registered, incident reports are filed, and **the Manifest Board is posted** on a public wall that people genuinely stand and read.

**Clerk: DEPUTY REGISTRAR HALVAR MEECH**, half-orc, forty years in the job, immune to charm, bribery, and enthusiasm. He is not corrupt. He is not helpful. He is *correct*, and he will explain at length which form they should have used.

**One secret:** Meech has noticed that three frontier routes were quietly delisted last year with no incident reports attached. It bothers him. He hasn't told anyone because nobody has asked him a question he considers well-formed.
> **That's a Tier 1 Guttering breadcrumb sitting behind a service window, and it's free the moment a player is polite to a bureaucrat.**

**How Meech knows (and what he'll say if asked well).** Every route change crosses his desk as a filing, and the rulebook is absolute: a delisting *must* carry an incident annex saying why the route closed -- a wreck, a hazard, a dried-up port. That's the form. Last year, three delistings came through with the annex field blank and nobody made to correct it, which to Meech is not a mystery so much as a *procedural obscenity*. He filed his own margin notes and said nothing, because no one asked. If a player asks a precise question ("who filed them?" / "what reason was given?"), he produces the three:
- **The Sable Crossing** -- the old lane out toward Amberwake. Delisted by a Pale Reach clerk; reason field blank; no successor route filed. (Amberwake is the Guttering's first casualty -- deep seed.)
- **Kestrel's Ladder** -- a chain of Verge waystations. Marked "route consolidated," except there is *no consolidation filing* to consolidate it into. The paperwork points at nothing.
- **The Tallow Run** -- to a frontier colony that, per its own filings, *is still there and still ordering provisions.* A live colony on a dead road. That one keeps Meech up.
He does not connect them to anything cosmic. He connects them to bad paperwork, which is worse, because bad paperwork means someone is *choosing* not to explain. (Pays off at M4, `Contracts.md`; ties to the Story Spine's Guttering.)

**One danger:** the CIV can suspend a charter faster than anyone can appeal it. House Obhal knows this.
**One opportunity:** Meech respects competence and paperwork, in that order, and Priscilla has both.

**A scene worth running: the Board, together.** The Manifest Board is posted here on a public wall, and people genuinely stand and read it. Bring the crew to it at least once as a group. Finny goes straight to their line and narrates it like sports. Somewhere down the wall, a sour rival clocks them and says it just loud enough to carry. Use **Rimmon Vex of the *Cutlass Sunday*** (ranked 344, `factions/The Manifest Board.md`), who takes being anywhere near the crew as a personal insult: *"Vane's boat. Three hundred forty-eighth. Didn't know they let the derelicts vote."*
- **Oz** takes it in perfect stride. He might warmly agree, then offer to buy the man a drink. Nothing lands on Oz that a thank-you note can't absorb.
- **Estra** is the one who bristles, which is the reveal. The captain who is bored by hull breaches goes very still, because the Board is the one wound she can't be dry about: she was top ten once, with a fleet, and 348th is a costume she chose and still hates wearing (`Captain Estra Vane.md`). She won't shout. She'll say one cold, exact thing and hold his eye until he remembers a prior appointment.
- If it tips over (Estra never starts it, but Vex might, or Finny's mouth might, or a helpful Warden might), it spills into a short, low-stakes brawl, best finished at the **Compass Rose**: shoving, a thrown drink, Oz setting people down like furniture and apologizing as he does, Brass already pricing the damages. Nobody is seriously hurt. It's the crew's first "we are a unit" beat and a rare crack of light through Estra's shell.

### 3. THE DELVERANE WINDOW: where data gets sold
A narrow office two levels up, all brass instruments and filing. Priscilla's recurring negotiation scene lives here.

**Broker: SENNA ORRICK**, human, early thirties, sharp, tired, and on a quota. She likes good data and has opinions about which ships bring it. She will be professionally condescending to a 348th-ranked crew exactly once, and then she'll see what they filed and stop.

**Senna is the crew's most important recurring relationship in Tier 1** and neither of them will realize it. She works for Delverane, the reform faction, and she's the thread that eventually reaches Aurel Delverane and the *Farthing*.

**One secret:** Senna has been quietly flagging anomaly reports to her house rather than the central archive. She'd call it good practice. Vassara would call it something else.
**One danger:** everything sold here goes into an archive Aeravellis is poisoning.
**One opportunity:** she'll pay above rate for data nobody else brings, and the Verge is data nobody else brings.

### 4. THE COMPASS ROSE (the bar)
Not rough, not nice. Long shared tables, indifferent food that never changes, lamps in old envelope-flasks, and the specific quality of being where crews go rather than where tourists go. It is the crew's living room away from the ship, and where most of what they need to say to each other gets said sideways over a drink.

**MADAM ULVE**, plasmoid, has been behind that bar longer than anyone can account for. She is unhurried, misses nothing, and says little. She greets Estra by name on the first visit and neither of them explains how they know each other. She keeps a ledger of who owes whom entirely in her head, and it is more accurate than the CIV's, so a tab at the Rose is a real and slightly dangerous thing to carry.

**The regulars (a small recurring cast):**
- **Bex Halloway**, captain of the *Grendel's Wake* off berth 39, the crew's next-dock neighbour and friendly rival. Ranked a comfortable few rungs above them and unbearable about it when she wins, generous about it when she loses, and secretly glad to have someone at the bottom to race. She is the friendly ceiling: beating her is a real, achievable win.
- **Dov Errin**, once captain of the *Steadfast Promise*, now a man with a grievance and a stool. The CIV impounded his ship on a paperwork technicality he still can't undo, and he will tell anyone the whole story twice. He is a bore and a warning: his tale is exactly how the machine crushes a small operator, which is Estra's Compact writ small, and buried in the third retelling is real, usable intel about who files the complaints and why.
- **Off-shift Route Wardens**, Tethyn enforcement in civilian coats, drinking where they can hear things. Inconvenient by default. One of them, **Warden Cathis**, is a decent and bored card player who will talk more freely across a dice table than she ever would on duty.
- **Old Pemberly**, a spacer so old nobody buys his stories and everybody buys his drinks, who swears low and delighted that he has heard *singing in the deep Astral* (the Vocalise and the berbalang rumour live in his mouth; `rules/Tables.md` §4).

**Tall tales and gossip.** This is the rumour engine of the campaign. Deal from the **Port Rumours** table (`rules/Tables.md` §4) as ordinary bar talk; several of those are the Guttering wearing a joke's clothes, and none are labelled. Between the true seeds, keep a stock of pure tall tales (a captain who married a mercane, a hold that was bigger inside than out, the ghost hull that only shows on Nightbreeze's books) so the real clues never stand out.

**The back table.** There is always a game going: **Scavver** (dice, closest to twenty) and **Liar's Berth** (bluffing dice), stakes small and mean. Rules in `rules/Downtime Games.md`. It's the best low-stakes way to hand the players a scene with Bex, a Warden, or a stranger with information they'd never sell but might lose.

**One secret:** Ulve's head-ledger is more accurate than the CIV's, and she is nobody's ally but her own.
**One danger:** the Wardens hear everything, and a bar brawl in front of them is a fine that comes off the account.
**One opportunity:** every rumour in the Bral Reach passes through this room within four days.

### 5. THE FLOAT MARKET: where you buy things
Three tiers of stalls in a low-gravity commons: chandlers, provisioners, outfitters, a scrivener, an apothecary, and a woman who sells only rope and is extremely serious about it. Goods drift. Everything is tethered. Haggling is expected and pricing is soft.

**Character shop lives here.** Full list below.

**One secret:** Brass has three contacts working this market and one of them is being watched.
**One danger:** pickpockets in low gravity are extraordinary.
**One opportunity:** anything on the standard equipment list, most of the ship catalogue's small items, and one interesting thing per visit.

### 6. THE UNDERWRITERS' HALL: Nightbreeze
Marble, quiet, and staffed by people who are unfailingly pleasant to everyone regardless of rank, which is somehow worse than being condescended to. Insurance is renewed here. Claims are settled here, slowly.

**One secret:** Nightbreeze's book tells them where every insured ship in Wildspace is and what it's carrying. They have never used this. Everyone knows they could.
**One danger:** a lapsed policy means every repair at 20 gp/HP comes out of pocket.
**One opportunity:** Aerynna's house. If Aerion ever wants a door into Nightbreeze, it's this building, and walking in has a cost he'll feel.

# THE CHARACTER SHOP

**Personal gear is a separate purse from the venture.** The 50,000 is capital and it is not theirs to spend on swords. Standard 5e starting wealth applies. **What the PCs buy, or whether they buy at all, is entirely theirs -- present the stalls, never the purchase.**

Prices are PHB standard unless noted. The Float Market carries everything mundane. Below is what makes it a *Wildspace* market, sorted into the four stalls worth naming. Each proprietor has one voice tic so you can play them again.

## The named stalls

**CORABEL'S, rope and rigging.** Run by **Corabel**, a rope-woman of terrifying seriousness who will not sell you a line she thinks you'll misuse and judges every knot she sees. *"That's a granny knot. On a tether. In vacuum. No."*
- **Tether line, 100 ft (25 gp).** Braided void-silk over a wire core, rated for the sudden yank when "down" changes on you. Every spacer should own one; half of them don't. Corabel will not sell you a length until she has watched you tie a knot she approves of.
- **Cold-iron tether, 60 ft (special, see Tables §5).** The same line with a cold-iron core, so nothing nonmagical can cut it. For when you need to be certain the thing on the other end stays attached.
- **Grav-boots (300 gp).** Heavy soled boots that grip a hull's gravity plane, letting you walk a ship's underside without minding which way "down" has gone. Slow and ugly and worth every coin the first time they save your life.
- **Climbing kit (25 gp).** Pitons, harness, and line, standard, but rated for vacuum and with every buckle checked in front of you.
- **Rope that remembers (uncommon; only if she likes you).** A coil that ties the last knot you tied with it, on command. Corabel sells it to someone whose knots she respects, which is a higher bar than money.

**THE SALT LOCKER, chandler and void kit.** **"Salt" Merrin**, a one-armed old void-hand who upsells safety gear with a horror story attached to each item. *"Void suit, light? Four hours of air. Man I knew had three and a half. You do the math on the last half."*
- **Void suit, light (180 gp).** Four hours of air in a soft envelope; disadvantage on Stealth, because it hisses.
- **Void suit, working (450 gp).** Eight hours, magnetic soles, no Stealth penalty, and the one Merrin actually trusts. The gap between the two suits, he'll tell you, is the gap between a walk and a funeral.
- **Envelope flask (40 gp).** Palm-sized, holds ten minutes of breathable air for one, for the gap between "the lock failed" and "we fixed the lock."
- **Signal lantern (60 gp).** Visible two miles in the void, with coded shutters for talking ship to ship without a word on any channel.
- **Cold-weather void liner (90 gp).** A quilted underlayer that grants resistance to the cold of an unheated hold or a dark-side dock. Hallow's Rest will make you wish you had bought two.
- **Astral compass (220 gp).** A brass instrument that gives advantage on checks to hold a heading in the Astral Sea. It reads the ordinary currents, not the living ones; Orena does the rest by feel.
- **Rebreather pearl (uncommon, see Tables §5).** Held in the mouth: ten minutes of air, once per day, for the spacer who plans to be somewhere they shouldn't breathe.

**PELL'S, apothecary.** **Pell**, a dohwar (cheerful, penguin-shaped) who lists side effects far too fast and far too happily. *"Void-sickness draught, works in minutes, mild numbness, do not operate a helm. Or do! Your choice!"*
- **Healer's kit (5 gp).** Ten uses, stabilizes a dying friend with no roll. Buy two.
- **Void-sickness draught (25 gp).** Ends one bout of the nausea that follows a gravity-plane flip.
- **Foul-air filter mask (110 gp).** Ignore the poisoned condition from foul air for 24 hours, for holds gone bad and ports that never were good.
- **Potion of healing (50 gp, limited).** Standard, 2d4+2, and Pell only ever has a few, because they keep selling.
- **Antitoxin (50 gp).** Advantage on saves against poison for an hour. Pell recommends it "for social occasions" and will not explain.

**QUILL &amp; SEAL, scrivener.** **Odwin Quill**, a precise little man who reveres correct paperwork and is, in spirit, Meech's only friend (they have never met). *"A charter copy uncertified is a napkin with ambition."*
- **Bound and locked ledger (35 gp).** A brass-cornered book with a real lock. To a certain quartermaster this is a holy object, and Aerion's is never further than arm's reach.
- **Manifest seals, set of 50 (15 gp).** Tamper-evident wax-and-wire seals that customs and Wardens like to see, which can make an inspection hours shorter.
- **Scrivener's kit (30 gp).** Forms, stamps, ink, and, crucially, the *correct* forms, so you never file a resupply on a 7-C again.
- **Certified charter copy (12 gp).** A stamped duplicate Meech will insist on and a Warden will demand.
- **Sound-slate (75 gp).** A wax plate that holds one minute of recorded sound. Orena will want several, and won't say why until much later.

## The One Interesting Thing

**Each visit to the Float Market, one uncommon item is available.** Roll on the minor-items table in `Tables.md` or pick. It is never the same thing twice and the seller is never the same person.

## What the Market Does Not Have

No rare or very rare magic items. No spelljamming helms (that's a yard purchase). No ship weapons above a ballista. Anything genuinely powerful requires a Delverane commission, a mercane contact, or Brass calling in a favour, and all three should be scenes rather than transactions.

## Running a Homecoming

Six beats, none longer than five minutes, and the whole thing takes twenty:

**Sprat** meets them on the gantry with news. **Meech** stamps the paperwork and complains about the form. **Senna** buys the data and Priscilla haggles. **The Board** goes up on the wall and Finny reacts. **The Compass Rose** for whatever the crew needs to say to each other. **Payroll and repairs** come out of the account, and then the next contract.

Two of those six should have something wrong with them. Never all six.
