# The Bral Reach — Home Port

The standing set. They'll come back here after every voyage, so it needs to be small enough to memorize and specific enough to miss.

**Canon:** the **Rock of Bral** is a mile-wide asteroid dominated by a single city, founded roughly 200 years ago by the pirate Captain Bral as a refuge for scoundrels, and gradually civilized by merchants who wanted somewhere stable to do business. 5e deliberately never fixes its location. Everything below the Rock itself is ours.

**The useful coincidence:** the Pale Reach incorporated within a generation of Bral's founding. The port created the trade and the trade created the cartel, and there are people on the Rock whose great-grandparents watched both happen.

---

## The Six Places

Keep it to six. Six is memorable. Everything else is scenery.

### 1. LOWSIDE BERTH 40 — where the ship lives
A cheap outer berth on the underside of the Rock, where the gravity is other people's problem and the rent is thirty gold a month. Forty is at the end of a gantry that flexes. The neighbours are the *Grendel's Wake* at 39 and, at 41, whatever the harbourmaster is trying to get rid of.

**Harbourmaster's runner: OTTIE**, twelve years old, knows everything, sells information for candy and will honour the deal. First NPC of every homecoming.

**One secret:** Berth 40 is cheap because the previous three tenants left owing money. Someone will eventually turn up looking for one of them and be unhappy about the answer.
**One danger:** the gantry.
**One opportunity:** nobody official comes down here.

### 2. THE CHARTER WINDOW — the CIV
A brass grille in a basalt wall with a queue in front of it. This is where charters are stamped, contracts are registered, incident reports are filed, and **the Manifest Board is posted** on a public wall that people genuinely stand and read.

**Clerk: DEPUTY REGISTRAR HALVAR MEECH**, half-orc, forty years in the job, immune to charm, bribery, and enthusiasm. He is not corrupt. He is not helpful. He is *correct*, and he will explain at length which form they should have used.

**One secret:** Meech has noticed that three frontier routes were quietly delisted last year with no incident reports attached. It bothers him. He hasn't told anyone because nobody has asked him a question he considers well-formed.
> **That's a Tier 1 Drift breadcrumb sitting behind a service window, and it's free the moment a player is polite to a bureaucrat.**

**One danger:** the CIV can suspend a charter faster than anyone can appeal it. House Obhal knows this.
**One opportunity:** Meech respects competence and paperwork, in that order, and Priscilla has both.

### 3. THE DELVERANE WINDOW — where data gets sold
A narrow office two levels up, all brass instruments and filing. Priscilla's recurring negotiation scene lives here.

**Broker: SENNA ORRICK**, human, early thirties, sharp, tired, and on a quota. She likes good data and has opinions about which ships bring it. She will be professionally condescending to a 348th-ranked crew exactly once, and then she'll see what they filed and stop.

**Senna is the crew's most important recurring relationship in Tier 1** and neither of them will realize it. She works for Delverane, the reform faction, and she's the thread that eventually reaches Aurel Delverane and the *Farthing*.

**One secret:** Senna has been quietly flagging anomaly reports to her house rather than the central archive. She'd call it good practice. Vassara would call it something else.
**One danger:** everything sold here goes into an archive [THIRD] is poisoning.
**One opportunity:** she'll pay above rate for data nobody else brings, and the Verge is data nobody else brings.

### 4. THE COMPASS ROSE — the bar
Not rough. Not nice. Long tables, indifferent food, and the specific quality of being where crews go rather than where tourists go. Run by **MADAM ULVE**, plasmoid, who has been behind that bar for longer than anyone can account for and who Reeve will greet by name on the first visit without explaining how.

**Bex Halloway** of the *Grendel's Wake* drinks here. So does **Dov Errin** of the impounded *Steadfast Promise*, who will tell anyone about it. So do off-shift Route Wardens, which is inconvenient.

**One secret:** Ulve keeps a ledger of who owes whom, entirely in her head, and it is more accurate than the CIV's.
**One danger:** the Wardens.
**One opportunity:** every rumour in the Bral Reach passes through this room within four days.

### 5. THE FLOAT MARKET — where you buy things
Three tiers of stalls in a low-gravity commons: chandlers, provisioners, outfitters, a scrivener, an apothecary, and a woman who sells only rope and is extremely serious about it. Goods drift. Everything is tethered. Haggling is expected and pricing is soft.

**Character shop lives here.** Full list below.

**One secret:** Brass has three contacts working this market and one of them is being watched.
**One danger:** pickpockets in low gravity are extraordinary.
**One opportunity:** anything on the standard equipment list, most of the ship catalogue's small items, and one interesting thing per visit.

### 6. THE UNDERWRITERS' HALL — Morraine
Marble, quiet, and staffed by people who are unfailingly pleasant to everyone regardless of rank, which is somehow worse than being condescended to. Insurance is renewed here. Claims are settled here, slowly.

**One secret:** Morraine's book tells them where every insured ship in Wildspace is and what it's carrying. They have never used this. Everyone knows they could.
**One danger:** a lapsed policy means every repair at 20 gp/HP comes out of pocket.
**One opportunity:** [MOTHER]'s house. If [THE PC] ever wants a door into Morraine, it's this building, and walking in has a cost he'll feel.

## The One Interesting Thing

**Each visit to the Float Market, one uncommon item is available.** Roll on the minor-items table in `Tables.md` or pick. It is never the same thing twice and the seller is never the same person.

## What the Market Does Not Have

No rare or very rare magic items. No spelljamming helms (that's a yard purchase). No ship weapons above a ballista. Anything genuinely powerful requires a Delverane commission, a mercane contact, or Brass calling in a favour, and all three should be scenes rather than transactions.

## Running a Homecoming

Six beats, none longer than five minutes, and the whole thing takes twenty:

**Ottie** meets them on the gantry with news. **Meech** stamps the paperwork and complains about the form. **Senna** buys the data and Priscilla haggles. **The Board** goes up on the wall and Finny reacts. **The Compass Rose** for whatever the crew needs to say to each other. **Payroll and repairs** come out of the account, and then the next contract.

Two of those six should have something wrong with them. Never all six.
