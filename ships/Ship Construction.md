# Ship Construction

Three independent choices: **Class** (size, cost, crew, capacity), **Profile** (how it's built), **Style** (what it looks like, with no mechanical effect at all).

Canon ties those together, so a "Squid Ship" is both a stat block and a squid. We've pulled them apart, so the players can build a fast light trader that looks like a scorpion if they want one.

**[C]** canon · **[A]** anchored to a published rate · **[H]** our invention

Classes through Capital are derived from the sixteen published hulls. **Canon tops out at Capital.** Everything above it is ours, and the point of the Great Ship tiers isn't bigger numbers, it's that scale changes what a ship *is*.

### Hull Expansion, growing into a bigger class **[H]**

A ship you've already named, fought in, and bled for doesn't have to be traded in to grow. A proper yard can expand a hull one class up at a time, lengthening the keel, adding gravity-plane capacity, reworking crew quarters, **keeping the ship's own history, name, and every upgrade already installed.** Slower and pricier than buying a new hull outright (expanding an occupied, already-fitted ship in place is genuinely harder than building one from a bare keel), but it's the only way to grow a ship you actually love rather than replace her.

| Expansion | Cost | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| Trader &rarr; Hauler | 15,000 gp | Crew 11&rarr;13, HP 250&rarr;300, keel 100&rarr;250 ft, speed 55&rarr;40 ft |
| Trader &rarr; Heavy | 22,000 gp | Crew 11&rarr;15, HP 250&rarr;400, cargo 20&rarr;30 t, keel 100&rarr;200 ft, speed 55&rarr;35 ft |
| Trader &rarr; Galleon | 28,000 gp | Crew 11&rarr;20, HP 250&rarr;400, keel 100&rarr;130 ft, speed 55&rarr;35 ft |

**Takes real time**, a real bastion-grade project (weeks in dock, not a same-day refit), and the Profile and Style chosen at purchase carry forward unchanged. Every existing upgrade, weapon, and crew-pool facility stays installed; nothing needs re-buying. The speed cost is real and worth weighing out loud: a bigger hull is slower, every time, no exceptions.

### What changes above Capital **[H]**

**Multiple helms.** One spelljammer cannot move a Liner. Great Ships need two to four linked helms crewed at once, which means a helm watch rotation and a payroll of attuned spellcasters. This is the main reason Great Ships are institutions rather than possessions.

**Stacked gravity planes.** Past 300 feet of keel a hull carries several planes instead of one. Decks stop being "above" and "below" and start being districts. People get lost. People live in parts of the ship you have never walked.

**Tender bays.** Carriers and above launch smaller craft. You don't fly a Carrier at a problem, you fly it near one and open the doors.

**Population.** A Liner has passengers. An Ark has residents, a market, births, and politics. At Ark scale the ship stops being a bastion and becomes a settlement, and the crew stop being a crew and become a government.

**Cost of operation** scales brutally. A Carrier runs roughly 12,000 gp a month. You do not fund that with courier contracts. Owning a Great Ship means you have become the thing that hands contracts out.

## 2. PROFILE

Pick one, applies to any class. Derived directly from canon: the three 40,000 gp hulls in the book are identical except for AC 19/15/13 traded against speed 25/35/50.

| Profile | AC | Speed | Signature |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sturdy** | 19 | −10 ft | Damage threshold 20 instead of 15 |
| **Balanced** | 15 | base | +1 weapon hardpoint |
| **Swift** | 13 | +15 ft | Break off from hull-to-hull contact without penalty |

A Swift Trader is 70 ft at AC 13, which is the Shrike. A Sturdy Heavy is AC 19 at 25 ft, which is the Turtle. Every published ship is reproducible and so is every one that isn't.

## 3. STYLE

**No mechanical effect whatsoever.** Hand this to the players and get out of the way. It is the fastest way to make a ship feel owned.

**Silhouette:** squid · scorpion · shrike or raptor · fish · moth · beetle · nautilus · turtle · whale · manta · dragonfly · traditional galleon · something with no name yet

**Hull material:** timber · bone · chitin · grown wood, still living · hammered metal · sailcloth over frame · crystal · salvage, mismatched

**Motive furniture:** sails · wings · fins · oars that touch nothing · nothing visible at all

**Detail, pick two:** a figurehead · running lights · a paint scheme · a name in a script nobody reads · scars kept deliberately · something bolted on that predates the current owner

## Crew-Pool Upgrades, Tiered **[H]**

Everything below is priced for the long haul, not just Tier 1. **Four tiers, keyed to the same level gates the bastion facilities already use (5, 9, 13, 17):** Tier I is dockside and affordable now; Tier II (roughly 5,000-25,000 gp) is a real investment with a visible power jump; Tier III (roughly 60,000-150,000 gp) is rare, bespoke work, the kind of purchase that defines an arc. **There is no Tier IV for sale here, on purpose.** The genuinely legendary versions of these (true resurrection, a bonded guardian spirit, acting twice in a round) exist in the campaign as one-off rewards the DM hands out, not line items any shop sells, so they're deliberately left out of this doc.

# SIGNATURE FEATURE

Every ship gets **one, free**, rolled or chosen at purchase. This is the narrative quirk and it is what makes her *her*. Extras cost 1,500 gp from the aftermarket, and the Major will absolutely sell one as an upgrade.

| d12 | Feature |
|---|---|
| 1 | **The float bay.** One hold has a low-gravity anomaly nobody can fix. Fragile cargo. Also a court. |
| 2 | **She talks.** Constant creaking. Whether it's structural is an unresolved argument. |

| 4 | **A berth that stays empty.** Complement is one higher than the crew. It's always made up. |
