# Aeravellis Windsong

## The Surface Read
Astral elf, 182, third of Vaelros's four sons. Title: **Senior Vice President of Delivery Operations**, a title the family handed him specifically because it sounds like a real job and isn't one. He doesn't build ships like the yard does, doesn't run routes like Aeric, doesn't play capital like Aeris. He gets handed newly finished hulls and is supposed to deliver them to their buyers. In practice, he and a loose entourage take each one joyriding first.

**Voice tic:** reaches for his own title as armor whenever he's actually being questioned, "per my role as SVP of Delivery Operations," said with complete sincerity, as if the sentence itself settles anything.

**Physical tic:** can't sit still the moment he's genuinely nervous, overcorrects with a bigger gesture or a louder boast exactly when he should be going quiet.

**Want, plainly:** to be taken seriously by somebody, once, without having to perform first. Nobody in the family has ever asked him what he actually wants to do, only assigned him the job nobody else wanted.

## The entourage, not a crew
A handful of friends and hangers-on who stuck around because he's generous with the house's money and nobody's ever checked his expense reports closely. Good, pointed contrast to the *Night Fury*'s own crew: bought loyalty, not earned. Worth playing at least one of them as genuinely fond of him anyway, not everyone around him is purely using him.

## The disaster: lost in the Amberwake
He took a client's ship, a significant commission, someone with real standing, out on what was meant to be a shortcut, cutting through unfamiliar space to beat the delivery time or just to show off. The shortcut ran through **the Amberwake**, the dead, fully guttered corridor nobody's navigated in forty years. No current to ride, no chart that held up, no way to simply fly back out the way they came in. A real scare, not a horror beat, disorientation, panic, a long stretch of genuinely not knowing if they'd get out, and the ship took real damage getting clear. He's been quietly covering the repair cost ever since rather than admit where he actually took it, which would mean admitting both the wreck and the unauthorized detour.

**What he actually knows, in fragments, not a clean account.** A half-remembered shape of the place. Possibly a damaged, partial chart made in the attempt to navigate back out. One vivid detail that stuck with him more than the rest, worth deciding when it matters, an abandoned waystation glimpsed and never approached, a feeling of being watched by something that was once there, the particular quality of the silence. He's frightened of the place in a way he'd never call fear out loud, and ashamed of the wrecked ship in a much more ordinary way. Getting either out of him is its own small scene.

## The debt: House Darkshade
Found him while he was quietly scrambling to make the client whole before the scandal could land, and offered the capital instead of letting him fail in the open. He owes it the way Darkshade collects, tracked in **Dray Holt's** favor ledgers (`factions/House Darkshade.md`), not currency changing hands twice. Vance Orleth doesn't need it repaid. He needs a compromised Windsong son available whenever leaning on the house is useful, this arc or much later. **Keep this connection unconfirmed to the crew for now**, hinted (an unexplained payment, a courier he's visibly relieved to see leave) rather than named, banked for a later reveal.

## Playing him
- Not a villain, a good-for-nothing who got one real scare and has been quietly drowning in the aftermath. Play the bravado as genuine self-belief half the time, not pure performance, he talks himself into his own confidence as much as he's selling it to anyone else.
- The family dynamic is the real engine: he and Aerion are both the family's underestimated sons, and they've ended up in opposite positions, Aerion left and built something real on his own; Aeravellis stayed, took the job nobody respected, and has nothing real to show for staying either. That gap is worth letting both characters feel, whenever they actually end up in a scene together.
- Once his debt and his trip to the Amberwake both surface, he becomes a strange, reluctant resource rather than a problem to simply punish, the one person in the family who's actually been where the crew is about to go.
