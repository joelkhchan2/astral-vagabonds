# The Wayfinder Knot -- what actually happened, and where they are now

> **GM-only.** This is the true account behind Orena's backstory in `npcs/Orena.md`. She knows none of this. Float pieces of it to Priscilla-equivalent (Gabrielle) only as they're earned in play; the whole point of the thread is that she's been searching with worse information than the table has.

## Who they were

Six childhood friends out of Leilon, a guild in name more than trade, the **Wayfinder Knot**: navigators and wayfinders for hire along the Sword Coast, young and good at it and hungry for a name. Orena founded it. The other five:

- **Dune Ashcombe** -- the practical one, already an informal second before any of this happened. Steady in a way that reads as unshakeable and isn't.
- **Sabine Wrack** -- rigger and the guild's best hand in bad weather, dry humor, doesn't perform feelings for an audience.
- **Ket Halvard** -- quiet even before the crossing, the one who noticed things first and said them last.
- **Tamsin Odell** -- the youngest besides Orena, quick, always first over a rail or up a mast.
- **Colin Aldous** -- Orena's closest friend on the crew, and older history than the rest: he was at **Vaelren Windsong's training camp** as a kid, the same summer young Orena and Aerion were both there (`sessions/Session 1.md`'s flashback camper who "also loves *Dragon Masters*"). He and Orena stayed close after; when she started the Knot, he was the first person she asked.

## What they were chasing, and why it worked

The **Stillwater Vocalise** is old sailors' folklore along that stretch of coast: a windless, silent night, unnaturally still, and somewhere under it a song. The legend is real, and it is exactly what `campaign/Story Spine.md` already defines it as, the death-song of a starving, dying kindori pod, heard at one of the rare places where the Material Plane's oceans run strangely close to the Astral Tide. Nobody in Leilon knows that. They only know the old stories, and the Knot went looking for one on purpose, the kind of glory-chasing a young, ambitious guild does without thinking too hard about the cost.

**Why a kindori was dying in an ordinary sea at all.** Kindori are Astral-native; this is the same mechanism driving the whole Guttering, a pod stalled by the missing predator pressure, starving, its astral-sense failing and grasping blindly for any trace of more of the Tide, the way a real dying whale beaches itself in shallow water on pure instinct. It drifted toward the nearest thin place it could sense, which happened to be a spot with its own name in maritime folklore, and started to die there.

**Why then.** Nothing targeted the Knot and nothing planned this. A pod's death has its own clock, months or years of slow starvation reaching its final hours, completely indifferent to who happens to be sailing past. They found the legend actually happening by pure bad timing.

## The crossing

The pod's final pulse, its last burst of failing astral-sense, tore the thin spot open all at once, and it didn't pull like a door, it pulled like a drain. Orena was at the rail, already leaning out trying to place the sound, when the pull started, and the water took her down one thread of that outflow. The ship, and everyone still aboard it, went down a different thread of the same outflow, together. Nobody chose the split. She simply wasn't attached to the same mass as the hull at the instant it happened.

**The landing broke the ship apart.** Not a clean arrival; a violent one. In the first chaotic minutes freeing themselves from the wreck on the Wildspace side, **Tamsin lost a leg** pulling someone else clear, a real act of nerve in the worst possible moment, not something that just happened to her. She spent months recovering and now works with a fitted peg leg, adapted, not slowed. All five of them survived. Orena came out of it on a different current entirely, alone, with no one, no wreck, no bearings, nothing.

## What the five of them did afterward

They had each other and a body count of zero, which is more than most people get out of something like this, and they had their trade. The Wayfinder Knot were navigators before any of this; that skill ports over to reading the Tide better than most people manage cold. They worked their way into a charter the slow way, the same climb the *Night Fury* is on now, and rebuilt around the only thing they were sure of.

**They have no idea whether Orena survived.** Same problem as hers, in reverse: no fixed point to search from, no way to know if she made it, and a hard lesson in exactly how badly a crossing can go. They mourned her as probably lost and kept going, the way you have to.

## The *Wayfinder*

Currently ranked **~99** on the Manifest Board, captained by **Dune Ashcombe**. Still doing exactly what the Wayfinder Knot always did: navigation and charting work for hire, which is why they're good enough to sit this high after only a few years of rebuilding from nothing. The founding five are still aboard; the crew has grown past them with hired hands over the years, a real, grown operation now, not four people frozen at the moment of the wreck.

**Built from nothing but what they carried out of the wreck.** The landing broke their original ship apart, but it broke apart, it didn't vanish, and in the chaotic minutes and days afterward they recovered a handful of pieces before the wreck site was lost to them for good. When they finally had enough of a charter to commission a real hull years later, they had those pieces worked into her rather than scrapped or sold: the original ship's bell, cracked but still rung, mounted by the helm; a length of carved rail from the old stern worked into the new helm console; a few lengths of rigging hardware, old and mismatched against everything else aboard, kept in her own lines rather than replaced. None of it is hidden. Anyone aboard who asks gets a plain answer: it's from the old ship. Nobody outside the crew has reason to ask what "the old ship" means.

**The name carries half the guild forward, on purpose.** The Wayfinder Knot, minus the Knot. To anyone who never knew the guild, it reads as an ordinary, slightly aspirational charter name. To anyone who did, it should land as obviously, deliberately unfinished, half a name left standing where two used to be.

## Where they are now, actively, not just waiting

**They never stopped looking, they just never had anything to look with.** No fixed point, no wreck, no way to know which direction "alone" even went. What's changed, quietly, GM-only: the *Wayfinder*'s climb up the Board has put them in rooms (survey contracts, chart exchanges, the same professional circuit the Compact's captains used to run) where fragments of information move that wouldn't have reached them five years ago. Nothing that points at Orena specifically yet, a half-remembered name from a charter-house clerk, a water genasi mentioned in someone else's gossip, a direction more than a destination. **Colin** is the one who chases every fragment personally and burns out on most of them; **Dune** has started quietly reining him in, not out of doubt, out of not wanting to watch him break on a dead end again. They're closer than they were. They are not close.

## The scattered hints (GM-only, arc 1)

**Reframed per the DM's call: this reveal now lands by arc 1's end, not deep mid-campaign.** The reunion itself still waits for arc 2 (see below), but Orena should have real, mounting reason to believe her crew is alive well before that, built from sensory half-memory rather than rumor alone, exactly the "seed, don't explain" approach. None of these require anything to have happened yet; they work the moment Orena is just being herself.

**The shanty.** The Knot had a work-song, Leilon dockhand tradition, never written down, just passed hand to hand on night watches and long hauls. Give it a real tune and a short, callable verse:

> *Call:* "When the stars go quiet and the water's black,"
> *Response:* "Hold a steady line, hold a steady line."
> *Call:* "Trust the hand beside you, you'll find your way back,"
> *Response:* "Hold a steady line, hold your crew."

Orena hums or half-sings a few bars at some unguarded moment, coiling rope, a long night watch, and can't say where she knows it from. The payoff lands later, retroactively: the first time she stands aboard the *Wayfinder* and hears it sung properly again, this earlier, throwaway moment should recontextualize itself in the player's own head. She already knew. She just didn't know she knew.

**The knot.** A specific locking hitch, call it **the wayfinder's bend**, taught by Leilon guild navigators specifically because it holds fast under load and releases clean with one pull, built for exactly the kind of emergency where a line has to come free in a hurry. Orena ties it without thinking, early and often, whenever she's handling rope. First beat: just a character moment, someone (Tack, a dockside rigger, a Route Warden) asks where she picked it up, she doesn't fully know. **Second beat, later, lands harder:** a rigger on another crew entirely ties the same bend, unprompted, and when asked, says something like "another crew taught us that, good outfit, real sticklers about doing it properly." That crew is, unnamed in the moment, the *Wayfinder*. This is the first secondhand trace that the Knot actually still exists out there, delivered through a technique instead of a name.

**The whistle.** A three-note descending signal the Knot used as kids for "all clear" or "coming about," muscle memory older than the guild itself. Lower-key than the shanty or the knot, same function, something Orena does once and nobody at the table needs to clock it the first time.

**Leilon texture.** Smaller, more ambient, doesn't need to point at the Knot specifically most of the time: a particular dockside sweet bread (**tide-cake**), a parting phrase locals use instead of "fair winds" (**"fair current"**), a counting cadence for timed rope-pulls ("one, two, hold" rather than "one, two, three"). Mostly these just make Leilon a real, remembered place that occasionally aches. Once or twice, raise one specifically near a Knot-adjacent contact (a Board captain who did business near Leilon, a chart-house clerk) so it reads as slightly less random a coincidence in hindsight.

**Sequencing.** The shanty, the knot's first beat, the whistle, and Leilon texture are all low-stakes early-arc plants, pure character color, no confirmation of anything. The knot's second beat ("another crew taught us") is the first one that actually confirms something real is out there, so it lands better once a few of the purely personal beats have had room to breathe, call it mid-arc.

## Research DC ladder: finding the Wayfinder

For whenever Orena (or the table) actively digs, not just waits for ambient hints to land. **DC 12 through 18 stay general, survival-possibility research, not leads on the *Wayfinder* specifically.** The fiction needs to establish that crossings like hers are survivable at all before it starts handing out ship-specific intel; jumping straight to "there's a charter called the *Wayfinder*" at low DCs would spoil arc 1's own payoffs early. Works with Investigation, History, an Insight-flavored gather-information roll at a port, or even a lucky Performance check if she sings the shanty somewhere the wrong person hears it. **Cumulative**: each DC's information is on top of everything below it, not a replacement for it. Scale the DC to the quality of the source available in the scene, a dockside rumor mill is a worse source than a chart-house's own trade gossip. Each tier below lists several specific, concrete facts to actually hand the player, not a vague gesture at "a rumor."

- **DC 12, survival is possible, in the abstract.**
  - Old sailors' and spacer accounts describe crossings much like hers: a thin place tearing open between an ordinary sea and the Astral, pulling a whole ship through at once.
  - At least some of those crossings are known to have landed survivors safely in Wildspace, not every such story ends in a wreck or a disappearance.
  - Nothing here is about her crew specifically. Just proof that what happened to her has happened to others, and some of them lived.
- **DC 15, the mechanism, and the odds.**
  - A scholar, chart-house record, or astrozoologist's notes describe the actual cause: a dying kindori's last pulse tearing a crossing open by force, the same thing that happened to her.
  - Whether you come through whole or scattered seems to depend on exactly where you are relative to the pull when it hits, attached to the hull or not. That detail should land close to home without confirming anything about her own crew yet.
  - Still general. A real, textured possibility, not a lead.
- **DC 18, it stops being abstract.**
  - A specific account surfaces: a crossing matching her own guild's disappearance, same stretch of the Sword Coast, same rough timeframe, is on record as having put survivors ashore in Wildspace.
  - No name, no ship, no number of survivors, just confirmation that whatever happened to the Wayfinder Knot that night, somebody on that crossing made it through alive on the other side.
  - This is the point Orena has real, specific reason to believe, not just hope, that her crew could actually be out there.
- **DC 20, a candidate, concrete now.**
  - A small charter outfit called the ***Wayfinder*** has climbed the Board unusually fast, on reputation alone, "came from nowhere a few years back," captained by one **Dune Ashcombe**, current rank ~99.
  - Dockside talk says the ship herself is strange to look at: old, mismatched salvage worked visibly into a hull that's otherwise too young to look that patched, a bell, carved rail, rigging hardware, nobody outside the crew knows why.
  - A clerk or rival mentions, almost as trivia, that "Wayfinder" is an odd half-name for a ship, like someone forgot the rest of it. Nothing yet proves this is them, but it's the first lead specific enough to actually chase.
- **DC 22, a direct match, unambiguous.**
  - A rigger aboard or adjacent to them ties a specific locking hitch, the wayfinder's bend, exactly the way Orena does, or someone hums a few bars of an old dockhand work-shanty she half-remembers from nowhere she can name.
  - Someone refers to the operation by its full old name, "the **Wayfinder Knot**," without knowing why that distinction should matter to anyone. Orena knows instantly. No more ambiguity: this is them.
  - **Colin Aldous**, specifically, named as one of the crew, or a crewmate's peg leg comes up as an identifying detail.
- **DC 25, whereabouts.**
  - A specific port the *Wayfinder* is due at on a specific job.
  - A contract they've bid that the *Night Fury* could plausibly cross paths with.
  - The name of a chart-house or broker they use regularly.
- **DC 27, it's mutual.**
  - Word that one crewmate, specifically, has spent years personally chasing fragments of a rumor about "a water genasi navigator," and takes every dead end hard. That's Colin, though the source may not have his name.
  - A second crewmate has quietly started reining the first one in, not from doubt, from not wanting to watch them break again. That's Dune.
- **DC 30, a direct line.**
  - An address or dock schedule precise enough to actually be there when they are.
  - A mutual contact who could broker an introduction outright, rather than leaving it to chance.
  - The point where "I know they're alive" becomes "I can be in the same room as them."

## The reunion hook, two-stage

**Stage one: the reveal, by arc 1's end.** Not a slow-burn mid-to-late beat anymore, built to land before arc 1 closes. The scattered hints above (shanty, knot, whistle, Leilon texture) run ambient through most of arc 1, low-stakes and personal. The knot's "another crew taught us" beat, or a research roll against the DC ladder above, is what tips it from ambient color into real, table-knowable fact: Orena's old crew is alive, out there, captaining the *Wayfinder*, climbing the same Board she is. This does not require the two crews to meet. It only requires Orena, and the table, to know for certain that there's someone to meet.

**Stage two: the actual encounter, arc 2.** Nobody goes looking in the sense of a chase. The bridge is still navigator talent as shop talk: as the *Night Fury* picks up reputation, "a water genasi navigator, genuinely gifted, joined some independent crew" is precisely the detail that travels the *Wayfinder*'s specific professional gossip, disproportionate to actual rank, because it's the one thing this crew can't help paying attention to. Once stage one has landed, both sides now have reason to actively close the distance rather than wait on coincidence, which is the real difference between the two stages: stage one is Orena learning they exist, stage two is someone from either side deciding to act on it. Whether that's Colin (who'd know instantly and hit hardest) or Dune (more careful about hoping) initiating is worth deciding when it actually happens rather than locking in now.

**After the reunion: where the Knot lands.** Two destinations, not mutually exclusive, both already live in the campaign's actual endgame machinery:

- **Estra's rebuilt fleet.** The Knot are working navigators who ported the exact skill the reborn Compact is being rebuilt around, chart by feel, no fixed routes, sell what you find instead of hoarding it. A crew that already proved the model works at small scale is close to the single best recruit Estra's long game could ask for, and it means Orena's reunion directly strengthens the thing Estra's been quietly building toward for fifteen years, not just a warm personal beat sitting beside the plot.
- **Vaelren's new house.** A fast-rising, well-regarded navigation and charting outfit is also exactly the kind of asset a new Fifth House contender would want locked down early, before Windsong, Crestfall, or Darkshade think to court them first. If the Knot's reputation is live by arc 2, Vaelren courting them (possibly through Aerion, who'd recognize the value instantly) is a natural beat, and it threads Orena's personal arc through Aerion's endgame the same way the campaign's been building overlap between the two leads all along.

**Keep both live rather than picking now.** Whether the Knot ends up fully in one, split between both (the founding five don't have to agree with each other about it, Colin and Dune in particular could land on opposite sides of that choice), or something that only resolves once the table's actually in the scene, is a call to make when the reunion itself is being built, not before. See `campaign/Story Spine.md`'s Vocalise-recognition beat (the whale fall) for the other half of this thread; that payoff and this one are cousins, not the same scene.
