# Finnick "Finny" Groles

Rigger / Deckhand / backup Gunner. Halfling. Loud, warm, gossips relentlessly, runs the game nights, and cannot keep a secret except his own.

## The surface read
The heart of the ship and its leaky pipe. Finny knows everyone's business within a day and shares it within two, always meaning well and always making it worse. He runs the crew's game nights, buys something stupid at every port, and gets the Manifest Board printed so he can pore over a ranking that never moves. When the crew wins a rung he throws a party. When they lose it he throws a smaller one. He feels every rank in the standings personally.

**Voice:** a firehose of gossip and enthusiasm, and hopeless at reading the room he's trying to cheer up.
**Tics:** the stupid souvenir from every port; the sending stone always in a pocket; the "ALMOST" note on the Board printout, crossed out and rewritten four times.
**Want:** to never again be a kid on a gantry rail with nothing to trade, and to find out that needing this crew doesn't put him back there.

**Off duty:** works a **sending stone** (a paired comms crystal) to trade gossip and make small deals with contacts across a dozen ports, hosts the crew game night in the galley (cards and dice, `rules/Downtime Games.md`), and studies the Manifest Board like scripture.

**Played, session 1: he'll cross a real line for it.** At Reefside he pickpocketed House Vellum's own steward for the lockbox's key on the spot, over Aerion's flat "bad idea" and everyone else's mild curiosity, purely because a locked box nobody's allowed to look in is exactly the kind of information he can't stand not having. Nobody turned him in. He still has the key; the crew doesn't know that for certain (`sessions/Session 1.md`). It's the same instinct as the gossip, pointed at something with real teeth this time, worth remembering the next time "harmless" curiosity is on the table.

## Why he cares about ships so much
Finny grew up **portless**, in a foundling house down in the rope city of the Rock of Bral, one of a dozen dock kids who slept three to a bunk and spent their days watching hulls come and go from a gantry rail. To those kids a ship wasn't a machine, it was the way out and the proof that somebody, somewhere, wanted a crew. A berth meant you existed. You counted. You had a place and people who noticed if you didn't show up.

He got his first crew berth at fifteen and never got over the miracle of it. That's why the Manifest Board is holy to him: it's the public scoreboard of belonging. To be on it at all, even 348th out of 351, means you are a real crew on a real ship, and a kid on a gantry rail could look up and see your name. That is worth a party every single time.

## Why gossip, specifically (the economics of it)
A portless foundling kid has no money, no name, and no house behind him, but he can generate one thing for free: information. Finny learned early that knowing something first, and knowing who wants to know it, is the one currency a kid with nothing can trade in, and that hoarding and dealing it is how you make sure you're never powerless again. That's why he trades other people's secrets so freely and guards his own so completely, it isn't a contradiction, it's the same instinct pointed two directions. Spending someone else's secret costs him nothing and buys him standing. Spending his own is the one trade he can't afford to make, because leverage isn't leverage once it's gone, and a boy who grew up with nothing learned that lesson all the way down. His real fear was never being pitied. It's being caught without anything to trade when it actually matters.

## Before this crew

The foundling house was called **Meadowlark's**, though nobody who grew up there remembers a Meadowlark, just **Sef**, who ran it for forty years on donated cots and whatever the dock crews left behind. Finny shared a bunk stack with two other kids who mattered: **Otto**, older, quieter, who taught him to read a manifest before he could read a book and left for a berth of his own at sixteen without saying goodbye (Finny still checks the Board for his ship's name, out of habit, and has never found it); and **Wick**, younger, who idolized Finny the way Finny idolized the crews on the gantry, and who died of a bad winter fever the year Finny turned thirteen, three weeks before a berth would have taken him off the Rock for the first time. Finny has never told anyone aboard about Wick. It's the second-truest reason the deposits to Sef never stop, the first being the kids sleeping there now, the second being a promise he never said out loud to a nine-year-old who didn't get to grow up and leave.

He got his first real berth at fifteen on a bulk hauler crewed mostly by retirees who took him on as a favor to Sef, and it was miserable, cold work, and he loved every second of it purely because it meant a name on a manifest and a bunk that was his. **Estra recruited him directly off that hauler four years later**, not for his rigging (competent, not exceptional) but because she'd heard, correctly, that he knew everyone on the Rock and everyone owed him a favor, and a captain quietly rebuilding a fleet from nothing needs exactly that kind of person aboard. He has never worked out that this is why she picked him, and would probably be thrilled and a little hurt in equal measure if he did.

## Who he's paying at Bral (the secret)
Every visit to the Rock, Finny quietly deposits part of his pay with **Sef**, the old woman who ran the foundling house he grew up in. She is aging now and the house is always short, and the money goes to the kids who sleep there today, three to a bunk, watching the hulls.

He will not say who or why, and it is the one secret the biggest gossip aboard keeps perfectly. Partly he doesn't want it to be a *thing*, partly he can't stand to be pitied, and partly it is the single private tender act of a man who gives everything else away. The crew has noticed the deposits and, in their one great kindness to him, has never once asked.

### Escalation ladder
- **Plant** (running now): the sending stone gossip, the souvenir at every port, the deposit the crew has quietly noticed and never asked about, the "ALMOST" note on the Board printout.
- **Pressure (trigger, from Plot Threads):** the crew docks at Bral. **Reveal paths:** a kid from the house recognizes him on the gantry; Sef falls ill or the house's rent is called in by a landlord who smells the Guttering's squeeze on the lower city; or Sprat, who knows everything, lets a corner of it slip for candy and then looks stricken.
- **Climax:** any reveal path turns the ship's warmest running gag into a real scene. The secret at Bral is the one piece of leverage Finny has never spent, on anyone, ever, so the moment the house is threatened and he has to ask the crew for help outright, he isn't just revealing a secret, he's trading the one asset his whole survival strategy was built never to trade. That's the actual stakes, bigger than Sef or the kids: does needing people cost him the independence he built this entire economy to protect, or does he learn it doesn't.

## Finny's gossip (drop one, or roll a d12)

The point of Finny is that he's a delivery system for character. Deal one of these whenever the ship is quiet, a transit is dull, or a player asks him a question he cannot resist answering. Each one hands the table a real thread about a crewmate, wrapped in a joke. He believes all of it; he's right about most of it.

| d12 | What Finny says |
|---|---|
| 1 | "Cap's mug used to say 'World's Best.' Somebody knifed those two words clean out and left the 'Captain.' I have six theories. Two of them make me sad." |
| 2 | "She turned down a job last month that would've cleared our whole debt. Didn't blink. Who *does* that? What does she know that we don't?" |
| 3 | "Tack leaves his maintenance log open where I'll read it now. Last night's entry was a poem. To a pipe. It rhymed. I've read it four times." |
| 4 | "A man in a very good coat asked Tack his 'make and serial number' at the yard. Tack answered him, cheerful as anything. I did not like that man, and I did not like that question." |
| 5 | "Brass swears she's never been to the Cold Anchorage. Brass has two guys at the Cold Anchorage. You do the math, because I can't and it keeps me up." |
| 6 | "She keeps a bag packed. Not a go-bag. A *gone*-bag. I found it once, put it back exactly, and didn't sleep that night." |
| 7 | "Oz still writes to the inspector who fined us. They write *back*. They're pen pals now. I am not part of it and I am, frankly, a little hurt." |
| 8 | "Ask Oz about his old company and his whole face closes like a hatch. Somebody hurt that big polite man, and I think he believes it was him." |
| 9 | "The bosun reads to Flurry when he thinks the deck's clear. Whole chapters. Does the voices. You didn't hear that from me and neither did I." |
| 10 | "Orena hums the same three notes on the night watch, in her sleep sometimes. Brass says leave it. Brass knows something. Brass always knows something." |
| 11 | "Don't tell anyone, but I set a little aside every time we hit Bral. Good reasons. Warm reasons. That's all you get, and it's more than I've told anyone, so we're close now, you and me." |
| 12 | "I know I talk too much. But somebody's got to carry all of it, and it might as well be the one who actually cares what happens to you lot." |

**On the two about the PCs (9 and 10):** these are Finny's *observations*, offered to the table as colour, never as fact the player has to accept. Aerion doting on Flurry and Orena's three notes (the Vocalise) are hooks the players can confirm, wave off, or quietly make true at their own pace. Finny is often right and always talking; what's real about a PC stays the player's call.

## With the two leads
- **Orena.** He brings her tea when she's up late at the chart table with her maps, and he never asks what she's looking for, because he knows what it is to look for your people. Of anyone aboard, Finny understands the search without needing it explained.
- **Aerion.** He runs the betting pool on Flurry's moods while Aerion insists, coldly, that she's just being dramatic. He would talk Aerion's ear clean off given the chance, and Aerion, who dotes in secret, lets him more often than he admits.

## Arc
The heart and the leak learns that his own secret is safe with this crew, that this is the first berth that was never going to put him back on the rail. If the foundling house is threatened, the crew helping is the moment Finny stops being the kid who got out and becomes the man who reaches back. Keep him warm. His growth is learning that spending his one real secret, actually needing people, doesn't cost him the independence he spent a lifetime building gossip into.
