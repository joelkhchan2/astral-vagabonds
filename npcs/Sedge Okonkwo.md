# Sedge Okonkwo, and Dessa Kell

Sedge, captain of the *Bright Arrears* (rank ~240-250, `factions/The Manifest Board.md`), and Dessa Kell, captain of the *Compact Standard* (rank 9), are a linked pair, not two separate threads. Both are former Vane Compact captains (`npcs/Captain Estra Vane.md`), and their history with each other is also their history with the Compact's fall.

## The surface read, Sedge
A former Vane Compact hand who took House Vellum's buyout outright when the fleet came apart, and has never once looked comfortable about it since. Ashamed and defiant in roughly equal measure: ashamed because some part of him has always suspected he was wrong, defiant because admitting that outright means admitting fifteen years of his own choices were built on a mistake.

**Voice:** quick to justify himself before anyone asks him to, the practiced ease of a man who's explained this decision to himself more times than to anyone else.
**Tic:** checks over his shoulder out of habit when Vellum's name comes up, even in casual conversation, even when there's no reason to.

## The old disagreement, not a lovers' spat
When the Compact came apart, both of them watched eleven independent captains get picked off patiently, one at a time, by a house with more money and more time than any of them had. They drew opposite conclusions. **Dessa's:** rebuild clean, stay independent, prove the thing is survivable. **Sedge's:** don't be the next easy target, get inside the machine instead of standing against it. He took Vellum's buyout believing it was the smart move, not a betrayal, and he genuinely tried to talk Dessa into taking the same deal. She refused flatly and rebuilt the *Compact Standard* on her own instead.

That's the real wound between them. Not jealousy, a real argument about how the world works, and time has only widened the gap since: she bet on herself and won. He bet on safety and has spent years growing quietly less sure of the price.

## What actually eroded him
Vellum's "safety" came with a cost that wasn't obvious when he signed: the *Bright Arrears* has been assigned, job by job, to routes that squeeze other small independents the same way the old Compact got squeezed, each one a little easier to justify than the last until they stopped being justifiable at all. He didn't see it coming. He can see it now, and it's the thing he can't say out loud, to Dessa or to himself.

**The exclusivity clause.** Vellum's buyout wasn't a clean payday, it came with a multi-year exclusivity term on the *Bright Arrears* written specifically so a captain can't walk the moment he regrets it. Leaving early means owing back a chunk of what he took, money he's long since spent. It's a genuinely practical trap underneath the shame: he's not choosing Vellum over Dessa, he's stuck in a contract he can't afford to break, and it's easier to be the man who chose wrong than the man who's just broke and cornered.

**Real fear, not just money.** He watched what Vellum's patience did to eleven captains who never even fought back. Walking away isn't just financially risky, it's personally frightening, because he has direct, lived proof of what that house does to people who stop being useful to it.

## Why Dessa finally asked him to stop
Not disapproval from a distance. Recognition, up close, of someone she still loves slowly turning into the exact thing that broke them both the first time. She can't put it on paper because it's an accusation, not a worry, and she isn't ready to say it that plainly either. That's grief and vindication in the same breath, which is worse than either alone. Session 4: she asked him to stop flying, full stop, no explanation offered; he couldn't explain to Orena why he couldn't just do it. The *why* was never spoken at the table, this is the answer underneath it, GM-only until it's earned on screen.

**Session 4, played:** Sedge broke down in front of Orena, confirmed the gossip (he's the "D" in it), and Orena pushed him to go find Dessa and say the real thing in person rather than keep hiding from it.

## He's said no before
At rank ~250, Sedge has had other offers over the years, better pay, a cleaner ship, a different flag. He's turned every one down, because none of them were actually different, just a new boss instead of Vellum, same shape, same risk, same fear of being made an example of if he tried to leave. A better job was never going to be enough to move him.

## Session 7: Estra's offer, and what actually tips it
Dessa's in port, word already circulating (`sessions/Session 7 Prep.md` &sect;4, Finny's gossip table). Whatever the table plays out between Sedge and Dessa directly, Estra's own approach happens alongside it, not instead of it, and it isn't a job offer.

**What she pitches isn't "come fly for me."** It's what she's quietly been building for fifteen years (`npcs/Captain Estra Vane.md`, "The Long Game"): a nomadic, chart-and-share network that makes Vellum's whole toll economy irrelevant, the thing Dessa already proved was survivable just by rebuilding clean, now organized into something bigger than one captain's stubbornness. Sedge isn't being asked to switch employers. He's being asked to help dismantle the reason a house like Vellum gets to have that kind of leverage over anyone in the first place.

**What makes it possible, not just tempting:** Estra isn't offering him an escape into nothing. She's offering to actually fund his way out.

- **Her own 20,000 gp**, savings quietly built over fifteen years at the bottom of the Board, specifically for a moment like this.
- **An 80,000 gp loan from House Nightbreeze**, real and plausible on their own terms, not a favor: Nightbreeze is already playing both sides of the Guttering denial, positioned as "an opportunist profiting whichever way Vellum falls" (`factions/House Nightbreeze.md`). Prying one of Vellum's own captains loose is exactly the kind of quiet leverage against Vellum they'd fund without ever being anyone's ally. No one aboard the *Night Fury* needs to broker this; it's Estra's own move, through channels the crew doesn't see.
- **100,000 gp total**, covering both the exclusivity clause's exit fee (deliberately punitive, Vellum's calculated cost of letting a captain walk) and the *Bright Arrears* herself, repurposed as Estra's long-planned second hull rather than a ship built from scratch.

**Why it tips him when nothing else has:** not safety, not even just Dessa alone. A real chance to be part of proving the system that broke him, and nearly cost him her, doesn't have to be the only way to fly. For the first time, leaving isn't jumping into nothing.

**Not resolved here.** Whether Sedge actually takes the offer, how Dessa reacts to it, and whether the *Bright Arrears* becomes Estra's second ship this session or down the line are all live, worth playing out rather than deciding on the page. If it lands, this is the first real, named step toward the Compact rebuilding itself, one actual captain at a time, not just a locked chest and a dream.
