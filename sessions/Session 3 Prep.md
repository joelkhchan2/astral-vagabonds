# Session 3 Prep

Working doc for whichever contract gets picked at the end of session 2. Payout now tracks danger across the board: **Quiet Water stays the cheap, genuinely safe pick**, and the two pricier options both carry real combat risk, on the level of last session's Bex fight, not a token scuffle. Don't run all three, prep the one the table actually takes.

## Quiet Water · 1,300 gp · the safe pick

No combat content by design. This is the one to steer toward if the table's still recovering from session 1 and 2 and wants a genuinely light night.

- **The hook:** the patriarch keeps almost-mentioning that he's heard the *Night Fury*'s name from somewhere he won't specify.
- **Branch A:** the crew lets it go. Uneventful, warm trip, good character material, no follow-up required.
- **Branch B:** someone pushes on it. Keep the reveal small and mundane if you go there, dockside gossip, an old business dealing with a house Aerion's family touches, nothing that needs to connect to the lockbox. Resist the urge to make everything the same conspiracy.

## The Ledger Run · 2,400 gp · real danger, first tier

**No Reefside.** The two archive stations are further out along the Windward Edge, unrelated to the ambush site, the crew has no reason to want back there yet and this job doesn't ask them to.

**The threat: Rimmon Vex, the *Cutlass Sunday*.** Established rival at rank 344 (`factions/The Manifest Board.md`), sour, opportunistic, takes being passed on the Board as a personal insult, and has been watching this crew since word got around they survived Bex. He's not trying to kill anyone, he's trying to find out if that fight was a fluke, and he'll take the survey data as a prize if he can get it without real cost to himself.

**THE CUTLASS SUNDAY.** A worse Swift Cutter, meaner than it looks, and a real step up from the tutorial-grade skiff at session 0. **AC 14 · HP 190 · Speed 55 ft · Damage threshold 14 · two ballistae · crew ~6.** Enough to actually hurt the *Night Fury* if the crew gets careless, not enough to win a straight fight if they don't.

**Branch points:**
1. **The approach.** A direct heading past Vex's usual hunting grounds (faster, on schedule) or a longer swing wide of it (safer, costs margin on the 18-day window). A real choice with a real cost either way.
2. **First contact.** Vex hails before he commits, testing. He'll take a bribe, back off if he reads genuine confidence, or press if he smells hesitation. Let the crew's response (pay him off, talk him down, call the bluff) actually decide what happens next, this is the scene, not the fight.
3. **If it goes hot:** a real ship-to-ship exchange, side initiative, both ballistae live, and Vex is willing to board if he thinks he's winning. Play it with the same weight as the Bex fight, injuries possible, gp on the line (the survey data itself is the prize he's after, not the crew). **He breaks off once it's clearly not going his way**, he's a scavenger, not a suicide, but "clearly not going his way" should cost the crew something to establish.
4. **Aftermath, if he loses:** a real dent in his standing at the Compass Rose, and a grudge that's earned rather than assumed. If he wins anything, even partial, play it as a genuine setback, not a wipe, this is Tier 1 and he's a recurring face, not a boss.

## Two Chairs Short · 3,200 gp · real danger, higher tier

**Reframed as a joint job, not a rescue.** The outfit is **Ollo Trask's *Quiet Sum*** (rank 346, `factions/The Manifest Board.md`), the crew's established cordial rival, the one who undercuts them on price and apologizes sincerely while doing it. He lost two of his four crew mid-contract and won't give up the fee, so he's offering to split it for a second hull to run the chaperone job *with* him, his ship and the *Night Fury* both, two captains, one contract. Reversing the usual dynamic (the undercutter needing them for once) is the point, play it warmly.

**Built-in texture:** two crews, two chains of command, on one job. Trask is relentlessly cheerful and genuinely not used to taking orders from anyone; let that create small, funny friction (whose call is it when the two ships disagree on approach) that doesn't need to escalate into anything, just color, the good kind of complication.

**The threat: an unmarked boarding hull, grappling jaws.** This is why Trask actually lost his people, not a rival poaching them. **Run it off Aeravellis's spec sheet, unnamed** (`factions/House Windsong.md`): a converted Balanced Trader, **AC 15 · HP 250 · Speed 35 ft · four ballistae and grappling jaws · crew 15.** That's a genuinely dangerous hull, comparable to the *Night Fury* alone, which is exactly why it needs to be a joint fight to feel fair rather than a wipe. **It wants easy cargo, not a fight**, and breaks off the moment the boarded target shows it can actually resist, which is precisely what Trask's short-handed crew couldn't do and this crew can.

**Branch points:**
1. **Getting the real story.** Does Trask admit up front what actually happened to his crew, or does it come out under pressure once the two ships are already committed to the job together? Either works; a reluctant confession mid-transit hits harder.
2. **The boarding.** The raider closes on whichever ship it reads as weaker, almost certainly the *Quiet Sum*, first. That puts real stakes on Trask's remaining two crew, named if you want the danger to land (give them a line each before this scene, per the usual NPC rule). This is where "more lethal, like last session" should actually live, someone who isn't a PC can plausibly get hurt or worse here, and it should feel like it could go that way.
3. **Resolution options:** fight it off together (the *Night Fury*'s guns are the deciding factor, a good spotlight for whoever's gunning), draw its attention away from the *Quiet Sum* onto the *Night Fury* on purpose (a real tactical choice, Aerion or Estra's call), or a partial loss, cargo or coin taken, everyone alive. Don't railroad toward the clean win.
4. **Aftermath.** Whatever happens, this is the strongest bottom-of-the-Board friendship beat available right now with Bex's slot gone quiet (`factions/The Manifest Board.md`). It also quietly deposits another data point toward the eventual Aeravellis reveal (M6, `campaign/Tier 1.md`), don't name him, don't explain the hull, just let the pattern exist for later.
