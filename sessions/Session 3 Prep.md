He's not trying to kill anyone, he's trying to find out if that fight was a fluke, and he'll take the survey data as a prize if he can get it without real cost to himself.

**THE CUTLASS SUNDAY.** A worse Swift Cutter, meaner than it looks, and a real step up from the tutorial-grade skiff at session 0. **AC 14 · HP 190 · Speed 55 ft · Damage threshold 14 · two ballistae · crew ~6.** Enough to actually hurt the *Night Fury* if the crew gets careless, not enough to win a straight fight if they don't.

## Two Chairs Short · 5,600 gp total, ~2,800 gp to the crew after the split · real danger, higher tier

**Reframed: a peer asking for help, not an undercutter needing a favor.** The outfit is **Captain Marlow Aldous's *Fair Return*** (Cutter-class, **rank 331**, new canon, added to `factions/The Manifest Board.md`'s mid-board), notably *above* the crew on the Board, not below. Aldous runs a small, professional escort-and-courier outfit, **nine crew normally, five now**, four lost this past month, none of it violent (see below). The job is a Stoneward-licensed chaperone run through the Tollmarch, and **Stoneward's own escort rules require two independently crewed hulls for a contract at this value**, the same enforcement category that put the *Tollkeeper* on the Board at all (`factions/The Manifest Board.md`). That's the real reason he needs a second hull, full stop, no amount of competence from his remaining five gets around a licensing requirement. Losing four in a month on top of that just means the *Fair Return* is running lean and tired going in, not desperate.

**What's actually being escorted, pick one rather than locking it in, four tons either way:**
- **Raw starglass** (default lean). Unrefined crystal, mined off a claim out past the Windward Edge, the material spelljamming helms are eventually cut from before any shipwright touches it. Valuable per pound, easy to fence, a shipwright doesn't ask a supplier where uncut stock came from the way a jeweler asks about a cut stone. Ties the raider's motive directly to Aeravellis's own fraud (`factions/House Windsong.md`): raw material funds a desperate operation without leaving much of a trail, exactly his situation.
- **Refined navigational instruments.** Precision-built, fragile, absurdly high value per crate, bound for a shipwright or a founding house's own fleet. Raises the stakes of careless handling in a fight (breakage, not just theft) and gives Aerion's ledger-eye something concrete to fuss over.
- **A sealed diplomatic pouch, correspondence for a founding house.** Heavier, stranger cargo, opens a soft connective thread to the still-unshared Amberwake letters if you ever want the crew to notice a pattern in who moves paper quietly through the Tollmarch. Best if you want tonight to plant something rather than just pay out.
- **A locked medical shipment**, antitoxin and healing draughts bound for a colony short on both. Shifts the whole job's urgency from money to people, a real ticking clock (a colony waiting on this) rather than an abstract cargo value. Good pick if the table wants the danger to feel like it costs something beyond gold if it's lost.

**The client stays thin regardless of which cargo you pick**, a competent, unremarkable concern out past the Edge who genuinely doesn't need a scene tonight, this job belongs to Aldous and the two crews. **Why Stoneward gates cargo like this behind a two-hull licensing rule, whichever version you run:** it's valuable, portable, and exactly the sort of thing that funds a desperate operation quietly, which happens to describe the raider's own situation. He doesn't need to explain the theft to anyone. He just needs it gone before anyone official comes asking.

**Why Aldous picked this crew, specifically, over anyone closer to his own rank.** He heard about the Bex fight secondhand, and he heard Finny's own line making the rounds at the Compass Rose, that they "jumped the Board without lifting a finger" (`Session 2.md`). He read that as exactly backwards: nobody jumps three ranks doing nothing. **A higher-ranked captain seeking this crew out on reputation, not convenience, is worth landing as a real beat**, it's a live, small proof of the campaign's standing theme that the number is not the story (`factions/The Manifest Board.md`). That's the reason he'd give out loud. There's a second, quieter one underneath it, below.

**Estra and Aldous: an old fleet, crossed without ever meeting.** Aldous served as ordinary crew aboard a different Vane Compact vessel, years back, under **Captain Oskar Bellamy** (full canon: `npcs/Captain Estra Vane.md`), never Estra's own ship, and the two never crossed paths directly. What he actually has of her is thin, a name every crew in the fleet knew as the Commodore's, nothing personal. **There was no single day the Compact ended for him.** Bellamy was squeezed out slowly, over months, no bribe and no confrontation, just every contract worth taking quietly going to someone else first, every honest client offered something faster and cheaper at the last minute, until flying independently just wasn't possible anymore. Aldous watched that happen to his captain in real time without ever fully understanding why, and it's the reason he sold rather than the Compact itself formally dissolving. **Aldous bought the ship from him and became her captain.** The *Fair Return* is that ship, an old Compact hull under a new name and a new coat of paint.

**How the recognition actually plays, in sequence, not all at once, and best saved for the rendezvous, the two ships' first real meeting, before the transit gets underway.** When the job's paperwork named the *Night Fury*'s captain, Aldous saw the surname Vane and it gave him a private, unspoken hope, he couldn't be sure it was the same woman, and said nothing about it to anyone, including his own crew, which is the real, second reason he chose this crew over one closer to his own rank. **The actual confirmation isn't a name, it's the ship.** Estra would know an old Compact-built Cutter's lines on sight, whatever paint and name it's flying under now, and her reaction to the *Fair Return*'s hull, not to Aldous himself, is what actually lands the beat. Play it visual and quiet: a held look a half second too long, a hand that stops on a rail, something she doesn't explain and he doesn't ask about, not yet. **This doesn't need dialogue to land, and shouldn't get much**, a look-and-move-on beat, not a scene that stops the session. If a PC notices and pushes on it later, that's a real, earned follow-up, not something to force tonight.

**Optional, GM-only, never spend without deciding on purpose:** if Estra's already-planted habit of quietly checking on her old fleet's fate (`Session 2 Prep.md` §4) has ever touched old Compact registry paperwork, she may have already seen Bellamy's name once, on a bill of sale, without it meaning anything at the time. Cheap, quiet irony if you want it. Not required.

**Bellamy himself stays a loose thread**, retired, hasn't been seen in years, whereabouts unknown even to Aldous. A name worth having on hand if the table ever wants to pull on it, nothing this session obligates you to use him for.

**ALDOUS'S CREW, THE FAIR RETURN. Nine normally, five now.**

Nobody's covering just their old job anymore: Perrin does first mate's work plus half of what Sennet used to handle personally; Dessa guns a mount built for two, alone; Wick pulls an engineer's watch and a rigger's watch on alternating days because there's nobody left to alternate with. **The unspoken rule holding it together: nobody says "short-handed" out loud.** Aldous set that tone without ever announcing it, and the rest follow it the way a crew follows a captain's mood on any ship. **Junebug is the one exception**, young enough that the rule hasn't fully taken with him yet, which is exactly why he's the one who might say the quiet thing out loud at the wrong moment, or the right one.

**Relationships aboard worth surfacing, pick one or two rather than running all of them, whichever a PC actually gets close to:**
- **Aldous and Perrin.** Fifteen years apart in rank and experience; Perrin was promoted into a job he didn't ask for and isn't sure he's earned. Aldous trusts him completely and has said so exactly once, badly, and neither of them has brought it up since.
- **Dessa and Wick.** Not romantic, just old crewmates, a friendship built entirely out of shorthand and shared shifts. She checks his engine readouts without being asked; he keeps a mug of something hot at her station without being asked. Neither would call it anything. The clearest surviving picture of what the *Fair Return* used to be at full strength.
- **Aldous and Junebug**, an alternate to the above rather than an addition. Less a friendship than a mentorship Aldous didn't plan on taking, he's short with him in front of the others and genuinely gentle with him alone, and hasn't noticed the contradiction yet.
- **Perrin and Dessa**, another alternate. Professional respect with an edge, two people who'd have been rivals for the same promotion in a full crew and both know it, never said aloud, not unfriendly, just present.

**How the two ships actually operate together, transit through the fight, present as live options rather than a fixed procedure:**
- **Comms.** *Either* an open channel checked on a fixed rotation at the top of every watch, disciplined, not left live and chattering, *or* old-fashioned signal lanterns and flag code, no magic needed, but genuinely vulnerable if weather or a current gets between the ships at the wrong moment. If the crew bought Sending Stones off the mercane (option 8, above), that's a real upgrade over either, worth a line of Aldous's own dry approval.
- **Watch rotation.** *Either* offset on purpose, the *Fair Return*'s watch changing at the half hour against the *Night Fury*'s so the two crews are never both half-alert at once (Aldous's idea, shows his competence without a speech about it), *or* synced, both ships resting and waking together, more efficient day to day but a real shared vulnerability window if the raider times an approach for it, a genuine tactical choice worth naming out loud if you want it to matter.
- **Chain of command, if it goes hot.** *Either* genuinely joint, Aldous deferring to the *Night Fury*'s side on tactics if they call it with confidence, having already watched them win one fight, *or* Aldous holding parity always, never quite deferring, real ongoing friction rather than easy cooperation. **Either way, he does not defer on anything touching his own crew's safety**, a real, playable friction point if a PC tries to give an order concerning one of his five people directly.
- **Downtime.** *Either* no formal docking, the two ships simply closing to signal range during the rest cycle, close enough a lit porthole on one is visible from the other's deck, *or* one real shared meal, an actual docking and a crew-mixer scene once during the transit, a stronger, warmer beat if the table has room for it tonight.

**The threat: an unmarked boarding hull, grappling jaws**, unrelated to why Aldous is short-handed, this is simply what's waiting in the lane. **Run it off Aeravellis's spec sheet, unnamed** (`factions/House Windsong.md`, crew 15 matches his fraud-hull exactly if you ever want to confirm the pattern later): a converted Balanced Trader, **AC 15 · HP 250 · Speed 35 ft · four ballistae and grappling jaws · crew 15.** Comparable to the *Night Fury* alone, which is exactly why this needs to be a joint fight to feel fair rather than a wipe. **It wants easy cargo, not a fight**, and breaks off the moment the boarded target shows it can actually resist.

**Branch points:**
1. **Getting the real story.** Does Aldous admit up front that he's down to five, or does it come out once the ships are already underway? A reluctant admission mid-transit hits harder, and it's a good spot for a little of the comedy above (Sennet Price's memorized resignation note, especially) to land before the tone turns serious again.
2. **The boarding.** The raider closes on whichever ship it reads as weaker. **Let that genuinely be an open question**, the *Fair Return* reads thin on sensors, but the *Night Fury* is the unknown quantity a raider might also misjudge. If it takes the *Fair Return*, Junebug is the name to reach for, a real crewmate in real danger, not an abstraction.
3. **Resolution options:** fight it off together (the *Night Fury*'s guns are the deciding factor, a good spotlight for whoever's gunning, and a real payoff if the Armory or Barrack bastion order below actually finished in time), draw its attention away from the *Fair Return* onto the *Night Fury* on purpose (a real tactical choice, Aerion or Estra's call), or a partial loss, cargo or coin taken, everyone alive. Don't railroad toward the clean win.
4. **Aftermath.** Whatever happens, Aldous says out loud, plainly, that he was right to come to this crew instead of someone closer to his own rank, that's the beat to land, not a party. This plants a genuine ally notably above the crew on the Board (a fresher, warmer version of the *Long Odds*/Ma Torrin dynamic sketched in `factions/The Manifest Board.md`'s mid-board section, worth deciding later whether Aldous grows into something similar), and opens a soft, obvious follow-up hook: does he go looking to crew back up to nine, and does this crew help him find people? It also quietly deposits another data point toward the eventual Aeravellis reveal (M6, `campaign/Tier 1.md`), don't name him, don't explain the hull, just let the pattern exist for later.

## Astral Sea event: the transit to the rendezvous

**Roll a d8 at the table, player's choice who rolls.** Not a DM pick, let the dice decide what the black throws at them this leg. Each entry below is written to run in ten to fifteen minutes with everything you need already in it, no further prep required. Spans the tone compass on purpose: 1 and 8 warm, 2 dread, 3 and 5 danger, 4 and 7 wonder, 6 eerie.

**1. Formation flying, warm.** The *Fair Return* hails with a specific, concrete request, not a vague "let's fly together": Aldous wants to run **echelon drills**, both ships flying a staggered diagonal off each other's wing so their gun arcs overlap and neither has a blind quarter, standard practice before two crews who've never fought side by side actually have to. **What this looks like at the table:** Aldous calls a heading and a spacing over the comm, and whoever's flying the *Night Fury* has to hold position relative to a moving ship rather than a fixed point, a DC 12 Vehicles (Space) check to settle smoothly into formation, and a second DC 12 a few minutes later when Aldous calls a turn and both ships have to reposition without breaking the overlap. **Succeed both and the *Fair Return*'s actual crew notice**, Perrin Oakes calls it "clean work" over the comm, Dessa Kray stops double-checking her gun mounts for a minute. **Fail either and nothing bad happens**, it's a drill, just a flatter, less impressive one, worth a laugh rather than a consequence. Tack is delighted regardless and starts narrating the *Fair Return*'s engine note back to its own crew like a doctor reading vitals. Costs nothing, plants nothing dark, this is pure competence-as-bonding.

**2. A prior kill, dread with a name attached.** The two ships pass a drifting scrap field, recent, unmistakably the same grappling-jaw damage the *Fair Return* already carries. **What's actually in the wreckage, specific and concrete, and unrelated to any of Aldous's own people:** bolts of undyed cloth, a crate of oranges gone soft and split in the cold, and one crewman's logbook, still legible, its last entry cut off mid-sentence ("--nothing on the scope, Rell thinks it's just--"). **Aldous goes quiet over the comm before anyone asks him to confirm it's the same raider.** Played with an awe half alongside the threat half per the standing tone-compass note (`CLAUDE.md`), the ordinary, unglamorous cargo and the cut-off sentence are what make the loss land as real rather than abstract, not a body, not a scream, just a thought nobody finished.

**3. A rare gathering of gray scavvers, danger-as-omen.** Reuse `rules/Tables.md` table 1, entry 6. **Gray Scavvers are the harmless, most common size class** (`references/Astral Sea Bestiary.md`, CR 1/4 each, medium, natural shoal-cleanup fauna), the kind of thing the crew has probably flown past a dozen times without remarking on it. **What makes this sighting worth a d8 entry is the size of the gathering**, a shoal of thirty or more, tight and restless, far more than would ever cluster for ordinary scraps. Per the Bestiary's own note, a gathering this size is a warning sign, they collect where something died. **Play it exactly as written: atmosphere, not a fight.** They scatter at the ship's approach and don't return fire if provoked lightly. What they were gathered around (a fresh kill, wreckage, nothing the crew can immediately identify) is the actual content of the scene, not the scavvers themselves.

> **GRAY SCAVVER** (`references/Astral Sea Bestiary.md`, verified, *Boo's Astral Menagerie*). Medium monstrosity, unaligned. **AC** 12 (natural armor) &middot; **HP** 22 (4d8+4) &middot; **Speed** 0 ft, fly 40 ft (wildspace/astral only) &middot; **CR** 1/4 (50 XP). **Bite:** +3 to hit, 5 (1d8+1) piercing.

**4. A kindori pod, wonder, and loaded specifically for Orena.** Reuse `rules/Tables.md` table 1, entry 2, as a full pod this time (six to nine adults) rather than the single distant sighting from session 1's chwinga vision (`Session 1.md`). **Concrete detail on what's riding one of them:** a colony of small, barnacle-shelled creatures called **driftlings**, harmless, filter-feeding on stardust in the kindori's wake, who've built a loose lattice of shell and bone on its back that catches the light like a second, smaller structure riding the first. Kindori are not background wonder to Orena, a dying one's death-song is what tore her original crew away from her. **Let the sighting land as genuinely beautiful before it lands as anything else.** If she wants to linger, a DC 13 Nature or Arcana check surfaces one true, small detail, their song carries further out here than sound should be able to, or one of the pod is riding a current that matches nothing on any chart the ship carries. Nothing that resolves the Vocalise, just a reminder it's still out there.

**5. Two brown scavvers, real danger, a genuine complication.** Reuse `rules/Tables.md` table 1, entry 6. **A step up from entry 3, this is a real encounter, not just atmosphere.** Two Brown Scavvers (`references/Astral Sea Bestiary.md`, CR 4 each, Large, opportunistic) have caught scent of the *Fair Return*'s wake, or of blood if the crew's already taken a hit this session, and are shadowing at a distance, closing slowly. **Per their own design note, they won't attack unless provoked or starving**, this is a chance to be driven off (a warning shot, a show of force, Oz simply standing visibly armed on the deck) rather than a fight to the death, and driving them off without a kill is the intended, rewarded outcome. If it does go hot, it's a real fight (CR 4 apiece is a genuine threat to a single PC caught alone) but not a campaign-defining one.

> **BROWN SCAVVER** (`references/Astral Sea Bestiary.md`, verified, *Boo's Astral Menagerie*). Large monstrosity, unaligned. **AC** 14 (natural armor) &middot; **HP** 90 (12d10+24) &middot; **Speed** 0 ft, fly 60 ft (wildspace/astral only) &middot; Skills Perception +5 &middot; Senses darkvision 120 ft &middot; **CR** 4 (1,100 XP). **Void Sense** (blindsight 30 ft, tracks by scent through vacuum). **Multiattack:** two Bite, +7 to hit, 14 (2d10+4) piercing each.

**6. A ship on the reciprocal heading, eerie.** Reuse `rules/Tables.md` table 2, entry 5. Another vessel, identical class to whichever ship the crew is aboard, on the exact opposite heading, close enough for a good look. **Nobody aboard either ship waves.** No hail answers, no distress signal, no weapons raised, just a ship that keeps its exact course and lets the gap close and then widen again without incident. If anyone tries a closer look (spyglass, a scrying attempt, a hard bank to pace it), the reflection breaks down the same way a detail resists being remembered, the harder you look, the less anyone agrees on what they actually saw. **No danger, no chase, no explanation.** Let it pass and be gone. This is the purest, least-explained eerie beat on the table on purpose, resist the urge to attach a reason to it.
