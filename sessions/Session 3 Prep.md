## Quiet Water · 1,300 gp · the safe pick

No combat content by design. This is the one to steer toward if the table's still recovering from session 1 and 2 and wants a genuinely light night.

**The client. OSRIC PRYNNE**, patriarch of House Prynne, a Chain merchant family with old manners and thin margins. **Voice tic:** speaks entirely in appraisals, "that'll do," "acceptable," "below the mark," never a plain yes or no. **Physical tic:** checks a brass chronometer he doesn't actually need to check, a tell for nerves he'd never admit to. **Want:** this match saves the family, and he knows it, and he hates that everyone at the Concourse will know it too.

**Who's actually being chaperoned. ISOLDE PRYNNE**, the eldest, pragmatic about the arrangement in a way her father mistakes for indifference. She isn't devastated and she isn't thrilled, she's watching everything, gathering information the way someone does when nobody's ever asked their opinion before. **Want:** to be taken seriously by exactly one person on this trip. Good spotlight material for whichever PC actually listens.

**Why Osric's heard the name, resolved and mundane on purpose.** House Prynne carries a **Nightbreeze policy**, and Nightbreeze's claims office has been buzzing about the *Grendel's Wake*'s total loss and the unranked crew that walked away from it, standard underwriter gossip, nothing sinister. Osric almost-mentions it because he's trying to work out whether hiring the crew that survived Bex Halloway is a good story to tell at the Concourse or a bad one, and he can't decide.

**Branch points:**
1. **The almost-mention.** The crew lets it go (uneventful, warm trip, no follow-up needed) or presses (Osric admits the Nightbreeze source easily, there's nothing to hide, and the scene becomes about *why he's so nervous about status* rather than a mystery to solve).
2. **A private moment with Isolde.** At some point on the voyage, she seeks out whichever PC has been paying attention to her rather than her father, and offers something real in return, a piece of Chain gossip, a genuine compliment about the ship, a small kindness. Let her be a person, not a plot device.
3. **At the Concourse, optional light color:** a rival merchant house present, a status-comedy beat (Oz's manners, Aerion's poise, the unnamed-ship-reads-as-heritage bit from `Dressing the Part` reused lightly), nothing that needs to escalate.

## The Ledger Run · 2,400 gp · real danger, first tier

**No Reefside.** The two archive stations are further out along the Windward Edge, unrelated to the ambush site, the crew has no reason to want back there yet and this job doesn't ask them to.

**The threat: Rimmon Vex, the *Cutlass Sunday*.** Established rival at rank 344 (`factions/The Manifest Board.md`), sour, opportunistic, takes being passed on the Board as a personal insult, and has been watching this crew since word got around they survived Bex. He's not trying to kill anyone, he's trying to find out if that fight was a fluke, and he'll take the survey data as a prize if he can get it without real cost to himself.

**THE CUTLASS SUNDAY.** A worse Swift Cutter, meaner than it looks, and a real step up from the tutorial-grade skiff at session 0. **AC 14 · HP 190 · Speed 55 ft · Damage threshold 14 · two ballistae · crew ~6.** Enough to actually hurt the *Night Fury* if the crew gets careless, not enough to win a straight fight if they don't.

**Branch points:**
1. **The approach.** A direct heading past Vex's usual hunting grounds (faster, on schedule) or a longer swing wide of it (safer, costs margin on the 18-day window). A real choice with a real cost either way.
2. **First contact.** Vex hails before he commits, testing. He'll take a bribe, back off if he reads genuine confidence, or press if he smells hesitation. Let the crew's response (pay him off, talk him down, call the bluff) actually decide what happens next, this is the scene, not the fight.
3. **If it goes hot:** a real ship-to-ship exchange, side initiative, both ballistae live, and Vex is willing to board if he thinks he's winning. Play it with the same weight as the Bex fight, injuries possible, gp on the line (the survey data itself is the prize he's after, not the crew). **He breaks off once it's clearly not going his way**, he's a scavenger, not a suicide, but "clearly not going his way" should cost the crew something to establish.
4. **Aftermath, if he loses:** a real dent in his standing at the Compass Rose, and a grudge that's earned rather than assumed. If he wins anything, even partial, play it as a genuine setback, not a wipe, this is Tier 1 and he's a recurring face, not a boss.

## Two Chairs Short · 5,600 gp total, ~2,800 gp to the crew after the split · real danger, higher tier

**Reframed: a peer asking for help, not an undercutter needing a favor.** The outfit is **Captain Marlow Aldous's *Fair Return*** (Cutter-class, **rank 331**, new canon, added to `factions/The Manifest Board.md`'s mid-board), notably *above* the crew on the Board, not below. Aldous runs a small, professional escort-and-courier outfit, four crew normally, and lost two of them to an unmarked raider mid-contract two weeks ago. The job is a Stoneward-licensed chaperone run through the Tollmarch, and **Stoneward's own escort rules require two independently crewed hulls for a contract at this value**, the same enforcement category that put the *Tollkeeper* on the Board at all (`factions/The Manifest Board.md`). Aldous can't fill his own second hull fast enough with a rank-appropriate crew, so he went looking for someone with actual nerve instead of the right number on a sheet.

**Why Aldous picked this crew, specifically, over anyone closer to his own rank.** He heard about the Bex fight secondhand, and he heard Finny's own line making the rounds at the Compass Rose, that they "jumped the Board without lifting a finger" (`Session 2.md`). He read that as exactly backwards: nobody jumps three ranks doing nothing. **A higher-ranked captain seeking this crew out on reputation, not convenience, is worth landing as a real beat**, it's a live, small proof of the campaign's standing theme that the number is not the story (`factions/The Manifest Board.md`). Estra in particular should get a moment with this, she commanded fleets that outranked everyone in the room once; being sought out by someone who still outranks her crew, on merit, is a strange and pointed thing for her to sit with quietly.

**ALDOUS'S CREW, THE FAIR RETURN.** Small and tight before the raider hit them.
- **Captain Marlow Aldous.** Human, fifties, career escort captain. Quietly proud, a little stiff about needing help, treats the arrangement as strictly professional rather than a favor, and needs a beat or two before he lets that crack. **Voice tic:** speaks in contract language even off the clock, "acceptable terms," "within scope." **Physical tic:** checks the manifest even when he already knows what's on it. **Want:** to bring his whole crew home this time.
- **Sennet Price**, first mate and gunner. All business, openly skeptical that a lower-ranked crew changes the math, the one who needs convincing rather than Aldous. Good foil if a PC wants a "prove it" beat before the fight.
- **Tamsin "Birdie" Voss**, the outfit's youngest, badly shaken by losing two crewmates she'd flown with for years. The clearest home for "someone who isn't a PC can plausibly get hurt again," give her a line early so it lands later if it needs to.

**The threat: an unmarked boarding hull, grappling jaws.** This is what already cost Aldous two crew on the first pass, and it's scheduled to be in the same lane again. **Run it off Aeravellis's spec sheet, unnamed** (`factions/House Windsong.md`, crew 15 matches his fraud-hull exactly if you ever want to confirm the pattern later): a converted Balanced Trader, **AC 15 · HP 250 · Speed 35 ft · four ballistae and grappling jaws · crew 15.** Comparable to the *Night Fury* alone, which is exactly why this needs to be a joint fight to feel fair rather than a wipe. **It wants easy cargo, not a fight**, and breaks off the moment the boarded target shows it can actually resist, precisely what Aldous's short-handed crew couldn't do the first time and this crew can.

**Branch points:**
1. **Getting the real story.** Does Aldous admit up front what happened to his crew, or does the professional distance crack under pressure once the two ships are already committed? A reluctant admission mid-transit hits harder, and it's a good place for Sennet Price to visibly disagree with how much he's telling strangers.
2. **The boarding.** The raider closes on whichever ship it reads as weaker. **This session, let that genuinely be an open question rather than an automatic pick**, the *Fair Return* is proven vulnerable (it already lost two crew to this exact hull) but the *Night Fury* is the unknown quantity a raider might also misjudge as an easy mark. Real stakes either way; Birdie is the name to reach for if the *Fair Return* takes it.
3. **Resolution options:** fight it off together (the *Night Fury*'s guns are the deciding factor, a good spotlight for whoever's gunning, and a real payoff if the Armory or Barrack bastion order below actually finished in time), draw its attention away from the *Fair Return* onto the *Night Fury* on purpose (a real tactical choice, Aerion or Estra's call), or a partial loss, cargo or coin taken, everyone alive. Don't railroad toward the clean win.
4. **Aftermath.** Whatever happens, Aldous says out loud, plainly, that he was right to come to this crew instead of someone closer to his own rank, that's the beat to land, not a party. This plants a genuine ally notably above the crew on the Board (a fresher, warmer version of the *Long Odds*/Ma Torrin dynamic sketched in `factions/The Manifest Board.md`'s mid-board section, worth deciding later whether Aldous grows into something similar). It also quietly deposits another data point toward the eventual Aeravellis reveal (M6, `campaign/Tier 1.md`), don't name him, don't explain the hull, just let the pattern exist for later.

## Astral Sea event: the transit to the rendezvous

**A real choice point for the DM, not the players**, pick one to run during the voyage out to meet the *Fair Return* and the raider's lane. Don't run more than one; each is written to stand alone and take about ten to fifteen minutes. Five options now, spanning the tone compass on purpose, weigh the pick against how session 2 landed and what the table needs before Two Chairs Short's fight.

**A. Formation flying, warm.** The *Fair Return* signals a request: mind if her crew runs a few close-formation drills alongside the *Night Fury* before they're relying on each other in a fight? **Play it as two crews sizing each other up through competence rather than conversation.** Good material: Orena and whoever's flying the *Fair Return* trading course corrections over an open channel, Tack delighted to compare notes with their engineer, Birdie visibly cheered up by having something to do with her hands. Ends on a genuine, earned "not bad" from Sennet Price. Costs nothing, plants nothing dark, pure found-family fuel before a fight.

**B. A wound in the Astral, eerie.** Reuse `rules/Tables.md`'s Astral Sea Encounters entry of the same name (table 2, d14, entry 14), or run it fresh: a stretch of the transit where the stars are simply *wrong*, spacing subtly off, one gone entirely, felt before it's seen. **Let Orena notice it first and alone, the same way the cold open worked in session 1**, then bring the rest of the bridge in once she flags it. This is a mild, unlabeled Guttering beat, resonant with the still-unshared Amberwake letters without explaining anything. Land it back in warmth fast, a joke from Finny or a steadying line from Estra, don't let it become the scene.

**C. A prior kill, dread with a name attached.** The two ships pass a drifting scrap field, unmistakably a wrecked hull, unmistakably recent, unmistakably the same kind of grappling-jaw damage the *Fair Return* already carries. **Aldous goes quiet over the comm before anyone asks him to confirm it's the same raider.** This is the session's dread beat if you want one before the real fight, played with an awe half alongside the threat half per the standing tone-compass note (`CLAUDE.md`), the wreck's own cargo manifest, still intact and legible, is a small, human, specific detail (spice, or toys, or nothing valuable at all) that makes the loss land as real rather than abstract.

**D. A kindori pod, wonder, and loaded specifically for Orena.** Reuse `rules/Tables.md`'s Wildspace Encounters entry 2, or run it as a full pod this time rather than the single distant sighting the chwinga's vision gave the table in session 1 (`Session 1.md`). Kindori are not background wonder to her, a dying one's death-song is what tore her original crew away from her; **let the sighting land as genuinely beautiful before it lands as anything else**, don't rush to make it heavy. If she wants to linger, a Nature or Arcana check can surface one true, small detail (their song carries further than sound should, or one of them is riding a current that doesn't exist on any chart), nothing that resolves the Vocalise, just a reminder it's still out there. Good pick if the table hasn't had a quiet Orena-specific beat in a while.

**E. Scavvers, danger, deliberately the lesser sighting.** Reuse `rules/Tables.md`'s Wildspace Encounters entry 6, ordinary scrap-scavvers (not the named **void scavver** from session 1's close encounter, a different, smaller threat entirely, keep that distinction clear at the table). They're circling something, a derelict, a kill, cargo venting from a punctured hull, and the interesting part is what they're circling, not the scavvers themselves. **A good place to seed one more unclaimed wreck into the Board's background texture**, or, if you want a harder pull, let what they're circling turn out to be another ship that lost the same fight the *Fair Return* barely survived. If the table's ready for the full void scavver as a deliberate second sighting instead, that's a bigger call, make it consciously per the open note in `Episode - The Quiet Hull.md`, not as a default upgrade of this option.

## Bastion action: Aerion's second facility

Live decision for this session, his pick alone (facilities are unpooled per PC, `rules/Rules & Systems.md` §6). Best run whenever the voyage has a quiet beat, mid-transit on whichever job gets picked, or back at port if the job doesn't leave room for it. **Whichever he picks, resolve the order too, not just the pick**, Aerion gets one bastion turn this leg same as every leg, and this is a natural moment to actually spend it.

- **Armory.** Roomy, Trade order, no prerequisite. Stockpiles weapons and armor; a stocked Armory equips Bastion Defenders when the ship is attacked. Fits his Bosun/Quartermaster combat-readiness angle directly, and pays off hard if the boarding action lands on the *Night Fury* rather than the *Fair Return*. **If he takes it and orders Trade this leg**, play the stocking as a real scene, maybe a haggling callback to his usual haggling instinct, and have the Armory's first real test be tonight's fight if the timing allows it. If the fight happens before the order resolves, that's a genuine, fair complication, not a punishment, just a real cost to note.
- **Barrack.** Roomy, Recruit order, no prerequisite. Houses Bastion Defenders, Recruit adds more up to the space cap. This is how the crew's muscle becomes mechanically real in a boarding action, the most literal answer to "who's actually defending the ship." **If he takes it and orders Recruit this leg**, a Barrack that isn't stocked with a garrison yet is honest, it doesn't pay off same-session, treat that as fine, this is a facility for the campaign's future boarding fights, not just tonight's.
- **Sanctuary.** Roomy, Craft order, requires a Holy Symbol or Druidic Focus. Not obviously his (Orena's Paladin focus already qualifies for it, and she took Library and Arcane Study instead), included for completeness but the weakest fit for Aerion specifically.

**Present Armory and Barrack as the live contenders**, Sanctuary's on the list but doesn't fit his character as cleanly. Don't decide it for him; let the pick say something about how he thinks about the ship's defenses now that Two Chairs Short has put a real boarding threat on the table. **A small, honest note either way:** neither facility can possibly finish equipping or garrisoning fast enough to matter in tonight's fight if the boarding happens early in the transit, that's fine and true to how Bastion orders actually work, the payoff is this crew's future, not a same-session power spike. Don't let that discourage the pick, just don't quietly fudge the timing to make it pay off early either.
