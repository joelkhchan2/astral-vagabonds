# Episode -- Two Houses, One Cake (drop-in, from the entertainer posting)

> **A light, standalone palate-cleanser.** Not story-bearing, not on any spine, no complication that needs to matter past this session. Pure sitcom rhythm: setup, escalation, a punchline that turns out to have a little warmth under it. Tone: PG, silly, competitive without ever turning mean. Good drop-in whenever the table wants a session that's all found-family comedy and zero stakes. **No rival crew this time.** The comedy engine is two insufferable old-money clients and one crew stuck serving both at once.

## The posting

*"Wanted: lively entertainer(s) for a children's joint birthday, discretion and diplomacy appreciated, pay very generous."* Reads like a slightly fussier version of the usual party gig. **Qualifier:** none printed, but "diplomacy appreciated" is doing more work than it looks like it is.

**Play the crew's own reluctance up front.** This is a step down from boarding hostile hulls and everyone at the table should be allowed to say so out loud. That reluctance is the setup the whole episode pays off.

## The clients

**House Ashby and House Talvane**, both a rung below the five founding Houses, the same social stratum as the Prynnes (`Quiet Water`, `rules/Contracts.md`), old money without quite enough of it to stop caring what the founding Houses think of them. **Their kids, Birdie Ashby and Milo Talvane, are best friends anyway**, birthdays four days apart, and this year the two of them insisted on one joint party rather than two separate ones. The houses have loathed each other for two generations over a failed marriage alliance neither side will describe the same way twice, and neither trusted the other to host alone. So they're co-hosting, technically, which in practice means each quietly issued the crew a separate, contradictory set of instructions and neither told the other they'd done it.

**Corvin Ashby**, head of house, arrives with two liveried footmen for an eight-year-old's birthday party and a majordomo who announces every guest by full name and lineage. **Voice tic:** clipped, transactional, treats every request as a matter of correct protocol, "the Ashby seal is presented before the cake is cut, that is simply how it is done." **Physical tic:** visibly recalculates the moment anything might read as deference to the Talvanes, a flicker of real panic under the composure. **Want:** to never once be seen losing, especially not to Mireille, especially not in front of the children.

**Mireille Talvane**, head of house, is relentlessly gracious in public and sharpens the instant nobody important is listening. **Voice tic:** compliments that land as insults on a two-second delay, "how wonderfully rustic, I do admire a crew that makes do." **Physical tic:** touches a piece of Talvane jewelry whenever Corvin scores a point, a tell she doesn't know she has. **Want:** the exact same thing Corvin wants, stated with better manners.

**Neither is a villain.** This is two proud, image-obsessed people who love their kids and cannot stand each other, dressed in real old-money absurdity: house crests on the place settings, a formal receiving line for eight-year-olds, competing toasts, and gifts that escalate from "generous" to "deeply inappropriate for a child" by the second hour (a miniature non-combat exotic pet, a ceremonial letter opener sized for a grown man, an offhand mention of a trust fund).

## The shape of the scene

1. **Two sets of orders.** Before the party even starts, the crew gets separately, privately briefed by each house with contradictory instructions: whose crest is the centerpiece, whose cake is cut first, which family's gift gets opened last so it lands as the finale. Aerion, from old money himself, should read this situation fluently the instant it's laid out, a good unscripted spotlight moment, his to play however he wants. The rest of the crew gets to just be baffled by how much furniture this argument requires.
2. **The impossible juggling act.** Running the actual party now means satisfying two escalating, opposed briefs in front of the same guests without either house noticing the other's brief exists. Play this as full sitcom rhythm, a small crisis every few minutes, each one solvable, each one immediately replaced by a bigger one. Give every NPC a real beat: Oz manning the receiving line with flawless, deadpan manners that somehow read as loyalty to both houses at once, Finny accidentally repeating a secret from one house's brief in front of the other, Brass bluntly refusing to serve a fourth competing cake, Tack narrating the whole event like a ship's log because that's simply who he is.
3. **The arms race.** Gifts, toasts, and favors escalate past reasonable and into genuinely absurd, each house trying to out-generous the other in front of the kids. This is where the pompousness should peak, played for pure farce, not malice.
4. **The kids don't care.** Partway through, Birdie and Milo clock exactly what's happening (their parents warring by proxy through party logistics) and simply ignore it, having a wonderful time together regardless of who cut which cake first. Their genuine joy, indifferent to all of it, is the thing that actually cracks the tension, not the crew's diplomacy. Play it as their choice, not scripted for them, they're NPCs and can be run however lands best at the table.

## Where it lands

**Not a clean truce.** Corvin and Mireille don't resolve two generations of grudge in one afternoon, and shouldn't, that's a bigger and different story than this one. What breaks, briefly, is the performance: watching their kids have a real, uncomplicated good time despite the whole apparatus built around them is the one thing neither house's pride can quite argue with. Let that land as a single quiet beat, then let both of them go right back to being insufferable on the way out the door.

**A good recurring texture, not a plot.** House Ashby and House Talvane can reappear anywhere old money shows up, a gala, a Board function, another over-produced family event, always competing, never quite at war, exactly the kind of background noise the campaign's founding-house world can use more of below the level of Windsong and Vellum.
