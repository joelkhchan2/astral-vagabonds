# Episode -- Two Houses, One Cake (drop-in, from the entertainer posting)

> **A light, standalone palate-cleanser.** Not story-bearing, not on any spine, no complication that needs to matter past this session. Pure sitcom rhythm: setup, escalation, a punchline that turns out to have a little warmth under it. Tone: PG, silly, competitive without ever turning mean. Good drop-in whenever the table wants a session that's all found-family comedy and zero stakes. **The comedy engine is the hosts**, two insufferable old-money houses, with a rival crew along for the ride as one house's own pompous choice rather than the point of the episode.

## The posting

*"Wanted: lively entertainer(s) for a children's joint birthday, discretion and diplomacy appreciated, pay very generous."* Reads like a slightly fussier version of the usual party gig. **Qualifier:** none printed, but "diplomacy appreciated" is doing more work than it looks like it is.

**Play the crew's own reluctance up front.** This is a step down from boarding hostile hulls and everyone at the table should be allowed to say so out loud. That reluctance is the setup the whole episode pays off.

## The clients

**House Ashby and House Talvane**, both a rung below the five founding Houses, the same social stratum as the Prynnes (`Quiet Water`, `rules/Contracts.md`), old money without quite enough of it to stop caring what the founding Houses think of them. **Their kids, Birdie Ashby and Milo Talvane, are best friends anyway**, birthdays four days apart, and this year the two of them insisted on one joint party rather than two separate ones. The houses have loathed each other for two generations over a failed marriage alliance neither side will describe the same way twice, and neither trusted the other to host alone. So they're co-hosting, technically, which in practice means each quietly hired their own entertainment and neither told the other.

**Corvin Ashby**, head of house, arrives with two liveried footmen for an eight-year-old's birthday party and a majordomo who announces every guest by full name and lineage. **Voice tic:** clipped, transactional, treats every request as a matter of correct protocol, "the Ashby seal is presented before the cake is cut, that is simply how it is done." **Physical tic:** visibly recalculates the moment anything might read as deference to the Talvanes, a flicker of real panic under the composure. **Want:** to never once be seen losing, especially not to Mireille, especially not in front of the children. **He hired the obvious, correct, expensive choice: the Lucky Star Revue.** Anything less would read as a concession.

**Mireille Talvane**, head of house, is relentlessly gracious in public and sharpens the instant nobody important is listening. **Voice tic:** compliments that land as insults on a two-second delay, "how wonderfully rustic, I do admire a crew that makes do." **Physical tic:** touches a piece of Talvane jewelry whenever Corvin scores a point, a tell she doesn't know she has. **Want:** the exact same thing Corvin wants, stated with better manners. **She hired the *Night Fury* crew on purpose, not out of frugality.** Choosing the unranked, unpolished option and calling it charming is its own status move, performed modesty aimed squarely at Corvin, who will never be able to tell whether she's slumming or scoring a point, which is precisely the effect she's after.

**Neither is a villain.** This is two proud, image-obsessed people who love their kids and cannot stand each other, dressed in real old-money absurdity: house crests on the place settings, a formal receiving line for eight-year-olds, competing toasts, and gifts that escalate from "generous" to "deeply inappropriate for a child" by the second hour (a miniature non-combat exotic pet, a ceremonial letter opener sized for a grown man, an offhand mention of a trust fund).

## The rival, along for the ride: Marchetta Vale and the Lucky Star Revue

Corvin's hire, a polished professional troupe: a bard running rehearsed illusion tricks, acrobats, a puppeteer, a routine tight enough to run identically at every stop on her circuit. **Voice tic:** performs warmth at adults the same way she performs it at children, projected and theatrical, "and wouldn't you all just *love* to see something wonderful." **Physical tic:** resets her cuffs and checks her reflection in anything shiny between bits, never lets a seam show. **Want:** to stay the obvious, uncontested choice on this circuit, and never again share a booking.

**She places the crew as the backup within the first minute**, assuming Mireille hired muscle in case her own act runs short. That reads as exactly the sting it should, and it's the spark that turns a reluctant, grumbling crew into one that suddenly, completely cares how this afternoon goes. She isn't the point of the episode, Corvin and Mireille are, but she's an excellent lens for Corvin's pomposity: hire the name everyone respects, and let her condescension do the work he'd rather not do personally.

## The shape of the scene

1. **Arrival, and two reveals at once.** The crew shows up to find the Lucky Star Revue already there, and it takes a beat for anyone to realize whose hire is whose: House Talvane brought the adventurers, House Ashby brought the professionals, and neither house intended for the other's choice to exist. Corvin and Mireille both pretend, instantly and badly, that this was the plan all along.
2. **Two sets of unspoken rules.** Each house has strong, unstated opinions about how their half of the party should go, whose crest gets pride of place, whose cake is cut first, which gift lands as the finale, and expects their own hired entertainment to simply know. Aerion, from old money himself, should read the whole shape of this within seconds, a good unscripted spotlight moment, entirely his to play. The rest of the crew gets to be baffled by how much furniture two families can put between themselves.
3. **The arms race, run through both crews.** Every trick the Revue lands, the *Night Fury* crew is implicitly on the hook to top, and vice versa, with Corvin and Mireille's rivalry riding shotgun on every exchange. Give every NPC a real beat: Oz consciously holding back so he doesn't win a strength game outright, Finny cheating and getting caught in the most Finny way possible, Brass patching a scraped knee with real medic hands instead of a prop bandage, Tack narrating the whole afternoon like a ship's log because that's simply who he is. Let Marchetta's rehearsed polish keep losing to something she can't fake.
4. **The kids don't care.** Partway through, Birdie and Milo clock exactly what's happening, two houses and two hired crews all warring by proxy through party logistics, and simply ignore it, having a wonderful time together regardless. Their genuine joy, indifferent to all of it, is the thing that actually cracks the adults' composure, not the crews' diplomacy. Play it as their choice, not scripted for them, they're NPCs and can be run however lands best at the table.

## Where it lands

**Not a clean truce.** Corvin and Mireille don't resolve two generations of grudge in one afternoon, and shouldn't, that's a bigger and different story than this one. What breaks, briefly, is the performance: watching their kids have a real, uncomplicated good time despite the whole apparatus built around them is the one thing neither house's pride can quite argue with. Let that land as a single quiet beat, then let both of them go right back to being insufferable on the way out the door.

**Marchetta remembers losing**, specifically, to something polish can't buy, and she's a good light recurring rival for the same lane of odd little jobs BoyBoy and Verity Gilt already occupy. **House Ashby and House Talvane are the better recurring texture**, though, always competing, never quite at war, exactly the kind of old-money background noise the campaign's founding-house world can use more of below the level of Windsong and Vellum.
