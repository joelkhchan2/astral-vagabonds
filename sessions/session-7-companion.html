Homecoming, and the Next Voyage.

A pure interlude at the Rock of Bral. No job, no battle. A quarterly all-hands that's really Estra teaching the crew something she's never explained, a refreshed Charter Window board, two rumor tracks, a 29-rank Manifest Board climb, a reunion that's really a rescue, a family of nine hundred-some tortles at war over a shell nobody will define, and the session that finally launches the Amberwake arc.

Everyone gets a goal tonight. Only one of them is telling the truth about hers.

The one rule that outranks this page

Never decide for the players. Orena and Aerion are Gabrielle's and Priscilla's. This page gives situations, options, and spotlight opportunities, never what they buy, say, feel, or choose. Violet boxes flag exactly where the next move belongs to a player.

How to use this page

Plum blocks are GM-only, never read aloud or shown to players. Blue Read aloud boxes are narration to say or paraphrase. Full prep and reasoning behind every call: sessions/Session 7 Prep.md, npcs/Sedge Okonkwo.md (the Dessa/Sedge/Estra arc in full), and npcs/Captain Estra Vane.md (her Long Game, now moving). Narration follows rules/DMing Craft.md.

Ship Goals: the Quarterly All-Hands

A workplace-comedy beat, right after the homecoming lands, before anyone's finished breakfast. Estra institutes this, a quiet echo of something she used to run back when she captained an actual fleet, though nobody aboard knows that's where it's from. She doesn't explain herself.

Read aloud

Estra's got the whole crew crowded into the galley before anyone's finished breakfast, Aerion already seated beside her with the ledger open to a page marked in his own hand. She doesn't raise her voice. She never has to. "Going forward, we're doing this properly. Once a quarter. Goals." Finny's visibly thrilled. Nobody else quite is.

Aerion's role, not his lines

He sits at Estra's right hand with the ledger open, bosun and quartermaster, visibly running the numbers side of the room. Nothing he says or does here is scripted, his own call entirely, same as whatever he answers when it's handed to the table below.

Company goals, read flat and dry by Estra

Each one has an ordinary surface meaning and a real one she never says aloud. The table only hears the surface.

"Bigger contracts. Real pay, not garbage work."

Surface: overdue common sense.
Underneath: gold enough to fund something bigger than one ship, the whole reason she's tolerated the bottom of the Board for fifteen years.

"Every route gets charted and filed. No exceptions."

Surface: professionalism.
Underneath: she's still quietly building the dataset her own instincts never had the science to back up, waiting for this crew's data to finally prove what fifteen years alone couldn't.

"Any contract touching House Vellum comes to me first."

Surface: ordinary vetting.
Underneath: an old enemy who buried a fleet once already, and she doesn't intend to be caught flat-footed by them a second time.

"Keep climbing the Board."

Surface: plain pride.
Underneath: rank is leverage, and leverage is the only currency that gets a fleet back.

Individual goals, one round, in voice

Dry and businesslike, no elaboration: "Line up financing for a second hull this quarter." Reads as a routine operational line to the room. What's actually behind it lands later this session, §7.

Then it's handed to the table, genuinely open

Aerion and Orena each get asked for theirs. Nothing scripted for either, this is theirs to answer or dodge.

The Manifest Board, Updated

Climbing to 311th, up from 340th, a 29-rank jump, the biggest single climb the crew's had.

Not a contract-value bump like session 3's five ranks. Real survey data for a founding house and Longscar driven off or fought outright are part of it, but the real weight is reputation by association, being seen in company with both Vaelren Windsong (a genuine top-20 captain) and Aurel Delverane herself completing a serious personal commission for her. The Board reads that kind of visible vouching as harder evidence than the job itself.

Who they pass on the way

Two for the Road (339), First Class (338), Green Hollow (336), then a long unremarkable stretch through the 320s, with two named crews worth a specific beat for climbing on their own merits in the same window: the Riptide Chorus (326, kindori riders, a strong season of her own, right after the Windward Edge's own kindori content), and Due Process (318, by-the-book efficiency finally paying off, a narrower, prouder pass than most). They land at 311th, just below Marlow Albus's Fair Return at 310th, who climbed one rank the same cycle and stays just ahead, close enough now to actually be neighbours.

The reception, mixed on purpose

Real cheers at the Compass Rose, Finny leading it, nobody holding back. A formal, visible acknowledgment at the Charter Window itself when the new number goes up. But also a few open glances and at least one outright glare from faces further up the board, a climb this size at this speed reads as a threat to somebody, not just a milestone. Don't name who yet unless the table wants a specific face attached; the discomfort is the point.

07

Dessa and Sedge, and Estra's Real Move

Sedge Okonkwo (the Bright Arrears, rank ~250) is in port. Full backstory on both of them, and on why this is bigger than a love-letter thread: npcs/Sedge Okonkwo.md, not duplicated here.

Last seen, session 4: Orena pushed Sedge to go find Dessa Kell (the Compact Standard, rank 9) and actually say in person what he couldn't explain, why he can't just stop flying when she asked him to.

Open, GM's call live

Whether Dessa is physically in port too, for a real, witnessed reunion scene, or whether this is Sedge reporting back on what happened since, changed, unchanged, or still unresolved. Either works. If the crew asks, Sedge's own guilt and gratitude toward Orena specifically is worth a real, earned beat regardless.

Why Dessa really asked him to stop

Not disapproval from a distance. Recognition, up close, of someone she still loves slowly turning into the exact thing that broke them both the first time. Years ago, Sedge took House Vellum's buyout believing it was the smart move, and genuinely tried to talk Dessa into taking the same deal; she refused and rebuilt the Compact Standard clean on her own instead. Since then, the Bright Arrears has been assigned, job by job, to routes that squeeze other small independents the same way the old Compact got squeezed. He didn't see it coming when he signed. He can see it now.

Alongside it, not instead of it: Estra makes her real move

This is her individual quarter goal from §3 landing in the flesh. She's quietly lined up 100,000 gp (her own 20,000 gp, saved for exactly this, plus an 80,000 gp loan from House Nightbreeze) to buy Sedge free of Vellum's exclusivity clause on the Bright Arrears, and bring both him and the ship into her own fleet as its second hull.

What she actually pitches himNot a job offer. What she's quietly been building for fifteen years: a nomadic, chart-and-share network that makes Vellum's whole toll economy irrelevant. Not "come fly for me." "Help prove the system that trapped you doesn't have to be the only way to fly."
Why it's 100,000 gp, not less

The Bright Arrears herself isn't the expensive part, a used ship usually costs less than a new build. The weight is Vellum's exit fee, deliberately punitive, the calculated cost of a captain walking. House Nightbreeze funds the 80,000 gp loan not out of charity, trivial money against their 77,000,000 gp book, and prying one of Vellum's own captains loose is exactly the kind of quiet leverage they're already playing for (factions/House Nightbreeze.md). The crew has no visibility into any of this and doesn't need any.

Not resolved here, genuinely live

Whether Sedge takes it, how Dessa reacts to learning about it, and whether the Bright Arrears actually changes hands this session or runs into session 8. If it lands even partway, this is the first concrete step toward Estra rebuilding the Compact with an actual former captain, not just a locked chest and a private dream.

Sable's Spotlight: the Half-Shell Wars

A real individual scene, triggered by coincidence, not a search. One of Sable's many siblings happens to be docked at the Rock.

Raph, leader of one of four warring factions among Sable's 927 siblings, all fighting, generations deep, over the Half-Shell, a legendary ancestral shell. What it actually does, or means, is deliberately never agreed on, each faction has its own inherited legend about it, which is exactly why the war's never ended. Played with total deadpan seriousness by everyone involved, which is the joke.

Beats to hit, not scripted dialogue

  • Raph genuinely doesn't expect to run into Sable here, warm and strange in equal measure.
  • He talks about the war the way someone discusses a decades-long legal dispute, exhausted, committed, utterly serious.
  • Sable's own reaction is hers to play live, embarrassment, pride, complicated homesickness, or some mix, not scripted.
  • Don't resolve the war or explain the Half-Shell's actual nature. This is color and family texture, not a quest hook, unless the table wants to pick it up later.

Vaelren: the Box Comes Back, and a Little Carrow Lore

A real scene, not a handoff narrated in a line. The crew retrieves the Vellum/Carrow correspondence box from Vaelren at the Compass Rose or his own room.

  • He hands it back without ceremony, easy and unbothered, per his standing voice. Worth one open beat: whether he looked inside it himself while he had it, his call to reveal or not, not decided here.
  • A little Carrow lore, in his own voice, not a lecture. He's old enough and well-traveled enough to have crossed paths with the Amberwake corridor or House Carrow's people once, long before its fall. Keep it personal and small: a story, a name he remembers, a feeling about the place, not exposition about why the corridor failed. Table color that seeds dread ahead of §10's offer.

The Pivot: Aurel's Offer, the Amberwake

Branch on how session 6 actually went.

If "Wyndham" was never unmasked

The offer still comes through the usual channel, Corwin Dunmore at the Farthing Station, or a direct written contract, Aurel's own name on it, the guise with the crew intact. She doesn't need to appear in person for this to land as a real escalation of trust.

If the disguise broke during session 6

Aurel asks them herself, in person, no more need to keep two faces apart in front of this specific crew. A real, heavier scene, worth landing with weight if this is the branch that fires.

The offer, either way: not just charting the symptom anymore, Aurel wants the crew to actually investigate the Stilling itself, specifically the delisted, Carrow-adjacent corridor and the dead stretch beyond it known as the Amberwake. This is the banked escalation hook finally cashing in, and the session that opens the Amberwake arc. Scope and pay are worth settling live rather than fixed here, bigger than Open Water, Open Season, a real expedition rather than a survey run.

What this launches, GM-only, not stated tonight

The kindori cull, the whale fall, the Carrow waystation, and Aeravellis's own thread all sit downstream of this contract. Nothing here should gesture at any of it yet.
