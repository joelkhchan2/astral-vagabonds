Astral Vagabonds · Session Zero Companion

Fitting out at the far end of a bad decade.

Five years ago the crew signed onto a venture that looked like a sure thing. Today they are near destitute, standing in a prior-service ship yard with the only real money they have seen in a long time and one night to spend it well. This is the reference for that night: how astral ships work, what every chair on the deck does, the hulls for sale, and the people asking for berths.

Danforth Certified Prior-Service Hulls. "The Last Honest Deal in Wildspace."

Superseded — session 0 has been played

This was the prep companion for running session 0. The game happened, and the outcomes are canon now: the crew bought a Sturdy Trader (left unnamed) and hired a lean crew of seven. For what actually got decided, see the Session Zero recap and the current crew, ship, and character docs. Treat everything below as the menu the table drew from, not as pending choices.

01

Where the crew is tonight

Set this up in a few minutes of table talk, then get to the yard. The point of the evening is a hull, a crew sorted into the right chairs, a name, and a fresh problem to fly toward.

The money on the table

Vaelros Windsong has handed his youngest son 50,000 gp and a posting at the frontier. It is the smallest share any of the four brothers received and the worst assignment by every measure the family respects. It is also the first serious capital this crew has touched in years.

Say this plainly

The 50,000 is venture capital, not personal wealth. Their own money is small and separate. This is the ship's money, and every silver of it is spoken for the moment they walk in.

The two facts that shape every choice
  • Piloting requires an attuned spellcaster holding concentration. Whoever flies cannot do much else while they fly.
  • A hired helmsman costs roughly 18,000 gp a year. It is the single largest recurring cost in the venture, and it decides whether their runway is measured in years or in months.
GM note

Shaded plum blocks like this one are for your eyes. Do not read them aloud. Everything else on the page is safe to show at the table.

The established crew

This is not a room full of strangers. Aerion has crewed with these people for five years, including the treasure hunt two years ago where he found the egg that became Flurry. Orena (Gabrielle's character) is the newcomer; she joined about six months ago. With a real ship at last, tonight is partly about confirming who stays in which chair and partly about the two or three berths still open. Anyone they turn down stays in the world and can turn up again later, on this deck or a rival's.

02

How astral ships work

Enough to fly one and enough to fear the bill. Six ideas do almost all the work: the medium, the roads, the air, the gravity, the helm, and the cost of everything.

Wildspace and the Astral Sea

Systems hang in the void, separated by enormous distances, all of it floating in a silvery medium called the Astral. In open space a ship cruises at 100 million miles per 24 hours, which turns a crossing between worlds into a voyage of days and weeks rather than a lifetime.

The roads

The Astral holds memory. Travel a path often enough and the passage wears a groove that other ships can follow. Those grooves are the roads, and the maps to them are property. The Pale Reach, a consortium of merchant houses, owns the good ones. Aerion's family sits inside it. That is the whole political weather in one sentence.

Air is a calendar

A ship carries a bubble of breathable air. At standard complement it lasts 120 days, and a larger crew burns it faster. On day 121 the air turns foul and everyone aboard is poisoned until they breathe fresh air again. Foul air becomes deadly 120 days after that. When two air bubbles touch, the smaller one takes on the quality of the larger.

How we track it

Not as a running total. It is a date on the voyage calendar. Either they beat the clock or they do not.

Gravity you can walk under

Each ship generates a gravity plane that cuts the hull in half lengthwise and reaches as far as the air does. You can stand on the underside of your own deck. When two ships make contact, the hull with more hit points remaining forces its plane onto the other.

Why this matters in a boarding fight

Board a healthier ship without a Plane Anchor and your own deck can flip under your boarders mid action. It is the reason a 2,600 gp fitting reads as boring and plays as decisive.

The helm

A spelljamming helm is a chair that only works for an attuned spellcaster. Sitting in it and steering the ship costs the pilot neither an action nor movement, so their turn stays free, but flying takes concentration exactly like holding a spell. A pilot cannot fly and maintain a concentration spell at the same time, and taking damage can knock them off the helm. Running a helm costs roughly 50 gp per day.

One person can fly a ship

The crew number on a stat block is the ideal complement with every weapon manned, not a minimum. A skeleton crew flies fine; it just cannot shoot much.

Power does not equal speed

The ship moves at its hull's listed speed no matter who is in the chair. A level 5 bard flies exactly as fast as an archmage. Nobody is buying a faster helm.

The concentration tax

Every voyage a player character flies, they are furniture. Every fight they join instead, the ship is not moving. This is the campaign's one genuinely hard recurring choice.

The cost of everything

Combat in this campaign is frightening because it is expensive, not because it is lethal. Two numbers carry that.

LineRateWhat it feels like
Hull repair20 gp / hit pointA 250 HP hull taking 100 points is a 2,000 gp bill, most of a month's income.
Weapon repair10 gp / hit pointCheaper than the hull, still real money.
Helm, running~50 gp / dayThe chair costs whether or not it moves.
Damage threshold15 to 20Any single hit below the threshold does nothing at all.

Ship combat runs on side initiative. Weapons take several actions to load, aim, and fire, and a hull can rotate to bring any weapon to bear regardless of where the target sits.

03

Roles and combat stations

Two different maps of the same crew. The first is the standing job each person holds day to day. The second is the station they take when the shooting starts, which anyone can crew and swap between fights.

The twelve chairs

A full complement is twelve: the two player characters and ten crew. The jobs below are what keeps a merchant ship alive between fights.

Captain and primary helm
Sets the voyage, holds the leash, and usually flies the ship. The one person aboard who has done all of this before.
Navigator Aerion
Plots courses, keeps the charts, tracks supplies and money. The de facto quartermaster, accountant, and fixer. Knows what is in every crate and who owes what.
Bosun Orena
Runs rigging, repairs, and crew discipline, assists with piloting and navigation, and keeps the ship's operations moving. Also the crew's field naturalist, filling a bestiary of astral creatures.
Chief engineer
Keeps the hull and the helm running, patches breaches, and is first to know when something mechanical is wrong.
Chief gunner
Runs the weapon crews and the gun bay. Ship weapons need several hands and several actions to fire, so this is a team, not a trigger.
Cook and deckhand
Sounds trivial, is not. Food is morale, and morale is what carries a crew through the 120-day provisions clock. A hot meal on day ninety is worth real hit points.
Surgeon
Stabilizes the badly hurt and keeps the injury logs. The difference between a scare and a funeral.
Lookout and scout
Eyes in the void. Sees the thing coming while there is still time to choose what to do about it.
Quartermaster's mate and cargo
Works the hold, the manifests, and the contacts at every port. The person who makes the navigator's plans real at ground level.
Security and heavy weapons
Boarding defense. The reason nobody takes this deck while they are standing on it.
Cabin crew, runner, helm backup
Carries messages, learns every job, and is quietly the ship's future. The one who grows into the helm chair somewhere down the line.
Second helm and rigger
The relief pilot and the hands aloft. Does twice the work and asks for nothing.

Combat stations

When a fight starts, every player character and any named crew member takes a station for the encounter. Stations are not classes. Anyone can crew any of them, and moving from the gun deck to the helm costs movement, not an action.

Pilot

Whoever is at the helm. Because flying is free, the pilot's own action is open every round, and it can be spent on one maneuver from the list below. Only one pilot maneuver per round, chosen when the ship's initiative comes up.

ManeuverEffectCost
Evasive ActionAttacks against the ship have disadvantage until the pilot's next turn.The ship's own weapons have disadvantage this round. Nobody can aim while she juks.
Full BurnThe ship's speed effectively doubles this round to close, disengage, or run.Attacks against the ship have advantage until the pilot's next turn. Hard to hide going that fast.
RamIf the hull has a ram, make one attack with the ram's damage, no weapon crew needed.The ship takes half the damage it deals, applied after its damage threshold.
Boarding ActionClose and lock with a ship your size or smaller (contested piloting check).Both ships are grappled. Neither can Full Burn or disengage until someone breaks it.

Gunner

Each weapon has a crew size and an action count to fire. A ballista needs three hands and three actions; a mangonel needs four or five. A gunner contributes their action to that sequence. More gunners means more guns firing per round.

Engineer

Not full repair, that is downtime and gold. In a fight the engineer can spend an action on an Emergency Patch: a DC 12 check that restores hit points equal to their level, once per encounter. It keeps the hull standing, it does not lower the repair bill.

Coordinator

Usually whichever character is not flying or gunning, running the fight from the deck. Spend an action to grant an ally advantage, impose disadvantage on an attacker, or let one station act again out of order. One coordinator benefit per round.

The natural spread for this table

Two characters cannot fill four stations. Expect Orena on a gun or coordinating and Aerion coordinating or engineering, with hired and default crew slotted into the rest. The shakedown fight is the first time to run this live.

04

The ship shop

A cluttered orbital yard: hulls in cradles at wrong angles, gantries, a scrap heap that is technically inventory, and a portable office that used to be a cargo container. The yard hands working on the ships are also the crew candidates, so nobody has to go anywhere else tonight.

The proprietor

"Major" Hoyle Danforth

A giff, seven feet tall, retired at a rank he awarded himself and uses constantly, in a blazer that fits nobody. He calls every hull "she," every defect "character," and every customer "Captain," including the ones who are not. He applies artillery vocabulary to situations that have nothing to do with artillery.

The thing that makes him work

He never lies. He sincerely believes every ship on the lot is excellent, which makes him impossible to catch out and far harder to argue with than a con man. Ask him a direct question and he answers honestly, including the answers that cost him the sale. Nobody asks. Name and species are entirely swappable.

The name

The yard's slogan is on the sign all evening. If they take it, the ship is the Last Honest Deal, and it is not ironic, it is a thesis. If they do not, she is theirs either way.

05

The interviews

The yard hands stop pretending to work and start asking about berths. Run three or four of these as real scenes and fill the rest offscreen. For an established crew, most of these are confirming a veteran back into their chair; the alternates are who they reach for when a chair is open or an old hand has finally had enough of flying broke.

How the alternates work

The default names below in accent type are the crew as written. The others are genuine options, none of them bad, all good in different directions, which is the only way the choice means anything. Whoever they turn down stays in the world.

Chief engineer

Whatever they buy is broken. There is already someone under one of these hulls with her legs sticking out.

Default3 gp / day

Tack

Autognome, cheerful, meticulous, no concept of personal space. Calls hull breaches "owies." Wildly overqualified; he was built for a vessel four times this size, so repairs happen suspiciously fast.

Philosophy: fixes what is wrong, and everything adjacent to it, unasked.

"Oh! Oh, is this my ship now? I'll need to learn all her names."
Ask him

What he was actually built for. He answers honestly and it is a much bigger ship than this. His creator is a Pale Reach shipwright.

alternate6 gp / day

Hessa Ombrey

Human, forties, ex-Tethyn yard foreman, immaculate coveralls, speaks in estimates. Genuinely excellent and knows exactly what she is worth. Double the going rate, non-negotiable.

Philosophy: fixes what is asked, precisely, and not one bolt more.

"I can have her sound in nine days. I can have her good in twenty. You'll want the nine."
Ask her

Why she left the Tethyn yards. Professional answer first, real one only if you ask twice. She would file a routine fit-out report with her old contacts out of habit. Not spying. Paperwork, which is worse.

Human, mid-fifties, supercargo and negotiator. Thirty years of arriving somewhere hostile and leaving with the cargo intact and nobody hurt. Better docking fees, better contract terms, better outcomes at inspections.

Complication, and a hook

If it comes to a fight he is useless and says so up front. He is also old enough to have crewed the treasure hunt two years back; if the players want a familiar face, he can simply be one of the crew from then. Offer it, do not assign it.

The tempting choice

Hiring Corwin instead of a gunner is real and defensible, and it is the one Aerion will be drawn to. It should be viable. It should also mean the first boarding action goes very badly.

Filled offscreen

Whoever they do not audition, the surgeon, lookout, quartermaster's mate, and rigger, comes aboard between sessions. The defaults are Dr. Quill, Reeve, Brass, and Morrow. Wysp is already aboard whichever hull they bought and is not, technically, hired.
