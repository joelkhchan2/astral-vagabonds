# Episode -- Sparky the Behir (drop-in, from the "lost pet" flyer)

> **Fires whenever someone answers the lost-pet flyer** (`rules/Contracts.md`, Special). Reads as a fetch-the-runaway-pet job, a giant's idea of a lost dog. Mostly is. **Sparky herself is never a combat target and is never harmed on-screen**, this crew's whole job is getting her home safe, not fighting a Huge monster. The real danger, and the real fight, comes from who else got to her first. Recurring antagonist seed: **the Trapline**, playable again across future exotic-animal jobs.

## The flyer

Giant handwriting, comically oversized on a normal sheet, corner folded down to fit the board: *"LOST: SPARKY, VERY GOOD GIRL, ANSWERS TO WHISTLE (TWO NOTES, HIGH THEN LOW). LAST SEEN NEAR THE BERTH. PLEASE DO NOT APPROACH IF SHE SEEMS FRIGHTENED, SHE ONLY SPARKS WHEN SCARED. REWARD: 500 GP."* Five hundred gold reads as a fortune to this crew and pocket change to whoever wrote it, which is the tell before anyone's even met the client.

**The client:** the crew of a storm giant trading vessel, in port for resupply, mortified and frantic in the specific way of people whose family pet got loose in a strange city. Play them exactly like worried dog owners, scaled up: too big for the room, too loud without meaning to be, and completely undone by this.

## Sparky

A young behir, huge, blue-scaled, six clawed legs, and to her family a lapdog. Comes running to a two-note whistle. Sits by coiling nose to tail. Shakes with one foreleg, delighted with herself every time. Sparks harmlessly along her scales when she's happy, the real lightning breath is reserved entirely for when she's genuinely terrified, and if the scene is run right, nobody should need to see it.

**Why she ran.** The crew was drilling their storm-sails, calling up a real squall to test the rigging. The first thunderclap came from her own family instead of the sky, and she bolted off her tether before anyone could stop her.

**Why she didn't come home.** Young and easily distracted, she chased something small and fast through the scrub past the berth and kept going long after she'd lost whatever she was chasing. By the time she stopped, she didn't know the way back, and she'd wandered into worse trouble than being lost.

## What's actually out there

**An old wild behir got to the territory first.** Not a villain, simply established, and Sparky wandered straight into ground he already holds. He's cornered her against a rock wall in a display of pure territorial aggression, and she's too young and too frightened to get past him on her own. Run him as a looming, scripted hazard rather than a straight fight, a single terrifying lunge if the party approaches carelessly is plenty. He isn't here to be killed, he's here to be gotten past, distracted, or redirected, and a clever crew that turns his aggression onto the second problem instead of fighting him head-on should be rewarded for the idea.

**The Trapline found her first, too.** A small, professional exotic-beast poaching outfit has been working this stretch for months, and a storm giant-bonded young behir, already flushed into the open by the wild rival, is the best catch they've had all year. Her scales alone are worth a fortune in weaponcraft, and a live specimen with a giant-house bond is worth more. They were closing in when the party arrives, and they will fight to keep the catch.

**Ressa Kade**, their leader, talks about every living thing she's ever caught in appraisal terms, "good coat, clean scales, no visible scarring, that's a shame to lose," never once raising her voice. **Physical tic:** measures things with her hands out of pure habit, a wrist, a doorway, a person's shoulders, sizing without meaning to. **Want:** the reputation of being the best in a small, ugly trade, more than the money itself. **Run her as a Bandit Captain (CR 2)**, reflavored with a net launcher in place of the standard loadout, restrain on a hit instead of bonus damage.

**Two trappers back her up**, silent and efficient, reflavored **Thugs (CR 1/2 each)** carrying snare lines that work the same way, restrain rather than extra damage. They fight to protect the catch, not to the death, and will disengage the moment it's clearly not happening for them today, the same professional calculation BoyBoy makes.

## The shape of the scene

1. **The trail.** Tracking Sparky is a real scene on its own: scorch marks too small to be the storm drill, claw gouges heading away from the berth, a witness who saw "something enormous, moving fast, scared." Reward good tracking with getting there before the Trapline finishes closing in, not with skipping the encounter.
2. **The three-body standoff.** The party arrives to find Sparky cornered by the wild rival, with the Trapline moving in from the other side, about to net a catch they think nobody's watching them take. Neither threat has clocked the party yet. This moment is the whole scene's fulcrum, give it real read-aloud weight and let the party plan before anything goes loud.
3. **The fork.** Fight the Trapline outright and let the wild rival's aggression become a live variable, either side's problem depending on how the fight moves. Redirect the wild rival onto the Trapline on purpose and let two threats fight each other while the party gets Sparky clear. Or try to talk the Trapline off, though Ressa Kade doesn't scare and doesn't bluff, this one is more likely to end in a fight than BoyBoy's does, and that's fine, it's built to be winnable.
4. **Getting Sparky moving.** She doesn't fight, doesn't flee well on her own while this frightened, and someone has to actually calm her down, a real Animal Handling, Persuasion, or Performance moment, not a formality. This is a good spotlight beat for whoever wants it, and it's worth letting the table find their own way in rather than scripting who does it.

## Aftermath

**The storm giants are exactly as relieved and mortified as advertised.** The reward lands as promised, and Sparky's family fusses over her the way anyone would over a very good, very silly dog who scared them badly. Play the reunion warm and a little funny, the tension of the fight resolving into pure relief.

**The Trapline remembers.** Ressa Kade doesn't hold a grudge the way BoyBoy might, she holds an assessment: this crew cost her a valuable catch and fought competently doing it, worth noting for next time. A good recurring antagonist for any future exotic-animal job on the board (Best in Show, Livestock, the Dog Show's own menagerie), always professional, never personal, and always circling the same trade the board keeps generating work in.

**The wild rival stays exactly what he was**, territory defended, nothing resolved and nothing owed. If the crew ever passes this way again, he's still out there, still holding the same ground, a piece of scenery worth remembering rather than a thread that needs pulling.
