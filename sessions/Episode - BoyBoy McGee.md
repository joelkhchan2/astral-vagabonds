# Episode -- BoyBoy McGee (drop-in, from the joke job board posting)

> **Fires whenever someone takes the 20 gp cookie delivery** (`rules/Contracts.md`, Special). Reads as a throwaway gag on the board. Isn't one. This is the campaign's version of session 1's lesson, don't over-engineer a "safe" encounter assuming it stays safe, write the hot version so it's easy to reach for live. Tone stays PG and mostly funny even at its hardest point; the danger is real, the horror is not.

## The twist

**BoyBoy McGee is a real person, and the name is not a joke to him, it's a weapon.** He's spent his whole life being underestimated because of it, so he built a whole method around that: post something too small and stupid to take seriously, see who shows up alone, and find out in five minutes what actually matters about a person, whether they're careful, whether they're capable, whether they're worth knowing. Most of the crews who answer this posting are exactly what the name promises: nothing. This time it isn't.

**He's not a street thug and this isn't a mugging.** He runs a small, genuinely competent outfit out of Lowside, information brokering, debt collection, the kind of quiet leverage business that thrives at the bottom of the Board where nobody official looks. He is dangerous, precise, and has never once needed to raise his voice to prove it.

## BoyBoy McGee

**Voice tic:** unnervingly, courtly polite, the register of a man who has never once needed to threaten anyone loudly. **Physical tic:** he finishes whatever food is in front of him before discussing business, always, no exceptions, and today that's the cookies. **Want:** to be read correctly, just once, by someone who isn't fooled by the name before they've met the man. That's the whole engine of the scheme, and it's also the door out of it.

**Run him as a Veteran (CR 3)**, reflavored: rapier and a hand crossbow, calm, economical, genuinely capable of hurting someone who mistakes him for a punchline. **Two to three Thugs (CR 1/2 each)** round out his crew, real fighters, not comic relief, positioned to block the alley's one exit before anyone notices they've done it.

## The shape of the scene

1. **The handoff.** A dead-end alley off Lowside, exactly as advertised. BoyBoy takes the tin, sits, and starts eating a cookie immediately, unhurried, while he looks the responder over. Small talk. He's genuinely curious who showed up.
2. **The tell.** Somewhere in the conversation, the responder either reads the room (the alley has exactly one way out and it's no longer as clear as it was when they walked in) or doesn't. This is a real Perception or Insight moment, not a forced reveal, let them notice on their own merits.
3. **The fork.** How the responder handles being sized up decides everything:
   - **Dismissive, scared, or trying to bluff past him** reads as exactly the nothing he expected. This is where it goes hostile, fast, and the fight is real: a Veteran and two Thugs against however many showed up, in a bad tactical spot, and it should feel genuinely dangerous for the first several rounds.
   - **Calm, honest, or willing to actually talk to him as a person** gets a different reaction entirely. He respects being read correctly more than he wants a fight, and a responder who treats him like a man instead of a joke has a real shot at walking out with nothing worse than a strange story, possibly a contact worth having later.
4. **The out, if it goes hot.** BoyBoy is a professional, not a maniac. If the fight turns clearly against his crew, or if reinforcements arrive (the rest of the crew, noticing someone's late), he calls it, pays a token "toll" for the trouble, and disengages rather than dying on this hill. This isn't a fight to the death on either side; it's a test that got expensive.

## Why this works as "surprisingly tough"

The whole joke of the posting is that nobody brings backup to a 20 gp cookie run. That's also exactly why it's dangerous: whoever answers it is likely to be alone, in a dead end, against a real threat they walked into expecting a punchline. Let the mismatch between the posting's absurdity and the fight's real stakes be the horror-comedy engine, the same one session 1 already proved this table responds to. Don't soften BoyBoy's crew to make the joke land easier. The joke *is* that it doesn't.

## Aftermath, either way

**If it went hostile and the crew won:** BoyBoy remembers being beaten, specifically, by name. That's either a live grudge or, if the fight was clean and the crew showed him mercy on the way out, the start of the contact relationship instead. Worth deciding on purpose rather than letting it default.

**If it stayed civil:** BoyBoy McGee becomes a new bottom-of-the-Board face, Brass-adjacent, an information broker who owes the crew nothing but remembers being read correctly for once. A good, cheap way to seed a future favor or a piece of gossip nobody else has.

**Either way, the posting keeps running.** He'll use the same trick on the next hungry crew that walks past the Charter Window. That's the business.
