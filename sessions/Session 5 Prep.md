## 2. Cold open: the derelict

**The hull has a name now: the *Quiet Verge*,** a small independent trader that ran supply between Verge colonies, the same shape of route Hallow's Rest depends on. Registry scraped clean, no distress call, no bodies, no violence, just a ship that stopped being lived in mid-motion, was deliberately abandoned in good order, and drifted for months before fetching up in a lowside berth at Bral.

**Read aloud, boarding her:**
> The airlock cycles on backup power, barely. Inside, the air still holds, but it's cold in a way that has nothing to do with temperature, the specific stillness of somewhere that used to have people breathing in it. Emergency lighting only, a dim amber strip along the deck. Somewhere forward, something ticks on an even interval, a system nobody's told to stop running. The deck under your boots is gritty in a way metal shouldn't be. What do you do?

**What actually happened, GM only.** The *Quiet Verge* was running her usual supply hop when the crossing started taking longer than the charts said it should, trip after trip, the helmsman's own log tracking it getting worse. She noted the Tide itself looked thinner along that stretch too: fewer bright threads in the water, more dark patches between them. Flow was dying before glow fully went out, the warning was there to read, if anyone had known what it meant. Nobody did yet. Then, mid-transit, the push stopped. Not blackness, not lost, the destination star sat exactly where the charts said, visible the whole time. The ship simply couldn't close the distance anymore: **becalmed**, momentum only, air and stores burning down on a fixed clock while home stayed in plain sight and unreachable. The captain's math was cold but sound: the hull's own stores wouldn't outlast the drift, but the two lifeboats had their own small engines and burned a fraction of the air and food per body. Jettison cargo to lighten what's left, split the crew into the boats, run for the last waypoint the charts still confirmed while the boats' fuel held out. Before they left, the helmsman set the empty hull's own dead-reckoning heading for the Rock of Bral, since an unsteered ship still needed to go somewhere useful if anyone ever found her. That's the months of drift accounted for, not luck, a last deliberate act by someone doing her job to the end. **Did the crew themselves make it? Genuinely unresolved.** A real open hook, answer it only if the table wants to chase it.

**The crew, named for the documents below.** Captain **Wren Ashby**, Helmsman **Ilsa Marrow**, Engineer **Pell Ostrander**, Cook **Dorram Keel**, Deckhand **Fen Caskey**, and one more unnamed hand. Six aboard, six accounted for between the two lifeboats.

**Who's aboard now.** Sometime during that long, unpowered drift, three things found their way into the open hull. **Two grell** (Medium aberration, CR 3 each, Monster Manual: floating brains with a beaked maw and a paralytic tentacle crown, blind, hunting entirely by sound) denned in the crew quarters, nesting in the torn bedding. Deeper in, something far bigger forced its way into the cargo hold through the bay doors and has been living there since: **a megapede** (Gargantuan monstrosity, CR 7, Boo's Astral Menagerie: a hull-length centipede that clings to derelicts and ambushes anything that lands nearby), the real boss of this hull, explaining why the hold reads shoved-through rather than ransacked. A small, harmless **flumph** fled the megapede early and has been hiding in the engineering vents ever since, the one creature here actually glad to see visitors. Full stat blocks for all three in the session companion's Reference section.

**The rooms.** A true point-crawl, six spaces plus the entry. Let the crew choose their own order after the airlock. Every room carries both a "what happened here" clue and, scattered through, a "something's living here now" clue, so none of the three feel sprung out of nowhere.

- **Airlock / entry. DC 10 Perception.** Read aloud: *The outer hatch is already cycling when you reach for it, sluggish, protesting on backup power. Inside, your breath doesn't fog the way it should. Somewhere forward, something ticks on an even interval, patient, mechanical, a system nobody told to stop. The deck under your boots is gritty in a way metal shouldn't be.* On a success: the grit is old organic residue, the trail splits forward, a heavier smear toward the cargo hold, a lighter one toward crew quarters. Two directions, two different things, though nothing says which is which yet.
- **Galley. DC 12 Perception.** Read aloud: *Pans still hang in their rack, swinging faint and slow from a draft that shouldn't exist in dead air. A pot's gone cold at the bottom, whatever was cooking left to finish alone. A tidy slate hangs by the stove, Dorram's own hand.* Clue: the cook's rationing note (full text in the handout PDFs), finished and routine, a crew still doing ordinary jobs long after they should have been worried. Second clue, same DC: claw gouges low on a cabinet door, old, scored deep, something's been digging for scraps long after anyone was left to mind it.
- **Crew quarters. DC 13 Investigation.** Read aloud: *Bunks are made, not military-tight, just made, the way you leave a room you plan to come back to. A sea chest sits open, half-packed, like whoever was filling it got called away before they finished.* Clue: a bunk roster confirms the ship was called the *Quiet Verge* before her registry got scraped. **DC 20:** a finished, unsent letter home from Fen Caskey (full text in the handouts), worried about the lane running thinner, trip over trip, but closing warm, not afraid. **DC 15 Perception, and this is the den:** bedding in the far corner bunk is torn up and nested, not slept in, matted and scaled, not a person's doing. **This is where the first real fight is likely to start**, two grell, hunting by sound alone in the dark. A lit torch or loud approach gives them away first, real quiet gives the crew the opening round.
- **Cargo hold. DC 13 Investigation.** Read aloud: *The hold isn't ransacked, it's shoved. Crates that should be stacked are scattered along one wall, dragged more than thrown, deep gouges scoring the deck plating between them, too wide and too deep for any person's tools.* Clue: a cargo jettison log (full text in the handouts) showing deliberate, methodical lightening, a controlled choice, not a panic dump. **Loot:** 150-400 gp of leftover goods (the standing side-gold rule), a locked strongbox (DC 13 to force or pick) worth another 100 gp, and a false panel behind the scattered crates, **DC 18**, a smuggler's cache the *Verge*'s own crew never found, another 100-150 gp in compact trade goods. **The megapede dens here.** This is the real boss fight, CR 7, well above the grell, and it should be run as one: give the table a real choice to retreat, seal the bay doors behind them, or come back better prepared, rather than a fight they're assumed to finish. The flumph's warning (engineering, below) is the table's best tool for walking in ready instead of surprised.

**The nest, once it can be searched safely** (the megapede dead, driven off, or well clear of it): tangled into shed scale and whatever else it's dragged back here over months of denning is a small, labeled ring of the ship's own duplicate keys, boxed up as non-essential cargo before the crew left (nobody needs spare keys in a lifeboat), one tag reading **CAPTAIN'S CABIN**. **DC 15 Investigation** to actually find the ring in the mess. This is the real prize for taking the megapede on rather than skirting it, a free, undamaged way into the captain's cabin to go with the fight's danger. The DC 15 force-the-door option stays available regardless, for a table that'd rather not go near the nest at all.
- **Engineering. DC 13 Dexterity save** on first entry: residual charge still live in a drained helm coupling arcs once, unprompted, 2d6 lightning to anyone standing too close, half on a save. Read aloud: *The air back here hums wrong, a coupling that should be dead still twitching like a nerve that doesn't know the limb's gone. Burn scoring climbs one wall in a long, deliberate arc, not an explosion, something straining and failing slowly.* This is the helm itself, having spent its last days reaching for current that wasn't there anymore and burning out trying. Once discharged, the room is safe. **DC 12 Investigation:** the engineer's maintenance log (full text in the handouts), confirming the crew knew exactly what was happening before the captain's final call. **DC 15 Perception finds the flumph**, cowering behind a loose panel, harmless and terrified of being knocked over. Left alone, it offers fretful telepathic chirps warning of "the big, hungry, loud one" deeper in, a usable warning about the megapede if the crew doesn't hurt it. **DC 18:** a choice of salvage worth taking, ask the table which they want rather than rolling for them, a partially intact gravity-plane coil, a sound backup helm coupling, or the ship's own sounding-lantern housing, each worth roughly 75-150 gp to the right buyer.
- **Bridge. DC 13 Investigation.** Read aloud: *Star charts are still pinned open to the last course anyone plotted. The helm seat is angled toward the window, toward a bright, ordinary, untouchable star.* Clue: the helmsman's logbook (full text in the handouts), entries getting shorter and more worried, transit times creeping up trip over trip, an offhand "fewer lights in the water tonight, maybe just overcast" that in hindsight was the Tide visibly thinning, then the final entry, mid-sentence, the moment the push gave out. **DC 15:** a second, separate scrap tucked into the log, the helmsman's own dead-reckoning course for an unsteered hull, back toward the Rock of Bral, her own plan, not luck. **Loot:** a navigation instrument, a real sellable item to Aurel Delverane specifically. **DC 20 (rare):** the waypoint the boats ran for is one the table already knows, worth deciding live which one lands hardest. **DC 25:** a locked drawer under the console, forced or picked, holds the one real magic item aboard, a **Lantern of Revealing**, old and unmarked, the kind of thing a careful helmsman keeps on hand for a dark crossing.
- **Captain's cabin, locked. DC 15 to force, or the duplicate key ring from the megapede's nest in the cargo hold (DC 15 Investigation there, once it's safe to search).** The core reveal, read it straight: the captain's log (full text in the handouts), calm through every entry, the handwriting never once changing under pressure. The last page does the math out loud, air and stores for the hull won't outlast the drift, the boats will outlast the hull by weeks if nobody wastes them, orders to jettison, to split the crew, to go. No blame, no panic, a closing line wishing whoever reads this well, whoever that turns out to be. **DC 20:** a second, unofficial scrap tucked in the same log, the only crack in the composure the whole ship shows.

**Tone note.** Played as wonder-adjacent dread, not horror. A competent crew solved a hard problem under impossible pressure and left clean; the megapede and the grell are both opportunistic squatters that moved in long after, not a sign anyone aboard came to harm. Nothing here should imply either creature was ever near the *Quiet Verge*'s actual crew. The megapede is this cold open's real teeth, big enough to end badly if the table charges it; the flumph's warning and the option to retreat and reseal the hold are both there on purpose.

## 4. Main content: Open Water, Open Season

**The posting, word for word, what the players actually read (`sessions/charter-window-board.html`, BR-1252):**
> **Open Water, Open Season.** *Survey, a creeping current, plus standing bounty.* Pay 3,100 gp, Days ~20. **Requester** Aurel Delverane, the Farthing Station survey office. **Route** Bral to the Windward Edge, the standing surf. **Details** The Windward Edge's standing surf has crept inward, and a once-reliable stretch of chart has gone unreadable. Delverane wants to know whether the old charts can still be trusted. RATING: OPEN, CLIENT OF RECORD (Delverane, Farthing Station).

**The deliverable, broken down, two separate things:**
- **3,100 gp flat**, paid for completing the survey and reporting back to Delverane, guaranteed regardless of what's actually found.
- **The navigation data itself**, sellable separately, Orena's own call whether and to whom (`rules/Bastions.md`'s cartography rules), by DC tier: **DC 10** an ordinary lane (50-300 gp), **DC 13** the lane brushing thinning water (300-1,500 gp, Orena's call whether the writeup is honest or softened, see below), **DC 16+** something genuinely unprecedented (1,500-10,000+ gp).
- **"Open Season" is the title's wordplay, not a requirement.** The board text never mentions killing anything. The standing CIV Open Season bounty just happens to apply to this stretch too, same as anywhere else, pure incidental opportunity, never the job's point.

**The Windward Edge itself.** Not a storm, not weather: the literal confluence of two real currents (the Tide is settled canon now as actual elemental water, `campaign/Story Spine.md`), grinding against each other hard enough to throw real water upward against nothing, a waterfall running backward. Where the spray catches the Astral Sea's own light behind it, it bends and streaks into shifting bands of color, an aurora made of actual falling water rather than light alone.
> Read aloud: *The standing surf comes into view before anything else does, a waterfall running the wrong way, real water blown upward off the collision of two currents and caught there, never quite falling. Where the spray catches the dark behind it, it streaks into shifting color, green and violet and silver-white, an aurora made of water instead of light. It hums, low, under everything, felt in the teeth more than heard. The air carries an unmistakable mineral-wet smell, rain on hot stone, water this far from anywhere water should be. Debris drifts along the boundary in slow spirals, caught between two different pulls. Somewhere past it, the chart just stops being reliable.*

**Why it's creeping inward, consistent with the Stilling's own mechanism.** This isn't one side dying outright, it's a confluence losing its balance. The charted lane's current held its ground against the deeper inflow because a resident kindori pod's regular passage kept reinforcing it (the established reinforcing-feedback rule). Once that pod stalled, the lane's own current weakened, and the boundary where the two bodies of water meet has been creeping into territory that used to belong to the stable, charted side.

**Longscar, the predator drawn to the stalled pod.** A named, known night scavver, earned its reputation the way sharks earn theirs: one old wound running the full length of its body, from a fight everyone agrees it clearly won. It's moved in on the stalled, weakened kindori pod, opportunistic rather than monstrous, exactly the established rule that a genuinely dangerous ambush predator turning up somewhere new is itself a sign the region's ecology just changed hands. Full stats in the companion's Reference section (reflavored Night Scavver, CR 5). The real choice it offers, never forced: help the stalled pod (non-lethal, a rescue-shaped scene), drive Longscar off without killing it, or take the bounty on a real, named, actively dangerous animal. All three stay live.

**Aurel Delverane, her established voice carrying any scene she's in**, narrates her own observations half to herself, chart-ink on one finger, wants to be the one who's right about the Edge before anyone senior listens. Full writeup, `npcs/Aurel Delverane.md`.

**Captain Thalassa, the Windward Edge's own raider, not a generic threat.** Triton, once a guardian of the deep water bordering the Elemental Plane of Water, now leading a small renegade crew (entry 6 below) out of the same desperation the whole Stilling runs on: her own waters have been receding, and the Houses' fixed-toll economy never had any use for triton territory to begin with. Piracy, for her, is a furious, deliberate way of making the surface world look. **Physical tic:** her trident is scored with tally marks, one for every ship she's stopped, most of them not destroyed. **Voice tic:** formal and clipped, old guardian-oath cadence even mid-raid ("by old oath, surrender your cargo"). Her vessel: the ***Drowned Oath***. A real "problem, not a villain," per the campaign's own house style, worth playing as someone the table could plausibly end up allied with rather than just defeating.

**A Research pointer, given how much is freshly live this session.** Orena's Library Research order (`rules/Bastions.md`) already covers House Nightbreeze, House Carrow, and the Stilling as topics; worth reminding the table this exists if anyone wants to dig into the Nightbreeze offer's real shape, the Carrow-adjacent corridor marker, or anything else freshly cracked open tonight, rather than treating it as locked to whatever's already been asked before.
