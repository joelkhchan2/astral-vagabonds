## 2. Cold open: the derelict

**The hull has a name now: the *Quiet Verge*,** a small independent trader that ran supply between Verge colonies, the same shape of route Hallow's Rest depends on. Registry scraped clean, no distress call, no bodies, no violence, just a ship that stopped being lived in mid-motion, was deliberately abandoned in good order, and drifted for months before fetching up in a lowside berth at Bral.

**Read aloud, boarding her:**
> The airlock cycles on backup power, barely. Inside, the air still holds, but it's cold in a way that has nothing to do with temperature, the specific stillness of somewhere that used to have people breathing in it. Emergency lighting only, a dim amber strip along the deck. Somewhere forward, something ticks on an even interval, a system nobody's told to stop running. The deck under your boots is gritty in a way metal shouldn't be. What do you do?

**What actually happened, GM only.** The *Quiet Verge* was running her usual supply hop when the crossing started taking longer than the charts said it should, trip after trip, the helmsman's own log tracking it getting worse. She noted the Tide itself looked thinner along that stretch too: fewer bright threads in the water, more dark patches between them. Flow was dying before glow fully went out, the warning was there to read, if anyone had known what it meant. Nobody did yet. Then, mid-transit, the push stopped. Not blackness, not lost, the destination star sat exactly where the charts said, visible the whole time. The ship simply couldn't close the distance anymore: **becalmed**, momentum only, air and stores burning down on a fixed clock while home stayed in plain sight and unreachable. The captain's math was cold but sound: the hull's own stores wouldn't outlast the drift, but the two lifeboats had their own small engines and burned a fraction of the air and food per body. Jettison cargo to lighten what's left, split the crew into the boats, run for the last waypoint the charts still confirmed while the boats' fuel held out. Before they left, the helmsman set the empty hull's own dead-reckoning heading for the Rock of Bral, since an unsteered ship still needed to go somewhere useful if anyone ever found her. That's the months of drift accounted for, not luck, a last deliberate act by someone doing her job to the end. **Did the crew themselves make it? Genuinely unresolved.** A real open hook, answer it only if the table wants to chase it.

**Who's aboard now.** Sometime during that long, unpowered drift, something found the open airlock and moved in. **A small hunting crew of three grell** (Medium aberration, CR 3 each, Monster Manual: floating brains with a beaked maw and a paralytic tentacle crown, blind, hunting entirely by sound) have denned in the cargo hold, the ship's darkest, quietest space. They've been aboard a while, long enough to leave real sign through multiple rooms before anyone meets one directly. Full stat block in the session companion's Reference section.

**The rooms.** A true point-crawl, six spaces plus the entry. Let the crew choose their own order after the airlock. Every room carries both a "what happened here" clue and, scattered through, a "something's living here now" clue, so the grell feel discovered, not sprung.

- **Airlock / entry. DC 10 Perception.** Read aloud: *The outer hatch is already cycling when you reach for it, sluggish, protesting on backup power. Inside, your breath doesn't fog the way it should. Somewhere forward, something ticks on an even interval, patient, mechanical, a system nobody told to stop. The deck under your boots is gritty in a way metal shouldn't be.* On a success: the grit is old organic residue, shed and scaled, nothing fresh. First hint something's using the hull as a den, not evidence of any struggle.
- **Galley. DC 12 Perception.** Read aloud: *Pans still hang in their rack, swinging faint and slow from a draft that shouldn't exist in dead air. A pot's gone to rust at the bottom, whatever was cooking left to rot alone. Someone's handwriting trails off mid-word on a slate by the stove.* Clue: the cook's unfinished rationing note, mid-sentence, pen still where it was set down. Second clue, same DC: claw gouges low on a cabinet door, old, scored deep, something's been digging for scraps long after anyone was left to stop it.
- **Crew quarters. DC 13 Investigation.** Read aloud: *Bunks are made, not military-tight, just made, the way you leave a room you plan to come back to. A sea chest sits open, half-packed, like whoever was filling it got called away before they finished.* Clue: effects packed with real care, a name tag or bunk roster confirms the ship was called the *Quiet Verge* before her registry got scraped. A small ordinary thing left behind by accident, a child's toy, a pressed flower in someone's logbook. Second clue, **DC 15 Perception:** bedding in the far corner bunk is torn up and nested, not slept in, matted and scaled, not a person's doing.
- **Cargo hold. DC 13 Investigation.** Read aloud: *The hold is mostly empty, and what's left is stacked, not scattered, crates lined along one wall like someone was choosing what to leave rather than fleeing what to grab.* Clue: a tally sheet showing cargo deliberately, methodically jettisoned, a controlled lightening, not a panic dump. **Loot:** 150-400 gp of leftover goods (the standing side-gold rule), plus a locked strongbox (DC 13 to force or pick) worth another 100 gp and a handful of trade goods. **This is the den.** DC 15 Perception finds shed scale and the half-eaten remains of something smaller behind the stacked crates before the crew notices they're being listened for. **This is the most likely room for the fight to actually start**, three grell, hunting by sound alone in the dark, blind but not slow. Calibrate for a fair, genuinely dangerous fight at level 5; a lit torch or loud approach gives them away first, real quiet gives the crew the opening round.
- **Engineering. DC 13 Dexterity save** on first entry: residual charge still live in a drained helm coupling arcs once, unprompted, 2d6 lightning to anyone standing too close, half on a save. Read aloud: *The air back here hums wrong, a coupling that should be dead still twitching like a nerve that doesn't know the limb's gone. Burn scoring climbs one wall in a long, deliberate arc, not an explosion, something straining and failing slowly.* This is the helm itself, having spent its last days reaching for current that wasn't there anymore and burning out trying. Once discharged, the room is safe. **DC 12 Investigation** after: a hurried but not panicked maintenance log in the engineer's own hand, flatly noting the helm's been "reaching for nothing" for three days running and there's nothing left to do about it from down here, confirming the crew knew exactly what was happening before the captain's final call.
- **Bridge. DC 13 Investigation.** Read aloud: *Star charts are still pinned open to the last course anyone plotted. The helm seat is angled toward the window, toward a bright, ordinary, untouchable star.* Clue: the helmsman's own logbook, entries getting shorter and more worried, transit times creeping up trip over trip, an offhand "fewer lights in the water tonight, maybe just overcast" that in hindsight was the Tide visibly thinning, then the final entry, mid-sentence, the moment the push gave out. **DC 15:** a second, separate scrap tucked into the log, a hasty heading and distance in a different hand, a dead-reckoning course for an unsteered hull, back toward the Rock of Bral. The helmsman's own plan, not luck. **Loot:** a navigation instrument, a real sellable item to Aurel Delverane specifically. **DC 20 (rare):** the waypoint the boats ran for is one the table already knows, worth deciding live which one lands hardest.
- **Captain's cabin, locked. DC 15 to force, or a key found in crew quarters on a DC 15 Investigation there instead.** The core reveal, read it straight: the captain's own log, calm through every entry, the handwriting never once changing under pressure. The last page does the math out loud, air and stores for the hull won't outlast the drift, the boats will outlast the hull by weeks if nobody wastes them, orders to jettison, to split the crew, to go. No blame, no panic, a closing line wishing whoever reads this well, whoever that turns out to be.

**Tone note.** Played as wonder-adjacent dread, not horror. A competent crew solved a hard problem under impossible pressure and left clean; the only real threat the table meets is the grell, an opportunistic squatter that moved in long after, not a sign anyone aboard came to harm. Nothing here should imply the grell were ever near the *Quiet Verge*'s actual crew.
