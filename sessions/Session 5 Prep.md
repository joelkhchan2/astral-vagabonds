## 2. Cold open: the derelict

**The hull has a name now: the *Quiet Verge*,** a small independent trader that ran supply between Verge colonies, the same shape of route Hallow's Rest depends on. Registry scraped clean, no distress call, no bodies, no violence, just a ship that stopped being lived in mid-motion and drifted into a lowside berth under her own leftover momentum.

**Read aloud, boarding her:**
> The airlock cycles on backup power, barely. Inside, the air still holds, but it's cold in a way that has nothing to do with temperature, the specific stillness of somewhere that used to have people breathing in it. Emergency lighting only, a dim amber strip along the deck. Somewhere forward, something ticks on an even interval, a system nobody's told to stop running. What do you do?

**What actually happened, GM only.** The *Quiet Verge*'s route simply stopped resolving mid-transit, the exact failure Hallow's Rest's own supply run suffered, a current thinning past the point of safe return. No fallback exists once that happens, no fixed stars to navigate by in the open dark, nothing to aim for. Rather than wait aboard a dying ship for the air to run out, the captain made a hard, competent call: jettison cargo to lighten the hull, and evacuate the whole crew by lifeboat toward the last waypoint the charts still confirmed, while there was still enough residual light in the dying current to steer by. The empty hull, stripped and powered down, was left to drift, and eventually found her own way to Bral on whatever current-drift was left, unsteered. **Did they make it? Genuinely unresolved.** A real open hook, answer it only if the table wants to chase it.

**The rooms.** A true point-crawl, six spaces plus the entry. Let the crew choose their own order after the airlock.

- **Airlock / entry.** No roll. The read-aloud above. Sets the tone: cold, quiet, nothing damaged, nothing wrong except the absence of anyone.
- **Galley.** **DC 12 Perception.** A half-finished note from the cook about rationing, mid-sentence, the pen still sitting where it was set down.
- **Crew quarters** (new room). **DC 13 Investigation.** Personal effects packed with real care, not panic, someone folded clothes. One small, specific, ordinary thing left behind by accident: a child's toy, or a pressed flower in a logbook that isn't the captain's. A name tag or a bunk roster confirms the ship was called the *Quiet Verge* before her registry got scraped, the first real clue to her name.
- **Cargo hold.** **DC 13 Investigation.** A tally sheet showing cargo deliberately, methodically jettisoned, a controlled lightening, not a panic dump. **Loot:** what wasn't fully cleared, 150-400 gp of leftover goods (the standing side-gold rule), plus a locked strongbox (DC 13 to force or pick) worth another 100 gp and a handful of small trade goods.
- **Engineering** (new room). **DC 13 Dexterity save** on first entry: residual charge still live in a drained helm coupling arcs once, unprompted, 2d6 lightning to anyone standing too close when it discharges, half on a save. A real moment of risk, not a trap meant to be disarmed, just a dying system letting go. Once it's discharged, the room is safe, nothing else to find.
- **Captain's cabin, locked.** **DC 15 to force, or a key found in crew quarters on a DC 15 Investigation there instead.** The captain's own log, calm and methodical through the whole entry, ending mid-sentence the day the current gave out. This is the core reveal, read it straight: the route stopped resolving, the decision to evacuate, no blame assigned, just a competent person doing the math.
- **Bridge.** **DC 15 Investigation.** The last plotted course, the waypoint they were lifeboating toward. **Loot:** a navigation instrument, a real sellable item to Aurel Delverane specifically, a nice tie to this session's other thread. **DC 20 (rare):** brushes a GM-only detail, the waypoint they were heading for is one the table already knows, worth deciding live which one lands hardest.

**Tone note.** Played as wonder-adjacent dread, not horror. Competent people solving a hard problem under impossible pressure, the same genre promise as the rest of the Guttering's Tier 1 symptoms. Nobody died here. That's the unsettling part, not a monster, a clean, controlled loss.
