# Two Chairs Short, a Ballad

*A recap of Session 3 in verse. For the actual-play record, see `sessions/Session 3.md`.*

Before the lines were cast off, before the Tollmarch dark,
the crew took one warm evening to mend their small, fond mark.
Aerion sold his costumes, seven sold at a decent rate,
and a blue-hooded stranger bought a shell for a turtle-mate.

He forged some blunted daggers, shoddy but they'd do,
while Tack built one small caterpillar out of spare parts and glue.
Orena asked the Library one question of her three:
the Amberwake has faded, a road nobody drives to see.

Then came the thing in transit, a shape too clean to be chance,
a dodecahedron, warm and pulsing, caught them both at a glance.
They passed the test. It opened. Each one asked it a thing,
and what came back was a vision, not an answer it would sing.

Orena asked of Vellum, and saw a man sit still
while his war room came apart around him, calm against his will.
*We write a note, we watch them,* was all the man would say.
The threat had been a bluff then. Someone still watched, either way.

Aerion asked the stars' own absence, and got something else instead:
Orena's own lost crossing, the water closing overhead,
a captain's voice that called her, a creature in the dark,
a song she'd carried since, now humming low and stark.
That song had a name. The Vocalise. She broke, right there, in front of them all,
and the whole crew felt the echo, though not every word would fall
to a telling. Finny worried. Tack went still and spare.
Brass was openly furious. Oz looked like he had more to bear.
And Estra, watching quietly, let a small relief show through,
as if some old familiar ache had just been proven true.

Three stowaways were found then, nine days deep in the hold,
Boy Boy McGee and his two crew, hungry, caught, and cold.
Estra knelt to his chassis and told him, calm and clear:
*leaving a ship doesn't cost you the captain you are, not here.*
They tied them, fed them, set them down. No plank, no debt repaid.
The old rivalry held steady, just a little less afraid.

Two days on, in private, the truth got handed over whole:
the note had been a bluff, and now Estra knew that for herself.
She wasn't new to Vellum's games, had stood on either side.
She asked if there was more unsaid. They let that question glide.

The *Fair Return* came alongside, her captain hat in hand
for Estra, one of the first seven, still proud to take her stand.
His crew came warm to meet them: a slug in bows named Meep,
a gunner glad for company, an autognome built to keep
the engines singing true, and a nervous second hand
who'd never seen a real fight, hoping he'd understand.

Two kindori calves rode with them, Hush and bright-eyed Glimmer,
orphaned, calling across the gap in a song that made them dimmer
distances feel smaller. Orena read them clean,
the kinship showing plain as day in everything between.

Then the circus came for real: clowns rigged high in the lines,
a raider ship, the Center Ring, and the dread the table designed.
The guns held true. The raiders' own ram turned back on their own hull.
Not one point of damage landed. The fight stayed, start to end, full
in the crew's own favor. Aerion's blade found the Ringmaster's neck
in the opening breath of battle, and the rest came apart in wreck.
A greater ship, a mothership, watched once, then turned and fled.
The Velvet Comet still lives on. Its prize was denied instead.

At the Institute they landed, and Cordelia paid in kind,
twenty-eight hundred gold for the crew, the full sum close behind.
Hush and Glimmer joined their pod, welcomed home at last,
and sang a learned duet that Orena knew from her past:
the Vocalise again, not the first song, but its echo carried on,
proof the grief could travel further than the one who'd mourned it, gone.

Home to port with coin in hand, five ballistae strong,
a line of credit standing, a floor they wouldn't go below.
Two seats still sit open: a cook who guns, a scout who does the same,
and somewhere past the Tollmarch, a comet waits to learn their name.
