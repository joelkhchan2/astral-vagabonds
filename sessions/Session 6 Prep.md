## 2. Cold open: a flashback, running water
Replaces a straight-into-Wyndham opening. Warm, not eerie, built to be a core memory for Orena, not plot delivery. Full crew roster and the knot's own two phrases, played here for the first time: `npcs/The Wayfinder Knot.md`.

**The trigger, present day, ordinary and unweighted.** Somewhere quiet, before Wyndham's scene below, someone asks Orena something plain: *"Hey, when's the last time you actually saw running water?"* Aerion's a natural pick, oldest friend aboard, five years of knowing exactly how much she doesn't talk about this, but any crewmate works, table's call. Whoever asks shouldn't mean anything by it.

**The tell.** Orena doesn't answer right away. A beat too long, hands going still, eyes somewhere that isn't the room.

**Into the memory, narrated setting, Orena played live by Gabrielle.**

**Read aloud, the shift:**
> The galley noise falls away. Underneath it, slowly, something else: real surf against a real shore, salt and wet rope, a fire burning low. Five other faces around it, years younger than they'll ever look again. Nobody here knows anything is coming.

**Colin leads it, true to his character.** Cheerful, optimistic, the Knot's natural center of gravity even though Orena founded it. He's telling the old dockhand legend tonight, the Stillwater Vocalise, half ghost story, half dare, the kind of thing a hungry young guild tells itself for the thrill of maybe chasing it someday. Something like: *"My gran swore she heard it once. Said it sounds like the whole sea's dying, and if you follow it, you find out why."* Played for excitement, not dread. This is the spark, not the disaster.

**Priscilla's pick, played live, not scripted in advance.** One of the other four, **Anchor Thistlewick** (dwarf), **Squall Netherby** (tabaxi), **Pim Oddsocks** (gnome), or **Scrap Fennimore** (halfling), is hers for this scene. Hand her nothing but the name and the one-line sketch in `npcs/The Wayfinder Knot.md`; whatever she makes of them in the room becomes real canon going forward, the same way Colin's love of *Dragon Masters* became real the moment it was played.

**Two pieces of banked texture get their actual first appearance here, not just a reference:**
- **The wayfinder's bend**, taught in this scene, not already known. Someone practical-handed (Anchor or Pim read well for this) shows young Orena the knot properly for the first time, quick-release, built for exactly the kind of emergency where a line has to come free fast. A small, ordinary skill-sharing moment, nothing ominous.
- **The old work-shanty**, sung properly, together, maybe the first and only time the table actually hears it in full rather than half-remembered: *Call:* "When the stars go quiet and the water's black," *Response:* "Hold a steady line, hold a steady line." *Call:* "Trust the hand beside you, you'll find your way back," *Response:* "Hold a steady line, hold your crew."

**The Knot's own two phrases, used in character, not explained.** Someone tells Colin to shut up mid-story with a grinning *"hold your line, Colin,"* the joking register. Later, as the fire's dying and people are peeling off to sleep, someone says *"see you on the other tide"* as a goodnight, the sincere one, unremarked on, just how they say goodbye.

**End the memory on something small and specific, not a statement.** The exact smell of the water that night, a joke that actually landed, nothing stated aloud that sounds like foreshadowing. The dread belongs entirely to the table, from hindsight, not to anyone in the scene.

**Back to present, quiet.** The memory just ends. Orena's actual answer to the original question is small: *"...a while."* Whoever asked doesn't push. A clean, optional spot for Flurry to do something small and warm nearby. Cut from here straight into Wyndham's scene below.

## 5. Flurry's spotlight: the Edge itself
A scripted wonder beat, not a table roll, landing when the ship actually reaches the confluence. This is the "sky at its healthiest" tell (`npcs/Flurry.md`), the warm register, not the eerie one, since the Windward Edge is abundance, not a guttering.

**Read aloud:**
> Flurry's at the forward viewport before anyone calls it. Paws up on the glass, breath already fogging it, chirping at something nobody else has spotted yet, the backward waterfall just now resolving out of the dark, color bleeding up through the spray like the sky itself is catching light from underneath.

- She's unmistakably delighted, not spooked, the opposite of her guttering tell. Worth a line connecting it to Orena specifically, the dragon and the navigator both reading the same good water, one by blood, one by ear, per the standing bridge between the two of them.
- This is a tell, not an explanation. Don't have her do anything mechanical, don't name what she's sensing. If a player asks why she's acting like this, the honest answer is "she just does," same as always.
- A clean spot for Aerion to have a small, private, undoted-on moment with her too, his choice entirely, nothing scripted.

**Orena's growing attunement, tier 1, lands in this same scene.** Estra asks her, half-curious, half-testing, whether the ship could actually navigate on the standing surf itself rather than just fly near it, real water, not void. **DC 12 Nature.** On a success, Orena doesn't just answer the question, she recognizes something underneath it: the way these two currents collide and flow mirrors the structure of the Astral currents she already reads by instinct, the same shapes at a different scale. Play it as a felt recognition, not an explanation, she couldn't say why it's true, only that it is. **Mechanical payoff, standing from here on: advantage on one Nature or navigation check per day**, specifically when reading currents or the Tide by feel rather than a chart. This is tier 1 of a growing track (`npcs/Orena.md`); say nothing about future tiers at the table, let it surface when it's earned.

**Escalation.** She overcommits to something flashy, a trick shot, a stunt dodge, whatever the table generates live, and it doesn't land clean. Public, a little embarrassing, nothing that actually hurts her.

**The turn.** In the quiet after, to whoever's nearby (Orena and Aerion both work, or either alone), she lets a real story about Splinter slip, unprompted, not performed. **The button.** Let her catch herself being sincere and immediately overcorrect back to bravado, a joke, a boast, taps her shell again. Sitcom rhythm, setup, escalation, a real feeling that gets one beat before the character defense mechanism kicks back in.
