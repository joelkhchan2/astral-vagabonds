## 2. Cold open: settling in with Wyndham Aldane
Short, warm, and it's the actual start of the voyage, not a recap. First morning underway with a researcher formally watching the ship work for the first time.

**Read aloud:**
> The galley's half full when she finds a seat, notebook closed, hands wrapped around a mug she hasn't actually drunk from yet. She's not hiding that she's watching, she's just doing it politely, glasses on, which means, if anyone's paying attention, she isn't really watching at all. Not yet.

- She asks a few genuinely curious, low-stakes questions over breakfast, nothing probing, the real observation hasn't started. Good spot for the crew to decide, unscripted, how much they actually want to perform for her versus just be themselves.
- A small, specific detail worth including: she takes her glasses off the moment something actually interests her, on when she's being polite, off when she's paying attention (`npcs/Wyndham Aldane.md`). If the table catches this pattern themselves over the session, that's the point, don't explain it.
- Good spot for Flurry to decide what she thinks of the new passenger, entirely her call live, sniff, ignore, steal something of hers, whatever the table finds funny.
- Ends when Estra calls the course set and the ship actually gets underway. Cut there.

## 3. Open Water, Open Season, launched
Full job terms, the deliverable breakdown, and the 40-entry Windward Edge encounter table: `sessions/Session 5 Prep.md` &sect;6. Run it as written. Suggested sequencing for this session, not a forced order, roll honestly where it doesn't matter and steer where it does:

1. **One or two lighter table entries early in the transit**, pure color or a small choice, to let the voyage breathe before anything costs something. Good candidates already built: the ordinary kindori pod (#1), the Weatheralls' *Kettle* passing at a distance (#11), or a dohwar trader (#15).

**Two small, low-stakes plants, somewhere in this early stretch (`npcs/The Wayfinder Knot.md`, GM-only).** None of this has landed at the table yet, and it's due before the real confirmation lands in session 7, so it needs to start now, ambient, not flagged as important.
- **The shanty.** At some unguarded moment, coiling rope, a quiet watch, Orena hums a few bars of an old work-song and can't quite place where she knows it from. No reaction needed from anyone, just a line of color.
- **The knot.** She ties a specific locking hitch (hers to name live, a quick-release bend) without thinking, the way she always does. First beat only: someone (Tack, a crewmate, anyone handy) asks where she picked it up, she genuinely doesn't know. Don't let this pay off yet, it's a plant, not the reveal.
2. **Flurry's spotlight beat** (below) as the ship actually nears the Edge itself, not from a table roll, a scripted beat marking the location's arrival.
3. **Sable's spotlight beat** (below) during a quiet leg or an Aerion/Orena Bastion turn, whichever the table's pace actually allows.
4. **Longscar and the stalled kindori pod** (#3/#4) if the session has room, a real non-lethal-vs-bounty choice already built, good mid-session texture before the climax. **Corrected: Longscar is void scavver tier (CR 11), not a lesser creature**, so this is a genuinely dangerous encounter on its own now, not a side nuisance.
5. **The climax: entry 20, the void scavver's ship-to-ship assault**, steered to happen this session rather than left fully to the die. Full ship-combat translation already written in the companion (AC 17, HP 180, CR 11, run through `rules/Rules & Systems.md` &sect;8 against the *Night Fury*'s own AC 19 / threshold 25). **Don't fold Longscar into this fight as a second attacker, both are apex tier now, and stacking two CR 11 threats isn't the fix.** If the table's crossed paths with Longscar earlier in the session, let the climax be the unnamed void scavver instead (apex predators hold territory from each other), or if Longscar's the one who shows up, back it with two to four brown scavvers (CR 4) for a fuller fight without doubling the apex threat.

**A Research/Reading the Ledgers pointer, since both are live mechanics now:** Orena's Library can dig into anything freshly relevant (the Edge itself, Aurel Delverane, Nightbreeze), and Aerion's Reading the Ledgers can run on top of a Storehouse or Smithy order rather than instead of it. Remind the table both exist if nobody reaches for them unprompted.

## 4. Flurry's spotlight: the Edge itself
A scripted wonder beat, not a table roll, landing when the ship actually reaches the confluence. This is the "sky at its healthiest" tell (`npcs/Flurry.md`), the warm register, not the eerie one, since the Windward Edge is abundance, not a guttering.

**Read aloud:**
> Flurry's at the forward viewport before anyone calls it. Paws up on the glass, breath already fogging it, chirping at something nobody else has spotted yet, the backward waterfall just now resolving out of the dark, color bleeding up through the spray like the sky itself is catching light from underneath.

- She's unmistakably delighted, not spooked, the opposite of her guttering tell. Worth a line connecting it to Orena specifically, the dragon and the navigator both reading the same good water, one by blood, one by ear, per the standing bridge between the two of them.
- This is a tell, not an explanation. Don't have her do anything mechanical, don't name what she's sensing. If a player asks why she's acting like this, the honest answer is "she just does," same as always.
- A clean spot for Aerion to have a small, private, undoted-on moment with her too, his choice entirely, nothing scripted.

## 5. Sable's spotlight: Splinter
A real individual scene, not just a line at dinner. Best trigger: Oz running gunnery drills with the three new hires, a banked idea nobody's paid off yet, and the kind of setup that lets Sable's bravado actually get tested in front of people.

**Setup.** Sable talks a big game before the drill even starts, "fastest turtle this side of space," taps her shell for luck. Judo, per the standing rookie-dare beat, is the one person aboard who'd actually call her on it.

**Escalation.** She overcommits to something flashy, a trick shot, a stunt dodge, whatever the table generates live, and it doesn't land clean. Public, a little embarrassing, nothing that actually hurts her.

**The turn.** In the quiet after, to whoever's nearby (Orena and Aerion both work, or either alone), she lets a real story about Splinter slip, unprompted, not performed. Keep his actual nature genuinely unconfirmed, per standing design, she believes every word of it, whether the table ever decides he deserved that belief is a question for much later, not this scene. Land it on her actual want: not to be the best, to have him mean it someday when he says he's proud of her.

**The button.** Let her catch herself being sincere and immediately overcorrect back to bravado, a joke, a boast, taps her shell again. Sitcom rhythm, setup, escalation, a real feeling that gets one beat before the character defense mechanism kicks back in.

## 6. Battle: the void scavver
Full translation already written: `sessions/session-5-companion.html`'s Void Scavver ship-combat note. Run it as a direct assault on the *Night Fury*'s hull through the ship-combat rules, not individual attack rolls against the crew. This is deliberately the hardest fight the table's faced since the Megapede nearly dropped Oz, calibrate it that way on purpose, real stakes, not a token scuffle, but survivable.

- Consider letting Wyndham Aldane's reaction to the fight read as slightly too composed for a passenger who's never seen one of these before, a small, honest tell that she knows more about void scavvers (and by extension the Stilling) than her cover requires, without saying so outright. Good seed for the ending hook below.
- The scavver breaking off rather than fighting to the death, consistent with how `references/Astral Sea Bestiary.md` frames the species (awe-and-flee, use sparingly, its scarcity is its meaning), is a legitimate outcome if the fight goes well for the crew, not a cop-out.

## 7. Return to port, or not
