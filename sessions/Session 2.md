The lockbox gets opened, the crew reads what's actually inside it, and a "cheap milk run" side job turns into the session's real fight. See `sessions/Session 2 Prep.md` for what was planned going in and `campaign/Story Spine.md` for what any of it means; this doc is the record of what actually happened.

Several planned beats from `Session 2 Prep.md` didn't run as scripted, most notably how the lockbox got opened, noted inline below and flagged at the top of that doc.

## Deciding what to do with the lockbox

Estra gathered the leads to ask what they wanted to do with it. Her read: word will get back to Bral that a Vellum outpost (Reefside) is gone, but nobody knows the crew is holding this. Her call: **keep it under wraps for now**, and find out what's actually inside so they know how much danger they're in. Oz offered to cast **Identify** on it.

**What Identify revealed:** an ordinary iron lockbox, not itself magical, carrying two spells. An **alarm spell** (loud sound on opening) and a **glyph of warding**, a frost rune that dumps a Cone of Cold on whoever opens it carelessly. Someone asked the obvious question: isn't there a key? **Aerion has it** (lifted off Factor Ren Calder's pocket at the Reefside handoff, session 1). Ruling on the spot: a glyph like this, meant to protect a delivery rather than kill the intended recipient, should have a built-in exception for the actual key. **Opening it with the key dispels the wards outright.**

**Oz opened it** (82 HP, the party's actual tank for this) rather than Estra (58 HP) risking the Cone of Cold if the ruling was wrong. It wasn't. Nothing went off.

**This replaces the planned Wren Ostler dispel scene from `Session 2 Prep.md` entirely.** No trip to a specialist was needed; the box opened clean, mid-transit, off the stolen key. Wren Ostler herself may still exist as a loose thread (see "Brass's contact," below) but she did not do this.

## What was inside

Two letters and a navigation chart, all played as physical handouts. Full text and chart art: `sessions/lockbox-vellum-correspondence.html` and `sessions/lockbox-amberwake-chart.html` (both already built and used almost exactly as written).

- **Letter 1, RY147, Stormwane.** Ivor Marsh, Registrar of the Amberwake Desk, Vellum House Office of the Archive, writing above channel to the Office of the First Archivist. The Amberwake corridor needed four corrections to its plotted line inside a year, the last two within a season of each other; two ships arrived late against headings he personally certified; he's walked the sightline off the Corvath Shoal three times and it doesn't resolve the same way twice. He'd rather cost the Archive a season of subscriber confidence than see his own name on a loss report. Full correction history attached.
- **Letter 2, RY148, Emberfall.** A cold Notice of Record: the Amberwake corridor is stricken from the public listing effective immediately, no disruption notice to existing chart-holders. Cited under Archive Policy 12.4, a corridor's listing lapses when its charter-holder's certification lapses, and **House Carrow's certification for the Amberwake lapsed RY148**. Framed as routine.
- **The chart.** A working Vellum Cartographic Service chart of the corridor between the Sallow Chain (Vellum) and a Carrow settlement in the Amberwake, showing six successive attempts to plot the same route, each closer together in time than the last, the sixth never finished and never re-filed.

**Reads from the table:** first instinct, half-joking, was "we found a treasure map." Real reads followed. **Orena (Survival, 11):** confirms something already suspected, this isn't new, the anomaly's been happening for fifteen-plus years judging by the letter's age and Carrow's status, and it's the kind of thing veteran navigators would notice and quietly sit on. **Aerion (Insight, natural 20):** his Windsong upbringing reads the politics immediately, Vellum buried this on purpose, and the crew is now holding evidence of a real scandal reaching into the Archive's own upper offices, tied to a defunct house.

**History checks on House Carrow** (both PCs, Aerion's 16 the sharper hit thanks to his upbringing): Carrow collapsed roughly forty years ago, a cartography house like Vellum, held the Board of Ledgers' now-empty fifth chair. Official story is financial ruin; the real story is murkier, whether Vellum simply outcompeted them or something else happened is unclear, and whether the whole house is actually gone (not just financially) is an open, dark rumor rather than a confirmed fact.

**Orena copied the chart** before returning it to storage (a failed Survival check, no new information, but the copy exists).

## Bastion actions, resolved at the table

Both PCs' facilities got used on the transit home, played as flavorful scenes rather than dry rolls.

**Orena, Library (a Research-style order, asked as a direct question: "have the stars been reappearing elsewhere?").** Investigation check, aided by Aerion giving up one of his own questions to help; landed at a weak 11 even with a luck point spent. Result was sailors'-tale tier, not a real lead: *"I watched a star go out and I could swear it appeared on my left"* trailing into a smudged, unfinished line about being attacked. Deliberately unreliable, Guttering-flavored, unlabeled as such. This uses up the Research order for this leg; treat any prior note about a DC 13 Investigation/History check on this same question as superseded by this actual roll.

**Aerion, Storehouse/Workshop (he treats them as one room).** Three attempts, mixed results:
- Performance check (hand harp, trying to "study the vocal waves" directly): rolled a 9, broke a string.
- A second attempt, writing a song about the crew's adventures: passable, can't finish the full piece until the harp's restrung at port.
- **The real reveal: Aerion is secretly a costume designer for pets**, running a quiet buyer network, tied into the campaign's standing **International Space Dog Show** hook (`Episode - The International Space Dog Show.md`). Storehouse rolls (a d20 price roll, a d20 quantity roll) landed rough this time, 15 gp each, 2 sold, 30 gp total. Orena has clocked this and pretends not to know; play that dynamic close to the vest, it's a good runner for future scenes. This retcons the earlier "young adult novelist" hobby joke, use costume designer going forward.

**The storehouse also picked up 2 common magic items** this leg via the crew's usual "roll for it" process (Orena's pick, one item effectively going to her and one to Flurry). Treat the storehouse's stock as incrementally growing salvage from wrecks the crew has seen, per the table's own framing, not a static list.

**Tack, unprompted:** quietly took hull plate, a torch, and wire from the storehouse while Orena and Aerion worked, off to do maintenance nobody asked for. A small, wordless beat between him and Aerion, the ship's two quietest crew members, that's worth remembering as an established dynamic rather than writing fresh next time.

## The interlude: cookies for Boy Boy McGee

A joke-tier posting off the board (20 gp, "urgent," a two-minute walk) came with a tin of cookies already attached, dropped off by the client alongside the contract itself. The crew took it as a light side errand before departure. **It wasn't one.** Full writeup and how to run it again: `Episode - BoyBoy McGee.md`.

**What happened:** the delivery point was a dead-end alley in the Low Side. Two ruffians brushed past on the way in, an attempted pickpocket both PCs shrugged off (Dex save, 18 each). At the alley: **Boy Boy McGee**, a halfling head fused into an eight-legged mechanical rig with a rocket backpack, backed by three more Burnouts who dropped in from the rooftops on their own thruster rigs. This went hot fast, not a scripted ambush, a genuine fight the table chose to escalate into once weapons came out on both sides.

**The fight:** a real exchange, both PCs took real damage (Orena down to 7 HP at one point off a sneak-attack crit plus a follow-up hit), Aerion opened with a self-buff for advantage and temp HP and then landed two heavy hits (21 and 17 damage) in his first round alone, bloodying Boy Boy immediately. Orena's **Javelin of Lightning** (looted session 1) caught both Boy Boy and a second Burnout in its line for 21 lightning damage each, finishing Boy Boy non-lethally.

**The reveal, via Insight (16) once the fight wound down:** they're not malicious, they're the actual crew of a wrecked ship, the *Bottle Rocket*, trying to earn enough to buy a hull that isn't theirs anymore. Named:
- **Boy Boy McGee**, their captain, reduced to two working legs and his head by the end.
- **Cal ("VertiCal")**, dagger-and-crossbow scout, ran the aerial dive-bomb sneak attack.
- **Gary ("Sugary")**, the one who actually baked the cookies used as bait, self-described sushi/hibachi chef.
- **Ken ("Kraken")**, a lapsed former doctor, ended up fighting as a rigger.

**The table tried to recruit all four onto the crew, and it didn't take.** The offer got made, roles even got loosely assigned in the moment (Gary as sous-chef under Brass, Ken as rigger, Cal as scout, Boy Boy told flatly it was gunner or nothing), but it didn't stick. **What actually resulted: the Burnouts are lifelong nemeses now, not crew.** Boy Boy remembers being beaten and talked down to by name, and the attempted hire lands as one more thing that didn't go his way rather than a fresh start. This matches `Episode - BoyBoy McGee.md`'s own aftermath design (a rival who wants a rematch, not blood) better than a recruitment would have, so run it that way going forward: BoyBoy and his crew are a recurring underworld rivalry, not shipmates. Don't fold them into `npcs/Crew Roster.md`.

**Both PCs leveled to 6** at the end of this fight (Orena: 2nd level Paladin, giving her multiclass spellcaster progression two 3rd-level slots; Aerion: an Ability Score Improvement).

Also: House Vellum's *Heavy Crown*, the Board's own top ship, hasn't left port in three weeks. **These three specific items are now consumed** (`Session 2 Prep.md` §4's Finny gossip table already had close matches at entries 2, 7, and 17, treat those three as already spent, don't re-serve them fresh).

This lands heavier than a general fallout beat, someone got into the captain's own private quarters to leave it. Prop already built: `sessions/vellum-we-know-note.html`. This is the session's actual cliffhanger, replacing whatever `Session 3 Prep.md` currently says about where the note landed.
