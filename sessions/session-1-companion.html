The Resupply Run.

The first voyage. Establish the crew, teach the loop, let both players win something with competence, and end on the image that launches the whole campaign: a star going out. Warm and funny for an hour, then one cold minute at the very end.

Keep it warm. The dread is a single image, and it is the last thing that happens.

How to use this page

Shaded plum blocks are GM-only — don't read them aloud. Blue boxes marked Read aloud are narration you can read or paraphrase. Everything is derived from sessions/Session 1.md and the current canon docs; where a number matters it's pulled from The Last Honest Deal.md, The Bral Reach.md, and Rules & Systems.md.

What this session must do

  • Show the crew as a found family doing a small, warm job together. This is the 70%.
  • Teach the loop: take a contract, provision, fly out, one thing happens, arrive, deliver, get paid, the operating cost bites.
  • Give both PCs a real win. Aerion solves a logistics/ledger problem; Orena reads the sky and finds the port by feel (plus a fight if Gabrielle wants one).
  • Plant three Guttering seeds as mild unease, never alarm.
  • End on the cold open: Orena on watch, a star gutters, the Vocalise.

The shape of the night

Six beats, descending from bright to cold. Roughly: twenty minutes at the Rock casting off, a short first flight, one thing in transit, the arrival where the real weight lands, a throwaway insult that sticks, and then the ending.

The one thing to protect

The cold open is the point of the whole session. Guard time for it — don't let a combat or a haggle run so long that you're rushing the last, quiet minute.

02

Run it like a season premiere

A pilot episode has one extra job beyond "tell a story": it introduces the show. By the time the credits roll the audience should know who these people are, what they want, how they bump against each other, what a normal week looks like, and that something larger is wrong. Session 1 does all of that inside the resupply run — here's the checklist under the plot.

The two PCs — establish the person and plant the want

Aerion Kyoya Windsong · Priscilla

Astral elf samurai; bosun, quartermaster, and cargo handler. The overlooked youngest son of a merchant house he's quietly planning to take from his father one day. Keeps the books, the tabs, and the debts, and secretly dotes on a baby dragon.

Introduce him by

handing him the ledger and the port problem in beat 4. Competence held quietly, a cool head under a money crunch, one flicker of the family wound if the Windsong name comes up. His want — his own house, his own terms — only needs to be visible, not spoken.

Orena · Gabrielle

Water genasi bard/paladin; navigator, helmsman-in-training, scout. The newest aboard (six months). She lost her whole guild, the Wayfinder Knot, to a song called the Stillwater Vocalise, and she alone survived. She is looking for them.

Introduce her by

letting her read the sky where the instruments fail (beat 4), and closing the night on her (the cold open). Her want — the crew she lost — gets its hook on night one when the Vocalise returns under the dying star.

The ensemble — one defining beat each

Don't introduce the crew in a lineup. Give each a single moment that shows who they are, scattered through the beats. Hit these lightly; the players will remember the person, not the exposition.

The two plotlines

A-plot — episodic

The run itself

Take the contract, provision, fly out, deliver, get paid, pay the bills. Self-contained: it starts and finishes tonight and the reward is competence — the triage solved, the port found, the fight won. This is the "normal week" the show will run on, and it's 70% of the session.

B-plot — the arc

Something is wrong with the sky

Under the ordinary job, three quiet symptoms and one personal note. It does not resolve — it's planted. The colony cut off by a failing road, the star that goes out on watch, the Vocalise: the first threads of the Guttering and of Orena's search, laid where the players will only understand them later.

The pilot's promise

End the night and the table should be able to answer: who's the crew, what do the two leads want, what does a job feel like, and what's the mystery? If all four are true, the premiere did its work — and the last of them lands in the final sixty seconds.

03

The job

Their first contract off the Charter Window board: a resupply run to a struggling Verge mining colony. Food, medicine, and parts to people who are proud and nearly out of both. Warm, a little sad, and full of small competence problems.

Hallow's Rest

Verge mining colony · Worlds & Places.md

A mining colony on a tidally locked moon: one face in permanent daylight, one in permanent dark, the town strung along the terminator where it's survivable. Nine hundred people, one dock, one bar, and a mine that's been three years from exhaustion for a decade. Long shadows that never move.

They need everything, all the time, and they pay in ore. (Swap in Seventy-One, or invent one, if you prefer.)

The seed that matters — why they're desperate

The colony's own regular supply route stopped working. Their runner set out for the nearest depot weeks ago and could not find it — the route had drifted, the charts wouldn't resolve, and they turned back late and empty, or limped in on fumes. So the colony isn't just low; they're cut off from the way they normally restock, and they don't understand why.

This is the whole campaign in miniature

Navigation failing has a body count, and these people are living it before anyone has a word for it. Hallow's Rest already re-files its coordinates every eighteen months and has stopped telling visitors why, because the last ships they told didn't come back. Don't explain any of that. Let it sit wrong.

The cargo — and a callback

Six tons of perishables, medicine, and mining parts. Keeping the perishable cargo good on the haul is a small, real problem worth one check. And if you want a running gag: one crate in the manifest is, absurdly, live snails, and Finny will not stop talking about them.

04

The six beats

Read-aloud is a starting point, not a script — paraphrase freely. The checks are suggestions; a good idea from a player beats a good roll. Two of the port beats should have something faintly wrong with them. Never all of them.

1

Casting off from the Rock of Bral

Homecoming-in-reverse · twenty minutes, six quick faces
Read aloud

Lowside Berth 40, on the underside of the Rock, where the gravity is somebody else's problem and the gantry flexes when you walk it. The Last Honest Deal sits fueled and loaded, creaking to herself in the dark. Somewhere above you the city hums. Down here, nobody official ever comes.

Run it as a quick homecoming (see The Bral Reach.md). Hit three or four faces, none longer than a few minutes:

Ottie · harbourmaster's runner, age 12"Heard you're finally shipping out. I know three things you'd want to know. First one's free. The other two are candy."
  • Meech at the Charter Window stamps the contract and corrects their paperwork at length. He is not helpful; he is correct. (A polite player who asks a well-formed question can, later, unlock his delisted-routes breadcrumb — not tonight.)
  • The Float Market for provisioning: Brass haggling with a soft-priced stall, Finny buying something stupid, tethered goods drifting in the low gravity.
Establish the money out loud

The operating cost is 600 gp/month for the seven of them (500 for six, +100 for the seventh), it comes straight out of the account, and they are broke. Let Aerion feel the ledger. This is the genre.

2

First flight

Estra at the helm, Orena shadowing · run the stations once
Read aloud

The berth clamps release and the Rock falls away beneath you, then above you, then wherever it likes. The helm-glow settles. For a moment the silver of the Astral is the only thing in every direction, and the ship is a warm bright bubble of air and lamplight moving through it at a speed the eye can't hold. Then Tack pats a bulkhead and says she's talking, and the moment's over.

Their first time flying the new ship as a system. Estra flies; Orena shadows to learn the helm. A short, low-stakes shakedown — run the crew stations once so everyone knows their post (Rules & Systems.md §8). Tack talks to the ship, Finny lashes the cargo, Oz checks the guns.

Flurry, quietly

Optional and free: as they cross into open sky, Flurry is content — pressed to a viewport, breath-frost on the glass. Nothing is wrong yet. Bank it so the opposite lands later. (See npcs/Flurry.md.)

3

Transit — one event

Pick ONE. Don't overload session one.

Choose a single piece of cosmic content, and add a short fight if you want the action beat here (see The Action Beat). Rotate the wonder each session — the sky is full of strange things, not just whales.

Read aloud — the chwinga (one option)

Something is sitting on the forward rail. It's about a foot tall, made of what looks like polished driftwood and starlight, and it has been there for an unknown length of time watching you work. When you look at it directly, it waves. It does not explain itself. It does not intend to.

  • Wonder options: the chwinga "astronaut" on the rail; a derelict with the table still set and no crew; a mercane vessel passing on business older than the cartel; a shoal of space guppies that adopts the ship; or, used sparingly, a distant kindori pod singing by light (awe, and a pull Orena can't place — save the heavy whale beats for later).
  • If Flurry lit up in beat 2, the kindori option is where she'd do it — the dragon and the navigator both feeling the good sky, one by blood, one by ear.
4

Arrival — the first symptom, with a face

Orena reads the sky · Aerion works the ledger

Getting there is slightly wrong. The run took a day longer than the charts allow, and the colony is a few hours out of position — Orena finds it by feel, not by the numbers. Let her earn that: a navigation read where the instruments disagree with the sky and she trusts the sky.

Read aloud

The charts put Hallow's Rest here. It isn't here. The star's right, the dark moon's right, the daylit face is exactly where it should be — and the town is four hours off from every number you have, sitting perfectly intact on the terminator with its one dock and its one bar and its long unmoving shadows. Orena, you don't find it by the instruments. You find it by feel.

Then the real weight lands. The colonists explain that their own supply runner couldn't reach the depot — the route just wouldn't resolve, and they turned back late and empty. They're not lazy or unlucky. The road stopped working, and they nearly starved for it.

Aerion's win

Give him a real problem to solve with the ledger and a cool head: they can't fully pay (they'd pay in ore, at a bad exchange), or a rival factor is squeezing them, or the triage of who-gets-what-first is a genuine choice. Let him solve it — don't hand him the answer. A warm, grateful, slightly awkward handoff, and pay collected.

Don't say the word

Nobody uses "Guttering." Nobody uses any word for it. Let the crew feel this is bigger than one colony without naming it.

5

"Vagabonds"

A throwaway insult that sticks
Read aloud

Somebody on the dock — another crew, a factor, a bored Warden — watches you finish the unload, takes in the battered hull and the mismatched crew, and says it not quite under their breath: "Estra's vagabonds." Like it's the punchline to a joke everybody already knows.

Let it land and let it stick. It's the crew's name now, and by session's end it should feel less like an insult and more like something they'd put on the hull.

6

The cold open

The point of the whole session · see §07

Night, ship quiet, heading home. Orena alone on watch. This is scripted in full in The Cold Open below — run it slow, then cut to black.

05

The action beat

The premiere's fight: the crew's first combat in their new ship, and every character's first chance to move. Keep it short — one good broadside from the Deal should nearly settle it. It's a confidence-builder and the first real run of the crew stations, not a scare.

Calibrate to the table

Gabrielle enjoys combat more than Priscilla, so give it teeth for her (a gun, the Coordinator chair, the choice to press) while keeping it winnable and quick. If your table is genuinely combat-light this week, you can shrink it to a single tense exchange or a piloting challenge and still get the "we ran the ship as a system" beat — but a premiere really does want its action scene.

Two ways to run it

A hungry scavver — a brown scavver reads the fitted-out hull as a wounded buffet. Pure wildlife, no malice; it breaks off when hurt. Wildspace texture, not a villain.

Damage threshold 20: any single hit under 20 does nothing. Gunnery: with the Sureshot Rotator and Ready Magazine, aiming and loading are both bonus actions, so one gunner fires a ballista in a single action once it's set up.

Crew stations (Rules & Systems.md §8)

  • Pilot — flying is free; spend the free action on one maneuver: Evasive Action, Full Burn, Ram, or Boarding Action.
  • Gunner — crews a ballista (see gunnery note).
  • Engineer — Emergency Patch: DC 12 check, regain HP = level, once per encounter.
  • Coordinator — grant advantage, impose disadvantage, or let a station act again. One benefit per round.
Calibration — let them choose

The threat should lose. When it breaks or begs off, hand the table a real decision: press the advantage (a prize, a body count, a loose end) or let it run. Neither is wrong, and it's your first read on what kind of captain-by-committee this crew is going to be.

06

The money is real

Competence under constraint is the substance of the genre. Do the math out loud at the dock and let the thinness bite.

LineAmountNote
Contract payout+1,400 gpSome or all possibly paid in ore, at a bad rate — Aerion's call
Operating cost−600 gpThe month, seven crew, all-inclusive (wages, food, air, upkeep)
Hull repairs−20 gp / HPOnly if the optional fight cost them hull
Berth 40 rent−30 gpCheap because the last three tenants left owing money

What that leaves

Not much, and that's correct. A clean run nets a few hundred gold over cost. One bad broadside and the repair bill eats the profit. They have a ~14,350 gp cushion in the account and Aerion's 10,000 gp trust-fund reserve he'd rather die than touch — runway measured in months, not years.

Deduct it at the dock

Make it a beat, not bookkeeping. The operating cost comes off the top when they get home; repairs are counted in hull HP at 20 gp each. Let Aerion be the one who says the number aloud, and let the number be a little worse than anyone hoped.

The Manifest Board

Completing the run posts them near the very bottom of the Board (~348th of 351). Finny prints it and throws a party. The three ships below them are a derelict, an impounded smuggler, and a vessel that's been on fire for six months.

07

The cold open

This is why the session exists. Everything before it is the crew being the crew; this is the one cold minute. Slow down. Give Gabrielle quiet and your full attention, and do not explain any of it.

Read aloud — slowly

It's late. The ship is asleep around you — Tack's engine ticking as it cools, someone snoring two bulkheads down, the hull murmuring the way it does. You're on watch, alone at the chart table, updating the log by lamplight, heading home.

You look up to check your bearing against the stars you steer by. And one of them is wrong. Off by a hair. Not where it was an hour ago, not where the chart says.

You check the instruments. Twice. The instruments are fine. The sky is wrong.

And while you're looking at it — while you're looking right at it — the star dims. And goes out. No flare, no sound. It's just gone, and the dark where it was is seamless, as though it was never there.

Except — if you hold very still — there's the faint edge of a note. A single held tone at the edge of hearing. You've heard it once before. You heard it the night you lost your crew.

Running it

Let the silence be long. Don't cut to another character, don't offer a check, don't answer the obvious question. If Gabrielle wants Orena to wake someone, she can — but Orena keeping it to herself for now is the stronger, truer choice, and the table should feel that. When it's landed: "That's where we'll stop." Cut to black. End of session.

Optional — Flurry knew

If you want one more chill: earlier that evening, Flurry wouldn't settle — wouldn't eat, hid in Aerion's coat, and nobody thought anything of it. Only in hindsight, next session, does anyone connect it. Don't point at it now.

Running reminders

  • Keep it warm and funny. The dread is one image at the very end.
  • Competence is the reward. Let them solve the triage, the port, the fight. Wins should feel earned.
  • Both PCs lead once. Aerion runs port and logistics; Orena runs navigation (and combat, if Gabrielle wants it). Estra defers and only steps in to teach.
  • Do not explain the ending. Let the table sit with it.

Where it goes next

Session 2+ is more of the voyage loop with the pattern thickening: more dark routes, more (rare) whales, and eventually Meech's "three frontier routes delisted with no incident reports" if a player is polite to him. Tier 1's job is to accumulate symptoms into a pattern the crew notices — and to let Orena's private thread start pulling them toward the edge.
