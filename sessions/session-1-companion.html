The Resupply Run.

The first voyage, run like a season premiere: introduce the crew, show the Rock of Bral for the first time, let the players choose a contract off the board, teach the loop, and end on the image that launches the whole campaign, a star going out. Warm and funny for an hour, then one cold minute.

Keep it warm. The dread is a single image, and it is the last thing that happens.

The one rule that outranks this page

Never decide for the players. Orena and Aerion are Gabrielle's and Priscilla's. This page gives you situations, options, and spotlight opportunities to offer them, never what they buy, say, feel, or choose. Where you see a violet box, it's a reminder that the next move is a player's, not yours.

How to use this page

Plum blocks are GM-only. Blue Read aloud boxes are narration. Numbers come from the canon docs. Printable props for the table are linked in §05. Narration follows rules/DMing Craft.md.

00

Cold open: the boot camp

Open the premiere before the title card with a playable flashback: the day Orena and Aerion met, years ago, at Vaelren Windsong's training camp. It plants the friendship the whole crew hangs on, gives both players a real scene with dice and choices, and introduces Vaelren for later. Aim for ten to fifteen minutes. They play their younger selves on their current sheets (it's a flashback, not a balance puzzle; everything here is easy on purpose).

Read aloud: years ago, a long way from here

A training drift under a borrowed sun. Bare rock, chalk lines, racks of padded practice blades, and the smell of sweat and cheap lamp oil. Twenty-odd students from twenty-odd worlds, and a lean, grey-templed swordsman named Vaelren Windsong who runs the place the way weather runs a coast, patient and without apology. This morning he reads two names off his list and points them at the same starting line. One of you is the overlooked youngest son of a merchant house, sent here because the house barely noticed you were gone. The other came a very long way, off the Sword Coast, and is the only student who came for the sea and not the sword. He looks the two of you over, and something in his face decides it's going to enjoy this.

Vaelren Windsong

the uncle, the teacher

Aerion's uncle, a "space samurai" who left the ledgers for a wandering blade. Warm, exacting, a little dangerous, and the reason Aerion carries a code at all.

Voice
Unhurried and amused. Answers a question with a better one.
Creed
"A person is worth what they can earn with their own two hands and their own held word."
Want
To see what these two become. He already suspects, and he pairs them on purpose.
The one rule, again: they play, you don't

This is the two leads' scene. Set the yard, play Vaelren and everyone else, run the dice, and hand every beat to the players. Never say what Orena or Aerion feels, wants, or decides; offer the moment and ask. The whole point is that they discover the first thread of this friendship, so the wins and the words are theirs.

1

The Wall (a paired obstacle)

Athletics & Acrobatics · teamwork is the lesson
Read aloud

Vaelren's obstacle run ends at a smooth stone wall, ten feet of it, no handholds, slick with the morning's damp. There is no ladder and no way around that he'll respect. He blows a whistle, starts a sand-timer, and says one thing before he walks off to watch: "The run's built for two. Figure out why."

The wall can't be soloed, and that's the point: it wants one of them thrown or boosted up, then that one hauling the other after. Let the players invent the plan and reward any version that needs both of them.

If they throw / boost (the classic)

  • Aerion launches Orena: Strength (Athletics) DC 12 for a clean, high throw.
  • Orena turns it into a catch at the lip: Dexterity (Acrobatics) DC 12.
  • Both succeed and she's up. One of two succeeds and she catches the edge and hangs there (laughing or swearing, the player's call) for a free second try.
  • Then Orena hauls Aerion up (Athletics DC 10) or drops a line for him to climb; working together grants advantage.

Running it

  • Other plans are good plans. Shoulders-and-jump, a thrown rope, a run-and-vault off a braced hand: anything that takes both of them earns the wall.
  • Failure is never punishing. A slip, a graze, a dry line from Vaelren, reset the timer, go again. Nobody is hurt and the clock never actually matters.
  • Going around is possible, and it is exactly the thing Vaelren will note, mildly, that they chose not to trust each other. Let that sit; don't lecture.
The beat under the beat

This is the physical birth of the trust the whole campaign rests on: the first time one of them literally put themselves in the other's hands. Do not narrate that for them. Let the plan they chose and the dice they rolled say it, and ask each player how it felt to be the thrower or the thrown.

2

The Bout (light combat, same side)

Padded weapons, nonlethal, fast and funny

Over the wall, Vaelren pairs them against another duo and calls a bout: first pair knocked down loses. Padded blades, nonlethal bludgeoning, everyone on the mat has done this a hundred times. Now the two of them are a side, and covering for each other is the whole game.

Read aloud: the rival pair

Two older students stroll up twirling their padded blades, already grinning like the outcome's decided. "The runt and the tourist," one of them says, loud enough. Vaelren drops a cloth. "Begin."

Offer the team move, don't script it

Somewhere in here, give each player the chance to cover the other: a shouted warning, a blade knocked aside, a boost into a leaping strike. Whether they take it, and how, is the chemistry, and it's theirs. If they'd rather show off solo, that's a character choice too.

Swap: a reason that isn't a rival

Prefer a common enemy to cocky teens? A stray astral critter blunders into the yard mid-drill (a startled, harmless thing the size of a dog, or a training construct that won't power down), and the paired students have to corral it together. Same weak stats, same nonlethal stakes, no bruised egos, and the "same side" is instant.

3

The Bunk (the chemistry beat)

Roleplay · the point of the whole cold open
Read aloud

Lights-out. You've been assigned the same bunk, top and bottom, and neither of you is tired. Somewhere down the row someone snores. Vaelren's lamp is still on in his quarters; you can hear him not-sleeping, the way teachers do. The dark is full of other people's breathing and the small metal sounds of a drift settling for the night.

Now hand it over and let it run. These are prompts to offer, never answers to give. Play Vaelren once from the doorway if the scene needs a spark, then get out of the way.

Conversation seeds (offer what the table bites on)

  • "Why did you really come here? Not the reason you gave Vaelren."
  • "What was the first thing you noticed about the other one today?"
  • "What do you want that you'd never say out loud in daylight?"
  • "What's the thing about home you're not telling anyone?"
Character choices, theirs entirely

Two gifts you can put in front of the players, to take or keep: does Aerion hint, even now, at the thing he's quietly saving toward? Does Orena talk about the guild waiting back home, still whole, the Wayfinder Knot she has not yet lost? What they reveal and what they hold back is the scene. Vaelren's creed hangs over all of it; let it land differently on each of them, however they choose to take it.

Vaelren's button

To close the flashback, Vaelren appears in the doorway without knocking, unbothered that it's the middle of the night: "Good. You found the wall's trick faster than most. And Windsong, stop counting the doors. She's not going anywhere." A beat. "Are you?" Then he's gone, and so is the light. Hold one second of dark, and cut.

Read aloud: bridge to now (the crew, months in)

Cut hard, and years forward. The same two, older, on a ship that runs like a body that has forgotten it was ever strangers. Brass is swearing at a pan at three in the morning. Tack is telling a bulkhead it's doing beautifully. Finny is halfway through a story nobody asked for. Oz is mending something that isn't his. Estra's light is on and her cards are out. A small silver dragon is asleep in the warm of the helm-glow. Orena has been aboard six months and it already feels like six years. And ahead, through the forward port, coming up gold against the black, is the Rock of Bral.

Why open here (the premiere shape)

A pilot has to do two things at once: introduce the show, and imply a history so the show feels lived-in. The boot camp gives you the deep past (these two chose each other long before the ship did), and the bridge gives you the recent past (a crew that has clearly been running for months). Play both warm and keep the whole cold open to about fifteen minutes, then roll the title and cut to the Rock. If a table would rather start in the present, run just the bunk beat and the bridge; the chemistry is the part you don't skip.

What tonight has to do

  • Open the show. The players meet the crew, meet the two leads, and get a feel for a normal working week. Underneath it, something is wrong with the sky.
  • Hand them the work. They pick a job off the Charter Window board. Then they run the loop once: provision, fly out, one thing happens, arrive, deliver, get paid, the bills bite.
  • Open a door for each lead, equally. Aerion gets the contract call, the delivery negotiation, a family beat, and a ledger that won't reconcile. Orena gets the helm, the sky that won't match the charts, and the watch. Whether they walk through is the players' call.
  • Plant three Guttering seeds, said small. Mild unease, never alarm. (Listed below.)
  • Land the ending. Two quiet scenes, Aerion with a dragon that won't settle and Orena alone on watch, and then the star.

Order of play

  1. Cold open: the boot camp. A playable flashback: the wall, a light bout, the bunk. How Orena and Aerion met. (§00) ~15m
  2. The Rock of Bral. First sighting, a few faces, the market open. (§04) ~40m
  3. The board. They read the postings and choose. (§05) ~15m
  4. Cast off and fly out. Run the stations, one wonder, then the action set-piece: a skiff, a scavenger, and one glimpse of something worse. (§06, §08) ~50m
  5. Arrival at Hallow's Rest. The first symptom, with a face on it. (§07) ~30m
  6. Pay, "vagabonds," and turn for home. (§07, §09) ~15m
  7. The cold open, in two scenes. (§10) ~15m
Protect the ending

The last fifteen minutes are the point of the session. If the market or the fight is eating the clock, cut it short. Do not arrive at the star out of breath.

The three seeds (drop them, don't sell them)

  1. The road ran long. The leg took a day more than the charts allow. Orena notices; nobody panics.
  2. The port has moved. Hallow's Rest sits a few hours off its charted spot. She finds it by feel.
  3. Someone nearly died of it. The colony's own runner set out for the depot and could not find the road. Came back empty. This is the one that matters. Say it plain, then move on.

Aerion's paper face of the same seeds: the filings swear these roads are current. His delivery proves they aren't. Hand him the clerk's-eye view.

Keep in your pocket

  • Both leads are players', not yours. Set situations in front of Orena and Aerion. Never write what they buy, say, or decide.
  • The ship has no name. It's "Estra's ship" on the docks. Play the gap; don't fill it.
  • The action beat is pre-rolled in §08 (a three-move set-piece) so it stays fast and fun.
  • Two dark beats, both brief. The void scavver glimpse on the way out (Estra's fear), and the star on the way home. Keep everything between them warm.
  • Don't explain the endings. Neither the thing that scared Estra nor the Vocalise gets a word tonight. Let the table sit in it.
02

Run it like a season premiere

A pilot introduces the show. By the end, the table should be able to answer four things: who is this crew, what do the two leads want, what does a job feel like, and what's the mystery? The resupply run carries all four, it leaves every choice in the players' hands, and it gives the two leads equal weight.

The two leads: what to offer, never what they do

Aerion Kyoya Windsong · Priscilla

Astral elf samurai; bosun, quartermaster, cargo handler. The overlooked youngest son of the Windsong trading house, quietly saving to buy his father's company out from under him. He runs the crew's day-to-day, keeps every book and tab and debt, and secretly dotes on a baby dragon.

His plot, and his spotlight

The mystery has a paper face, and it's his. Where Orena reads the sky going wrong, Aerion reads the books going wrong: a route the filings swear is current that his own delivery proves is a day and a half long, a colony quietly re-filing its own coordinates. Offer him the ledger that won't reconcile. On top of that: the contract choice is his (Estra hands it over, §05), the delivery is his negotiation (§07), and his family is close tonight (the Underwriters' Hall is his mother's house, §04). His want, his own house on his own terms, is Priscilla's to reveal at her pace.

Orena · Gabrielle

Water genasi bard/paladin; navigator, helmsman-in-training, scout. Newest aboard (six months). She lost her whole guild, the Wayfinder Knot, to a song called the Stillwater Vocalise, and she alone survived. She is looking for them.

Her plot, and her spotlight

The mystery has a sky face, and it's hers. A leg that runs long, a port out of position, and at the end a star that goes out and the edge of a song she has heard exactly once. Offer her the helm to shadow or take, the arrival read, and the final watch. How Orena responds, and what she does with the Vocalise when it returns, is Gabrielle's entirely.

The A-plot, and the two-faced B-plot

A-plot, episodic

The job they chose

Take a contract off the board, provision, fly, deliver, get paid, pay the bills. It starts and finishes tonight, and the reward is competence. This is the "normal week" the show runs on, roughly 70% of the session.

B-plot, the arc

Something is wrong, and both leads feel it

Under the ordinary job, the same wrongness shows two faces. Orena reads it in the sky. Aerion reads it in the books: filings that don't match the road, a colony re-filing its coordinates, numbers that swear a dead route is fine. Nothing resolves tonight; it's all planted. Give the sky beats to Gabrielle and the paper beats to Priscilla, and the mystery belongs to them equally. The arc lands whatever contract they pick (§05).

Balancing the two leads (a self-check while you run)

It is easy to let a navigator's session become the navigator's session. Guard against it. The rough split tonight: Orena owns casting-off and the helm, the arrival read, and the final watch. Aerion owns the contract call, the delivery negotiation, the family thread, and the ledger that won't reconcile. They share the wall, the bout, and the fight. If one player has been quiet for fifteen minutes, that is your cue to hand their lead the next beat.

Two things a premiere needs, and this session almost forgot

Give every crewmate one moment

Tick each off across the night so the found family actually lands:

  • Estra: hands Aerion the contract, and breaks once (the Board, or the void scavver).
  • Tack: the creaking argument with Aerion, and "hold together, Deborah" under fire.
  • Brass: the morning dinner roll, and a "guy" she offers at the port.
  • Finny: reads the Board aloud and throws a party at 348th; drops one piece of gossip.
  • Oz: defuses a tense dock moment, and stays polite through the fight.
  • Flurry: content in flight, and refuses to settle at the end.

Roleplay prompts for the leads

Drop one when a scene has room. They invite character, never answers:

  • Aerion: "Your mother's house is one floor up. Do you go in? What would it cost you?" and "The numbers don't lie, and they don't add up. Who do you tell?"
  • Orena: "You've flown this stretch before. When did it start feeling wrong?" and "The helm is yours if you want it. Do you take it, or shadow Estra one more run?"
  • Both: "You've known each other longer than anyone aboard. What does this crew still not know about you two?"
The ship has no name yet, so use it

Canon: the ship is deliberately unnamed. In the fiction it's "Estra's ship," or "the Vane boat" on the docks. Lean into it. Finny keeps floating names nobody approves, and naming her is a live thread for a later session. If a player offers a name they love, bank it; don't impose one.

The crew, up close

The five NPC crew plus Flurry, all the DM's to play. Give each a single defining moment tonight: a voice, a tic, and a want is enough to make them a person. (The two PCs aren't here: they're the players'.) Running gags in npcs/Crew Comedy Bits.md.

How the crew see each other

These five are the ones who stayed when the old ship blew up and the rest of the fourteen walked. That's the emotional core: they chose to be broke together. Estra parents through structure and trust, never affection. She eats every meal with them, remembers things nobody told her, and keeps one rule above the rest: no one sleeps in the helm room alone after a bad job. Finny is the heart and the leak, Brass is the fixer as much as the medic, Oz's manners run exactly opposite to his size, and Tack is slowly convincing everyone the ship talks back. Seven people wearing fourteen hats, pretending it's fine.

The two leads, together

Orena and Aerion aren't strangers thrown aboard. They trained together as teenagers under Aerion's uncle, the "space samurai" Vaelren Windsong, got made to share a bunk, and came out lifelong friends. Years later, when Aerion's ship came apart and he needed a navigator he trusted, he found her and brought her on. Their friendship predates the crew, and it's the beam the whole found family is nailed to. Play it in the small moments: the shorthand, the old argument reheated, the thing only one of them knows about the other.

Off-duty aboard (what they do on a quiet watch)

The players wanted to see the crew's downtime. Deal one when a transit is slow or a PC wanders in.

  • Estra: solitaire alone at night, and a private journal in a locked book that nobody has read.
  • Tack: polishing and talking to the ship, and the maintenance log that's secretly a diary.
  • Brass: cooking for real at 0300 and trialing new recipes; reading old medical monographs to earn the license she never got.
  • Finny: working a sending stone to trade gossip and small deals across ports; hosting game night; studying the Board.
  • Oz: mending shared gear, writing thank-you notes, whittling little figures, brewing tea for whoever has the watch.
  • Flurry: hoards small shiny things, "helps" Tack with tiny parts, naps in the helm-glow, and has adopted Oz (rides his shoulder) and Orena (curls up when she plays or hums).
  • Orena & Aerion (offer, don't script): her charts, the viewport, an instrument played soft; his ledger, his reading, and (per Finny) chapters read aloud to Flurry. What's true is the players'.
04

Arriving at the Rock of Bral

The crew lives here, but the campaign hasn't shown it yet, so give it a real arrival. Then hit a few of the six faces of their Bral (Sprat, the Charter Window, the Delverane window, the Compass Rose, the Float Market, the Underwriters' Hall), and let the market be open. Each face keeps a secret (locations/The Bral Reach.md); tonight let only one or two carry a faint wrongness and play the rest warm.

Read aloud: first sighting

You see it long before you arrive: a whole mountain of a city burning gold against the black. A mile of grey rock crusted over with spires and gantries and ten thousand lit windows, stacked wherever they could be made to cling, and around it a slow endless storm of ships coming and going.

As you slip into the lower berths the sound reaches you first through the opening lock, then the smell: smoke and hot metal, fish and spice, ten thousand people and their dinners. A bell rings somewhere. A hawker calls your ship the wrong name. You're home, for whatever that's worth.

Three textures, one per scene (don't front-load)

Gravity is a local argument (a dropped coin falls sideways; locals never notice). Everyone's from somewhere else (giff dockers, dohwar hawkers, a mercane the crowd parts for). It remembers being lawless (an untended shrine to the pirate Captain Bral; marble up top, rope down below). Full detail: locations/The Bral Reach.md.

Their Bral, in brief

A mile-wide asteroid under a single city, founded about two hundred years ago by the pirate Captain Bral as a bolt-hole and civilized since by merchants who wanted somewhere stable to do business. One gravity plane runs the long axis, so the underside is "down-is-up" and newcomers walk into walls. The upper city is marble; the lower city is rope. One quiet fact worth knowing and never saying: the Pale Reach incorporated within a generation of Bral's founding, so there are people here whose great-grandparents watched both the port and the cartel be born. Bral is enormous and indifferent. Their Bral is six places and a handful of faces. Reference art.

What they can do here

  • Work the window: pick a contract off the Board and get it stamped (Meech), and sell the last voyage's survey data (Senna, above rate for anything off the Verge).
  • Provision: the Float Market for void kit, medicine, rope, and paperwork. One uncommon item is always for sale.
  • Listen: the Compass Rose. Every rumour in the Reach passes through within four days.
  • Money: renew the hull's insurance at Nightbreeze, then payroll and repairs come off the account.
  • Downtime the crew runs on their own: the galley game night (cards and dice), the terrible shore-leave portrait, Finny working his sending stone and making his quiet deposit to someone he won't name, Brass vanishing to "see a guy," Oz befriending the meanest person in a rough bar.

The faces to hit

Sprat, the runner · Berth 40

Twelve years old, knows everything, sells information for candy and honours the deal. First face of every homecoming.

Meech, the Charter Window · the CIV

Deputy Registrar Halvar Meech. Half-orc, forty years behind the grille, immune to charm and bribery. He isn't corrupt and isn't helpful. He's correct. Stamps the chosen contract and explains which form they should have used.

Senna Orrick, the Delverane window · two levels up

Human, early thirties, sharp and tired and on a quota. Buys survey data and has opinions about which ships bring it. Professionally condescending to a 348th-ranked crew exactly once, then she reads what they filed and stops. The Compass Rose · the bar

Not rough, not nice: where crews drink rather than tourists. Run by Madam Ulve, plasmoid, who greets Estra by name on the first visit and never explains how they know each other. Bex Halloway of the Grendel's Wake drinks here, so does Dov Errin of the impounded Steadfast Promise (who will tell you all about it), and so do off-shift Route Wardens, which is inconvenient.

Meech's breadcrumb (only if a player earns it)

If someone asks Meech a genuinely precise question, he'll produce three frontier routes delisted last year with the incident-reason field left blank. To him that's a procedural obscenity, not a mystery. The Sable Crossing (toward Amberwake; filed by a Pale Reach clerk, no reason, no successor route). The back table (gambling, full rules)

There's always a game going, stakes small and mean (1 to 10 gp a hand fits their money). Both PCs like a game, so roll it for real at the table. Best played for information: a side bet is how Cathis, Errin, or Bex say things they'd never sell.

Scavver (nerve). Everyone antes. On your turn roll a d6 at a time, adding to your total, and stop whenever you like. Over 20 and the scavver gets you: bust, out of the hand. Highest total 20 or under takes the pot; ties split. A house dealer (Big Pol) plays last and holds on 16+.

Liar's Berth (bluff, 3+ players). Each player secretly rolls 3d6 under a cup. On your turn, claim how many of a face are on the whole table (e.g. "five threes"); each claim must raise the last (higher count, or same count of a higher face); ones are wild. Or call "Berth" to challenge: reveal all dice. If the claim was true, the caller loses a die; if false, the claimer loses a die. Lose all three and you're out; last player holding dice wins.

Cheating: Dexterity (Sleight of Hand) vs the sharpest opponent's passive Perception shifts one die or one count. Getting caught at the Rose costs a favour with Ulve; at a rough table it starts the brawl.

The voyage out

1

Casting off & first flight

Run the crew stations once
Read aloud

The clamps release and the Rock falls away beneath you, then above you, then wherever it likes. The helm-glow settles. For a moment the silver of the Astral is the only thing in every direction, and the ship is a warm bright bubble of air and lamplight moving through it faster than the eye can hold. Then Tack pats a bulkhead, tells Deborah she's doing beautifully, and the moment's over.

Their first time flying her as a system. Run the crew stations once here so everyone knows their post: who pilots, who's on Gertrude and Susan, who watches, who coordinates from the deck. The assignments are the crew's and the players' to sort; Estra teaches, she doesn't dictate. The full rules live right here so you never have to look them up.

Crew stations (the rules)

  • Pilot. Flying is free (not an action). Each round spend the free action on one maneuver: Evasive Action (attacks vs the ship have disadvantage, but the ship's own guns do too this round), Full Burn (speed doubles to close or run, but attacks vs the ship have advantage), or Boarding Action (contested Piloting to lock ships together). Piloting takes concentration, so the helm can't also hold a spell. One maneuver per round.
  • Gunner. Crews a ballista. Normally 3 crew and 3 actions to fire; with this ship's Sureshot Rotator and Ready Magazine, one gunner fires it in a single action. Ballista: +6, 16 (3d10).
  • Engineer. Emergency Patch: Technology or Intelligence check DC 12; on a success the hull regains HP equal to the engineer's level, once per encounter. Temporary; it doesn't reduce the repair bill.
  • Coordinator. One benefit per round: grant advantage on an ally's roll, impose disadvantage on an enemy, or let one station act again out of turn.

2

Transit: the wonder (play this one in full)

One real scene, not a die roll

Rather than roll a random encounter for the premiere, run one wonder properly and let it breathe. The default is a chwinga astronaut, fishing from the back of a space guppy, because it's warm, funny, interactive, and asks nothing of the plot. Combat is a separate beat, in §08; a menu of swaps is at the bottom.

Read aloud: something is fishing off the bow

Two days out, on a quiet watch, a single fat space guppy drifts up alongside the hull, unhurried, finning at nothing. Riding on its back, astride it like a pony, is a figure about a foot tall in a bulbous handmade spacesuit of driftwood and glass and starlight. It is holding a little rod, and it has cast a line and a hook out into the empty black, and it is fishing. It has clearly been at this a while. When it notices you noticing, it lifts one small gloved hand and waves, then goes right back to watching its line.

It's a chwinga astronaut (a tiny nature-spirit of the Astral that has kitted itself out for the void, reference art), harmless and never a fight, mounted on a tame space guppy. It has no Common and no interest in explaining itself; it speaks in small images pressed gently into your head, a warmth, a picture, a feeling. It will pace the ship for a day or two, and fish the whole time.

What it does (offer these; let the players choose how to meet it)

  • It's fishing, and it'll show you for what. Ask, and it presses an image into your head of the catch it's after: a star that hasn't been born yet, a half-heard song, a fish made of light. It never reels anything in that you can see, and it doesn't seem to mind.
  • It'll share the rod. If a crewmember sits down and fishes alongside it, that's the whole scene. Let them "catch" something small and strange after a while (no roll needed; just describe the gift the void hands up).
  • The guppy is friendly. It noses the hull, follows the ship, and lets Flurry chase it in slow delighted circles. Flurry is fascinated and perfectly calm, which quietly tells the table the sky here is fine.
  • It likes warm and shining bait, not food: offer it a Warmstone, a coin, one of Flurry's shed scales, and it will thread the treasure onto its hook and cast it joyfully into the dark, as if that were the entire point.
  • It can give a gift. Before it and the guppy peel away, it presses a small blessing into the hand of anyone who was kind to it (a charm: advantage on one save, a heading that feels true for a day, a token that stays warm). Offer the chance; whether a PC earns it or wants it is theirs.
How it leaves, and the whisper under it

At some quiet moment the guppy turns, the chwinga reels in its empty line, tips an imaginary hat, and the two of them fin off into the black. Play it warm; this is the 20% wonder, not a plot beat. The faint seed (optional, deniable): once, the chwinga stops fishing, looks a long moment along the leg ahead, and pointedly will not cast its line that way. Flurry looks the same direction. The water in front of the ship is thinning, and the little astronaut won't fish a stretch that's going quiet. Don't explain it. File it beside the cold open.

  • Swaps, if you'd rather (rotate weekly): a derelict with the table still set and no crew; a mercane vessel on business older than the cartel; a colour that isn't on the spectrum drifting past, incurious; or, sparingly, a distant kindori pod singing by light. Full menu: rules/Tables.md §1.

Arrival: Hallow's Rest

Where the weight lands. A terminator mining colony on a tidally locked moon, and the first Guttering symptom with a human face. (If they took a different contract, swap the destination and keep the symptom.) Full detail: Worlds & Places.md.

Getting there is slightly wrong. The run takes a day longer than the charts allow, and the colony sits a few hours off its charted spot. The instruments disagree with the sky, and that's a navigation read on the table. What the navigator makes of it is the player's.

Read aloud: making planetfall

You come in over the daylit face and it never gets easier to look at: a whole hemisphere of raw white glare, no dawn, no dusk, just on. The town runs along the seam where the light dies, a crooked line of low buildings, ore-tips and lashed-down roofs following the terminator around the moon, half of every street in permanent gold and half in permanent black, and shadows that lie down long and never, ever move. The dock is a single scaffold clung to a spur of rock. As the lock opens: cold, and the smell of turned stone and machine-oil and, faintly, someone's dinner. A dozen people are already waiting, trying not to look as glad to see you as they are.

Administrator Sesla Vint

Runs the colony. Proud to a fault, would rather ration than beg. Thanks you like it costs her; keeps a tally-book she never quite closes. Her want: keep nine hundred people alive without becoming a place that gets pitied.

Coll Aday

The young runner who went for the depot and couldn't find it. Came back late, empty, on fumes, and blames themselves. Won't meet your eye. "The route just wasn't there. I flew the numbers three times." The seed's human face.

Big Pol

Runs the Long Shadow, the warmest room in town. Feeds anyone who's short, knows every rumour on the terminator. Comic warmth to balance the ache.

Situations to set down (the players decide how to meet them)

Payment: the colony can't fully pay in coin and will offer part in refined ore at a poor rate. That's a negotiation, not a foregone loss. Triage: more is needed than was ordered (medicine or mine-parts, the sick or the shaft); Vint will live with the crew's call. The offer: Vint carefully asks if they'd be the colony's regular runner, badly paid and reliable. Yes is a recurring hook. No opens a thread instead: swallowing her pride, Vint offers an extra 100 gp if the crew will at least help her find someone dependable, put the word out at Bral, keep an ear open for a runner who won't vanish. A decline becomes a small paid errand, and they end up part of the colony's lifeline anyway. Put these on the table; let the players choose.

Aerion's set-piece: the payment, with numbers

Give the ore negotiation real teeth so it plays as a scene, not a line, and make it Aerion's the way the arrival read is Orena's. The colony owes 1,400 gp, holds maybe 600 in coin, and offers the rest in refined ore.

  • The rate is the fight. The Verge rate on their ore is poor, roughly 60% of Bral value. Aerion can push it with Charisma (Persuasion) or a hard-eyed Intelligence read against Vint's pride: DC 13 for a fair rate, DC 16 to get it and leave her dignity intact. Insight tells him she'll ration herself thin before she'll be pitied, so the win is a deal she can call fair, not charity.
  • The triage is the ethics. More is needed than was ordered, and medicine for the sick and parts for the failing mine can't both land first. No wrong answer, just a call the crew has to own; Vint lives with whatever they decide.
  • The paper is the seed (his half of the arc). Reconciling the job, Aerion finds the colony's own filings list their supply route as "current" and on-position. His delivery just proved it is neither. The books say the road is fine; the road is not fine. He is the second person aboard to catch the sky lying, and the only one holding the proof in writing. Don't resolve it; let it bother him.
The one that matters (don't say the word)

Somewhere in the delivery, Coll's account lands: the depot route that wasn't there. Play it as a person's failure and grief, and not as a clue. Nobody uses "Guttering," or any word for it. This is the first symptom with a body count.

More at Hallow's Rest

Things to do (it isn't only the delivery)

  • The Long Shadow, after dark. Big Pol's bar is the warmest room on the moon, and the whole town funnels through it. A dice game runs most nights (Scavver and Liar's Berth, full rules back in §04), and it's where the crew hears what the colony will never say to Vint's face.
  • The dark-side prospect. An old prospector, Meggs, swears the richest seam runs out under the permanent-night face, past where the heat gives out, and the last crew to check came back one short and won't say what turned them around. A ready side-hook; pays in ore and goodwill.
  • The standing shadows. Terminator kids tell it: the shadows never move because they're waiting, and if yours ever moves on its own, the moon has decided to keep you. A ghost story, close enough to the real eeriness to leave unexplained.
  • The mine. Three years from exhaustion for a decade. The colony's whole economy hangs on a seam everyone knows is nearly done. If the crew wants to change this place instead of just supplying it, the mine is the lever.

The senses (drop one per scene)

  • One light, no in-between. Bright-side shades, dark-side lamps, the whole town squints, and the unmoving shadows unsettle newcomers within the hour.
  • Cold and close. Heat is rationed to the terminator strip; twenty feet dark-side and it bites.
  • Everything is patched. Nothing here is new. Every machine was broken and fixed by someone who couldn't afford to replace it, which is the whole colony, really.
If they didn't take the Cold Run (portable arrivals)

The spine holds whatever they picked: a leg that runs long, a port a little out of position, a Guttering seed, and the cold open on the way home. Swap the destination and the human face.

  • Sealed and Sworn (a Sallow Chain port): the weight lands at the halfway Warden checkpoint. The off-shift Warden gripes that three lanes "won't resolve" and blames the crew's charts first. The sealed case is Aerion's nerve scene; whether they peek is theirs.
  • The Rock and Back (a Chain waystation): no tragedy here, so the seed is lighter. Even the milk run runs a day long, and a hauler mentions its sister depot went quiet. Spend the saved time on the crew (the portrait, handball, dinner).
  • Passenger Work (the Windward Edge): the "arrival" is really the passenger. Aurel Delverane asks Orena about the currents and takes "the sky is off" seriously, and her instruments read the same wrongness. The seed arrives as an ally who already half-believes.

Then run the action beat (§08) en route and land the cold open (§10) on the way home.

"Vagabonds," then home

Read aloud

Somebody on the dock, another crew or a factor or a bored Warden, watches the unload, takes in the battered unnamed hull and the mismatched crew, and says it not quite under their breath: "Estra's vagabonds." Like it's the punchline to a joke everybody already knows.

08

The action beat, with pre-rolled rounds

The premiere's action beat is one set-piece in three moves: a boarding scrap with a bottom-of-the-Board rival, a scavenger drawn to the noise, and then one glimpse of something that empties the sky. It's tuned to cost the hull nothing (the lesson is "we can take a hit"), the enemy dice are pre-rolled so you can stay in the fiction, and it ends on the campaign's keystone seen once and fled, not fought.

Calibrate to the table

Gabrielle enjoys combat more than Priscilla does, so give it teeth for her (a gun, the helm, the Coordinator chair) while keeping it quick. Combat-light week? Shrink the scrap to one exchange and go straight to the scavver and the glimpse. Whether the crew fights, boards, or talks their way clear is, as ever, the players'.

Move 2: something answers the noise (brown scavver, round 3)

A brown scavver (a shark-bodied Wildspace scavenger, reference art) arrives out of the dark, drawn to the damage and the exposed crew. Move 3: the thing the scavver was running from

Here's the turn. The brown scavver doesn't just flee because it's hurt. It flees because something worse is here, and for one moment the crew sees it too.

Read aloud: out past the fight

The scavver breaks and runs, and it isn't running from you. Out past the drifting skiff, a long way off and not nearly far enough, something moves. Bigger than a ship. Pitch black, so black it reads as a hole cut in the stars, and it slides through the dark without a sound and without hurry. The little scavver is fleeing it the way a fish flees a shark. Wherever that shape is pointed, the sky around it feels emptied, like a held breath. It has not noticed you. You very much want to keep it that way.

Estra breaks, and takes the helm

This is the reveal of the whole session, and it isn't a monster stat block, it's Estra's face. The captain who is bored by hull breaches goes white. Whatever that thing is, she has seen one before, and it is the only thing in the sky that frightens her. Play it straight and quiet: she crosses to the helm and takes it out of Orena's hands without a word, or gives the order to run in a voice that cracks, and she burns them away hard. Every instinct in her says run all the way back to Bral and never speak of it.

Hand the helm to the table

Orena has been flying this leg to practice for Hallow's Rest, so make the helm a real moment for Gabrielle, two ways, her choice:

  • Estra takes over, and Orena feels the captain's hands close over hers on the helm, feels her afraid, which Orena has never seen. She yields it or she doesn't.
  • Estra is too rattled to fly, and it falls to Orena to get them clear while the captain grips the rail. Her first time flying them out of real danger, and she does it, or improvises trying.

Then the second choice, once they're clear and breathing: run home to Bral (Estra's raw instinct, and a fine cliffhanger), or steady the ship and press on to the delivery that a colony is starving for. Recommend pressing on so the session completes its loop, but it's the crew's call, not yours.

GM only: what it was, and don't spend it

It's a void scavver (CR 11, twenty feet of pitch-black hide and teeth, reference art), the exterminated apex predator the entire campaign turns on. Do not fight it and do not name it. Session 1 gets only a distant, terrifying glimpse: they never close, it never engages, nobody has a word for it, and Estra won't explain. The first close encounter and the understanding come much later (sessions/Episode - The Quiet Hull.md). Tonight it plants two things at once: the keystone, and the crack in Estra. File it beside the cold open, and keep everything between here and the star warm to balance the two dark beats.

Crew stations (recap of §06)

  • Pilot. Flying is free; spend the free action on one maneuver: Evasive Action, Full Burn, or Boarding Action. (Ramming is the hull's ram attack, a separate option, not one of these three.)
  • Gunner. Crews a ballista (one action, with the upgrades).
  • Engineer. Emergency Patch: DC 12, regain HP = level, once per encounter.
  • Coordinator. Grant advantage, impose disadvantage, or let a station act again. One per round.

Funny under fire

  • Tack murmurs "hold together, Deborah" to the helm as she takes the hit.
  • Finny turns off the stove first, every time, even mid-combat.
  • Oz says "excuse me" to whoever he was mid-sentence with before sprinting to his post.
  • Oz stays scrupulously polite through the violence: "Terribly sorry" as he throws a boarder, "no offence meant, big fellow" as he fires on the scavver. He means every word, and he does not stop.
  • Brass pockets something valuable "just in case we have to abandon."
  • Estra never stands up, until the one moment she does, and that is when everyone should be afraid.

When repairs do bite

Repairs are 20 gp/HP. This fight was tuned to cost nothing, but a real one won't be: a single 23-HP hull hit is 460 gp, more than half a routine job's whole take-home. That's the lesson to bank for later, not to spring tonight. Let Aerion's math make combat frightening because it's expensive.

The cold open, two scenes

This is why the session exists. Everything before it is the crew being the crew. Give both leads a quiet end beat, Aerion up with a dragon who won't settle and Orena alone on watch, and let the star land on the second. Set the scene, offer the stimulus, and hand each moment to its player. Don't narrate their feelings for them.

Read aloud: Scene one, Aerion

It's late, and Flurry won't settle. She won't eat, not even the thing she always eats. She paces the cabin, climbs into your coat and back out, presses against the hull and pulls away from it, trilling low in a register you've never quite heard from her. There's nothing wrong that you can find. No fever, no hurt, no draft. She just will not be still, and she keeps turning to look at the same stretch of empty dark outside the port, as if something out there has her whole attention and she can't tell you what.

Hand it to Priscilla

This is Aerion's scene: his baby is frightened of nothing, at midnight, and won't be comforted. Ask what he does, and don't answer why. He can wake someone, sit up with her, check the ship, dismiss it. Whatever he chooses, Flurry does not settle. (She's a barometer; the sky is wrong. npcs/Flurry.md.)

Read aloud: Scene two, Orena on watch

Elsewhere on the ship, the same hour. You're alone at the chart table, updating the log by lamplight, heading home. You look up to check your bearing against the stars you steer by, and one of them is wrong. Off by a hair. Not where it was an hour ago, not where the chart says.

You check the instruments. Twice. The instruments are fine. The sky is wrong.

And while you're looking right at it, the star dims. And goes out. No flare, no sound. It's just gone, and the dark where it was is seamless, as though it was never there.

Except, if you hold very still, there's the faint edge of a note. A single held tone at the edge of hearing. You've heard it once before. You heard it the night you lost your crew.

Running the two scenes

You can play them in sequence or intercut them, Aerion soothing a dragon who won't be soothed at the very moment Orena watches the star go out, so the table feels they're the same event before anyone says so. Then stop. Don't cut away, don't offer a check, don't answer the question. Give each player the beat and let it sit. When it's landed: "That's where we'll stop." Cut to black.

What the note actually is (GM only, long game)

The Vocalise is a kindori death-song, the dirge of a starving, stranded pod as its water goes dark, and it lingers and echoes in dead water long after. It's a real, recurring sound in the world, not only Orena's memory. Plant it as a drip across the campaign (tonight's note; a half-heard echo on a dark leg; a rumour of "singing in the deep") so it pays off much later at a whale fall, where an elder kindori's song is still echoing and Orena recognizes it. Don't explain any of this now. (`campaign/Story Spine.md`, `campaign/Plot Threads.md`.)
