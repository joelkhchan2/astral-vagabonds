The Resupply Run.

The first voyage, run like a season premiere: introduce the crew and the two leads, show the Rock of Bral for the first time, teach the loop, let both players win with competence, and end on the image that launches the whole campaign — a star going out. Warm and funny for an hour, then one cold minute.

Keep it warm. The dread is a single image, and it is the last thing that happens.

How to use this page

Plum blocks are GM-only — don't read them aloud. Blue Read aloud boxes are narration to read or paraphrase. Numbers are pulled from the canon docs (The Last Honest Deal.md, The Bral Reach.md, Rules & Systems.md, Crew Roster.md). Narration follows rules/DMing Craft.md: two or three senses, broad strokes then detail, ground the strange in the ordinary.

Guard time for the cold open. Don't let a haggle or the fight run so long you're rushing the last quiet minute — it's the point of the night.

02

Run it like a season premiere

A pilot introduces the show. By the end, the table should be able to answer four things: who is this crew, what do the two leads want, what does a job feel like, and what's the mystery? The resupply run carries all four.

The two PCs — establish the person, plant the want

Aerion Kyoya Windsong · Priscilla

Astral elf samurai; bosun, quartermaster, cargo. The overlooked youngest son of a merchant house he quietly plans to take from his father one day. Keeps the books, the tabs, the debts — and secretly dotes on a baby dragon.

Introduce him by

handing him the ledger and the port problem at Hallow's Rest. Competence held quietly, a cool head under a money crunch, one flicker of the family wound if the Windsong name comes up. His want — his own house on his own terms — only needs to be visible, not spoken.

Orena · Gabrielle

Water genasi bard/paladin; navigator, helmsman-in-training, scout. Newest aboard (six months). She lost her whole guild, the Wayfinder Knot, to a song called the Stillwater Vocalise, and she alone survived. She is looking for them.

Introduce her by

letting her read the sky where the instruments fail (arrival), and closing the night on her (the cold open). Her want — the crew she lost — gets its hook on night one when the Vocalise returns under the dying star.

The two plotlines

A-plot — episodic

The run itself

Take the contract, provision, fly, deliver, get paid, pay the bills. It starts and finishes tonight, and the reward is competence: the triage solved, the port found, the fight won. The "normal week" the show runs on — 70% of the session.

B-plot — the arc

Something is wrong with the sky

Under the ordinary job: three quiet symptoms and one personal note. It does not resolve — it's planted. The colony cut off by a failing road, the star that goes out on watch, the Vocalise: the first threads of the Guttering and of Orena's search.

The ship has no name yet — use it

Canon: the ship is deliberately unnamed. In the fiction it's "Estra's ship," or "the Deal" only in your notes. Lean into it — the dockhands call it "the Vane boat," Finny keeps floating names nobody approves, and naming her is a live thread you can pay off in a later session when the crew has earned it. If a player proposes a name they love, that's a gift; bank it.

Seven people wearing fourteen hats and pretending it's fine. Give each one a single defining moment tonight — a voice, a tic, and a want is enough to make them a person and to play them again. Running gags in npcs/Crew Comedy Bits.md.

04

Arriving at the Rock of Bral

The crew lives here, but the campaign hasn't shown it yet — so give it a real arrival. Then hit a few of the six faces, provision, and cast off. The whole port sequence should take about twenty minutes; two of the six faces have something faintly wrong with them, never all of them.

Read aloud — first sighting

You come up on the Rock the way everyone does: from below. First a bright smudge against the black, then it resolves — a mile of tumbling grey stone with a city growing off the top of it like barnacles on a hull, spires and gantries and lamplit windows stacked wherever they'll cling, all of it gold against the silver of the Astral. Ships hang around it in their hundreds, at every angle, none agreeing on which way is up.

As you slide into the underside berths, the gravity turns over. Your stomach lurches; "down" swings ninety degrees; and the city is suddenly above you and the void is a floor you could fall into. Then the lock opens and the smell arrives: smoke, hot metal, fish, spice, ten thousand people and their dinners. Bar noise. A hawker. Somewhere, a bell. You're home, for whatever that's worth.

Three textures to sprinkle — one per scene, don't front-load

Gravity is a local argument (a dropped coin falls sideways; newcomers walk into walls; locals never notice). Everyone's from somewhere else (giff dockers, dohwar hawkers, a mercane the crowd parts for — nobody looks twice at a crew like theirs). It remembers being lawless (an untended shrine to the pirate Captain Bral; marble up top, rope down below). Full detail: locations/The Bral Reach.md.

The faces to hit

Ottie — the runner · Berth 40

Twelve years old, knows everything, sells information for candy and honours the deal. First face of every homecoming.

Ottie"Heard you're finally shipping out. I know three things you'd want to know. First one's free. The other two are candy."

Free one: a Hallow's Rest hauler limped in last week, empty, spooked. Candy ones: the Board updated; and Bex Halloway's been asking what the Vanes are hauling.

Meech — the Charter Window · the CIV

Half-orc, forty years behind the grille, immune to charm and bribery. Not corrupt, not helpful — correct. Stamps the contract and explains which form they should have used.

Meech"This is filed on a 7-C. A resupply of this tonnage wants a 7-C annex. I'll allow it. Once."
Free breadcrumb, not tonight

If a player is genuinely polite and asks a well-formed question, Meech will mention three frontier routes quietly delisted last year with no incident reports. Save it — that's an M4 thread.

The other four, in a line

Senna Orrick at the Delverane window (buys survey data; professionally condescending to a 348th-ranked crew exactly once). Madam Ulve at the Compass Rose (greets Estra by name; neither explains). The Underwriters' Hall (Nightbreeze — Aerion's mother's house; walking in has a cost he'd feel). The Manifest Board on the public wall, where Finny goes straight to check the ranking.

The market always has exactly one uncommon item for sale, never the same twice. Roll or pick from Tables.md §5. Good tonight: a sound-slate, engraved (holds ten minutes, not one) — Orena wants it badly.

05

The job

Their first contract off the board: a resupply run to a struggling Verge mining colony. Food, medicine, and parts to people who are proud and nearly out of both. Warm, a little sad, full of small competence problems.

Hallow's Rest

Verge mining colony · Worlds & Places.md

A mining colony on a tidally locked moon: one face in permanent daylight, one in permanent dark, the town strung along the terminator where it's survivable. Nine hundred people, one dock, one bar, and a mine that's been three years from exhaustion for a decade. Long shadows that never move.

They need everything, all the time, and they pay in ore.

The seed that matters — why they're desperate

The colony's own regular supply route stopped working. Their runner set out for the depot weeks ago and could not find it — the route had drifted, the charts wouldn't resolve, and they turned back late and empty. They're cut off from the way they normally restock, and they don't understand why.

The whole campaign in miniature

Navigation failing has a body count, and these people are living it before anyone has a word for it. Hallow's Rest already re-files its coordinates every eighteen months and has stopped telling visitors why, because the last ships they told didn't come back. Don't explain any of it. Let it sit wrong.

The cargo — and a callback

Six tons of perishables, medicine, and mining parts. Keeping the perishables good on the haul is one honest check. And the running gag: one crate is, absurdly, live snails, and Finny will not stop talking about them.

06

The voyage — the beats

Read-aloud is a starting point, not a script. The checks are suggestions; a good idea from a player beats a good roll. This is the flight out, the one thing that happens, and the arrival where the weight lands.

1

Casting off & first flight

Estra flies, Orena shadows · run the stations once
Read aloud

The clamps release and the Rock falls away beneath you, then above you, then wherever it likes. The helm-glow settles. For a moment the silver of the Astral is the only thing in every direction, and the ship is a warm bright bubble of air and lamplight moving through it faster than the eye can hold. Then Tack pats a bulkhead, says she's talking, and the moment's over.

Their first time flying her as a system. Run the crew stations once (Rules & Systems.md §8) so everyone knows their post: Estra or Orena piloting, Tack or Finny on Gertrude and Susan, Oz on watch, Aerion coordinating from the deck. Tack talks to the ship, Finny lashes the cargo, Brass rolls the morning d4.

Flurry, content

Free and optional: as they cross into open sky, Flurry presses to a viewport, breath-frost on the glass, perfectly happy. Bank it so the opposite lands later (npcs/Flurry.md).

2

Transit — one event

Pick ONE. Don't overload session one.

Choose a single piece of cosmic content, and add the fight only if you want the action beat here (see Combat). Rotate the wonder each session — the sky is full of strange things, not just whales.

Read aloud — the chwinga (one option)

Something is sitting on the forward rail. About a foot tall, made of what looks like polished driftwood and starlight, and it has been there for an unknown length of time watching you work. When you look at it directly, it waves. It does not explain itself. It does not intend to.

  • Wonder options: the chwinga on the rail; a derelict with the table still set and no crew; a mercane vessel passing on business older than the cartel; a shoal of space guppies that adopts the ship; or, used sparingly, a distant kindori pod singing by light (awe, and a pull Orena can't place — save the heavy whale beats for later).
  • If Flurry lit up in beat 1, the kindori option is where she'd do it again — dragon and navigator both feeling the good sky, one by blood, one by ear.
3

Arrival — the first symptom, with a face

Orena reads the sky · Aerion works the ledger

Getting there is slightly wrong. The run took a day longer than the charts allow, and the colony is a few hours out of position. Let Orena earn the find: the instruments disagree with the sky, and she trusts the sky.

Read aloud

The charts put Hallow's Rest here. It isn't here. The star's right, the dark moon's right, the daylit face is exactly where it should be — and the town is four hours off from every number you have, sitting perfectly intact on the terminator with its one dock and its one bar and its long unmoving shadows. Orena, you don't find it by the instruments. You find it by feel.

Then the weight lands. The colonists explain that their own runner couldn't reach the depot — the route wouldn't resolve, and they turned back late and empty. They're not lazy or unlucky. The road stopped working, and they nearly starved for it.

Aerion's win

Give him a real problem for the ledger: they can't fully pay (they'd pay in ore, at a bad rate), or a rival factor is squeezing them, or the triage of who-gets-what-first is a genuine choice. Let him solve it — don't hand him the answer. A warm, grateful, slightly awkward handoff; pay collected.

Don't say the word

Nobody uses "Guttering." Nobody uses any word for it. Let the crew feel this is bigger than one colony without naming it.

4

"Vagabonds," then home

A throwaway insult that sticks
Read aloud

Somebody on the dock — another crew, a factor, a bored Warden — watches you finish the unload, takes in the battered unnamed hull and the mismatched crew, and says it not quite under their breath: "Estra's vagabonds." Like it's the punchline to a joke everybody already knows.

Let it land and let it stick. By the end of the campaign it should feel less like an insult and more like something they'd paint on the hull — the name the ship doesn't have yet, waiting.

07

The action beat — with pre-rolled rounds

The premiere's fight: the crew's first combat in their new ship, and every character's first move. Keep it short — one good broadside should nearly settle it. It's a confidence-builder and the first real run of the crew stations, not a scare. Enemy dice are pre-rolled below so you can stay in the fiction; ignore any line the moment the story wants otherwise.

Calibrate to the table

Gabrielle enjoys combat more than Priscilla — give it teeth for her (a gun, the Coordinator chair, the choice to press) while keeping it winnable and quick. Combat-light week? Shrink it to one tense exchange or a piloting challenge and still get the "we ran the ship as a system" beat.

Primary: Dev Ostrel's skiff · ship duel

The scrap-crew leader from the yard shakedown comes back in a light raider skiff, wanting revenge or a score — a recurring face with a grudge (or a debt, if they let him go before). The clean first ship-vs-ship fight.

Crew stations (Rules & Systems.md §8)

  • Pilot — flying is free; spend the free action on one maneuver: Evasive Action, Full Burn, Ram, or Boarding Action.
  • Gunner — crews a ballista (one action to fire, with the upgrades).
  • Engineer — Emergency Patch: DC 12 check, regain HP = level, once per encounter.
  • Coordinator — grant advantage, impose disadvantage, or let a station act again. One benefit per round.

Funny under fire

  • Tack whispers "it's okay, Darling" to the helm as she takes the hit.
  • Finny turns off the stove first, every time, even mid-combat.
  • Oz says "excuse me" to whoever he was talking to before sprinting to his post.
  • Brass pockets something valuable "just in case we have to abandon."
  • Orena has the ship moving before anyone finishes buckling in.
08

The money is real

Competence under constraint is the substance of the genre. Do the math out loud at the dock and let the thinness bite.

LineAmountNote
Contract payout+1,400 gpSome or all possibly paid in ore, at a bad rate — Aerion's call
Operating cost−600 gpThe month, seven crew, all-inclusive (wages, food, air, upkeep)
Hull repairs−20 gp / HPOnly if the fight cost hull — e.g. the skiff's round-3 hit is 23 HP = 460 gp
Berth 40 rent−30 gpCheap because the last three tenants left owing money

What that leaves

Not much, and that's correct. A clean run nets a few hundred gold over cost; one solid hit and the repair bill eats it. They have a ~14,350 gp cushion in the account and Aerion's 10,000 gp trust-fund reserve he'd rather die than touch — runway in months, not years.

The Manifest Board

Completing the run posts them near the very bottom (~348th of 351). Finny prints it and throws a party. The three below them: a derelict, an impounded smuggler, and a vessel that's been on fire for six months.

Make it a beat, not bookkeeping

The operating cost comes off the top when they get home; repairs are counted in hull HP at 20 gp each. Let Aerion be the one who says the number aloud, and let the number be a little worse than anyone hoped. That's the show.

09

The cold open

This is why the session exists. Everything before it is the crew being the crew; this is the one cold minute. Slow down, give Gabrielle quiet and your full attention, and do not explain any of it.

Read aloud — slowly

It's late. The ship is asleep around you — Tack's engine ticking as it cools, someone snoring two bulkheads down, the hull murmuring the way it does. You're on watch, alone at the chart table, updating the log by lamplight, heading home.

You look up to check your bearing against the stars you steer by. And one of them is wrong. Off by a hair. Not where it was an hour ago, not where the chart says.

You check the instruments. Twice. The instruments are fine. The sky is wrong.

And while you're looking at it — while you're looking right at it — the star dims. And goes out. No flare, no sound. It's just gone, and the dark where it was is seamless, as though it was never there.

Except — if you hold very still — there's the faint edge of a note. A single held tone at the edge of hearing. You've heard it once before. You heard it the night you lost your crew.

Running it

Let the silence be long. Don't cut away, don't offer a check, don't answer the obvious question. Orena keeping it to herself for now is the stronger, truer choice. When it's landed: "That's where we'll stop." Cut to black. End of session.

Optional — Flurry knew

One more chill, if you want it: earlier that evening Flurry wouldn't settle — wouldn't eat, hid in Aerion's coat, and nobody thought anything of it. Only in hindsight, next session, does anyone connect it. Don't point at it now.
