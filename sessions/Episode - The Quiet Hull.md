# Episode -- The Quiet Hull (drop-in horror-lite episode)

> **A one-shot / half-session you can drop between missions, best around level 6-7.** Not on the contract spine. The crew boards what looks like an abandoned vessel; something is still aboard, and it killed the original crew. This is the campaign's **scariest** episode -- suspense, an empty ship, a lurking predator -- but it stays inside the guardrail: uncanny and tense, **not gore, not undead, not true horror.** The dread is *"we are not alone in here and we can't see it yet,"* and it pays off with a proper campaign-scale reveal. Tone: mild T at its peak, then back to warmth. See `campaign/Story Spine.md`.

## The pitch

A derelict hangs in the dark on the crew's route -- a merchant hull, or a survey ship, or (best) a **whaler**, intact, air envelope holding, running lights dead. No distress beacon, no wreck damage, no answer to a hail. Salvage law says finders-keepers on an abandoned hull, and the crew is always broke, so aboard they go. It is *pristine and wrong*: meals half-eaten, a log that stops mid-sentence, everything in its place and **no one there.** Something got in, hunted the crew through their own corridors, and is still here -- and it is very good at not being seen.

**The two-tier reveal (the point of the episode):**
1. **The killer aboard is a night scavver** -- a sleek, near-black ambush predator that slipped in through a hull breach, has been living in the ducts and dark holds, and picked the original crew off one by one. This is the **winnable, scary fight.** (CR 5, `references/Astral Sea Bestiary.md` -- Shadow Hunter, advantage in the dark, advantage vs. surprised. Deadly in a dark ship, beatable once the crew brings light and works together.)
2. **The reason the derelict is *here*, in the deep dark, is worse:** a **void scavver** -- the apex predator of the deep, twenty feet of pitch-black hide and teeth, old and large enough to eat a spelljammer whole -- is cruising this dead region. The whaler fled into the dark to escape it, took a breach doing it, and that breach let the night scavver in. The void scavver is the campaign's **first sight of the exterminated keystone predator, alive.** It is **not a fight at level 6-7** (CR 11); it is the *oh-god-leave-now* reveal. The crew glimpses it, understands what it is, and runs. (See "Why this matters to the arc.")

## Why this matters to the arc

Void scavvers are the keystone the whole campaign turns on: civilization exterminated them to make the lanes safe, and *that* extermination is what broke the sky (the trophic cascade in `campaign/Story Spine.md`). So the crew's first real look at a living one is a big deal, and this episode is a superb place to spend it:

- **It's supposed to be nearly gone.** Whalers and the houses have hunted void scavvers to the edge for a century. Seeing one alive, out in the deep dark, is *rare* -- the kind of thing an old spacer wouldn't believe. Plant the wonder-inside-the-terror: this enormous, silent, ancient thing is beautiful, and it is one of the last of its kind, and nobody aboard knows yet that killing its kin is why the stars are going out.
- **The whaler detail lands the theme without a lecture.** If the derelict is a *whaler*, the ship that came out here to hunt the predator was killed *by* the predator it was hunting -- and later, when the crew learns the cascade, this memory recontextualizes hard: *we watched what happens when there are still a few left, and we understand now why there are so few.* Don't say any of that at the table now. Let it sit and detonate in Tier 2.
- **Payoff seed:** "we saw a void scavver, alive, in the deep" becomes evidence later that the species isn't fully gone -- that restoration is *possible*, that there are wild breeding grounds still out there to protect (the Tier 3 cure). Hope, planted inside the scariest hour of the campaign.

## The reveal, staged (don't show the big one too early)

Structure it as a *narrowing*: the crew thinks they know what they're dealing with, twice, before they actually do.

1. **"The ship's just empty."** Salvage mindset. Creepy but lucrative. Let them relax and start counting money.
2. **"Something's still aboard."** Signs of the night scavver: scratched-out breach, a hold that's been *lived in*, a noise, a thing that moves at the edge of the dark. Now it's a hunt in a haunted house -- their fight.
3. **"Why did this ship come *here*?"** They fight or evade the night scavver and read the log / see out a viewport -- and there, filling the black outside, silent, is the void scavver. The thing that made a whaler flee into a dead region to die. **Leave now.** The climax is not a boss fight; it's an *escape* -- undock, kill the lights, drift silent past something that could end them, everyone at their station, no room for error. Competence under terror, which is the genre.

## Running the fear (inside the guardrail)

- **The scares are absence and anticipation, not gore.** The original crew are *gone*, not butchered on-screen -- an empty ship, a stopped log, a child's toy, a door barricaded from the inside that didn't work. Imply the loss; never wallow in it. No bodies as set dressing, no viscera. (Guardrail: `campaign/Story Spine.md`, `campaign/Campaign Bible.md`.)
- **Darkness and light are the mechanic.** The night scavver owns the dark (advantage while unseen). The crew's counterplay is *light, sound discipline, and teamwork* -- exactly the competence-under-constraint the campaign runs on. Reward them for using the ship against the thing: seal bulkheads, vent a compartment, flood a hold with light, herd it. Make cleverness beat it, not just damage.
- **Keep the crew together and let them be funny under stress.** Found family doesn't switch off because it's scary -- Finny narrating his own terror, Oz standing in front of everyone, Brass eating something to cope, Tack asking the ship where the thing is (and the ship, unsettlingly, answering). The comedy is the pressure valve that keeps this mild-T instead of grim.
- **The void scavver is awe as much as horror.** When they finally see it, give it a beat of terrible beauty -- vast, ancient, unbothered, gliding. Not a demon; an animal, the biggest one they've ever seen, doing what it was made to do. That framing is what makes its later meaning (we killed these, and doomed the sky) land as tragedy rather than triumph.
- **End on the ship, warm.** They get out (with salvage, or with nothing but their lives and a story). The last beat is the crew back aboard the *Last Honest Deal*, too rattled to sleep, ending up in the galley together at 0300 while Estra makes coffee. Home. Always land it home.

## Both PCs lead

- **Orena:** she's the one who *feels* the void scavver before they see it -- the wrongness, the silence, the water-sense of something enormous in the dark -- and she's the helm hand who threads the silent-running escape. If Gabrielle wants the fight, the night scavver is a genuine, frightening combat and hers to command; if she wants the tension, the escape is a navigation set-piece. Either way she reads the danger first.
- **Aerion:** the salvage assessment, the log reconstruction (what happened here, in what order -- an inventory of a disaster), and the cold call on when the salvage stops being worth the risk. His arc-flavor: the ledger says *this cargo would clear a month's debt* and the ship says *leave*, and he's the one who has to choose. Competence and nerve, on paper and under pressure.

## Adjustable knobs

- **Dial the fear up or down** by how much of the night scavver you show and how close the void scavver gets. For a milder table, the night scavver is more heard than seen and the void scavver is a distant silhouette; for a table that wants the thrill, run the full hunt-then-escape.
- **The derelict's identity** sets the theme: a **whaler** (best -- ties the predator's meaning to the cull), a **survey ship** (ties to the Guttering data thread -- their logs are Aerion's evidence), or a plain merchant (pure scare, arc-light).
- **Salvage payoff:** let them leave with *something* if they earn it -- a piece of the whaler's take, a chart, an instrument (a Locker/Empty-Frequency seed if you want one) -- so the terror also advances the plot or the purse.
- **Don't reuse the void scavver as a fight** until the crew is high enough to survive it, and even then it should be a set-piece, not a random encounter. Its scarcity is its meaning.
