02

Cold Open: the Quiet Verge

The derelict hull both players asked to board, docked at the Rock of Bral, unclaimed. A true point-crawl, six spaces plus the entry, full stat blocks in Reference. Every room's document is also a standalone printable handout, sessions/handouts/, linked inline below.

A small independent trader that ran supply between Verge colonies, the same shape of route Hallow's Rest depends on. Registry scraped clean, no distress call, no bodies, no violence, just a ship that stopped being lived in mid-motion, was deliberately abandoned in good order, and drifted for months before fetching up in a lowside berth here.

Read aloud, boarding her

The airlock cycles on backup power, barely. Inside, the air still holds, but it's cold in a way that has nothing to do with temperature, the specific stillness of somewhere that used to have people breathing in it. Emergency lighting only, a dim amber strip along the deck. Somewhere forward, something ticks on an even interval, a system nobody's told to stop running. The deck under your boots is gritty in a way metal shouldn't be. What do you do?

What actually happened, GM only

The crossing started taking longer than the charts said it should, trip after trip, the helmsman's own log tracking it getting worse: fewer bright threads in the water, more dark patches between them. Flow was dying before glow fully went out. Then, mid-transit, the push stopped entirely. Not lost, becalmed: the destination star sat exactly where the charts said, visible the whole time, but the ship couldn't close the distance. The captain's math was cold but sound, the hull's stores wouldn't outlast the drift, but the two lifeboats would, if nobody wasted what they were given. Jettison cargo, split the crew, run for the last confirmed waypoint. Before they left, the helmsman set the empty hull's own dead-reckoning heading for Bral, a last deliberate act, not luck, accounting for the months of drift. Did the crew make it? Genuinely unresolved, a real open hook, answer it only if the table chases it.

The crew, named for the handout documents: Captain Wren Ashby, Helmsman Ilsa Marrow, Engineer Pell Ostrander, Cook Dorram Keel, Deckhand Fen Caskey, and one more unnamed hand. Six aboard, six accounted for between the two lifeboats.

Who's aboard now

Three things found their way into the open hull during the drift. Two grell denned in the crew quarters, nesting in the torn bedding, blind, hunting entirely by sound. Deeper in, a megapede forced its way into the cargo hold through the bay doors and has lived there since, the real boss of this hull. A small, harmless flumph fled the megapede early and has been hiding in the engineering vents, the one creature aboard actually glad to see visitors. Full stats, Reference.

The rooms

Let the crew choose their own order after the airlock. Every room carries both a "what happened here" clue and a "something's living here now" clue, so none of the three creatures feel sprung out of nowhere.

Airlock / entry · DC 10 Perception

The outer hatch is already cycling when you reach for it, sluggish, protesting on backup power. Inside, your breath doesn't fog the way it should. Somewhere forward, something ticks on an even interval, patient, mechanical, a system nobody told to stop. The deck under your boots is gritty in a way metal shouldn't be.

On a success: the grit is old organic residue, the trail splits forward, a heavier smear toward the cargo hold, a lighter one toward crew quarters.

Galley · DC 12 Perception

Pans still hang in their rack, swinging faint and slow from a draft that shouldn't exist in dead air. A pot's gone cold at the bottom, whatever was cooking left to finish alone. A tidy slate hangs by the stove, Dorram's own hand.

Clue: the cook's rationing note, finished and routine, a crew still doing ordinary jobs long after they should have worried. sessions/handouts/01-cooks-rationing-note.pdf. Second clue, same DC: claw gouges low on a cabinet door, old, scored deep.

Crew quarters · DC 13 Investigation, the first fight likely starts here

Bunks are made, not military-tight, just made, the way you leave a room you plan to come back to. A sea chest sits open, half-packed, like whoever was filling it got called away before they finished.

Clue: a bunk roster confirms the ship's name. sessions/handouts/02-bunk-roster.pdf. DC 20: a finished, unsent letter home from Fen Caskey, worried about the lane running thinner but closing warm. sessions/handouts/03-fens-letter-home.pdf. DC 15 Perception, the den: bedding in the far corner bunk torn up and nested, not slept in. Two grell, hunting by sound; a lit torch or loud approach gives them away first, real quiet gives the away team the opening round.

Cargo hold · DC 13 Investigation, the real boss fight

The hold isn't ransacked, it's shoved. Crates that should be stacked are scattered along one wall, dragged more than thrown, deep gouges scoring the deck plating between them, too wide and too deep for any person's tools.

Clue: a cargo jettison log, deliberate and methodical, not a panic dump. sessions/handouts/04-cargo-jettison-log.pdf. Loot: 150-400 gp leftover goods, a locked strongbox (DC 13) worth 100 gp more, a false panel, DC 18, another 100-150 gp in trade goods. The megapede dens here, CR 7, well above the grell. Give the table a real choice to retreat and reseal the bay doors rather than assume they finish it.

The nest, once it's safe to search

Tangled into shed scale is a small, labeled ring of the ship's own duplicate keys, boxed as non-essential cargo before the crew left, one tag reading CAPTAIN'S CABIN. DC 15 Investigation. A clean, undamaged way into the captain's cabin, the real prize for taking the megapede on rather than skirting it. The DC 15 force-the-door option (below) stays available regardless.

Engineering · DC 13 Dexterity save on entry

A drained helm coupling arcs once, unprompted, 2d6 lightning to anyone standing too close, half on a save.

The air back here hums wrong, a coupling that should be dead still twitching like a nerve that doesn't know the limb's gone. Burn scoring climbs one wall in a long, deliberate arc, not an explosion, something straining and failing slowly.

Once discharged, the room is safe. DC 12 Investigation: the engineer's maintenance log. sessions/handouts/05-engineers-maintenance-log.pdf. DC 15 Perception finds the flumph, cowering behind a loose panel. Left alone, it offers fretful telepathic warnings about the megapede, a real, usable hint. DC 18: a choice of salvage worth taking, ask the table which rather than rolling for them, a gravity-plane coil, a backup helm coupling, or the sounding-lantern housing, each worth 75-150 gp.

Bridge · DC 13 Investigation

Star charts are still pinned open to the last course anyone plotted. The helm seat is angled toward the window, toward a bright, ordinary, untouchable star.

Clue: the helmsman's logbook, entries getting shorter and more worried, then the final entry, mid-sentence. sessions/handouts/06-helmsmans-logbook.pdf. DC 15: a second scrap, the helmsman's own dead-reckoning course for Bral. sessions/handouts/06b-helmsmans-course-scrap.pdf. Loot: a navigation instrument, sellable to Aurel Delverane specifically. DC 20 (rare): the waypoint ties to somewhere the table already knows. DC 25: a locked drawer under the console, a Lantern of Revealing, the one real magic item aboard.

Captain's cabin, locked · DC 15 to force, or the key ring from the nest

The core reveal, read it straight: the captain's log, calm through every entry. sessions/handouts/07-captains-log.pdf. The last page does the math out loud and orders the abandon-ship. No blame, no panic, a closing line wishing whoever reads this well. DC 20: a second, unofficial scrap, the one crack in the composure the whole ship shows. sessions/handouts/07b-captains-private-scrap.pdf.

Tone note

Played as wonder-adjacent dread, not horror. A competent crew solved a hard problem under impossible pressure and left clean; the megapede and the grell are both opportunistic squatters that moved in long after, not a sign anyone aboard came to harm. The megapede is this cold open's real teeth; the flumph's warning and the option to retreat and reseal the hold are both there on purpose.

Rounding off the Bral Visit: the Float Market

Picks up the same session-4 visit, not a fresh arrival. Two threads left on this stretch of dock before departure: this, and the derelict above, either order.

Read aloud, transition

Charter window's behind you now, the postings sorted, Finny already working the gossip he picked up. Two things left on this stretch of dock before you're back in the black: whatever's worth buying at the Float Market, and that empty hull sitting unclaimed past the second gantry.

Virelai, the weather-orb stall

Not a novelty vendor, an actual djinni working a market stall. Her orbs are captured fragments of real skies, taken from specific worlds and systems, a djinni can reach places no ship bound to the physical currents can anymore. Is she archiving pieces of dying, cut-off worlds, or collecting them toward some purpose of her own? Don't answer it.

Physical tic: a faint, lazy wind moves around her and her merchandise constantly. Voice tic: everything described in weather ("that's a stormy question"). Her line: "The sky doesn't mind sharing, if you ask nicely."

The flat orbs, a real clue, never named as one

A handful of orbs show nothing, no weather at all. Not broken, accurate: skies already gone fully stilled, nothing left to capture. She sells them cheap, 5 gp, mostly to be rid of them. DC 15 Arcana if pressed confirms it's not a malfunction. No further explanation. Worth deciding later whether a "flat" orb ever correlates with somewhere the crew's been.

Ormund and Pell Vey, plasmoid siblings

Ormund is meticulous, catalogues inventory down to exact counts, and briefly loses his solid shape when flustered. Pell is chaotic and warm, hands out nicknames on sight, and absorbs small items into her own gelatinous body "for safekeeping," then genuinely forgets where she put them. Ormund's want: the shop taken seriously. Pell's want: everyone having a good time, profit a distant second.

The Lockbox Thread Continues: House Nightbreeze

Not House Vellum this time, House Nightbreeze, Aerion's mother's house. They insure the whole Reach, which means they hold the claims data from the Reefside disaster, and their own numbers on what was supposed to be aboard that checkpoint don't fully reconcile.

Prudence Hale, senior underwriter, Nightbreeze

Precise, controlled, the house's whole ethos in one person. Voice tic: talks entirely in risk and probability, never a flat statement where a hedge will do. Physical tic: idly counts something while she talks, a stylus tapped against her other hand. Want: close this quietly and cleanly, no paper trail, a professional doing her job, not a threat.

The approach, worded carefully

Wherever the table's naturally gathered (the Float Market or the dock near the derelict both work). She never says "lockbox." The offer: 20,000 gp, clean, no questions asked, for "anything recovered from the Reefside checkpoint that never made it onto any claim." This forces a real choice, not a third option to sell: deny having anything (a real lie, to a house that insures them and already half-suspects otherwise), or admit something exists and decline the sale outright. If the table somehow wants to actually sell, that's not blocked by the fiction, but it's a real point of no return, flag it live.

Left deliberately open

Whether Aerynna knows about this, or sent it herself, isn't decided. She's the swing vote on Aerion's own Fifth House bid, so the personal stakes are real either way. Decide live, don't lock it now.

Main Content: Open Water, Open Season

The survey job picked at the end of session 4, finally flown. The real content, out at the Windward Edge, where the crew's own charted lane is losing ground to a real, literal confluence.

BR-1252, the posting as readOpen Water, Open Season. Survey, a creeping current, plus standing bounty. Pay 3,100 gp, Days ~20. Requester Aurel Delverane, the Farthing Station survey office. Route Bral to the Windward Edge, the standing surf. Details: the Windward Edge's standing surf has crept inward, and a once-reliable stretch of chart has gone unreadable. Delverane wants to know whether the old charts can still be trusted. RATING: OPEN, CLIENT OF RECORD (Delverane, Farthing Station).

The deliverable, two separate things

  • 3,100 gp flat, paid for completing the survey and reporting back to Delverane, guaranteed regardless of what's actually found.
  • The navigation data itself, sellable separately, Orena's own call whether and to whom: DC 10 an ordinary lane (50-300 gp), DC 13 the lane brushing thinning water (300-1,500 gp, her own call whether the writeup is honest or softened), DC 16+ something genuinely unprecedented (1,500-10,000+ gp).
  • "Open Season" is the title's wordplay, not a requirement. The board text never mentions killing anything. The standing CIV bounty just happens to apply here too, pure incidental opportunity.
Read aloud, arriving at the standing surf

The standing surf comes into view before anything else does, a waterfall running the wrong way, real water blown upward off the collision of two currents and caught there, never quite falling. Where the spray catches the dark behind it, it streaks into shifting color, green and violet and silver-white, an aurora made of water instead of light. It hums, low, under everything, felt in the teeth more than heard. The air carries an unmistakable mineral-wet smell, rain on hot stone, water this far from anywhere water should be. Debris drifts along the boundary in slow spirals, caught between two different pulls. Somewhere past it, the chart just stops being reliable.

Why it's creeping inward: not one side dying outright, a confluence losing its balance. The charted lane's current held its ground against the deeper inflow because a resident kindori pod's regular passage kept reinforcing it. Once that pod stalled, the lane's own current weakened, and the boundary has been creeping into territory that used to belong to the stable, charted side.

Longscar, the predator drawn to the stalled pod

A named, known night scavver, earned its reputation the way sharks earn theirs: one old wound running the full length of its body, from a fight everyone agrees it clearly won. Opportunistic, not monstrous, moved in on a weakened pod. The real choice it offers, never forced: help the stalled pod (non-lethal, a rescue-shaped scene), drive Longscar off without killing it, or take the bounty on a real, named, actively dangerous animal.

Aurel Delverane carries any scene she's in with her established voice, narrating her own observations half to herself, chart-ink on one finger. Full writeup, npcs/Aurel Delverane.md.

Captain Thalassa, the Windward Edge's own raider

Triton, once a guardian of the deep water bordering the Elemental Plane of Water, now leading a small renegade crew (encounter table entry 6 above) out of the same desperation the Stilling runs on: her own waters receding, the Houses' fixed-toll economy never caring about triton territory to begin with. Piracy as a furious, deliberate way of making the surface world look, a real "problem, not a villain." Physical tic: her trident is scored with tally marks, one for every ship she's stopped, most not destroyed. Voice tic: formal, clipped, old guardian-oath cadence even mid-raid ("by old oath, surrender your cargo"). Her vessel: the Drowned Oath.

Orena's Library Research order (rules/Bastions.md) already covers House Nightbreeze, House Carrow, and the Stilling as topics, worth reminding the table this exists for the Nightbreeze offer, the Carrow-adjacent corridor marker, or anything else freshly live tonight.
