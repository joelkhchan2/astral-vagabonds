Threshold 20. Any single hit under 20 damage does nothing to her at all. Small guns bounce off clean. This is why the Helm's job is rarely "dodge everything," it's steering around the hits that actually count.

02Your Job in One Line

Flying the ship costs nothing. It isn't your action and it isn't your movement, it's free, every single round you're in the chair. That free action buys you one maneuver. You can also skip the maneuver entirely and do something else with your real action, fly straight and cast, fight, help another station, whatever the round calls for.

  • You pick exactly one maneuver per round, never more than one.
  • The maneuver lasts until your next turn, not the whole fight.
  • Nobody else can fly while you're in the chair. One Helm, one pilot, one maneuver.

03The Three Maneuvers

This is the whole menu. Read the catch on each one as carefully as the effect, the catch is usually the actual decision.

Defense

Evasive Action

Every attack aimed at the ship has disadvantage until your next turn.

The catch: the ship's own weapons also fire at disadvantage this round. Nobody can get a clean shot while she's juking. Call this when the ship is taking more than she's dishing out, not as a reflex.
Speed

Full Burn

The ship's speed doubles this round. Close distance, run down a target, or put real space between you and a fight you don't want.

The catch: attacks against the ship get advantage until your next turn. Going that fast makes her a much easier target to hit. Burn to close or burn to flee, never burn just because you can.
Grapple

Boarding Action

A contested Piloting or Dexterity check locks your hull onto a target your size or smaller, close enough for people to cross between decks.

The catch: both ships are grappled now, locked together until somebody breaks it. Neither side can Full Burn or disengage while the lock holds. You're choosing a boarding fight, not just a position.

04Which One, This Round

A quick gut-check, in order. Stop at the first one that's true.

  1. Are we losing the exchange, taking more than we deal? Call Evasive Action and buy the crew a round to turn it around.
  2. Do we need to close on someone, or get away from something? Call Full Burn, and own that you're a bigger target while you do it.
  3. Does someone need to physically cross onto that other deck? Call Boarding Action, and know neither ship is going anywhere until the lock breaks.
  4. None of the above? Fly straight, skip the maneuver, and spend your actual action on something else, a spell, a check, backing up another station. The Helm doesn't have to do a trick every round.

05Rules That Actually Bite

  • You can't hold a concentration spell while flying. Piloting takes concentration of its own. The chair and a concentration spell are mutually exclusive, pick one.
  • Taking damage can knock you out of the chair. A caster flying the ship is a committed decision for the round, not a free seat to duck in and out of.
  • Moving to a new station costs your movement, not your action. Switching seats mid-fight is legal and sometimes correct.
  • Estra flies by default. You're training up as the second pilot. Either of you can take the chair; it's never locked to one person.

07Why Any of This Matters

The real cost of a fight

The ship is tough. She shrugs off small hits and rarely goes down outright. What she racks up is a repair bill, 20 gp for every point of hull damage, due the moment you make port. A fight you win slowly can cost more than a fight you walk away from. Flying clean isn't caution, it's the account balance.

Winning isn't always shooting. Boarding, outrunning, or refusing a fight you don't need are on the table every single time, and they're usually the cheaper answer.
