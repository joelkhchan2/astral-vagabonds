What the Sky Just Did.

Session 1 ran hot and ended heavy: a real ambush, a boarding fight, a close void scavver, a star dying on watch. This one opens by deflating that on purpose, does its real business in the middle, and leaves session 3 room to be the genuinely relaxed one. Everything needed to run it tonight, without opening another file, is on this page.

Warmth first. The lockbox is the only cold thing in the room, and it stays quiet.

The one rule that outranks this page

Never decide for the players. Orena and Aerion are Gabrielle's and Priscilla's. This page gives situations, options, and spotlight opportunities, never what they buy, say, feel, or choose. Violet boxes flag exactly where the next move belongs to a player.

How to use this page

Plum blocks are GM-only. Blue Read aloud boxes are narration to say or paraphrase. The two lockbox handouts are live links in §07, pull them up on a second screen or phone to actually show the table. Narration follows rules/DMing Craft.md.

Cold open: the loot argument

Cuts hard against session 1's ending on purpose. No mention of the star yet. Open on the burn back to Bral, the fight hours behind them now, and let the first thing that happens be completely mundane and very funny.

Read aloud

The fight is hours behind you now, and the adrenaline's finally draining out through your feet. Someone's spread the take from the Grendel's Wake across the mess table: an oil that shines faintly slick even sitting still, a potion the color of old iron, a javelin that ticks very quietly to itself, and 1,021 gold counted into stacks that keep almost toppling in the ship's gentle drift.

The beats, in order

  • Aerion already has the ledger out, itemizing everything as it's handled, more running commentary than a scene of his own.
  • Finny immediately claims "finder's fee" on something he didn't find.
  • Brass wants the potion "for medical reasons," transparently not medical reasons.
  • Oz presents the loot like a small ceremony, utterly sincere, which somehow settles the room faster than anyone arguing did.
  • Flurry, still keyed up from a fight she wasn't part of, works her way around the room checking everyone over (nudging, sniffing, one dramatic huff), fixating on a minor scratch while ignoring an actual bruise on someone else.
The beat to land last

Somewhere in the middle: Aerion says something like "we are not naming the javelin," and someone immediately starts naming it anyway. Let that have its laugh. Then, last, after the joke's already landed: Flurry finishes her rounds at Aerion and holds up one paw, plainly asking him to kiss it better. She asks. What he does about it, in front of the whole crew, is entirely Priscilla's to play. Whatever he does or doesn't do, cut right there. No joke stacked on top of it.

03

Orena and Estra: what the sky just did

Orena caught a current dying on watch, session 1's closer. This is Estra's scene to receive it, not as an infodump. One mechanic, not a chain of them: Estra explains the what, never the why. She's certain about what she saw and honestly unsure what caused it, because her own crews had the art of following the living roads, not the science of why they moved.

Play this differently than session 1

The void scavver got Estra visceral and badly shaken, a wound touched by accident. Don't run the same shape twice. Tonight she's brisk, almost bored, treating a dead route as routine occupational math rather than a fresh nerve. The dismissal itself is the tell, a captain this good doesn't get that clinical about something that costs her nothing.

Read aloud: the helm room, late

Orena finds her where she always is at this hour: one lamp lit, a hand of solitaire going nowhere, the dented tin mug long since gone cold. Estra doesn't look up right away. She already knows why you're here.

1

She lets Orena describe it first

Drawing out a witness, not delivering a lecture

Doesn't rush to explain. Asks what she saw, exactly, in her own words. Estra confirms it rather than narrates it: yes, that's what it looks like.

2

The system is fine

First thing she settles

Because it's the thing that will eat at Orena worst if left open: nobody died there. Whatever world was on the other end of that light is having an ordinary day right now, sun up, people going about it, none of them touched.

3

What actually went dark was the road

The table-facing rule of the Guttering

Not the star, the current, the living glow that ran along it, the same light every ship out here steers by because there's nothing else to steer by. No fixed stars to shoot a bearing off, no dead reckoning, no backup method. Lose that glow and there was never a second way of finding the place again.

4

She closes it, not opens it

The one crack is non-verbal

Flat, closer to a foreman clocking a broken part than a captain confessing anything: this happens, it's not the first route she's watched drop off a chart, it won't be the last. No vague hint dangled. The tell: she's mid-hand of solitaire when Orena found her; she never finishes it, sweeps the cards before the game resolves, says she's turning in. She never leaves a hand unfinished. Gabrielle can clock that or not, nothing needs to be said about it either way.

Estra, plain and clipped"Systems don't die. Roads do. Somebody's sun is still coming up over there right now, same as always, and they've got no idea we just watched their whole world go quiet from a thousand miles off. Nothing ends out here. It just stops being findable."
Estra, closing the door"It happens. Not the first route I've watched fall off a chart. Get some sleep, you're not going to think your way out of it tonight."
Hand it back to Orena, within the beats above

She can push on the mechanics (what happens to anyone caught inside, whether it's ever come back) and get real answers. If she pushes on Estra personally, tonight is the night that door stays shut, on purpose, by a captain choosing not to hand her a second grief. If Orena asks why it happens, Estra can honestly say she doesn't know, only that it's happened often enough she's stopped calling it chance. That admission of not-knowing is the seed for Tier 2, not a dodge.

What this scene covers, at a glance
  • Confirms Orena's sighting was real without over-explaining the mechanism (the cause stays locked for Tier 2).
  • Establishes: a guttered system is alive and intact, only unreachable. "Cut off, not killed."
  • Establishes why there's no workaround: the living current was the only method there ever was.
  • Gives Estra a deliberately different reaction than session 1's scavver scene, so "Estra has history with this" doesn't repeat itself in shape.
  • Plants that history anyway, through one unexplained physical tell, not a spoken hint.
  • Models the DM's information policy for the whole arc: Estra knows symptoms, not cause.
04

Aerion: inventory, Tack, and Flurry

Three linked beats, not three separate scenes. They flow into each other over the course of the homecoming prep.

Inventory and the ledger

Aerion's natural spotlight: settling the loot from the cold open, reconciling the account against the 1,800 gp contract plus the ~400 gp discretion tip and the 1,021 gp hoard, and formally logging the lockbox into the Storehouse, the first real use of the room with his name on it. This is where the crew semi-officially decides who knows what about the box going forward.

What's already in the Storehouse

A good question to expect: it's the cargo hold formalized into a real Trade facility, not empty shelving. Dry rations and water in bulk, spare hull plate and rope, the usual spelljammer odds and ends, whatever's left of the Bottle Rocket-adjacent junk and ordinary trade stock Aerion's been turning over for side-gold. The lockbox and tonight's loot get logged here, which is what makes it feel real for the first time.

1

The stocking decision: three common items for the crew

Aerion's player picks

Let Aerion's player pick three common-rarity items to add to the Storehouse as shared crew stock, paid for like any other supply purchase (treat each as 50-150 gp). Pull from the table in §08, or let him name something not on the list and adjudicate it live. This is Aerion doing what he actually does, provisioning competently, and it's fun: hand three small magic toys to the whole crew and watch what happens over the next few sessions.

2

A quiet one with Tack

Why this pairing specifically

He saved the old ship's tattered flag and reattached it here; give Aerion a scene actually helping Tack with post-fight repairs, hull patching, a scorched line resecured, something with his hands. The pairing isn't arbitrary. Aerion and Tack are the two people on this crew who express devotion entirely through upkeep and competence, never through a word of it. Tack is oblivious to social cues in a way Aerion never has to armor himself against, so he can say the earnest thing Aerion would never let himself say first.

Tack, mid-task, unaimed at anyone"She held. She always holds. I don't know what I'd do if she didn't hold."

Let Aerion just be present for it. One competent, undemonstrative person letting another one's undefended love for the same object land on him without flinching.

3

Flurry, the private follow-through

Not a second ask

The cold open already spent the public version of this beat, the paw held up in front of everyone, whatever Aerion chose to do about it. This scene is what happens once it's just the two of them: the actual tending to the paw, or the actual conversation about why he didn't, no audience left to perform "purely practical" for. Give Priscilla a quiet moment to take it exactly as far as she wants, shaped entirely by how the public beat landed. The private half of one beat, not a new one.

05

Bastion actions: facilities chosen

Corrected canon: facilities are unpooled, each PC has their own two level-5 slots, four total. Orena took both Library and Arcane Study rather than choosing between them; Aerion's second slot is still open for a future session.

Storehouse · Aerion, chosen

Roomy, Trade order, no prerequisite. Turns cargo space into steady income by buying low and selling high on bulk goods, the mechanical engine behind the side-gold the crew already earns. Tonight's inventory beat is the room's first real use.

Library · Orena, chosen

Roomy, Research order, no prerequisite. Digs up information or lore, the direct mechanical version of "the survey work is secretly the investigation." Obvious hireling fit: Orena, chart room made literal. The pick with the most immediate plot traction, since Research orders are a clean way to formally resolve later "what do we actually know about X" questions instead of hand-waving it.

Arcane Study · Orena, chosen, needed a ruling

Roomy, Craft order, requires an Arcane Focus or a tool used as a focus. Nobody aboard is a true arcane caster, so this needed a table ruling to be legal at all: the ruling made was that the spelljamming helm itself counts, a magic item a spellcaster attunes to and channels power through daily. This formalizes the helm room Estra and Orena already use. Craft output: an Arcane Focus, a Book, or a Spell Scroll.

Aerion's second slot · still open

Armory, Barrack, or Sanctuary are the live candidates (rules/Rules & Systems.md §6). A future session's decision, not tonight's.

06

Back to the Rock of Bral

Session 1's fallout, mapped before the standard homecoming beats. Reefside was loud, a real ambush, a boarding fight, a void scavver feeding on a founding house's own checkpoint, so it shouldn't arrive at Bral silently. Weave these in as texture on the way through the port, not a briefing.

Reefside hasn't outrun them home

They're arriving at Bral roughly as fast as the news could, so whatever the dockside eventually says, the crew is close to the first real account to reach port, not the last to hear a warped version of their own story. Good texture: a regular at the Compass Rose or Sprat catching wind of something happening out past the Verge and pressing for details. What they choose to say, downplay, or leave out is what starts shaping the story everyone else eventually tells.

The Manifest Board update, as a public moment

348th to 345th, posted at the Charter Window, part vacancy (the Grendel's Wake gone) and part earned climb from Sealed and Sworn. Worth watching Estra read it as a real, mixed beat, some of this she earned, some of it she was handed. Finny throws a party regardless of which part he understands.

Bex Halloway's neighbours have opinions

And they're wrong in different directions. Rimmon Vex of the Cutlass Sunday reads her destruction as one less obstacle and says so with zero tact. Ollo Trask of the Quiet Sum is genuinely rattled, a friendly rival actually dying does something to the bottom of the Board's small social world. Neither knows anything real, this is rumor and reaction, not a resolution to her open fate.

A small, unsigned tremor from Vellum

Not a visit, not an accusation. Two words on a plain slip of paper that turns up somewhere it shouldn't, tucked into the ship's mail at berth, waiting on Aerion's ledger, slid under a door: the "WE KNOW" note. No signature, no further detail, nothing to act on. GM-side: Vellum's quiet watch testing a reaction, not the real conversation, still keyed to the crew's next completed contract. Play it for a genuine chill, then let it sit unresolved.

Standard homecoming beats

Payroll, no repairs needed

The Night Fury came out of Reefside without hull damage, so this is just the month's 600 gp operating cost, not a repair bill, before anything else gets fun.

The navigation-data check, run as a joint scene

Not Orena alone. Pulling her own survey back-catalog and cross-checking it against Meech's three delisted routes and the lockbox's Vellum correspondence is Orena's instinct, but the data half is Aerion's ledger, the same dying-frontier-routes pattern that's his thread too. Run it as the two of them at the same table, her charts against his numbers, each noticing a piece the other's method would've missed. Worth doing on purpose: this crew needs more scenes that treat Aerion and Orena as a working pair, and this is a built-in place to do it. Neither of them solves anything tonight, they notice it together, before anyone tells them to look.

  • The Sable Crossing, the old lane toward Amberwake. Delisted by a Pale Reach clerk, no reason given.
  • Kestrel's Ladder, marked "consolidated," but no consolidation filing exists to point to.
  • The Tallow Run, to a colony that's still filing provisioning orders. A live colony on a dead road.

Board, places, and stores

Pick a new contract

Any of the three new postings in §08. Quiet Water is the lowest-danger option if the table wants a genuinely light one to close on.

07

The lockbox, opened tonight

Wren Ostler, new this quarter at the Compass Rose, does the dispel. How much of the truth the crew gives her for it is the actual scene, she casts reliably but wants the real story as her price, not gold. The reveal itself is the beat to land, not what the crew decides to do about it.

Wren Ostler · the Compass Rose, new this quarter

A wandering hedge-mage who unmakes things for a living: wards, curses, locks, whatever somebody paid too much to put up and now needs taken back down. Middle-aged, sun-worn, never removes her gloves (a burn scar she won't discuss, from a job that went wrong young).

Voice
Talks to whatever she's unwarding like it's a spooked animal, low and steady, entirely unconscious of doing it.
Price
Doesn't take coin for the good jobs. She wants the story: how the ward got there, who set it, what it's protecting and from whom, told straight. Cheap jobs she'll take gold for; a 5d8 glyph on a case with a Sallow Chain house's fingerprints is not a cheap job, and she'll know it the moment she reads the working.
Tonight
Casts reliably, but not instantly and not for free. Once she's heard the whole story, she's heard it too. What she does with that afterward is an open question worth keeping open.
Play the chart before the letters

Something a crew can physically unroll and watch a route die in the spacing of the handwriting, no check required to feel that.

Inside the box

Two letters

A Registrar's genuine plea for a public advisory on the failing Amberwake corridor, and, over a year later, House Vellum's cold Notice of Record striking it from the chart, citing a reason (Archive policy 12.4, a lapsed certification) that quietly doesn't add up against the dates in the first letter. No reply to Marsh is ever shown.

📜 Show the letters

The Amberwake's retired chart

Heavily corrected across five years, the correction dates crowding tighter and tighter, weeks apart instead of seasons, before the final attempt trails off unfinished, never reaching the far side.

🗺 Show the chart
The seam, GM-side only, don't hand it over

Read together, the notice has cause and effect backward: the corridor died first and dragged House Carrow's business down with it, not the other way around, and nobody has lined up the two dates yet. The deeper truth, nowhere in either letter, ties House Vellum directly to profiting from Carrow's fall. Seed it; don't explain it.

Cut before the strategy conversation

Let the table sit with what they're holding and what it might mean. Whether they use it, sit on it, or take it to Estra is a thread for later sessions, not something to resolve tonight. This is the real cliffhanger, quieter than a fight, and it should be allowed to be that.

The three postings on offer

Quiet Water · 1,300 gp · 15 days · 3 tons

House Prynne wants their eldest, Isolde, chaperoned to a betrothal meeting at the Larkspur Concourse, well above anywhere this crew has docked. Complication: patriarch Osric Prynne has heard the Night Fury's name from somewhere he won't specify. The safe, low-danger pick. No qualifier, a nameless unranked hull is exactly what discretion-buyers want.

The Ledger Run · 2,400 gp · 20 days · 5 tons

A Delverane clerk needs survey backlogs hand-carried between two Windward Edge stations because the usual courier route "isn't resolving cleanly." Complication: the Cutlass Sunday has been shadowing couriers since word got around this crew got jumped and won. Real ship-to-ship danger, calibrated fair. Qualifier (soft): Sealed and Sworn plus Senna's personal vouching covers it.

Two Chairs Short · 5,600 gp total, ~2,800 gp to the crew · 22 days · 4 tons

A small outfit lost half its crew mid-contract and offers to split the fee for a second hull to run it jointly. Complication: it wasn't poaching, it's an unmarked boarding hull with grappling jaws hitting soft targets in the Tollmarch. Highest pay and most danger this rotation, calibrated survivable specifically because it's a joint fight. No qualifier printed, but word of last week's boarding win is why they asked this crew specifically.

Standing: Open Season · pay scales, never rotates off

The CIV's bounty on void-lane predators, proof by fin-clip. Gray 5 gp, Brown 250 gp, Night 750 gp, Void 10,000 gp plus a Board incident-log line. Always available, claimed whenever a scavver crosses their path. Play as unexamined normal work; the Tier 2 reckoning is a much later beat.
