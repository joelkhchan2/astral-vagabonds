# Session 5 -- The Quiet Verge

The crew dinner welcoming Tomlin, Judo, and Sable, a Float Market haggling spree, a derelict ship crawl that turned into the session's real fight, and two separate approaches from House Nightbreeze that both point the same direction. See `sessions/Session 5 Prep.md` for what was planned going in; this doc is the record of what actually happened, reconciled from the full transcript. **Open Water, Open Season still hasn't launched**, the crew is headed for the Windward Edge instead, picked up live at the table. See session 6.

## Time passed
The table set the pace itself: about a week of downtime between missions is the going rate for this crew, and the session opens roughly four days after the brown scavver kill that closed session 4.

## The crew dinner
Played largely as prepped, with the details the table actually generated:

- **Tomlin cooks the welcome meal**: a party tray of lasagna and cardamom-maple sticky buns, "from his homeland." His bag of holding is confirmed to be, functionally, a thousand-jar spice rack, so full the jars sit visible on top of the demiplane rather than inside it. Brass tastes the lasagna, declares it the best thing she's ever eaten, and visibly tears up walking away.
- **Estra toasts all three new hires** by name (Sable gives no last name) and confirms all three are gunners.
- **Flurry inspects each new hire in turn**: sniffs Tomlin, sneezes, then dives headfirst into his spice bag until he fishes her out. Judo draws both blades on sight of her, sheathes them, and gives a full 90-degree bow; she bows back and approves. She bites Sable's shell experimentally and never really stops, by the end of the meal Sable's shell is just her scratching post.
- **Tomlin and Finny bond immediately.** Finny's first instinct is to ask what black market the spices came from ("you're not the first to have that conversation with me"); by the end of the meal he's got an arm around Tomlin calling him "kid."
- **Orena talks with all three new hires** in character, welcomes each individually, gets nothing deeper than surface pleasantries (confirmed: at this point she doesn't know any of their motives or dreams beyond what the table already knows from session 4's hiring).
- **Tomlin, talked to at length by Orena**, confirms he's on his fifth "rumspringa" from the material plane, plans to eventually go home and become a chef like his parents wanted, and that his real quest is tracking down the recipe for an actual Heroes' Feast. He's on probation. He does not yet have a read on whether Orena counts as the hero from that feast; he's keeping an open mind.
- **Estra does the onboarding paperwork rounds** herself: pay, benefits, house rules about keeping the ship in order. **Wyndham Aldane is present but her observation period hasn't started yet**, she confirms it begins on the crew's next mission, not this one.
- **Flurry falls asleep in Tomlin's lap**; he doesn't move her all night and is still there, asleep, when the lights come back on in the morning.

## The Float Market
- **Scrolls and potions**, bought off a pair of plasmoid-slime merchants with stretched Flubber-like bodies: cure wounds and detect magic scrolls (300 gp after a 21 Persuasion check talked the price down from a baseline ask).
- **Goggles of Night**, haggled from 1,200 gp down to 850 after a help action and a 24, Orena's final offer (724 gp) fell short and the deal died there; she gave it up.
- **Orena's birthday**, revealed mid-haggle. Aerion covers her short 150 gp as a birthday gift, no strings, which she calls him out for softening on. Aerion also reveals he's been quietly keeping her second pair of slippers.
- **Aerion's Storehouse upgrade, confirmed and locked.** A 400 gp investment buys a standing stall in the Float Market. Going forward, his Trade-order roll (now covering the costume business specifically) is **1d20+5 for price, times 1d20+5 for quantity sold**, rolled once per visit back to the Rock of Bral (or on the road, once that's established at the table). It's public knowledge the goods come off this ship; it is not public knowledge Aerion makes them himself, only that he deals in them.
- **Reading the Ledgers, first use.** Combining the Storehouse and the Smithy, Aerion can now spend a Bastion action on a free Insight or Investigation check to find out business intel instead of rolling for straight income. First roll (10): the crew's next destination, House Vellum territory, is charted and "safe," so no price swings there; the general principle this reveals is that trade goods pay more the more dangerous or uncharted the destination, because rarity of access drives the premium.
- **Aerion crafts Flurry a cloak** from the 50 lb of Windsong fabric he'd been holding, a gift for her modeling ambitions. A strong roll against DC 20 lands it as a magic item: night-sky-colored with intricate holes positioned so Flurry's own scales read as stars through them. While worn, Flurry gets **+1 to Performance**. Orena gives it to her as thanks for covering the birthday shortfall; Flurry cries on the spot.

## Prudence Hale's offer
Approached Aerion and Orena directly after their shopping. Introduced herself fully: senior underwriter of House Nightbreeze, badge 9068, abacus in hand. Offered a signed check for **20,000 gp** for anything the crew had on House Vellum out of Reefside, correctly noted they'd been tailed. Aerion and Orena told her the evidence had blown up in the Reefside explosion (true) and otherwise had nothing to disclose (a lie that held, 24 against her Insight read). She concluded Estra plays things closer to the chest than she'd guessed, not that she'd been lied to, and left the offer on the table as withdrawn once declined, not standing. Reported straight to Estra afterward.

**Estra's reaction matters.** She confirms House Vellum is actively investing in repairing Reefside station, meaning the house hasn't let the incident go, and that the crew's own vision (the polyhedral dice) suggests Vellum doesn't know they have the chest, though that read could be dated now. She tells them she'd likely have made the same call, but that handing this off to anyone right now is too dangerous without knowing what it actually is. **Then she goes further than her usual pattern:** she confirms she and Aerion had discussed, before it fell through, buying a second charter and making him **operations manager between ships**, and admits, unprompted, that it's her private dream to one day captain a fleet again. Full beat: `npcs/Captain Estra Vane.md`.

**The chest moves.** Rather than carry it into Vellum territory, Estra entrusts her locked chest to **Vaelren Windsong**, in town a few more days, reasoning he's trusted and strong enough that nobody takes it from him. He agrees without hesitation. He also gives Aerion and Orena **one of his unicorn phoenix Fury's feathers** as a one-use sending stone (regrown feathers taken afterward bring the total to five).

## Sprat, and the rumor mill
The dockside orphan who trades rumors confirms, for a gold piece at a time: a tailing lady (Prudence) seen headed for the Compass Rose; "Vaelren's got a weird bird now" (Fury, misdescribed); a failed marriage proposal between the first mates of two unnamed crews at the Compass Rose (the woman from a ship called the *Velvet Choir*); and, unprompted, that "people have their eye on us," confirming Prudence's claim from earlier. New, minor hook: **Penny**, another rumor contact Sprat answers to or competes with, named but not yet built out.

## Uncle Vaelren, and Prudence's history
Orena and Aerion follow Prudence to the Compass Rose and find her already a drink deep with Vaelren. On a natural 20 Perception check he clocks them listening in and waves them over rather than making a scene. Prudence tries to read Orena directly (and does, landing a line about self-doubt that stings); Orena's own Insight on Prudence comes back blank, she's genuinely inscrutable.

**The family history, confirmed:** Prudence has been married four times across three men, divorced from all four, and one of the three was a man of House Nightbreeze, which is what makes her a known quantity to the Windsongs by marriage rather than blood, and specifically wary territory for Vaelren. His read on her, once she's gone: *"I trust her as far as I could throw her. Pretty far. But I don't throw women."*

## The derelict *Quiet Verge*
A small trader, drifted into a reserved berth under its own dead reckoning, registry intact as **the *Quiet Verge***. Oz and Judo come along as muscle. Both PCs' perception checks on approach confirm the ship is badly overdue for maintenance, rusted hull corners, unmaintained turbines, the kind of ship that shouldn't have flown in this state.

**Inside**, organic residue smeared across the deck leads room to room:
- **Galley:** cold, abandoned cookware, old claw marks on a cabinet, and a ration log reading week 9, holding flour and water steady with some to spare, a crew that still believed they'd make it to the next stop.
- **Crew quarters:** neatly kept bunks, and **two Grells** nested in a pile of bedding, woken by the party's approach. A real fight (both Grells down without serious injury, Orena's Javelin of Lightning doing the heavy lifting on the second). Found: a bunk roster (64 crew, a small ship for that number) and an unclaimed suit of **shepherd's chainmail** (+1 Animal Handling, disadvantage on Stealth).
- **Captain's cabin** (locked, broken in on an Athletics check): **Captain W. Ashby's final log**, the crew abandoned ship in two lifeboats after running out of water, set the *Verge* on a last-resort dead-reckoning course back to the Rock of Bral "in case anyone ever finds her." A torn scrap from a crewman named Wren insists they "did the math and did it right," not panic.
- **Bridge:** star charts still pinned to the last plotted course, the helm dead, no power left to attune to it. A rare **sextant** found here (used only when a crew no longer trusts its own charts). A second scrap of paper, in weekly log entries, tracks the ship's actual crisis: by week 6 crossings were running hours long against the charts; week 7, fewer stars in the sky; week 8, six hours long and unnervingly quiet; week 9, the charts stop meaning anything, the stars are moving, and then nothing. The same creeping astral wrongness the main Stilling mystery runs on, surfacing here as background texture rather than being named.
- **Engineering:** a lightning trap off the drained helm core catches Aerion for 12 (resisted) as he and Judo dive clear; Oz ducks out and back in. A maintenance log tracks the same decline from the inside, a coupling running hotter each day with nothing anyone could do about it from down in engineering.
- **Cargo hold:** the real fight. A **Megapede** (CR 11, well above the planned CR 7, this ran as the session's actual boss) had nested in the dragged-together cargo. A hard fight: its Life Drain necrotic bite nearly dropped Aerion outright, and **Oz took the hit meant for him** by swapping places on a reaction, absorbing 60 of his 82 HP across the fight and ending it in single digits. The party's heaviest hitters (Orena's Thunder Smite knocking it prone, Judo's guns finishing it) closed it out. Loot: the keys to the captain's cabin (found after the door was already broken), 300 gp of cargo (golden goblets), a **Cloak of Many Fashions**, an **Ensorcelled Missive**, a **Mace of Terror**, and a **Lantern of Revealing**. Aerion claimed the cloak and the missive; the mace and lantern went to ship stores unclaimed.

## Charting the Windward Edge
With both Bastion actions free, Orena rolled her Survival-based charting (21) for **189 gp**, then rolled it again out of curiosity for a comically bad 36 gp (two natural 1s on the multiplier roll). The DM narrated the actual mechanic of Astral Sea travel for the first time at the table: ships move by catching naturally occurring currents, and one working theory (not confirmed, deliberately left open) is that the currents are worn into existence by the accumulated memory of everyone who's traveled the same route before, the same thesis Wyndham Aldane is quietly chasing. The route led to the **Windward Edge**: a point where the Astral Sea's current collides with a portal into the Elemental Plane of Water, the collision reading as a waterfall running backward, upward, refracting into an aurora of green, violet, and silver-white light. Orena's chart of the route is sellable for **1,100 gp**.
