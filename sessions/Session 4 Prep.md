## 2. Cold open: the flashback, then home port and the hiring

**Open the session here, before anything present-day.** Not Aerion's private memory this time, a real shared cold open the whole table watches, the same move session 1 and session 3 both used to start on a charged image rather than a recap. The night Aerion actually chose to leave the family business, two beats:

**Beat one, the function.** A real House Windsong gala, big enough that the whole estate's lit for it. **The occasion isn't sentimental, it's practical:** Vaelros is courting support for Windsong's bid on the Board of founding houses' long-vacant fifth seat, House Carrow's old seat, empty since Carrow's collapse decades back (`factions/Houses & Holdings.md`). The sparring showcase at its center is the house's answer to being asked to prove its strength, literally: Aeric and Aeris both fight, Champion-clean, exactly the display the room is here to grade.

**It's also, as far as Aerion knows, a Tuesday.** Nothing marks this one as different from a dozen he's stood through before. Played for exactly that: nobody in the room, least of all him, has any reason to think it's the last one.

**Aerion isn't a guest here, he's staff with a family name.** He's running the gift ledger and the seating chart at a side table near the kitchens, the actual competence that makes the evening function, while his brothers do the part people applaud. Nobody thanks him for the ledger. Nobody's supposed to.

**Two guests worth playing, both planted for later, neither explained tonight:**
- **Factor Perrin Hale, House Vellum.** Here on behalf of a house that doesn't need to care who wins the seat, which is exactly why it can afford to be charming to everyone. Unhurried, soft-spoken, remembers a name on the second meeting rather than the first and makes a point of it. Exchanges maybe four sentences with Aerion over the gift ledger, pleasant, forgettable. **This is, in hindsight, the first time this table ever met anyone from Vellum.** Play him warm and harmless. He is neither, later.
- **Agent Vance Orleth, House Darkshade.** Sent to see exactly how hard Windsong is campaigning for the same seat. Dry, watchful, the sort of man who compliments the silverware while counting the place settings. **It's Orleth who suggests, lightly, to Vaelros, that a "friendly exhibition" between the houses' own blades would read well to the room**, test the hospitality, see what Windsong's bench looks like past the two sons everyone already expected to watch fight. Vaelros agrees instantly. This is where Aerion's token bout actually comes from: not generosity, a rival house's idea of a joke, and Vaelros never once clocks that it's being used on his own son.

**The exchange that lands the theme, before the bout, the only real conversation of the night:** Vaelros checks the ledger over Aerion's shoulder and says, not unkindly, entirely sincerely: *"Every guest in this room is a line in a column, son. Some pay out, some cost us, and the trick of running a house is knowing which is which before they've finished their drink. You have a good hand for numbers. Learn to have it for people too."* Aerion isn't asked if he wants to fight. He's told the room needs to see a Windsong blade, and he's the one spare.

**The bout itself**, against a Darkshade retainer named Oren Fitch, bored and unbothered, is short and almost polite. Fitch barely extends himself; the room's attention is already drifting to the next course. Aerion loses clean, no drama, nobody lingers on it. That's the whole insult: not a loss that costs him anything, a loss that costs him nothing because nobody was watching closely enough for it to matter.

**One small, unremarked detail, Aerion's alone to notice:** the Darkshade retinue's travel cloaks are a genuinely clever cut, weighted at the hem to hang right in zero-g without looking stiff. He says nothing. He remembers it anyway. *(A quiet, early, unforced seed for the costume business, never spoken aloud, don't explain it.)*

**The room empties.** Hale's already gone by the time it winds down, having said his goodbyes to Vaelros with a warmth that will look very different in hindsight. Orleth lingers a moment too long near the door, satisfied with something nobody else in the room has named yet.

**Beat two, the ultimatum, same night, right after, still raw.** Guests gone, Vaelros finds him alone. No shouting, flat and final: *"We move goods through open space. A man who can't defend what's his doesn't keep it long, that's not philosophy, that's arithmetic. Your brothers understand that. Whatever you're doing with Vaelren's blade, it isn't that."* And then, sharper, aimed at the one thing Aerion actually chose for himself: *"And look at where it got him. We eased him out for the house's own good, and you want to inherit his same end?"* The ultimatum: give it up, commit fully to the business, or lose his place in it.

**What's scripted versus what's open.** Vaelros's words in both beats, the brothers' postures (Aeric uncomfortable with a win he didn't ask for, Aeravellis visibly struggling, whichever of them actually fights Aerion if anyone does), the room itself, all written above, usable as-is. **Everything Aerion actually says or does in response stays open.** Don't script his words.

**Cut hard to the present**, the usual "years later" snap, no lingering transition needed. **Arrival back at the Rock of Bral, played warm.** Rank update lands here (see above), Finny's party, the usual homecoming texture. This is also where crew hiring actually happens, not improvised cold later.

**Where the hiring actually comes from, a very small crew scene first.** It's Brassica's birthday, or near enough that she's decided it is, which is itself a live argument aboard this ship: in the deep Astral, physical time and aging stop reading the same way, nobody's visibly older for the months they've spent out here, and the crew has never settled whether a birthday means anything once the body's stopped keeping score.

**The debate, quick, warm, nobody's actually mad:**
- **Tack** doesn't get it, flatly, no malice: you haven't aged a day since the Astral started doing whatever it does to people, so what exactly are the candles counting. Arithmetic, to her, same instinct as everything else she measures.
- **Finny** won't let that stand: it's not about counting years, it's about the day, and a crew that stops marking days is a crew that's stopped being people to each other.
- **Oz**, if he weighs in: giff don't mark birth, they mark commissioning, the day you were sworn in matters more than the day you were born. Offers it as simple fact, not a correction of anyone.
- **Estra** lets it run without settling it. If pushed, one dry line: the day doesn't matter, marking it does, now somebody pass the plate before Brass takes it personally.

**Brass ends the debate by making a cake,** which was never actually a question for her. It is, predictably, terrible: a worse-than-usual escalation of her existing cook-duty d4 (`npcs/Crew Roster.md`), the kind of bad that earns its own roll. **Everyone who eats a slice rolls a Constitution saving throw, DC 13, with disadvantage** (two things wrong with the batch, stacked). A failure is a rough few hours, poisoned, nothing lasting, nothing that needs a healer, just a bad night and a worse morning.

**The punchline is the point.** Somebody, Estra most likely, says out loud what's been obvious for a while: that's it, they're hiring an actual cook the moment they make port. **This is the in-fiction reason the hiring happens tonight**, not an abstract session-3 to-do item, a birthday cake that nearly put half the crew on their backs.

**Where they're found.** The Compass Rose board, a direct referral (maybe from Albus's crew, a nice callback if the table wants it), or just dockside, found asking around. Let the PCs' own approach decide this rather than scripting it, it only changes how many show up and in what order.

**Run it as back-to-back mini-interviews, three per slot, not two long scenes.** Nobody needs full depth live, each candidate below has one entrance beat and one natural follow-up moment, enough to improvise from cold. Run as many as the table has patience for; the actual hire is whichever one or two land, not a predetermined pick.

## 3. Social beat: Aerion's, at the Compass Rose

**Vaelren is at the Compass Rose, unannounced, when the crew comes through.** Aerion's uncle, trainer, and the model for his own restraint, never actually played at the table before, only referenced (`factions/House Windsong.md`). **The visual is the whole joke before he says a word:** a loud Hawaiian shirt, socks and sandals, nothing that reads as "space samurai" at all, a direct punchline against both the composed trainer Aerion remembers and the severe, buttoned Vaelros the table just watched in the cold open. He tells Aerion plainly that he wasn't driven out for failing to measure up to anything, he left on purpose, and he's happier for it.

**His actual lines, scripted, landing the takeaway directly at Aerion rather than just describing his own choice:** *"Your father called it getting eased out. I call it the best decision I ever made. There's no ceremony in it, kid, I just stopped showing up to things that made me miserable, one at a time, until none of them were left."* And when Aerion pushes back: *"Lost? No. I won. Nobody tells you winning looks like this, but it does."* Then, quieter, the real point: *"You've stood like that since you were twelve, I know, shoulders squared like the whole house falls down if you ever once unclench. Nobody's going to blame you for setting it down a minute. Not even you, if you let yourself."* And: *"You don't have to win his argument, kid. You could just stop having it. Be whoever you actually are with that sword, not whichever version keeps the ledger looking good on paper."* **Both Vaelros and Vaelren consider the outcome a total victory, just defined completely differently**, worth letting that sit unresolved rather than picking a winner.

**The present-day payoff, now carrying the full weight of the cold open:** the table just watched Vaelros call Vaelren a failure, that same night, years ago. Vaelren, sitting across from Aerion right now, genuinely happy, is the living, undeniable rebuttal to that framing, whether or not either of them says so out loud. **Everyone at the table feels this now, not just Aerion**, that's the whole reason the flashback opened the session instead of staying a private memory. Let it sit without over-explaining it. How Aerion actually reacts to Vaelren in the present stays entirely open, don't script his words.

## 4. Social beat: the Carrow letters, and Estra's own question hanging in the air

**Estra already knows the note was a bluff.** What's still unresolved, the real content of this beat: the Carrow/Amberwake letters themselves, and Estra's own pointed question from that conversation, whether there wasn't something more Orena had wanted to ask the dodecahedron. She hasn't pressed, but she asked on purpose, and it's sitting there unanswered.

**A joint Orena/Aerion scene, the two of them alone with what's still unshared.** Nothing forces a decision here, the point is surfacing the question out loud between the two of them before it has to be answered to Estra directly.

**What's actually at stake, for the scene, not to be stated outright:** the letters tie House Carrow's fall directly to House Vellum profiting from it, decades before any of this, and Estra's own locked star chart already carries unexplained Amberwake annotations (`factions/Houses & Holdings.md`). Handing this to her means handing her a piece of her own fleet's history she's never had. Not telling her means answering her direct question with another evasion, which she'll likely clock.

**Branch points:**
1. **Do they even agree with each other.** Aerion's read (practical, political, a Windsong son who grew up around exactly this kind of institutional maneuvering) and Orena's read (more instinctive, protective of Estra specifically) don't have to land the same place. Let them actually disagree if the players want that.
2. **If they decide to tell her:** a real scene, played straight, not resolved off-screen. Estra's reaction is hers to have, not scripted here, but the material she's reacting to (Carrow tied to Vellum, decades deep) is heavy enough to earn a real beat, and it directly answers the question she already asked.
3. **If they decide to wait, or to answer her question some other way:** that's a legitimate, standing choice, not a failure state. Note it plainly as still-open going into session 5.

### The top 3 (full depth)

**A. Dressing the Part · 2,500 gp · 20 days · 2 tons**
A minor noble house needs a reputable, prestigious charter to carry the family to a grand event well off the common lanes: a confirmation gala at a marble station, a houses' summit, a season ball where the guest list is the whole point. *Complication:* the crew is 340th on a Board that counts, flying a hull under a borrowed name. The job is to *pass*: keep the rank buried, keep Tack from introducing Deborah to the valet, make the tattered flag read as heritage instead of salvage. **Qualifier (soft, and the whole premise):** the posting technically asks for "a reputable, established charter." The family's budget doesn't stretch to one, which is the only reason this crew got the call, and passing in person is the job.

*Spotlight:* Aerion's real poise, the family game he actually knows how to play, lands as pure competence this time rather than pain, a direct tonal counterweight to the Vaelren/Vaelros scene the same session. Oz's flawless manners carry the whole imposture, a rare real spotlight for him this session. Good money for farce, with a real bite if the mask slips.

**B. Last Known Position · 2,800 gp · 14 days · real danger, a rescue, not a courier run**
The *Second Wind*, a small independent trader captained by **Dez Harrow** (crew of four), went silent two days ago near a dead-hulk field, hasn't answered a single hail since. The client is **Oskar Venn**, Harrow's business partner on the mainland, worried and paying well for someone to find out what happened and render aid if there's aid left to render. **Not a social grudge, not another Manifest Board rival**, the danger here is a **Night Scavver** (CR 5, verified, `references/Astral Sea Bestiary.md`, "the one you use when you want a scary scavver fight"): AC 15, HP 105, Speed fly 60 ft, Shadow Hunter (advantage on Stealth in the dark, advantage against a surprised target), Multiattack two Bite (+7, 16 (2d10+5), DC 14 Strength or knocked prone).

**The stricken ship is mostly intact**, but something's latched onto it and kept anyone aboard pinned down. **C. Open Water, Open Season · 3,100 gp · 20 days · survey plus bounty**
**The posting comes directly from Aurel Delverane's own survey office at the Farthing Station** (`locations/Worlds & Places.md`), not an anonymous clerk. The Windward Edge's standing surf, the wall of silver light where two currents shear, has been creeping inward, the Guttering wearing its most beautiful face, and it's made a specific, previously-reliable stretch of chart unreadable. Delverane wants to know whether the old charts can still be trusted along that stretch, and Orena is the one who can actually answer that. A standing CIV Open Season bounty rides alongside on any scavvers encountered (gray 5 gp, brown 250 gp, night 750 gp, per the Contracts board), incidental to the survey, not the job's point.

**What's actually out there, concrete, by DC tier (Survival/Nature, per `Bastions.md`'s cartography rules):**
- **DC 10:** an ordinary, usable lane, low value (50-300 gp).
- **DC 13:** the lane brushes a patch of visibly thinning Tide, a small, real Guttering symptom, mid value (300-1,500 gp). Orena's own call whether the writeup she sells is honest or softened.
- **DC 16+:** the thinning traces to a physical marker, an old corridor signpost from a route the Charter Window delisted, unprecedented value (1,500-10,000+ gp). Quietly the same pattern Meech has been privately tracking (`locations/The Bral Reach.md`), pure color unless the table chooses to pull on it, never forced this session.

**Why this one's interesting:** it's the first job built specifically to let Orena's new Library mechanics (Research and the cartography table) actually carry the session's main content instead of being a bastion-turn aside. A real DC-scaled charting roll, a real payout tied to what's actually out there, and her own call on whether to sell the data or hold it.

**Aurel Delverane, fleshed out, since this is her first real on-screen scene, not just a name on a posting.** Thirty-one, younger daughter of a founding house that's never quite forgiven her for choosing fieldwork over a boardroom. Runs survey and acquisition out of the Farthing Station personally, hands dirty, not delegated. **Voice tic:** narrates her own observations out loud, half to herself, half to whoever's nearby, a habit from working alone too long. **Physical tic:** chart-ink on at least one finger, never quite washed off between legs. **Want:** to be the one who's right about the Edge's surf creeping inward before anyone senior takes it seriously. Per existing canon, she and the crew are "neighbours and neither knows it yet," she works the Windward Edge, they work the Verge, and she's flagged as the single most likely person in Wildspace to become a real ally. Worth letting this job be the actual first meeting, not just a posting with her name on it.
