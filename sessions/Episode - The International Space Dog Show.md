# Episode: The International Space Dog Show (future hook, drop in whenever it's earned)

> **A short, warm, fully optional scene or half-session.** Not on the contract spine, no complication required. The lightest possible palate-cleanser: pure found-family payoff for a bit the table has already been building since session 1. Tone: PG, silly, and completely sincere about it. See `npcs/Flurry.md` for the standing hook and `npcs/Crew Comedy Bits.md` for the outfits gag it grows out of.

## Where this comes from

Session 1, at the Float Market: Aerion had a tailor at Bral making Flurry custom show outfits (glitter and non-glitter versions of a matching bow tie for himself), and floated, off-hand, entering her in the **International Space Dog Show.** Nobody has followed up on it yet. That's the point, it's been sitting there, warm and a little ridiculous, waiting for a session with room to breathe.

**Don't force it into a plot.** This isn't a mission and it doesn't need a complication bolted on. Run it when the table has just come off something heavy (the lockbox, a hard fight, a genuine loss) and needs one scene that is purely, uncomplicatedly good.

## The pitch

The Show comes to the Rock of Bral, or somewhere on the crew's route, once a year: a travelling exhibition of pets, mascots, and "companion creatures" from every corner of Wildspace. Full program in `sessions/handouts/dog-show-program.pdf`. Run by the **Astral Kennel Concordance**, founded some one hundred and eighty years ago by the astral elf **Ophelia Rask**, who grew tired of watching the founding houses turn every gathering into a negotiating table and wanted one event where that was expressly, formally forbidden. She presides still, ancient and sharp, and the no-business rule on the judging floor still holds, by long tradition rather than any enforceable law. It is exactly as self-important and exactly as beloved as a real dog show, transposed wholesale into the black.

**Nine categories, two lanes, three entries each.** Physical, bracketed by size class (Tiny through Huge): Best Presentation (House Delverane), The Long Run (House Stoneward), The Proving Round (House Stoneward), Strength of Pull (House Stoneward), Fastest in Show (House Delverane), The Cargo Run (House Stoneward, hauling sandbag loads post to post). Open field, species and size both irrelevant: Most Convincing Trick (House Vellum), Best Costume (House Nightbreeze, Aerion's own natural category to care about), Best Vocalization (the Concordance itself). Every entrant, Flurry included, may register for up to three, never more. Best in Show isn't entered directly, Ophelia Rask chooses it herself from among the category champions, a real 20,000 gp purse this cycle, sponsored by Lord Wendell Carraway. Longest Serving Ship's Animal is honorary, presented rather than competed for.

**Four founding Houses sit active judging seats: Vellum, Stoneward, Delverane, Nightbreeze.** The fifth chair, House Carrow's, sits ceremonially empty at every cycle's judging table too, the same "dusted weekly" empty seat the Cartógrapha itself carries (`factions/Houses & Holdings.md`), never explained to anyone who doesn't already know the story. Windsong doesn't get a seat, they haven't won that chair yet. Two panels of three, no judge crosses lanes: physical lane is Magistrate Yevelda Thorn (Delverane), Commodore (Ret.) Ossian Vale (Stoneward), and Baroness Isolde Fenmark (independent, pedigree-obsessed); open field is Madam Quill (Nightbreeze), Factor Renshaw Vell (Vellum, judges by precedent and "the record"), and Professor Aldric Wren (independent, hard to impress with anything derivative).

**Real mechanics for every category**, an Animal Handling, Performance, Acrobatics, or Athletics check against a 12/15/18 DC ladder (ordinary placement, a real contender, a genuine shot at winning), the Proving Round running 13/16/19. Best Costume stays unrolled, judged on craft and the entrant's own evident tolerance for wearing it. A failed check is never disqualification, just an ordinary showing.

**A real field, not just color: 31 entrants.** Full roster and the ten-year list of past Best in Show winners in the program PDF; several past champions are still actively competing this cycle. Worth naming here: **Plume**, a smoke dragon wyrmling visibly older and bigger than Flurry, far more theatrical, handled by the status-obsessed **Barnaby Voss**, who narrates her accomplishments to anyone standing nearby whether they asked or not. Entered in Best Presentation, Most Convincing Trick, and Best Costume, the same lane Flurry's most likely to compete in. **A real, standing rival, the Aerion/Flurry-side mirror of Verity Gilt and Paragon**, condescending toward smaller and younger competitors in a way that reads as entirely practiced.

**The halftime show: a kindori splash show**, a trained young pod performing in a purpose-built elemental-water arena, real cosmic wonder (now fully consistent with the Tide's own settled canon as literal elemental water, `campaign/Story Spine.md`), and a genuine beat for Orena specifically even inside an Aerion-centered episode. Two smaller acts also run: the Windward Wings (a synchronized aerial flight troupe) and the Space Clowns (the established campaign bit, played straight, genuinely funny with a controlled edge).

**Who else is there, real continuity rather than new faces:** the *Fair Return* crew (Marlow Albus's) with **Tam Oakes** and their plasmoid pet **Puddle**, echoing Ormund and Pell Vey from the Float Market; the Weatheralls' *Kettle* with a pet of their own; Rimmon Vex's *Cutlass Sunday*, entered in nothing, present anyway, a natural source of friction; a real top-ten Manifest Board name, pick live from `factions/The Manifest Board.md` rather than a pre-written one; and, confirmed, **Uncle Vaelren, with Fury the unicorn phoenix**, finally paying off the standing banked thread (`npcs/Aerion Windsong.md`, `factions/House Windsong.md`), "the two pets actually meet." Land this one deliberately, not as a throwaway cameo.

**Vendor row and on-site services** include grooming stalls, a merchandise row, concessions, and, if the crew took the on-site costume designer offer (`sessions/Session 5 Prep.md` §5), **Aerion's own commission booth**, meaning his previously secret business is now operating in full public view for the length of the show.

**A light conspiracy menu, none forced:** a pickpocket ring working the crowd, a judge quietly leaned on by someone (Rimmon Vex is the natural candidate), and a House representative (Nightbreeze or Vellum) visibly watching the crowd rather than the competition, letting the Nightbreeze/lockbox tension from `sessions/Session 5 Prep.md` §3 cast a distant shadow without forcing it into a light episode. A short table of "dirty tricks" (bribing a judge, swapping a rival's grooming kit, planting a rumor, misfiling paperwork), each with a DC to pull off and a separate DC to be caught, same petty, funny, nobody-actually-gets-hurt register as the posting below.

**Flurry is, obviously, not a dog.** She is also, obviously, the most interesting thing anyone at this show has seen in years. Play the mismatch for comedy: judges who don't know the etiquette for addressing a silver dragon, other entrants (an astral hamster, somebody's void-adapted mastiff, a giff's ceremonial warhound) who either adore her or are extremely nervous, and Aerion, coldly insisting the whole time that entering her is "purely practical, for the tailor's investment."

## Running it

**Aerion leads.** This is his bit and his character beat: the shadow-king who does the ridiculous, sentimental thing and will not admit to a single feeling about it. Give Priscilla the negotiation with the judges, the paperwork (there is, delightfully, paperwork), and the moment someone calls his bluff kindly ("you knitted the bow tie yourself, didn't you") and he has to decide whether to lie about it.

**Orena and the rest of the crew are the gallery.** This is a spotlight-sharing scene by nature, everyone gets a beat: Oz intimidating a rival entrant's owner into a friendly truce, Finny running odds on the categories, Brass grudgingly admitting Flurry's coat looks good, Tack narrating Flurry's "training regimen" (there wasn't one). Let the table find the jokes; don't script them.

**Flurry cannot actually compete the way a real dog would**, she's two years old, easily overwhelmed, and the *Devoted*/non-combat rule in `rules/Stat Blocks.md` still applies, which rules out The Proving Round specifically, not the whole show; the "she's a baby, not a prop" spirit should govern whichever of her three categories actually get picked. The warmest version of this episode has her freeze up, get scared of the crowd or the judging platform, and the crew's real win is comforting her, not placing well. **A category she wins by accident, doing something unglamorous and true to herself, beats a category she wins by performing.**

## The beat to land on

Whatever else happens, end on Aerion. Whether Flurry places, panics, or simply refuses to leave Orena's side the whole time, give Priscilla one clean, quiet moment where the "purely practical" mask slips exactly once and the table sees, unmistakably, how much this actually mattered to him. Then let the crew never bring it up again, on purpose, the same way they never mention the outfits.

## The pseudonym (planted session 4, cash in whenever this runs)

**Finny's idea, not Aerion's.** The moment entering becomes a real possibility, Finny points out the obvious problem: the Windsong name on an entry form for a dragon in a novelty pet pageant is exactly the kind of thing that gets back to Aerion's father. The fix, in Finny's mind, is a fake identity, and he pitches names on the spot, rapid-fire, delighted with himself, getting worse as he goes: *"Chauncey Glimmerbottom." "Sir Reginald Frostwhisker the Third, Esquire." "Just Clancy. Clancy's clean. Nobody suspects a Clancy."* Whether Aerion takes one of these, rejects all of them, or produces something even worse out of pure spite is his to land, not scripted here. If a pseudonym actually got chosen, use it on the entry paperwork when this episode runs; if the table never landed on one, Finny can just pitch a fresh batch live.

## Adjustable knobs

- **Standalone cold open or full half-session:** works as a five-minute scene between other business, or as the entire lightweight session if the table needs one.
- **A running rival:** for continuity, Flurry now has one. See below.
- **A soft plot hook, if you ever want one:** one of the judges or exhibitors is from a settlement the crew hasn't met yet, or recognizes a species/heritage marker on Flurry that raises a quiet question (never an answer) about her lunar blood. Entirely optional; the episode works with zero plot content at all.

## The rival: Verity Gilt

A recurring show veteran, the kind of person the Show exists for. Three consecutive category wins with her void-adapted mastiff, a wall of ribbons at home, and a genuine terror that this is the year the streak ends. **Voice tic:** talks about her own dog like a portfolio, "proven bloodline," "the grooming budget paid for itself again," warmth switched on and off for whoever's watching. **Physical tic:** straightens something on herself the instant she's rattled, a cuff, a pin, a stray hair, never the dog, always herself. **Want:** not the ribbon. To still be the one everyone else measures themselves against.

**Her mastiff, Paragon.** Enormous, immaculate, void-black coat groomed to a shine, genuinely a good dog underneath the show polish. Not a villain, just an animal doing what he's trained to do, and part of the comedy is that Paragon and Flurry would probably get along fine if nobody's owner were involved.

## The petty rival posting (job board twist, drop in whenever it fits)

**A companion contract, not a replacement for the scene above.** Somewhere on the rotation, before or during a Show stop, a posting goes up that doesn't say what it actually is any more than BoyBoy's cookie job did.

**"Wanted: Discretion."** No payout printed, negotiable in person. *"A client with an interest in the outcome of the International Space Dog Show seeks a capable, close-mouthed crew to ensure, by any means necessary, that a certain entrant does not place this year. Discretion paid at a premium."*

**Verity Gilt is the client**, and she will not say the word sabotage out loud, not once, she calls it "levelling the field" and means every syllable of the euphemism. Meeting her is its own scene: gracious, precise, name-drops judges she's cultivated for years, and never quite explains why this particular entrant has her rattled enough to hire outside help for the first time in three seasons.

**What "any means necessary" actually means at this table**, since nobody is hurting an animal to win a ribbon: bribing a judge, swapping Paragon's rival's show treats for something merely unflattering, starting a rumour about a competitor's grooming regimen, misfiling a rival's entry paperwork, staging a small, harmless mishap at exactly the wrong moment. Petty, funny, and completely beneath the dignity of everyone involved, which is the entire joke. **Keep it there.** This posting never gets a hard version the way BoyBoy's does; the whole bit is that the stakes are this small and everyone's still treating them like they're enormous.

**The reveal, if Flurry's entered:** somewhere in the meeting, Verity produces a program, or a rumour, or a judge's shortlist, and the entrant she wants discouraged is **Flurry**. Let this land as its own beat, don't rush past it. She has no idea whose dragon she's asking the crew to sabotage; the posting went up before she ever learned the ship's name. That's the whole gag: a crew took a job to knock out their own dog.

**Hand it to the table, don't resolve it for them.** Once the reveal lands, what happens is entirely theirs to decide, not a script: keep the fee and throw the whole thing on purpose, keep the fee and quietly do nothing, tell Verity exactly who Flurry belongs to and watch her recalibrate in real time, or turn the sabotage around on Paragon out of spite and then feel bad about it. Any of these is a good scene. None of them is yours to write in advance.

**If Flurry isn't entered yet**, the posting still runs fine on its own, the client wants some other unlucky entrant discouraged and the crew gets a small, silly moral-hazard job with no personal stake. Bank it now, cash the Flurry version in whenever she's actually competing.

**Aftermath:** however it lands, Verity remembers. A loss to underhanded means still stings, a refusal still earns a strange respect, and either way she's a standing rival now, worth a cameo at the next Show stop, sharper, better prepared, still absolutely certain she's the injured party.
