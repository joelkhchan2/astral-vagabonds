Three Chairs at the Board.

Session 2 ran quiet on purpose. This one hands the table a real, live choice, three postings, one pick, made cold at the Manifest Board with no steer toward the biggest number. Whichever they take, the rest of this page is built to run without opening another file. Only one of the three job sections below will ever be live in a given sitting, use the tabs.

The table picks the danger tonight. Don't pick it for them.

The one rule that outranks this page

Never decide for the players. Orena and Aerion are Gabrielle's and Priscilla's. This page gives situations, options, and spotlight opportunities, never what they buy, say, feel, or choose, and never which job they take. Violet boxes flag exactly where the next move belongs to a player.

How to use this page

Plum blocks are GM-only. Blue Read aloud boxes are narration to say or paraphrase. §04 is tabbed, click the posting the table actually takes and ignore the other two, they're there for reference, not to be run in parallel. Narration follows rules/DMing Craft.md.

03

Astral sea event: the transit

Roll a d8 at the table, player's choice who rolls. Not a DM pick, let the dice decide what the black throws at them this leg. Each entry runs in ten to fifteen minutes with everything needed already in it. Spans the tone compass on purpose: 1 and 8 warm, 2 dread, 3 and 5 danger, 4 and 7 wonder, 6 eerie.

1. Formation flying · warm

Aldous hails for echelon drills, both ships flying a staggered diagonal so their gun arcs overlap. Whoever's flying the Night Fury holds position relative to a moving ship, DC 12 Vehicles (Space) to settle into formation, a second DC 12 when Aldous calls a turn. Succeed both and the Fair Return's crew notice, Perrin Oakes calls it "clean work." Fail and it's just a flatter drill, a laugh, no consequence. Tack narrates the Fair Return's engine note back like a doctor reading vitals.

2. A prior kill · dread, with a name attached

A drifting scrap field, recent, the same grappling-jaw damage the Fair Return already carries. In the wreckage, unrelated to Aldous's own people: bolts of undyed cloth, a crate of oranges gone soft, and a crewman's logbook cut off mid-sentence ("--nothing on the scope, Rell thinks it's just--"). Aldous goes quiet over the comm before anyone asks. Awe half alongside the threat half, the ordinary cargo and the unfinished sentence make it land as real.

3. A gathering of gray scavvers · danger, as omen

Reuse rules/Tables.md table 1, entry 6. Gray Scavvers (stat block below) are harmless, common shoal-cleanup fauna, per references/Astral Sea Bestiary.md. What makes this one worth a result: a shoal of thirty or more, far past ordinary. A gathering this size is a warning sign, they collect where something died. Atmosphere, not a fight, they scatter at the ship's approach. What they were gathered around is the actual content of the scene.

4. A kindori pod · wonder, loaded for Orena

Reuse rules/Tables.md table 1, entry 2. A full pod (six to nine adults) this time, not the distant single sighting from session 1. One carries a colony of small, barnacle-shelled driftlings, filter-feeding on stardust, their lattice nest catching the light like a second structure. Kindori aren't background wonder to Orena, a dying one's death-song took her original crew. Let it land beautiful first. DC 13 Nature or Arcana surfaces one true detail, nothing that resolves the Vocalise.

5. Two brown scavvers · real danger, a genuine complication

Reuse rules/Tables.md table 1, entry 6. A step up from entry 3, this is a real encounter. Two Brown Scavvers (stat block below) shadow the ships at a distance, closing slowly. Per their design note, they won't attack unless provoked or starving, a warning shot or a show of force (Oz visibly armed on deck) drives them off, which is the intended, rewarded outcome. If it goes hot, it's a real fight, CR 4 apiece is a genuine threat to a PC caught alone, but not campaign-defining.

6. A reciprocal ship · eerie

Reuse rules/Tables.md table 2, entry 5. Another vessel, identical class, on the exact opposite heading. Nobody aboard either ship waves. No hail answers. It keeps its exact course and lets the gap close and widen without incident. A closer look makes it harder to describe, not easier. No danger, no chase, no explanation. Let it pass. Resist the urge to attach a reason to it.

7. An ancient object · astral wonder

Reuse rules/Tables.md table 2, entry 8. A perfect stone dodecahedron, roughly the size of the Night Fury's own hull, carved edge to edge with glyphs in no known script, worn soft the way stone wears, not metal. Radiates a faint, even warmth, no magic aura, no visible seam or entrance. DC 15 Arcana or History surfaces one true detail: the glyph motifs echo half-remembered Astral Sea legend, older than any current house. Leave it exactly this unresolved.

8. A mercane trading vessel · warm, loaded for Aerion

Reuse rules/Tables.md table 1, entry 14. A mercane vessel signals a standing offer, not a hail, hold heading, they'll come alongside. The inverse of Aerion's usual haggling scenes: mercane don't negotiate, the price is the price, stated once, flat, unbothered whether the crew buys anything. A genuine, rare chance at uncommon and rare stock, the kind The Bral Reach.md keeps off the regular market on purpose.

04

The job: Two Chairs Short

Three standalone branches, kept as tabs. Two Chairs Short is tonight's job, already chosen at the table. Quiet Water and The Ledger Run stay fully built and banked for a future rotation, reference only.

No combat content by design. Steer toward this one if the table wants a genuinely light night.

OSRIC PRYNNE · the client

Patriarch of House Prynne, a Chain merchant family with old manners and thin margins. Voice: speaks entirely in appraisals, "that'll do," "acceptable," "below the mark," never a plain yes or no. Tic: checks a brass chronometer he doesn't need to check, a tell for nerves he'd never admit to. Want: this match saves the family, and he knows everyone at the Concourse will know it too.

ISOLDE PRYNNE · who's actually being chaperoned

The eldest, pragmatic about the arrangement in a way her father mistakes for indifference. Watching everything, gathering information the way someone does when nobody's ever asked their opinion before. Want: to be taken seriously by exactly one person on this trip.

Why Osric's heard the name

House Prynne carries a Nightbreeze policy, and Nightbreeze's claims office has been buzzing about the Grendel's Wake's total loss and the unranked crew that walked away from it. Standard underwriter gossip, nothing sinister. Osric can't decide if hiring the crew that survived Bex Halloway is a good story to tell at the Concourse or a bad one.

Branch points

  1. The almost-mention. The crew lets it go (uneventful, warm trip) or presses (Osric admits the Nightbreeze source easily, nothing to hide, the scene becomes about why he's so nervous about status).
  2. A private moment with Isolde. She seeks out whichever PC has been paying attention to her, offers something real in return, Chain gossip, a genuine compliment, a small kindness. Let her be a person.
  3. At the Concourse, optional light color. A rival merchant house present, a status-comedy beat, nothing that needs to escalate.

No Reefside. The two archive stations are further out along the Windward Edge, unrelated to the ambush site.

RIMMON VEX · the Cutlass Sunday, rank 344

Sour, opportunistic, takes being passed on the Board as a personal insult. Not trying to kill anyone, trying to find out if the Bex fight was a fluke, and he'll take the survey data as a prize if he can get it without real cost to himself.

THE CUTLASS SUNDAY

A worse Swift Cutter, meaner than it looks. Enough to actually hurt the Night Fury if the crew gets careless, not enough to win a straight fight if they don't.

Branch points

  1. The approach. A direct heading past Vex's usual grounds (faster, on schedule) or a longer swing wide of it (safer, costs margin on the 18-day window).
  2. First contact. Vex hails before he commits, testing. Takes a bribe, backs off if he reads genuine confidence, presses if he smells hesitation. Let the crew's response decide what happens next, this is the scene, not the fight.
  3. If it goes hot. Real ship-to-ship exchange, side initiative, both ballistae live, Vex boards if he thinks he's winning. Same weight as the Bex fight, injuries possible. He breaks off once it's clearly not going his way, but that should cost the crew something to establish.
  4. Aftermath, if he loses. A real dent in his standing at the Compass Rose, a grudge earned rather than assumed. If he wins anything, play it as a genuine setback, not a wipe, he's a recurring face, not a boss.

Reframed: a peer asking for help, not an undercutter needing a favor. The outfit is Captain Marlow Aldous's Fair Return (Cutter-class, rank 331, above the crew on the Board), nine crew normally, five now, four lost this past month, none of it violent. Stoneward's licensing rule is the real reason he needs a second hull, full stop, no amount of competence from his remaining five gets around it. The losses just mean the ship is running lean and tired going in, not desperate.

What's actually being escorted · pick one, four tons either way

  • Raw starglass (default lean). Unrefined crystal, cut into helm cores later, valuable and easy to fence. Ties the raider's motive directly to Aeravellis's own fraud (factions/House Windsong.md), funding a desperate operation with no trail.
  • Refined navigational instruments. Fragile, absurdly high value per crate. Raises the stakes of careless handling in a fight, and gives Aerion's ledger-eye something to fuss over.
  • A sealed diplomatic pouch, correspondence for a founding house. A soft connective thread to the unshared Amberwake letters, if you want tonight to plant something rather than just pay out.
  • A locked medical shipment, antitoxin and draughts for a short colony. Shifts urgency from money to people, a real ticking clock beyond gold.

The client stays thin regardless, a competent, unremarkable concern past the Edge; this job belongs to Aldous and the two crews. Whichever cargo, it's the kind of thing that funds a desperate operation quietly, which happens to describe the raider's situation.

Why Aldous picked this crew specifically

He heard about the Bex fight secondhand, and he heard Finny's own line making the rounds, that they "jumped the Board without lifting a finger" (Session 2.md). He read that as exactly backwards. A higher-ranked captain seeking this crew out on reputation, not convenience, is a real beat, a live proof of the Board's own standing theme, the number isn't the story. That's the reason he'd give out loud. There's a second, quieter one underneath it, below.

Estra and Aldous: an old fleet, crossed without ever meeting

Aldous served as ordinary crew aboard a different Vane Compact vessel, never Estra's own ship, never crossed paths with her directly. His own captain, Oskar Bellamy, was the one who gathered the crew to say the Compact was over and they were released. Bellamy flew the hull independently a while longer, then sold it. Aldous bought it and became her captain. The Fair Return is that ship, an old Compact hull under a new name.

When the job's paperwork named the Night Fury's captain, Aldous saw the surname Vane and it gave him a private, unspoken hope, that's the real second reason he chose this crew. The confirmation isn't a name, it's the ship, Estra would know an old Compact-built Cutter's lines on sight. Her reaction to the hull, not to Aldous, is what lands it: a held look a half second too long, a hand that stops on a rail, nothing explained. Best saved for the rendezvous, the two ships' first real meeting. No dialogue needed, a look-and-move-on beat, not a scene that stops the session.

Optional, GM-only: if Estra's habit of checking on her old fleet's fate has ever touched Compact registry paperwork, she may have already seen Bellamy's name once, meaning nothing at the time. Cheap irony, not required. Bellamy himself stays a loose thread, retired, whereabouts unknown.

Who's gone

After two sessions with real violence in them, let this be the tonal counterweight, genuinely funny and a little sad rather than one more body count, none of it the raider's doing.

  • Sennet Price, former first mate/gunner. Poached mid-contract by a founding house's private fleet for real money. Left a two-line resignation note Aldous still has memorized.
  • Tamsin "Birdie" Voss, former junior deckhand. Homesick. Walked off at the last port, back to her family's orchard, no note even.
  • Corlin Dey, former rigger/engineer. A malfunctioning teleportation circle sent him several systems off course. Fine, as far as anyone's heard, hasn't found a ride back.
  • Mabb "Feathers" Corrin, former rigger. A travelling performance troupe flagged them down mid-transit and Mabb never got back on the ship. Aldous won't say which act.

Who's still aboard · stretched thin, running a nine-berth ship with five

  • Captain Marlow Aldous. Human, fifties, career escort captain, holding the crew together and insisting it's "within scope." Voice: contract language even off the clock. Tic: checks the manifest even when he knows who's on it, and isn't. Want: to stop losing people.
  • Perrin Oakes, first mate, stepped up from second when Sennet left. Steady, a little overwhelmed, holding the schedule together.
  • Dessa Kray, gunner. Sharp, businesslike, double-checks her equipment since the losses started and won't say why.
  • Wick Pell, engineer. Quietly exhausted, keeping nine-person systems running with five people's hands.
  • Tobin "Junebug" Ashworth, youngest, newest aboard, openly anxious. The clearest home for real danger landing on someone who isn't a PC.

Nobody's covering just their old job anymore: Perrin does first mate's work plus half of what Sennet used to handle; Dessa guns a mount built for two, alone; Wick pulls an engineer's watch and a rigger's watch on alternating days. The unspoken rule holding it together: nobody says "short-handed" out loud. Aldous set that tone without announcing it. Junebug is the one exception, young enough it hasn't fully taken with him, which is why he might say the quiet thing at the wrong moment, or the right one.

Relationships aboard · pick one or two, not all

Aldous and Perrin

Fifteen years apart in rank; Perrin was promoted into a job he isn't sure he's earned. Aldous trusts him completely and has said so once, badly, and neither's brought it up since.

Dessa and Wick

Not romantic, just old crewmates, built entirely out of shorthand and shared shifts. She checks his readouts unasked; he keeps a hot mug at her station unasked. The clearest surviving picture of the ship at full strength.

Aldous and Junebug · alternate

Less a friendship than a mentorship Aldous didn't plan on taking. Short with him in front of others, gentle alone, hasn't noticed the contradiction yet.

Perrin and Dessa · alternate

Professional respect with an edge, two people who'd have been rivals for the same promotion in a full crew and both know it. Never said aloud, not unfriendly, just present.

How the two ships actually operate together · live options, not a fixed procedure

  • Comms. Either a disciplined open channel checked at the top of every watch, or old-fashioned signal lanterns and flag code, no magic needed but vulnerable to weather. Sending Stones (mercane option 8, if bought) beat either.
  • Watch rotation. Either offset on purpose, the Fair Return's watch changing at the half hour against the Night Fury's so neither crew is ever both half-alert at once, or synced, more efficient but a real shared vulnerability window if the raider times it right.
  • Chain of command, if it goes hot. Either genuinely joint, Aldous deferring on tactics once he's seen them fight, or Aldous holding parity always, real ongoing friction. Either way, he does not defer on anything touching his own crew's safety.
  • Downtime. Either no formal docking, ships closing to signal range with a lit porthole visible across the gap, or one real shared meal, an actual docking and a crew-mixer scene, a warmer beat if there's room for it tonight.

THE RAIDER · unmarked, run off Aeravellis's spec sheet

AC15
HP250
Speed35 ft
Guns4 ballistae + jaws
Crew15

A converted Balanced Trader, comparable to the Night Fury alone, exactly why this needs to be a joint fight to feel fair. Unrelated to why Aldous is short-handed, this is simply what's waiting in the lane. It wants easy cargo, not a fight, and breaks off the moment the boarded target shows it can actually resist.

Branch points

  1. Getting the real story. Does Aldous admit up front he's down to five, or does it come out once the ships are underway? A good spot for a little comedy (Sennet's memorized resignation note) before the tone turns serious.
  2. The boarding. Let which ship the raider targets be a genuine open question. If it's the Fair Return, Junebug is the name to reach for, a real crewmate in real danger, not an abstraction.
  3. Resolution options. Fight it off together (the Night Fury's guns decide it, a real payoff if the Armory or Barrack order below finished in time), draw its attention onto the Night Fury on purpose (a real tactical choice), or a partial loss, cargo or coin taken, everyone alive. Don't railroad toward the clean win.
  4. Aftermath. Aldous says out loud he was right to come to this crew instead of someone closer to his own rank. That's the beat, not a party. Plants a genuine ally above the crew on the Board, opens a soft hook (does he go looking to crew back up to nine, and does this crew help), and quietly deposits another data point toward the eventual Aeravellis reveal (M6), don't name him, don't explain the hull.
05

Bastion action: Aerion's second facility

His pick alone, facilities are unpooled per PC. Best run whenever the voyage has a quiet beat, mid-transit, or back at port if the job doesn't leave room for it. Resolve the order too, not just the pick, Aerion gets one bastion turn this leg same as every leg.

Armory · live contender

Roomy, Trade order, no prerequisite. Stockpiles weapons and armor; a stocked Armory equips Bastion Defenders when the ship is attacked. Fits his combat-readiness angle directly, and pays off hard if the boarding lands on the Night Fury rather than the Fair Return. If the fight happens before a Trade order resolves, that's a fair complication, not a punishment.

Barrack · live contender

Roomy, Recruit order, no prerequisite. Houses Bastion Defenders, Recruit adds more up to the space cap. The most literal answer to "who's actually defending the ship." A Barrack that isn't garrisoned yet tonight is honest, this facility pays off in future fights, not this one.

Sanctuary · listed for completeness

Roomy, Craft order, requires a Holy Symbol or Druidic Focus. Orena's focus already qualifies for it and she took Library and Arcane Study instead. Weakest fit for Aerion specifically.

Don't decide it for him

Present Armory and Barrack as the real contenders. Let the pick say something about how he thinks about the ship's defenses now that Two Chairs Short has put a real boarding threat on the table. Neither facility can possibly finish in time to matter in tonight's fight if the boarding happens early, that's true to how Bastion orders work, don't fudge the timing to make it pay off early.
