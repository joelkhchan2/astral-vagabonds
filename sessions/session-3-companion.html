Three Chairs at the Board.

Session 2 ran quiet on purpose. This one hands the table a real, live choice, three postings, one pick, made cold at the Manifest Board with no steer toward the biggest number. Whichever they take, the rest of this page is built to run without opening another file. Only one of the three job sections below will ever be live in a given sitting, use the tabs.

The table picks the danger tonight. Don't pick it for them.

The one rule that outranks this page

Never decide for the players. Orena and Aerion are Gabrielle's and Priscilla's. This page gives situations, options, and spotlight opportunities, never what they buy, say, feel, or choose, and never which job they take. Violet boxes flag exactly where the next move belongs to a player.

How to use this page

Plum blocks are GM-only. Blue Read aloud boxes are narration to say or paraphrase. §03 is tabbed, click the posting the table actually takes and ignore the other two, they're there for reference, not to be run in parallel. Narration follows rules/DMing Craft.md.

No Board scene needed, the pick already happened at the table last session. Open on prep and departure instead.

Read aloud

Provisions stacked, a route already plotted for the Quiet Sum's rendezvous point. Four new faces are still learning where things live belowdecks, and one of them keeps calling the storehouse "the vault" no matter how many times he's corrected.

Good places to open

Departure prep, provisioning, the Burnouts settling into their new posts (a little awkward, a little too grateful, on purpose), or Aerion's second bastion facility decision (§04) if the table wants it up front rather than mid-voyage.

Whoever's quiet, lead with them

If the table wants a scene here

03

The job: Two Chairs Short

Three standalone branches, kept as tabs. Two Chairs Short is tonight's job, already chosen at the table. Quiet Water and The Ledger Run stay fully built and banked for a future rotation, reference only.

No combat content by design. Steer toward this one if the table wants a genuinely light night.

OSRIC PRYNNE · the client

Patriarch of House Prynne, a Chain merchant family with old manners and thin margins. Voice: speaks entirely in appraisals, "that'll do," "acceptable," "below the mark," never a plain yes or no. Tic: checks a brass chronometer he doesn't need to check, a tell for nerves he'd never admit to. Want: this match saves the family, and he knows everyone at the Concourse will know it too.

ISOLDE PRYNNE · who's actually being chaperoned

The eldest, pragmatic about the arrangement in a way her father mistakes for indifference. Watching everything, gathering information the way someone does when nobody's ever asked their opinion before. Want: to be taken seriously by exactly one person on this trip.

Why Osric's heard the name

House Prynne carries a Nightbreeze policy, and Nightbreeze's claims office has been buzzing about the Grendel's Wake's total loss and the unranked crew that walked away from it. Standard underwriter gossip, nothing sinister. Osric can't decide if hiring the crew that survived Bex Halloway is a good story to tell at the Concourse or a bad one.

Branch points

  1. The almost-mention. The crew lets it go (uneventful, warm trip) or presses (Osric admits the Nightbreeze source easily, nothing to hide, the scene becomes about why he's so nervous about status).
  2. A private moment with Isolde. She seeks out whichever PC has been paying attention to her, offers something real in return, Chain gossip, a genuine compliment, a small kindness. Let her be a person.
  3. At the Concourse, optional light color. A rival merchant house present, a status-comedy beat, nothing that needs to escalate.

No Reefside. The two archive stations are further out along the Windward Edge, unrelated to the ambush site.

RIMMON VEX · the Cutlass Sunday, rank 344

Sour, opportunistic, takes being passed on the Board as a personal insult. Not trying to kill anyone, trying to find out if the Bex fight was a fluke, and he'll take the survey data as a prize if he can get it without real cost to himself.

THE CUTLASS SUNDAY

AC14
HP190
Threshold14
Speed55 ft
Guns2 ballistae
Crew~6

A worse Swift Cutter, meaner than it looks. Enough to actually hurt the Night Fury if the crew gets careless, not enough to win a straight fight if they don't.

Branch points

  1. The approach. A direct heading past Vex's usual grounds (faster, on schedule) or a longer swing wide of it (safer, costs margin on the 18-day window).
  2. First contact. Vex hails before he commits, testing. Takes a bribe, backs off if he reads genuine confidence, presses if he smells hesitation. Let the crew's response decide what happens next, this is the scene, not the fight.
  3. If it goes hot. Real ship-to-ship exchange, side initiative, both ballistae live, Vex boards if he thinks he's winning. Same weight as the Bex fight, injuries possible. He breaks off once it's clearly not going his way, but that should cost the crew something to establish.
  4. Aftermath, if he loses. A real dent in his standing at the Compass Rose, a grudge earned rather than assumed. If he wins anything, play it as a genuine setback, not a wipe, he's a recurring face, not a boss.

Reframed as a joint job, not a rescue. The outfit is Ollo Trask's Quiet Sum (rank 347), the crew's cordial rival, who lost two of his four crew mid-contract and won't give up the fee, so he's offering to split it for a second hull to run it with him. Reversing the usual dynamic, the undercutter needing them for once, is the point. Play it warmly.

Built-in texture

Two crews, two chains of command, on one job. Trask is relentlessly cheerful and genuinely not used to taking orders from anyone, let that create small, funny friction (whose call is it when the two ships disagree) that doesn't need to escalate, just color.

THE RAIDER · unmarked, run off Aeravellis's spec sheet

AC15
HP250
Speed35 ft
Guns4 ballistae + jaws
Crew15

A converted Balanced Trader, comparable to the Night Fury alone, exactly why this needs to be a joint fight to feel fair. It wants easy cargo, not a fight, and breaks off the moment the boarded target shows it can actually resist.

Branch points

  1. Getting the real story. Does Trask admit up front what happened to his crew, or does it come out under pressure once both ships are already committed? A reluctant confession mid-transit hits harder.
  2. The boarding. The raider closes on whichever ship it reads as weaker, almost certainly the Quiet Sum, first. Real stakes on Trask's remaining two crew, named if you want the danger to land. This is where someone who isn't a PC can plausibly get hurt, and it should feel like it could go that way.
  3. Resolution options. Fight it off together (the Night Fury's guns decide it, good spotlight for whoever's gunning), draw its attention onto the Night Fury on purpose (a real tactical choice), or a partial loss, cargo or coin taken, everyone alive. Don't railroad toward the clean win.
  4. Aftermath. Strongest bottom-of-the-Board friendship beat available with Bex's slot gone quiet. Also quietly deposits another data point toward the eventual Aeravellis reveal (M6), don't name him, don't explain the hull, let the pattern exist for later.
04

Bastion action: Aerion's second facility

His pick alone, facilities are unpooled per PC. Best run whenever the voyage has a quiet beat, mid-transit on whichever job gets picked, or back at port if the job doesn't leave room for it.

Armory · live contender

Roomy, Trade order, no prerequisite. Stockpiles weapons and armor; a stocked Armory equips Bastion Defenders when the ship is attacked. Fits his combat-readiness angle directly, pays off hard if Two Chairs Short gets picked.

Barrack · live contender

Roomy, Recruit order, no prerequisite. Houses Bastion Defenders, Recruit adds more up to the space cap. The most literal answer to "who's actually defending the ship."

Sanctuary · listed for completeness

Roomy, Craft order, requires a Holy Symbol or Druidic Focus. Orena's focus already qualifies for it and she took Library and Arcane Study instead. Weakest fit for Aerion specifically.

Don't decide it for him

Present Armory and Barrack as the real contenders. Let the pick say something about how he thinks about the ship's defenses now that a real boarding threat may be on the table tonight.

The three postings, full text

Quiet Water · 1,300 gp · 15 days · 3 tons

House Prynne wants their eldest, Isolde, chaperoned to a betrothal meeting at the Larkspur Concourse. Complication: patriarch Osric Prynne has heard the Night Fury's name from somewhere he won't specify. The safe, low-danger pick.

The Ledger Run · 2,400 gp · 20 days · 5 tons

A Delverane clerk needs survey backlogs hand-carried between two Windward Edge stations. Complication: the Cutlass Sunday has been shadowing couriers since word got around this crew got jumped and won. Real ship-to-ship danger, calibrated fair.

Two Chairs Short · 5,600 gp total, ~2,800 gp to the crew · 22 days · 4 tons

A small outfit lost half its crew mid-contract and offers to split the fee for a second hull to run it jointly. Complication: an unmarked boarding hull with grappling jaws hitting soft targets in the Tollmarch. Highest pay and most danger this rotation, calibrated survivable specifically because it's a joint fight.

Standing: Open Season · pay scales, never rotates off

06

Ending the session

Close on whichever live thread the session's own events made loudest, don't force one the table's choices didn't earn.

If it stayed quiet

The Vellum note in Estra's quarters, the unshared Amberwake letters and House Carrow lead, or Bex's fate are all still sitting there, unresolved, and any one of them can carry the hook if nothing louder happened tonight.

If it went hot

Two Chairs Short's raider quietly deposits a data point toward the eventual Aeravellis reveal, don't name him, don't explain the hull, just let the pattern exist for whoever's paying attention.
