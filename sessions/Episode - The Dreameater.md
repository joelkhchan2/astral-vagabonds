# Episode -- The Dreameater (drop-in bottle episode)

> **A one-shot / half-session you can drop between any two missions.** Not on the contract spine; run it when you want a change of pace, ideally after a heavy beat (the cull, a family blow, a bad job). It's a **bottle episode** -- one location (the ship), the whole cast, no board, no port. Pure found-family workplace comedy wrapped around an eerie, gentle mystery, with **one arc clue smuggled inside.** Tone: PG to mild T. The horror is "there's something aboard we can't see," never gore. It resolves with *understanding*, not a kill. See `campaign/Story Spine.md` for what the clue means.

## The pitch

Something drifted aboard on the last dark leg and it is **eating the crew's dreams.** It is invisible -- truly invisible, not hiding -- until the crew works out how to perceive it. While it feeds, everyone sleeps badly: shared nightmares, memories that aren't theirs, mornings where two people swear they had the same dream. Tempers fray, competence slips, the ship starts making small mistakes. It is not malevolent. It is a lost, hungry animal that wandered in out of the cold, and the "monster hunt" turns, at the last beat, into a rescue.

**The creature -- the wistling** (ours; call it a *dream-lamprey* if a spacer's being crude). A palm-to-forearm-sized astral drifter, translucent as a jellyfish, all trailing filaments and a slow inner light. In the deep Astral it grazes the faint dream-residue that clings to sleeping minds the way plankton clings to a current -- normally harmless, a few motes off a hundred passing ships. This one is **young, alone, and starving**, blown off its drift into a guttering zone where there were no dreams to eat, and it followed the first warm, crowded, dreaming thing it found: the ship. It isn't hunting the crew. It's a duckling that imprinted on a food source. It doesn't know it's hurting anyone.

**Why it points at the arc (and at Orena):** dream-residue pools thickest around **grief and longing** -- the mind chewing the same loss over and over. The wistling drifts toward the saddest sleeper aboard, and that's Orena, still dreaming the night she lost her crew. Feeding on her, it doesn't just take the dream -- it *replays* it, dim and secondhand, into the others. And the dream it's been eating and echoing carries the one detail Orena has never been able to hold onto while awake: **the Vocalise, and a shape in the dark her waking mind can't reconstruct** (a bearing, a face, a word her lost crewmate said as the region sealed -- pick the clue you want to plant). The creature is an accidental *witness*, replaying evidence nobody asked for. (See "The smuggled clue" below.)

## The two questions that carry the episode

1. **How do we see something that's invisible?** (The investigation -- competence and cleverness, the hopium pleasure.)
2. **Once we can see it, what do we do with a scared, starving animal that means us no harm?** (The turn -- from hunt to rescue, the found-family heart.)

## The reveal -- how they learn to see it

The wistling is invisible to ordinary sight and even to most magic, because it half-exists in the dream-layer, not the waking one. **Two crew members can perceive it, and that's the key the players assemble** (this is the default; adjust to your table -- see the note):

- **Flurry sees it.** Aerion's baby silver dragon fixes, hackles up, on an empty corner of the galley and *will not stop staring* -- tracking something slowly across the room that nobody else can find. (Comedy first: everyone assumes Flurry's being a dramatic baby. Flurry is right.) A young dragon's senses reach a hair into the astral; to Flurry the thing is as obvious as a cat is to a dog.
- **Orena hears it.** As the sleeper it feeds on, Orena keeps waking to the faint edge of the **same note** -- the Vocalise -- and, half-asleep, can feel *where in the ship* it is. Awake, she can't. The clue is that her attunement to the water and the song is the sense that catches it.

**The players' job is to put those two together:** follow Flurry's stare, cross-check it against where Orena "heard" it, and rig a way to make it visible to everyone. Reward cleverness with multiple paths:
- **Make it visible:** flour/chalk dust in the air, steam, Brass's cooking smoke, a fine mist -- the filaments disturb it. Or douse the lights and catch its faint inner glow. Or *feed it on purpose* (someone sleeps, watched) and mark where the dream-residue bends toward.
- **Detect it:** *detect magic* shows a faint aberration-adjacent smudge; a sending/comprehend-style effect gets a wordless pulse of **hunger and fear**, not malice -- the first hint it's a scared animal, not a haunting.
- **Corner it:** it flees toward warmth and sleepers, not away -- so the crew realizes, uncomfortably, that the way to find it is to offer it exactly what it's been taking.

## The turn -- it's not a monster

The moment the crew gets a real look at it -- trembling, translucent, tangled in the galley rafters, dimming because it's still starving even while feeding -- the episode flips. **It's terrified of them.** The "hunt" was never a hunt. Now the problem is a rescue, and it has real texture:

- **You can't just put it out the airlock.** Back into the guttering dark, alone, it dies -- and the crew that just learned it's a frightened animal has to decide whether they're the kind of people who do that. (They are not. That's the point.)
- **It can't stay.** It's eating their sleep; a crew that can't rest can't fly, and the ship is already thin on everything. Kindness has a cost and the ledger is real (Aerion will say so).
- **The solve is understanding, not violence.** Options, all valid: navigate it to a living current where dream-residue actually pools (Orena's skill -- the cure and the personal are the same again); rig a "feeder" it can graze without draining a sleeper dry, then release it somewhere with a real drift; find it a pod of its own kind (a seed for later -- there's a *shoal* of these out there, and a healthy sky has room for them). The warm ending is the crew choosing to get it *home* instead of getting rid of it.

## The smuggled clue (the reason to run this)

While the wistling fed on Orena, it replayed her recurring dream into the whole crew -- so **the whole table has now seen the night she lost her crew, secondhand and dim.** Most of it is grief. But the dream carries one hard detail her waking mind has never been able to hold, and now other people saw it too. Plant **one** of these (whatever your Tier needs):

- **A bearing / a shape of the sky** as the region sealed -- something Orena can later match against a chart or the ancient instrument. (Points at *finding the Locker*.)
- **A last word from a specific crewmate** -- a name, a place, a "go back for --" cut off. (Points at M3 / the scattered-crew thread; ties to whoever Gabrielle named at session 0.)
- **The Vocalise itself, clearer than she's ever heard it awake** -- confirming for the table that her private note and the guttering are the same phenomenon. (Points at the Story Spine's core: her grief and the cosmic cure are one navigation.)

Play it as a gift the creature didn't know it was giving. Orena gets to decide how much she lets the crew have seen -- a real character beat about being known. **Do not over-explain it.** One image, offered once.

## Running it (beats)

1. **Cold, funny open.** Small malfunctions and short tempers: two crew snap over nothing, someone botches a routine job, Finny's convinced the ship is haunted (he's 80% joking, and 100% right). Establish that everyone slept badly and nobody's connecting it yet.
2. **The dreams escalate.** Two crew compare notes and realize they had *the same* dream. Then a third. The dream is somebody's grief and it isn't anybody's here (it's Orena's, but don't tell them that yet). Now it's a mystery, not a mood.
3. **Flurry knows.** The dragon's staring bit lands. Comedy, then the crew stops laughing.
4. **The investigation.** They rig a way to see it (their cleverness, multiple paths above). Big table energy -- everyone contributes a skill. This is the competence-porn stretch.
5. **The reveal and the turn.** They see it. It's terrified. The genre flips from hunt to rescue mid-scene.
6. **The choice.** Airlock it (they won't), keep it (they can't), or get it home (they will -- and Orena's navigation is the road). Let them solve it their way.
7. **The clue, and home.** The smuggled detail surfaces, Orena decides what to do with having been seen, the wistling drifts off toward a living current, and the crew has a warm, tired, slightly-changed dinner. Back to the workplace comedy. End on the crew, always.

## Tone guardrail

PG to mild T (matches `campaign/Story Spine.md`). The fear is *"something's aboard we can't see and it's in our heads a little"* -- uncanny, not grotesque. No possession, no gore, no one gets hurt in a lasting way; the nightmares are sad and strange, not traumatic. And it **lands in warmth**: the monster is a scared baby animal, the crew are the kind of people who take it home, and the last beat is dinner. If the table starts getting genuinely spooked, lean harder on Flurry and Finny until it's funny again.

## Adjustable knobs

- **Who perceives it:** default is Flurry-sees + Orena-hears. Swap freely -- Tack (talks to the ship, feels the "extra passenger"), a crew member with a dream-heavy background, or Aerion's insomniac ledger-keeping catching the pattern in the *errors* before anyone catches the cause. Whatever gives a quieter player the spotlight.
- **How arc-heavy:** run it as pure fluff (drop the smuggled clue, keep the rescue) if the campaign needs a breather, or load the clue if you want to advance Orena's thread. Both work.
- **The shoal seed:** mention, or don't, that there are more of these out in the deep -- a gentle hook for a future "we found where they live" wonder beat.
