# Episode: The Dreameater (drop-in bottle episode)

> **A one-shot or half-session you can drop between any two missions.** Not on the contract spine. It is a **bottle episode**: one location (the ship), the whole cast, no board, no port. Workplace comedy wrapped around an eerie mystery that turns into a real fight, with **one arc clue smuggled inside.** Tone: PG to mild T. The monster is a **Feyr** (CR 5) and it can be killed. Run it after a warm session, not a heavy one. See `campaign/Story Spine.md` for what the clue means.

## The pitch

Something drifted aboard on the last dark leg and it is **eating the crew's dreams.** It is invisible until the crew works out how to see it. While it feeds, everyone sleeps badly: shared nightmares, memories that aren't theirs, mornings where two people swear they had the same dream. Tempers fray, competence slips, the ship starts making small mistakes. Then the thing stops waiting for people to sleep.

**The creature: a Feyr** (*Boo's Astral Menagerie*, aberration, CR 5; stats in `references/Astral Sea Bestiary.md`). Spacers call it a **dreameater**. A grey, skull-shaped thing with five eyes and three mouths, no need for air, and the ability to turn itself invisible whenever it likes. It stows away in a ship and comes out at night to eat the fear off sleepers. It was starving out in the guttering dark, where nothing dreams, and it followed the first warm crowd of sleeping minds it found: the *Night Fury*. It has Int 4. It is an animal with a very bad appetite, and it will fight to keep eating.

**Why it points at Orena:** fear tastes strongest on grief and longing, the mind chewing the same loss over and over. The Feyr goes for the saddest sleeper aboard, and that is Orena, still dreaming the night she lost her crew. What it doesn't finish digesting leaks back out into everyone nearby, dim and secondhand. The dream it has been eating carries the one detail Orena has never been able to hold onto while awake: **the Vocalise, and a shape in the dark her waking mind can't reconstruct** (a bearing, a face, a word her lost crewmate said in the last seconds before the pull took the ship; pick the clue you want to plant). The creature is an accidental witness, holding evidence nobody asked for. See "The smuggled clue" below.

## The two questions that carry the episode

1. **How do we see something that's invisible?** (The investigation: competence and cleverness, the hopium pleasure.)
2. **How do we beat something that fights back, inside the ship we live in?** (The fight: nobody wants a hull breach, a wrecked galley or a ruined cargo hold.)

## Mechanics: how to run the Feyr

Pull the block from `references/Astral Sea Bestiary.md` (Feyr, CR 5): **AC 16, HP 88, fly 50 ft. (hover), Int 4, immune to frightened, darkvision 120 ft.** Multiattack is Bite plus Tentacle, plus at-will self **Invisibility** and **Nightmare Fuel (1/day)**. The Bite and Tentacle damage and the exact Nightmare Fuel text are not in this repo. **Check them against the book before running.** The rulings below are my suggestions, and adjust them at the table.

- **Nightmare Fuel is the nightly feed.** Once per night it feeds on one sleeper (it needs a sleeping or incapacitated target). The first nights it picks Orena. Ruling: a creature it fed on wakes drained and takes **one level of exhaustion**, removed by the next undisturbed long rest. This is the "everyone slept badly" mechanic, so the crew can feel the cost without anyone getting badly hurt.
- **Invisibility.** Check whether the book ends it when the Feyr attacks. If it does, **the first Bite is the reveal**, and it fades again on its next turn. If it doesn't, the crew needs the visibility tricks below to fight it at all. Either way it **hits and fades**: strike from the dark, retreat to the rafters or the crawlspace, come back.
- **Dust ruling.** A creature coated in flour, soot or fine mist shows as an outline. Attackers who can see the outline don't suffer the disadvantage for an invisible target, and the Feyr can't hide while coated. It can shake the coating off, which costs it its movement.
- **Immune to frightened.** Intimidation and fear effects do nothing. Pressure has to be physical.
- **No air needed.** The vacuum does not kill it. The airlock is a way to evict it, not to execute it.
- **Hot and cool dials.**
  - **Hotter:** it feeds on a second sleeper the same night, or it goes for the helm and the ship's course drifts while the crew chases it.
  - **Cooler:** at half HP it flees invisibly into the hull and stays hidden for a round, so the crew gets a breath and a plan. Fewer attacks per round while Brass, Oz or Estra are in the room to share its attention.
  - **Lethality:** it is built to threaten and not to kill. If someone drops, the Feyr's instinct is to feed, which gives the crew a turn to pull them out.

## The reveal: how they learn to see it

The Feyr is invisible to ordinary sight and most casual magic. **Two crew members can perceive it, and that is the key the players assemble** (default; adjust to your table):

- **Flurry sees it.** Aerion's baby silver dragon fixes, hackles up, on an empty corner of the galley and **will not stop staring**, tracking something slowly across the room that nobody else can find. (Comedy first: everyone assumes Flurry is being a dramatic baby. Flurry is right.) A young dragon's senses reach a hair into the astral.
- **Orena hears it.** As the sleeper it feeds on, Orena keeps waking to the faint edge of the **same note**, the Vocalise, and half-asleep she can feel *where in the ship* it is. Awake, she can't. What she does with that is hers.

**The players' job is to put those two together:** follow Flurry's stare, cross-check it against where Orena "heard" it, and rig a way to make it visible. Reward cleverness with multiple paths:
- **Make it visible:** flour or chalk dust in the air, steam, Brass's cooking smoke, a fine mist. The dust ruling above does the rest.
- **Detect it:** *detect magic* shows a faint aberration smudge in the rafters. A mind-reading effect gets a flat pulse of hunger with nothing behind it, no thought, no malice, no plan. That is the first sign it is an animal, and the first sign it won't negotiate.
- **Corner it:** it goes where the sleepers are. The crew works out, uncomfortably, that the way to find it is to offer it exactly what it has been taking, and the way to ambush it is to be awake while someone sleeps.

## The fight

It lairs somewhere in the ship, and the crew's job is to find out where. **Hiding spots (pick one):** the galley rafters over Brass's stores, the cargo hold behind Aerion's crates, the crawlspace under the helm. It fights where it lives.

**Environment (a ship interior is the hazard):**
- **Tight quarters.** A Large flyer in a corridor has no room to circle. Doors, hatches and narrow spaces are the crew's tools.
- **Cargo.** Crates are cover and also Aerion's inventory. Every stray fireball has a price.
- **The helm.** A Feyr that gets into the helm room puts the ship's course at risk.
- **The galley.** Open flame and loose flour. The dust is a visibility tool and a fire hazard in the same room.
- **The airlock.** A real option, not a trap. Getting a flying invisible thing to it is the puzzle.

**Multiple ways through (tactical, environmental, a mix):**
- **Fight it.** Dusted and cornered, it is a CR 5 with 88 HP. The crew's NPCs can each contribute (Estra's command, Tack and the ship's systems, Brass's kitchen, Finny's bad ideas, Oz). The PCs decide what they do.
- **Flush it out.** Vent steam or drop the lights to drive it toward the airlock, then open it. It survives and is gone. It may come back (see below).
- **Break its feeding.** It follows sleepers. A decoy sleeper with the crew waiting, or a sedated crew member as bait, ends the stalking and starts the fight on the crew's terms.

**When it dies:** it comes apart into a slow cloud of pale motes, the dream-residue it has been eating, and they drift out through the ship. Everyone aboard gets a half-second of a memory that was theirs, one they hadn't thought of in years. That is the **awe half** of the episode. Orena's motes are the dream, clearer than she has ever had it awake, and the smuggled clue lands here (see below).

**If it is driven off instead:** it was starving, so it doesn't stay gone. Seed for a later session (mine, speculative): it follows the *Night Fury*'s heat at a distance and tries again, hungrier.

## The smuggled clue (the reason to run this)

While the Feyr fed on Orena, it replayed her recurring dream into the whole crew, so **the whole table has now seen the night she lost her crew, secondhand and dim.** Most of it is grief. But the dream carries one hard detail her waking mind has never been able to hold, and now other people saw it too. Plant **one** of these (whatever your Tier needs):

- **A bearing or a shape of the sky** in the last seconds before the pull took them. Flavor and dread, and it doesn't point at anything mechanically findable. That thin place is long gone (`npcs/The Wayfinder Knot.md`).
- **A last word from a specific crewmate:** a name, a place, a "go back for" cut off. Whoever is named is alive and thriving on the *Steady Line*, and Orena doesn't know it yet.
- **The Vocalise itself, clearer than she has ever heard it awake.** Confirms for the table that her private note and the guttering are the same phenomenon. This points at the Story Spine's core: her grief and the cosmic cure are one navigation.

Play it as a gift the creature didn't know it was giving. Orena decides how much she lets the crew have seen, which is a real character beat about being known. **Do not over-explain.** One image, offered once.

## Running it (beats)

1. **Cold, funny open.** Small malfunctions and short tempers. Two crew snap over nothing, someone botches a routine job, Finny is convinced the ship is haunted (he's 80% joking and 100% right). Everyone slept badly and nobody is connecting it yet.
2. **The dreams escalate.** Two crew compare notes and realize they had the same dream. Then a third. The dream is somebody's grief and it isn't anybody's here. Now it's a mystery and not a mood.
3. **Flurry knows.** The dragon's staring bit lands. Comedy, then the crew stops laughing.
4. **The investigation.** They rig a way to see it. Everyone contributes a skill. This is the competence-porn stretch.
5. **First contact.** The Feyr strikes in the dark, the scary beat. Short. The reveal is a skull-face in a cloud of flour, and the table gets one real jump.
6. **The fight.** Cornered, dusted, shoved, burned or flushed, their call. Let them solve it their own way and ask how they did it.
7. **The release, and the clue.** It comes apart as light. The smuggled detail surfaces. Orena decides what to do with having been seen.
8. **A warm, tired dinner.** Back to the workplace comedy. End on the crew, always.

## Tone guardrail

PG to mild T (matches `campaign/Story Spine.md`). The fear is "something is aboard that we can't see, and it is in our heads a little," and then it has a face. No possession, no gore, no lasting harm: the exhaustion clears with a good night's rest, and the death is light and not blood. It **lands in warmth**: the crew is the kind of people who open every hatch and share the soup afterward.

**Counterweight to a hot table:** your Session 1 drift was toward the dark. If the fight starts getting grim, lean on Flurry, Finny and Brass until it is funny again. Cut the second feeding. Let the Feyr flee at half HP and give the crew a breath. Tell the table the nightmares are over and the soup is hot.

## Adjustable knobs

- **Who perceives it:** default is Flurry sees and Orena hears. Swap freely: Tack (talks to the ship, feels the "extra passenger"), a crew member with a dream-heavy background, or Aerion's insomniac ledger-keeping catching the pattern in the errors before anyone catches the cause. Pick whatever gives a quieter player the spotlight.
- **How arc-heavy:** run it as pure fluff (drop the smuggled clue, keep the fight) if the campaign needs a breather, or load the clue to advance Orena's thread.
- **How hard the fight is:** see the hot and cool dials under Mechanics. A table that just won a big fight wants the cooler version.
- **Where it came from:** it can be a stowaway from a derelict, or a starving thing from a guttering zone that followed the ship's warmth. The second ties it to the Story Spine and costs nothing.
